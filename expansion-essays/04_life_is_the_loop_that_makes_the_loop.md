# life is the loop that makes the loop

A living cell builds the membrane that allows it to keep building the membrane.

Its metabolism produces components.

Those components maintain the reactions that produce components.

The boundary separates the cell from the world.

The separated cell must exchange with the world to preserve the boundary.

Life begins to sound circular.

That is not a flaw in the explanation.

The circle may be the phenomenon.

Life is the loop that makes the loop.

---

The theory of autopoiesis was developed by Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela to describe the organization distinctive of living systems.

An autopoietic system continually produces the components that constitute the network producing those components.

The network also generates and maintains a boundary, and the boundary enables the network to remain a distinguishable unity.

The system does not merely contain a factory.

The factory produces the walls, tools, and workers that keep the factory identifiable as one system.

Self-production and boundary-production are one process.

---

Autopoiesis does not mean self-sufficiency.

A cell requires energy and matter from its environment.

It depends upon temperature ranges, chemical conditions, and ecological relations.

The closure is organizational, not material.

Matter flows through.

The pattern of production persists.

A whirlpool also preserves a pattern while water passes, but a living system actively produces the components and constraints enabling its own continued organization.

It does not merely endure flow.

It regulates the conditions under which flow can sustain identity.

The boundary is maintained by the activity it bounds.

---

Proclus uses a very different language for a structure that can appear unexpectedly similar.

In the *Elements of Theology*, he develops the category of the self-constituted, *authypostaton*.

A self-constituted being is capable of reverting upon itself. Its activity is not exhausted by passing outward into something else. It returns to its own source and, through that return, possesses itself.

For Proclus, souls are self-moving and self-reverting in a way bodies are not. This self-relation supports their separability and immortality within his metaphysical system.

The soul is not produced by assembling parts from outside.

It has a mode of causal self-possession.

---

The Greek idea is not equivalent to autopoiesis.

Proclus is describing incorporeal realities in a hierarchical cosmos. Autopoiesis describes molecular organization in living systems.

A cell’s self-production does not prove an immortal soul.

A self-constituted soul cannot be measured through membrane chemistry.

Yet both frameworks resist the same crude model.

A being is not fully explained as a product assembled by causes that remain entirely external to it.

The being participates in producing or constituting the unity through which external causes can affect it.

The receiver helps create the receiver.

---

This becomes clearer through reversion.

For Proclus, every effect remains in its cause, proceeds from it, and reverts toward it.

Reversion is not simply physical return.

A being turns toward the source of its unity and completion.

Self-reversion is the special case in which activity bends back upon the being itself.

The soul knows itself.

It moves itself.

Its outward action remains rooted in an inward relation.

To be self-constituted is not to have no cause above oneself. Proclus does not make souls absolute.

A self-constituted being depends upon higher causes while possessing a real internal causal loop.

It is not independent.

It is not merely passive.

---

Autopoietic closure has the same paradoxical shape.

The cell depends upon an environment it did not create.

But environmental inputs become parts of the living system only through the system’s own organization.

Nutrients do not enter a neutral container and announce their function.

The cell’s membrane, enzymes, gradients, and regulatory networks determine what counts as nutrient, toxin, signal, or waste.

The environment provides causes.

The organism interprets them through the boundary it maintains.

A cause becomes biologically meaningful only after entering a loop already organized around continued existence.

---

Recent work on autopoietic systems emphasizes that boundaries are not always simple walls.

There can be definitional boundaries, physical boundaries, and boundaries created through structural coupling with a niche.

An organism changes its surroundings.

The changed surroundings stabilize the organism.

A bacterium modifies a microniche.

A plant transforms soil, humidity, light, and microbial relations.

The effective boundary of life can extend into processes usually called external.

The loop recruits part of the world into the maintenance of the loop.

Selfhood becomes ecological.

---

This is where the image of a sealed individual fails.

The cell is distinct.

Its distinction is achieved through exchange.

The organism is autonomous.

Its autonomy is continuously supported by dependencies.

A living boundary does not end relation.

It organizes relation.

Proclus’s self-constituted soul is likewise not isolated from higher causes. Its self-reversion occurs inside participation.

The highest autonomy is not causeless independence.

It is the capacity to receive causes without being reduced to a passive surface.

A self has an inside because it can return effects into its own organization.

---

The connection becomes stranger when time is introduced.

A 2025 interdisciplinary paper on self-reference in living systems distinguishes physical “natural time” from representational time—the temporality of memory, anticipation, learning, and prediction that living systems generate.

A living system does not only persist from moment to moment.

It acts relative to what has happened and what may happen.

Its current state can represent previous disturbances and possible futures.

The loop therefore cannot be a static circle.

It unfolds.

The system returns to itself as something changed by its own history.

Self-reference becomes a spiral.

---

Development makes this obvious.

An embryo produces tissues that alter the conditions of later development.

The early organism changes the landscape through which the later organism must pass.

It acts upon the rules of its own future action.

Metamorphosis is even more dramatic.

One body reorganizes itself into another while preserving enough continuity for the process to remain one life.

The system must destroy structures that once maintained it and build structures able to maintain a new identity.

Autopoiesis includes controlled self-interruption.

The loop survives by changing what the loop contains.

---

Proclus’s metaphysical reversion is more stable.

The soul returns toward its source and true being rather than rewriting itself through molecular development.

Yet his system also treats self-knowledge as constitutive.

A being becomes more fully itself by turning toward the principle through which it is what it is.

Identity is not mere persistence.

It is successful relation to source.

The living analogy suggests a naturalized version:

An organism remains itself by continually re-establishing the conditions under which its activity counts as one organized life.

Both accounts make selfhood an achievement rather than a lump.

---

The analogy breaks at consciousness.

Autopoiesis alone does not establish subjective awareness. A system may be organizationally closed without experiencing itself.

Proclus’s soul is intrinsically living, knowing, and incorporeal.

The terms “self” and “self-reference” operate at different levels.

To say a cell produces itself functionally is not to say it possesses reflective self-consciousness.

The comparison should sharpen categories, not blur them.

There may be continuities among self-maintenance, agency, cognition, and consciousness.

There may also be decisive transitions.

The existence of a loop does not tell us what it feels like from inside.

---

Still, life’s circularity challenges ordinary causal grammar.

We ask:

What made the organism?

Parents.

Genes.

Chemistry.

Evolution.

Environment.

All are correct.

But once alive, the organism becomes one of the causes maintaining the organism.

It repairs damage, selects inputs, changes behavior, constructs niches, and modifies its own future probabilities.

The effect enters the causal story as an agent.

What was produced begins producing the conditions of its production.

Linear explanation bends.

---

This has practical consequences for medicine.

A machine can often be repaired by replacing a broken component.

A living system responds to intervention from inside its own regulatory loops.

The same drug can have different effects depending upon physiological state, history, and feedback.

Healing is not always direct construction.

Sometimes the system must be persuaded, destabilized, retrained, or given conditions under which its own repair loops can resume.

The physician does not stand outside inert matter.

Treatment enters an agent that interprets treatment.

---

The same structure appears in character.

A habit produces actions.

The actions reinforce the identity supporting the habit.

“I am this kind of person” becomes a boundary selecting which experiences count as possible.

The loop can stabilize a life.

It can also trap it.

Change requires more than inserting one new thought. The network producing the old boundary must begin producing another.

A different action.

A different environment.

A different relation.

Identity changes when the loop starts making evidence for a new loop.

---

A living cell builds the membrane that allows it to keep building the membrane.

The sentence sounds circular because life is not a product finished once.

It is a pattern continually returning through its own activity.

Proclus calls the deepest self-related beings self-constituted.

Biology calls a different kind of closure autopoietic.

Do not confuse soul with cell.

Notice the shared disturbance.

A true individual is not merely bounded from outside.

It participates in producing the boundary through which it becomes an individual.

Life is the loop that makes the loop.

And death begins when the process can no longer return to itself strongly enough to remain one.
