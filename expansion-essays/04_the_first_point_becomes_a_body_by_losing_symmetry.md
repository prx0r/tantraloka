# the first point becomes a body by losing symmetry

A perfect point has no direction.

No front.

No back.

No inside large enough to divide.

It can be centre, but it cannot yet be a body.

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* begins with bindu: the concentrated union of Śiva and Śakti.

From this point, triangles appear.

From triangles, mandalas.

From mandalas, letters, senses, worlds, and bodily centres.

Creation begins when undivided possibility loses symmetry.

The first point becomes a body by losing symmetry.

---

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* is a compressed Śrīvidyā text organized around three “signals” or encoded domains:

cakra;

mantra;

pūjā.

Diagram.

Sound.

Ritual enactment.

Its first chapter explains the Śrīcakra through an interplay of five Śaktis and four fires.

Their union produces the nine triangles.

The central bindu is described through several triads: subject, object, and means of knowledge; will, knowledge, and action; fire, moon, and sun.

The point is not empty.

It is a compressed relation before relation has expanded into visible geometry.

---

The site’s summary describes Śakti first appearing as a pulsating essence associated with vowels.

Sound differentiates.

The central point flowers into the nine enclosures of the Śrīcakra.

Letters correspond to levels of the diagram.

The goddess, mantra, yantra, body, and cosmos are treated as different presentations of one patterned power.

The system is not constructed by attaching independent symbols after the fact.

The same generative relation appears geometrically, phonically, ritually, and bodily.

One process changes medium.

---

Symmetry provides a modern language for the opening movement.

A perfectly symmetric state has multiple equivalent directions.

No position is privileged.

A circular colony may initially possess no obvious axis.

Development requires a break.

One region becomes different.

A gradient forms.

Cells encounter unequal signals.

The first asymmetry makes subsequent organization possible.

Head and tail.

Inside and outside.

Left and right.

One pattern begins because the system can no longer remain equally available to every pattern.

Form is possibility narrowed into direction.

---

Organoids and gastruloids allow researchers to study this transition.

These are cell assemblies grown under controlled conditions that reproduce selected features of development.

They do not become complete organisms.

But they can self-organize, establish axes, differentiate regions, and produce reproducible patterns without an external engineer positioning every cell.

A 2025 arXiv study examined two-dimensional stem-cell-derived gastruloids.

Small colonies spontaneously broke symmetry.

Larger colonies remained centre-symmetric under the reported conditions.

Colony size changed which pattern was stable.

The system’s geometry helped decide whether a privileged direction would emerge.

---

The researchers combined imaging with a Turing activator–repressor model and reactive boundaries.

In such models, interacting signals spread at different rates and regulate one another.

A local fluctuation can grow instead of disappearing.

Activator encourages pattern.

Inhibitor constrains its spread.

Boundaries reflect, absorb, produce, or reshape signals.

The final pattern depends not only upon molecular components but upon domain size and edge conditions.

The colony does not contain a tiny map of its future axis.

Its form emerges from dynamics that make some perturbations self-amplifying.

The centre becomes unstable enough to produce direction.

---

This resembles the bindu only at a structural level.

The Tantric point is a metaphysical and ritual concentration of consciousness and power.

The gastruloid centre is a spatial region within a physical experiment.

The nine triangles are not Turing patterns.

Vowels are not morphogens.

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* did not predict organoid biology.

But both systems force the same conceptual reversal:

A point is not the simplest finished object.

It can be the unstable compression from which organized difference emerges.

The centre is productive because relations are folded into it.

---

The relation between size and symmetry is especially important.

We often imagine developmental rules as scale-free instructions.

Do this, then that.

But cells interact through diffusion lengths, mechanical stress, electrical coupling, and contact networks.

A rule that creates one pattern in a small domain can create another in a large one.

The whole changes the meaning of local signals.

A molecule does not carry one command independent of geometry.

Its effect depends upon where it is, how far it spreads, and what boundary returns its influence.

The mandala is not merely content.

Its enclosure changes causation.

---

The Śrīcakra makes enclosure explicit.

An outer square.

Lotus circles.

Intersecting triangles.

A central bindu.

Practice may move from outside inward or inside outward.

Each level is defined through relation to centre and perimeter.

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* describes presiding forms of Tripurasundarī for each enclosure, with distinct mantras and powers.

The same goddess is differentiated by position within the whole.

A part receives identity from the mandala it inhabits.

This resembles developmental positional information.

A cell’s fate depends not only upon what it contains, but upon where it stands within a field.

---

Topological defects add another bridge.

A 2025 arXiv study of multicellular rosettes modelled clusters in which five or more cells meet around a common centre.

The researchers found that local symmetry breaking through curvature-inducing defects could favour rosette formation, while tissue fluidity, cell deformability, and alignment affected stability.

They compared the model with neural populations derived from induced pluripotent stem cells.

The rosette is visually close to mandala geometry.

That resemblance is not evidence of historical or metaphysical identity.

Its value is mechanistic.

Centres can arise because local packing cannot remain uniform.

Geometry creates an organizational demand.

---

A defect sounds like failure.

In pattern formation, a defect can become a centre.

A uniform sheet cannot organize every relation without tension.

Curvature accumulates.

Cells reorient.

A rosette appears.

The same principle recurs in crystals, liquid crystals, tissues, and fields.

Broken uniformity creates a site around which order reorganizes.

The “imperfection” is not outside the pattern.

It is the event through which a new scale of pattern becomes possible.

Creation may require a local impossibility in the old symmetry.

---

The organoid study also reported a transition in growth dynamics.

Early expansion followed one regime.

Later growth approached arrest through another.

The shift resembled a dynamical phase transition: the same components entered a new collective mode once size, feedback, and boundary conditions crossed a threshold.

This matters because form does not always grow by adding more of the same.

A system can accumulate quietly and then change its governing pattern.

The centre that once preserved symmetry can become the condition for symmetry breaking.

The mandala image captures this threshold aesthetically.

Each enclosure is not merely another ring added to an unchanged point.

The expansion changes what the centre can mean.

---

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* gives this emergence a different emotional tone.

The bindu is union, pulsation, desire, and self-awareness.

Difference does not arise because perfection fails.

It arises because power expresses itself.

The triangles are not damage to unity.

They are unity becoming capable of relation.

Subject, object, and knowing emerge without becoming absolutely separate.

The diagram preserves their common centre even while multiplying distinctions.

Creation is symmetry breaking without metaphysical exile.

The point remains present in every enclosure as origin and orientation.

---

Developmental biology cannot make that metaphysical claim.

Once symmetry breaks in an embryo, physical processes produce real asymmetries.

One region is not secretly identical to every other region in function.

The head cannot be treated as interchangeable with the tail.

Yet the organism retains unity at a higher level.

Different tissues participate in one body.

The centre does not erase specialization.

It coordinates difference.

This is where the Tantric image becomes useful without becoming scientific explanation.

Unity is not uniformity.

A body is one because its parts are different in a coordinated way.

---

The same applies psychologically.

A person seeking perfect inner symmetry may try to eliminate conflict, preference, and direction.

But action requires asymmetry.

One value must become more important.

One path must be taken.

One boundary must form.

The problem is not differentiation.

It is differentiation that forgets the whole.

A healthy centre allows parts to specialize without declaring independence.

A rigid ego tries to become the only centre.

A fragmented life contains many local centres unable to coordinate.

The mandala offers another image:

centre as relation rather than domination.

---

A perfect point has no direction.

The *Yoginīhṛdaya* imagines bindu as concentrated power before visible division.

Organoid science shows physical collectives generating axes when symmetry becomes unstable.

The traditions do not prove one another.

They meet in a structural insight.

Form begins when equal possibility is broken by a relation that can propagate.

The first point becomes a body by losing symmetry.

Not by abandoning unity.

By translating unity into differences organized strongly enough to remain one.
