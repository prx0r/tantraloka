# life survives by never becoming fully stable

A dead system can be perfectly still.

A living system cannot afford that kind of peace.

Temperature shifts.

Membranes leak.

Signals arrive.

Cells divide.

Attention moves.

The organism must preserve itself while continually changing the processes through which preservation occurs.

Too much order becomes rigidity.

Too much disorder becomes dissolution.

The *Spandakārikā* names reality through *spanda*—the subtle pulse of consciousness.

Complexity science approaches a related problem through metastability and criticality.

Life survives by never becoming fully stable.

---

Spanda does not mean a physical vibration measurable in hertz.

The tradition does not claim that ultimate consciousness shakes like a string.

Spanda is the dynamic power through which awareness expands, contracts, manifests, and reabsorbs experience while remaining itself.

Every perception contains movement.

Attention opens toward an object.

Thought contracts around a distinction.

Desire projects a future.

Recognition returns the movement to its source.

The pulse is not one event inside consciousness.

It is consciousness’s capacity to become events without losing continuity.

---

Kṣemarāja’s Spanda tradition repeatedly directs attention toward thresholds.

The instant between one cognition and another.

The turn between outward activity and inward awareness.

The moment when intense emotion interrupts ordinary thought.

The beginning and ending of desire.

These intervals matter because the usual organization of experience temporarily loosens.

The old state has weakened.

The new state has not fully stabilized.

The practitioner is not asked to worship confusion.

The interval is valuable because multiple organizations become possible there.

The pulse becomes visible at the transition.

---

Metastability gives this a scientific analogue.

A metastable state is locally stable but not final.

The system can remain there for a significant time, resist small disturbances, and then transition to another state.

A protein conformation.

A neural assembly.

A social consensus.

A chemical phase.

Metastability allows temporary order without permanent fixation.

The system holds a pattern long enough to function and releases it when conditions change.

A mind with no stable states cannot think.

A mind unable to leave a stable state cannot learn.

---

Criticality is related but not identical.

Near a critical transition, systems can become highly sensitive, display long-range correlations, and support fluctuations across many scales.

The popular phrase “edge of chaos” suggests that living systems optimize themselves at one universal sweet spot between order and disorder.

The evidence is more complicated.

Criticality has been reported in neural activity, gene regulation, collective behaviour, and other biological systems.

But power laws and broad fluctuations do not by themselves prove criticality, and not every function benefits from maximal sensitivity.

The criticality hypothesis remains influential and contested.

Life may use several dynamical regimes rather than one universal edge.

---

A useful distinction is this:

criticality concerns behaviour near a transition.

metastability concerns temporary residence among states.

A system can display metastable switching without being exactly critical.

It can approach criticality only during specific tasks.

It can regulate distance from instability according to context.

Robust when damage threatens.

Sensitive when information is scarce.

Flexible when the environment changes.

Stable when coordination matters.

The system changes how changeable it is.

---

A 2025 arXiv paper on allostatic control of persistent states models this directly in a spiking neural network.

The authors couple an allostatic regulatory circuit to a ring attractor so that a persistent activity pattern can move when reference conditions change.

An attractor normally stabilizes one representation.

Allostatic control changes the target.

The network preserves a coherent state while relocating it.

The result is not evidence for Spanda metaphysics.

It demonstrates a general principle:

adaptation requires control over stability itself.

The system must know, functionally, when to hold and when to shift.

---

Another current framework models living components through time-varying energy landscapes.

The interactions among components alter the landscapes guiding each component’s behaviour.

The parts adapt.

Their relations change.

Those changed relations alter future adaptation.

Function emerges because the landscape is not fixed.

This is complexity in a strong sense.

The rules shaping local motion are themselves affected by the motion.

The map changes beneath the traveller.

Living dynamics are historical because every transition can modify which transitions become easy next.

---

Spanda philosophy begins from the same intuition at an ontological level.

Consciousness does not move inside a pre-existing container.

Its activity articulates the field in which movement appears.

Expansion produces objectivity.

Contraction produces subjectivity.

Neither is final.

The pulse is self-referential.

The power that creates a state is present in the transition that dissolves it.

This is why liberation is not one permanently frozen state of mystical absorption.

A realization incapable of surviving movement would remain another metastable enclosure.

Recognition must include the return to activity.

---

The term *unmeṣa* means opening or unfolding.

*Nimeṣa* means closing.

The world appears through their interplay.

Opening without closing would scatter into unlimited differentiation.

Closing without opening would leave no manifested experience.

The pulse requires both.

Complexity science would describe a system balancing integration and segregation, exploration and exploitation, persistence and change.

The Tantric account treats these not merely as engineering trade-offs but as expressions of freedom.

Reality is alive because unity can differentiate and difference can return.

---

This offers a better interpretation of emotional instability.

Not every fluctuation is spiritual pulsation.

Rapid shifts can be painful, dysregulated, or dangerous.

Metastability is functional only when transitions remain coordinated.

A nervous system trapped in threat may switch repeatedly without reaching safety.

A depressive system may become too deep an attractor.

An anxious system may become excessively sensitive to small perturbations.

Health is not movement for its own sake.

It is an enlarged repertoire of states with reliable pathways among them.

Freedom requires both mobility and recoverability.

---

Valence helps select the pathways.

A state feels better or worse relative to the organism’s needs and expectations.

Positive valence can indicate movement toward preferred conditions.

Negative valence can indicate worsening control, unexpected error, or threat to viability.

These interpretations are not settled into one universally accepted formula.

But valence clearly changes the landscape.

Pleasure stabilizes some actions.

Pain destabilizes others.

Fear narrows exploration.

Curiosity can make uncertainty attractive.

The organism does not move neutrally among states.

It is pulled and repelled through a terrain of concern.

---

The Spanda tradition would say desire, fear, anger, and astonishment can reveal the pulse because strong valence reorganizes the field quickly.

The object becomes total.

Then the state breaks.

In that break, awareness may recognize itself as larger than the configuration that briefly occupied it.

The method is not to intensify harmful emotion deliberately.

It is to notice that even the most convincing state has an onset, shape, and dissolution.

The state moves.

The capacity in which movement appears does not need to become the state.

---

Integrated-information research has also been used pragmatically to compare dynamical complexity across systems.

One study found that measures from integrated information decomposition captured signatures of metastability, criticality, distributed computation, and emergent structures across several model classes.

This does not prove a theory of consciousness.

It suggests that complexity involves more than local activity or global order alone.

Interesting systems combine information that is shared, unique, redundant, and synergistic.

The whole does something no isolated part can do, while parts retain differentiated contributions.

Complexity lives between total fusion and total independence.

---

That sentence could describe the Spanda cosmos.

If every appearance collapsed into one undifferentiated light, no world would arise.

If every appearance were absolutely separate, no consciousness could relate them.

The pulse preserves both.

Unity differentiates without fragmentation.

Difference returns without erasure.

The system remains one by moving among forms rather than by preventing forms.

The highest stability is not immobility.

It is the capacity to survive transition.

---

Practice therefore changes its goal.

Do not seek a state that never changes.

Seek recognition that remains through changing states.

Calm arises.

Calm passes.

Insight arises.

Insight becomes memory.

Activity resumes.

The question is whether the centre of identity must be rebuilt from each temporary attractor.

If awareness identifies completely with the state, every transition feels like death.

If awareness recognizes the pulse, transition becomes one of its powers.

---

A dead system can be perfectly still.

A living system persists by regulating how it leaves one condition and enters another.

Complexity science calls some of this metastability, criticality, attractor switching, and adaptive landscape change.

The *Spandakārikā* calls the deepest dynamic *spanda*.

The languages should not be collapsed.

The shared lesson is exact:

life survives by never becoming fully stable.

Not because stability is false.

Because a stable form is alive only while it remains able to answer the next disturbance.
