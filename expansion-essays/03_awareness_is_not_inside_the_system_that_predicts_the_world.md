# awareness is not inside the system that predicts the world

A living system must remain partly hidden from the world to remain itself.

Its internal states cannot be directly driven by every external fluctuation.

A membrane filters.

A nervous system samples.

A Markov blanket formalizes the statistical partition through which inside and outside influence one another indirectly.

The system survives by not being completely open.

Awareness appears to do the opposite.

Every boundary, sensation, and hidden state becomes present within it.

The system requires separation.

Awareness is the field in which separation is known.

Awareness is not inside the system that predicts the world.

---

Active inference begins with limited access.

External states cannot be read directly by internal states.

They influence sensory states.

Internal dynamics respond to those sensory states and generate active states that alter the external world.

The system must infer hidden causes from partial signals.

A brain does not encounter photons as objects already labelled “tree.”

It receives patterns produced through receptors and neural pathways.

A cell does not perceive the full chemical environment.

It encounters receptor states, membrane voltage, molecular concentrations, and mechanical changes.

To act, the system builds or embodies a model of what lies beyond its blanket.

Its world is mediated from the beginning.

---

This mediation is not a defect.

Without selective access, no organism could maintain organization.

Every environmental change would enter directly.

No controlled interior would exist.

The blanket creates a statistical veil and a workable interface.

Because the system receives limited information, it can compress uncertainty into action-relevant distinctions.

Food.

Threat.

Temperature.

Partner.

Obstacle.

The organism does not need the world in itself.

It needs enough structure to preserve and extend its form.

A model is not a failed copy of reality.

It is a tool shaped by the costs of embodiment.

---

Pratyabhijñā philosophy also begins with manifestation rather than an unmediated object existing entirely on its own.

Utpaladeva and Abhinavagupta call an appearance *ābhāsa*.

The blue vase appears.

The body appears.

A thought appears.

The experience of being an observer behind the eyes appears.

No object becomes knowable independently of the event through which it shines to a subject.

But the Śaiva conclusion differs sharply from active inference.

The subject is not merely a hidden internal system constructing beliefs about external causes.

The deepest subject is consciousness itself—the luminous and reflexive field in which internal, external, sensory, and active states all become manifest.

---

This is the first place where easy synthesis fails.

A Markov blanket divides variables inside a model.

Pratyabhijñā consciousness is not another variable inside that partition.

If awareness were simply the internal states, sensory states would remain external to awareness until internally represented.

Yet sensory states, representations, bodily feelings, and the distinction “internal” are all experienced.

The lived field does not present itself as one hidden node inferred from another.

It presents the entire relation.

The blanket can explain why the organism lacks direct causal access to external states.

It does not by itself explain why mediated access appears as experience.

The veil explains limitation.

It does not explain light.

---

Active-inference theorists increasingly address consciousness directly.

A 2024 preprint on the minimal theory of consciousness implicit in active inference identifies commitments shared across models: hierarchical generative processes, temporally deep inference, counterfactual structure, precision control, and global coordination.

These proposals can generate testable hypotheses about conscious access, reportability, perceptual organization, and state changes.

They matter.

But they remain computational and functional.

A model can describe which information becomes globally available or integrated without settling why availability has a felt character.

The gap should not be dismissed merely because the formalism is broad.

A map of every route through a city is not yet the fact that the city is visible.

---

Pratyabhijñā begins where this gap becomes the foundation.

Consciousness is *prakāśa*, illumination.

But illumination without reflexivity would resemble a lamp that reveals objects without knowing that it reveals.

Consciousness is therefore also *vimarśa*—self-apprehension, the power by which illumination is present to itself.

This does not require a second observer watching the first.

Such a structure would create an endless regress.

Self-luminosity means that awareness does not need to infer its own occurrence through another representation.

Pain may be misinterpreted.

Its presence is not inferred.

A thought may be false.

Its appearing is immediate.

Awareness does not wait for evidence that awareness is happening.

---

The active-inference system, by contrast, is built from inference.

Internal states parameterize beliefs about hidden causes.

Prediction error changes the model.

Action changes sensory evidence.

The organism remains viable by continuously updating or fulfilling expectations.

This loop can include self-models.

A human can infer bodily condition, social identity, agency, and emotional state.

The model may include the claim “I am aware.”

But the Pratyabhijñā philosopher asks:

To what does this self-model appear?

A model of the self is another appearance.

Its accuracy can change.

The presence of the model cannot be explained by the content it represents.

---

This does not refute active inference.

The two accounts answer different questions.

Active inference asks how bounded systems maintain themselves and guide action under uncertainty.

Pratyabhijñā asks what makes any bounded system, uncertainty, model, and action manifest.

One is a formal and scientific research programme.

The other is a metaphysics and phenomenology of consciousness.

The scientific model can be correct about organismic access even if consciousness is fundamental.

The Śaiva metaphysics can be internally coherent without predicting which neural circuits control precision.

Conflict appears only when one account claims to exhaust the question addressed by the other.

---

The bridge becomes useful through the concept of contraction.

A Markov blanket limits direct coupling.

A living agent receives the world through a restricted interface.

Kashmir Śaivism describes finite subjectivity as *saṃkoca*, contraction.

Universal consciousness appears as one knower with limited knowledge, limited agency, one body, one place, and one temporal horizon.

The resemblance is real.

Both finite agents are defined through restricted access and control.

Both act from incomplete information.

Both construct a meaningful world from partial relation.

But the direction differs.

Active inference builds the agent from organized dynamics.

Śaivism interprets organized dynamics as a local form of consciousness’s self-limitation.

---

Imagine a castle with narrow windows.

The windows protect the interior and define what can be seen.

A Markov blanket explains the architecture of the openings: which signals enter, which actions leave, and how the inside remains statistically distinct.

Pratyabhijñā asks about the daylight by which castle, window, landscape, and observer are visible.

The daylight is not located in one room.

Nor does it erase the wall.

A broken wall changes the system.

Light does not need the wall to become light.

This is why liberation cannot mean simply destroying the organism’s blanket.

Without controlled boundaries, the body dies.

Recognition changes identity, not cellular permeability.

---

This distinction protects spiritual practice from biological confusion.

One might hear “the self is not bounded” and neglect bodily limits.

Sleep, nutrition, safety, consent, and psychological regulation remain conditions of the finite organism.

Universal awareness does not make the nervous system invulnerable.

A person can recognize spacious consciousness while experiencing trauma, illness, or distorted prediction.

The Markov-blanketed organism still requires care.

Nonduality does not abolish homeostasis.

It changes the claim that homeostatic selfhood is the whole of what awareness is.

---

It also protects science from metaphysical inflation.

A Markov blanket is not evidence that the universe is conscious.

Conditional independence does not entail self-luminosity.

Nested blankets do not automatically produce nested subjects of experience.

A tissue can possess a statistical boundary without a private inner world.

A model may identify candidate structures associated with agency or consciousness.

It cannot simply define experience into existence.

The word “internal” is dangerous here.

Internal to the model does not mean inwardly experienced.

Geometry has an inside without feeling enclosed.

---

The practical intersection lies in observing self-models without confusing them with awareness.

The body predicts danger.

A thought says:

I am unsafe.

Attention narrows.

Action prepares withdrawal.

These processes belong to the organism’s attempt to maintain viable states.

They should not be dismissed.

But the thought, bodily contraction, and prediction error all appear.

Recognition creates a small freedom.

The content is real as content.

It does not become the final definition of the field knowing it.

The model can update because awareness is not identical to one prediction.

---

Active inference describes change through new evidence, revised priors, and different policies.

Pratyabhijñā describes liberation through recognition of the subject in which every model appears.

Combined carefully, they suggest two movements.

First, improve the organism’s models.

Test fear.

Correct harmful expectations.

Create safer action loops.

Second, notice that no model, however accurate, contains the awareness to which accuracy appears.

The first movement heals finite prediction.

The second loosens metaphysical captivity to the predictor.

Neither replaces the other.

---

A living system must remain partly hidden from the world to remain itself.

Its blanket makes an inside possible.

Awareness does not need to cross that blanket because the blanket itself appears within awareness.

This does not mean consciousness magically reaches through every physical barrier.

It means the concepts “internal,” “external,” and “mediating boundary” are present as distinctions inside the field of experience and inquiry.

Awareness is not inside the system that predicts the world.

The person is.

The organism is.

The model is.

Awareness is the light in which the entire prediction loop becomes visible—

and recognition begins when the loop stops claiming to be the source of the light.
