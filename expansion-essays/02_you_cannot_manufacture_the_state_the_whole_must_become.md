# you cannot manufacture the state the whole must become

A magnet does not become magnetized because one atom receives an inspiring instruction.

Temperature falls.

Interactions become strong enough.

Local spins begin aligning.

A collective pattern appears.

The order was impossible to locate in any isolated part before the transition.

You can change the conditions.

You cannot command the emergent state one particle at a time.

The *Akulavīra Tantra* makes an equally severe claim about realization.

Ritual, breath control, chakra concentration, pilgrimage, mantra, and philosophical argument do not manufacture the supreme condition.

The real path is *sahaja*—spontaneous—and its sign is *samarasa*, complete assimilation.

You cannot manufacture the state the whole must become.

---

The text belongs to the Matsyendranātha-related corpus preserved with the *Kaulajñānanirṇaya* materials.

Its stance is deliberately radical.

It dismisses rival systems, ritual paraphernalia, austerity, alchemy, and even yogic techniques as insufficient for realization of Akulavīra.

“Akula” names Śiva beyond the manifested cluster of powers.

“Kula” names Śakti as the cluster.

Their living non-separation is *samarasa*: one taste, perfect assimilation, equilibrium without the erasure of manifestation.

The text distinguishes an artificial path, *kṛtaka*, from the spontaneous, *sahaja*.

The distinction is not between effort and laziness.

It is between constructing a state and recognizing an order no isolated technique can produce by itself.

---

Complexity science calls certain macroscopic variables **order parameters**.

A fluid has countless molecular positions and velocities.

Near a phase transition, a small set of collective variables can capture the large-scale organization.

Magnetization summarizes alignment.

Density difference distinguishes liquid and gas.

The order parameter is not another microscopic component.

It is a property of the coordinated whole.

Once it appears, it can govern the behaviour of parts in return.

The macrostate becomes causally relevant.

---

This is why changing a control parameter differs from controlling the order parameter directly.

Temperature, pressure, coupling strength, or external field can alter the conditions under which collective order becomes stable.

The experimenter can cool the magnet.

They do not separately instruct every spin to align.

Near the transition, fluctuations explore possibilities.

Above a threshold, local interactions reinforce one another and the system settles into an ordered phase.

The control parameter prepares.

The order parameter emerges.

Confusing these produces a fantasy of direct control over collective organization.

---

The *Akulavīra* attacks the spiritual form of this fantasy.

Breath retention becomes a control parameter.

Posture becomes a control parameter.

Mantra repetition becomes a control parameter.

Study, diet, ritual, attention, and relationship to a teacher alter the conditions of the person.

They may increase stability, sensitivity, or receptivity.

But the ego then assumes that enough manipulation should produce realization as a manufactured output.

The finite controller attempts to assemble the condition in which its claim to central control becomes transparent.

The project defeats itself.

---

Current computational work tries to discover collective variables in systems too high-dimensional to understand microscopically.

A 2025 arXiv paper learns low-dimensional variables that preserve transition rates between metastable molecular states.

Another learns variables while respecting translational, rotational, and permutation symmetries.

The purpose is not merely compression.

A useful collective variable must preserve what matters dynamically:

which states are long-lived;

which transitions are possible;

how often one state becomes another.

A bad summary looks simple and destroys the process.

A good order parameter captures the system’s real possibilities of transformation.

---

This offers a rigorous interpretation of *samarasa*.

Samarasa is not a pleasant mood added to the person.

It is a collective organization in which previously conflicting distinctions become phases of one functioning whole.

Body and awareness.

Kula and Akula.

Action and stillness.

Student and guru.

The text even collapses meditator, meditation, and object of meditation into one field.

The state is macroscopic.

One thought cannot contain it.

One sensation cannot certify it.

Its presence is inferred from how the entire system moves.

---

Causal-emergence theory makes the claim stronger.

Erik Hoel’s 2025 “Causal Emergence 2.0” argues that different scales can make unique causal contributions even when the system is microscopically reducible.

A macroscale description may lose microscopic detail yet preserve causal structure more effectively.

The macrostate is not supernatural.

It is a scale at which interventions and effects become more intelligible.

A computer program is physically realized in circuitry.

The program-level description still captures causes the transistor-level description obscures.

Reduction is possible.

Reduction can remain causally lossy.

---

The Akulavīra implication is that realization may be causally real at the level of the whole person without being one hidden substance.

A practitioner can possess correct doctrines while the organism remains organized around fear, acquisition, and separation.

One subsystem has changed.

The order parameter has not.

Conversely, a global shift can reorganize thought, perception, value, and action together.

No single component is the realization.

Their relations have entered another regime.

The state exists at the scale of coordinated life.

---

This explains why reports of realization are difficult to evaluate.

A person can reproduce the vocabulary.

Display altered affect.

Enter unusual states.

Perform rituals.

None necessarily reveals the order parameter.

The strongest evidence appears through cross-context stability.

Does the recognition remain under conflict?

Does it enlarge rather than narrow responsiveness?

Does it reduce compulsive self-defence without damaging practical judgment?

Can the person return from absorption into ordinary relation?

A collective variable earns its status by preserving transition structure.

A realization earns trust by organizing the transitions of life.

A collective variable can also remain hidden from every local observer. Each component follows its own rule, while the macroscopic order becomes visible only when the system is viewed across enough space and time. This is another reason realization cannot be certified by one sensation or thought: the relevant evidence may be the long-range coordination of the life rather than the intensity of any internal episode.

---

The text’s rejection of technique should therefore not be read as empirical advice to abandon training.

Its polemic attacks causal inflation.

A method can become necessary without becoming sufficient.

Cooling is necessary for one phase transition.

Cooling does not invent magnetism.

Meditation may reduce noise.

Ritual may reorganize value.

A teacher may introduce a new reference frame.

These alter the landscape.

The state remains *sahaja* because, when it appears, it belongs to the whole rather than to the isolated action credited with producing it.

---

The distinction resembles spontaneous symmetry breaking.

Before the transition, several macroscopic organizations may be possible.

The laws are symmetric.

The realized state selects one orientation.

Nothing in the local rule alone names the final direction.

History and fluctuation matter.

Afterward, the chosen pattern becomes stable and shapes local behaviour.

In practice, many possible identities remain latent.

Training weakens one attractor.

A relationship introduces perturbation.

A moment of recognition selects another organization.

The transition may appear sudden while preparation was long.

---

A collective transition also has a characteristic delay. Conditions can cross a threshold while the old phase persists briefly, then reorganize rapidly. This helps explain why preparation and realization can look disproportionate: long invisible changes alter coupling among parts before one visible event reveals that the system has already become capable of another order.

---

Yet spiritual systems introduce something phase-transition models do not contain.

Truth.

A magnetized state is not more metaphysically correct than an unmagnetized one.

A psychologically coherent state can be delusional.

Collective order alone is neutral.

Samarasa must not be reduced to any globally integrated configuration.

The Tantric claim is that the integration recognizes Akula and Kula as inseparable.

Scientific models can describe coordination and transition.

They cannot determine the truth of this metaphysics.

The bridge concerns emergence, not verification.

---

There is another danger.

The phrase “you cannot manufacture it” can protect teachers from accountability.

Failure is blamed on the student’s lack of grace.

Contradictions become signs of profundity.

The *Akulavīra* itself elevates guru and direct realization above textual debate.

Historically, such structures can preserve living transmission.

They can also concentrate authority.

A modern reading must distinguish epistemic humility from immunity to evidence.

A state that cannot be manufactured may still be examined through its consequences.

---

The personal lesson is gentler.

Stop trying to command every part into peace.

One subsystem cannot bully the whole into integration.

A frightened body, ambitious intellect, exhausted attention, and isolated social world will not become *samarasa* because the verbal mind declares nonduality.

Change conditions.

Create communication.

Reduce chronic threat.

Practise another response.

Let local parts experience a future they can jointly support.

The whole may then enter an organization no single part could announce in advance.

---

A magnet does not become magnetized because one atom understands the theory.

Order appears when relations cross a threshold.

The *Akulavīra Tantra* says the supreme state is not one more product of ritual machinery.

It is *sahaja*—the spontaneous condition in which Kula and Akula are one taste.

You cannot manufacture the state the whole must become.

You can prepare the system.

You can stop reinforcing the old order.

You can learn to recognize the transition.

But the final coherence belongs to the collective life—

not to the part that wants credit for causing it.
