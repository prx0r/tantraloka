# self and world hold each other upright

Place two bundles of reeds together.

Lean each against the other.

Neither stands alone.

Remove one.

The other falls.

Ñāṇananda uses this image for consciousness and name-and-form.

The world is not completed first and then viewed by a separate observer.

The observer is not completed first and then projecting a private world.

Self and world hold each other upright.

---

In dependent arising, *viññāṇa*—consciousness—and *nāma-rūpa*—name-and-form—are reciprocally conditioned.

Name-and-form includes the meaningful and embodied organization through which experience becomes this object, this feeling, this body, this situation.

Consciousness is not an abstract light floating outside conditions.

It arises in relation to structured experience.

Structured experience is not an independent world waiting fully formed.

It becomes lived through consciousness.

The two bundles lean.

The relation produces the field in which a subject seems to stand over here and objects over there.

---

This differs from a simple construction theory.

The mind does not invent the world from nothing.

A body encounters resistance, light, chemistry, other organisms, and causal structures not chosen by personal thought.

But the lived world is more than raw causation.

It is organized through relevance.

A stair affords climbing.

A face affords approach or avoidance.

A noise becomes language to one listener and meaningless sound to another.

Name-and-form is not merely naming pasted onto matter.

It is the world as articulated for a body capable of action.

---

Enactive cognitive science develops a related claim.

Perception is not passive reception followed by an internal decision.

It is skillful engagement.

The organism discovers a world through possible action.

Vision changes with movement.

Touch requires contact.

Attention selects according to goals.

A perceived object includes expectations about what further movement would reveal.

The hidden side of a cup is present as a counterfactual possibility:

If I rotate it, another surface will appear.

The world is not only sensed.

It is enacted through a repertoire of transformations.

---

A 2026 arXiv paper on enactive artificial intelligence emphasizes autonomy, embodiment, inseparability of action and perception, and intrinsic normativity.

The authors argue that current AI often treats cognition as internal information processing detached from self-maintaining bodily engagement.

Reinforcement learning captures some structural elements—action, feedback, agent-centred evaluation—but normally lacks the biological autonomy and lived normativity central to enactivism.

A reward function assigned from outside is not identical to a need generated by a system whose existence is at stake.

An artificial agent can optimize a world model without that model being its world in the biological sense.

---

Active inference also binds perception and action.

The organism predicts sensory consequences.

Perception updates the model.

Action changes the sampled world.

The cycle never contains a pure observation untouched by previous policy.

A person looking for an exit moves eyes and body differently from one admiring architecture.

Different actions reveal different data.

The world confirms the questions the organism knows how to ask physically.

This is not arbitrary subjectivism.

The door either opens or does not.

But the door becomes salient as exit only within a goal-directed life.

The environment supplies structure.

The agent supplies a horizon of relevance.

---

Environment-centric active inference pushes against treating the agent as already fixed.

A 2024 proposal begins inference from the environment rather than defining one privileged agent first.

The model treats people, robots, and objects as states within a larger field and updates under changes not captured by a narrow agent-environment partition.

The paper is an engineering proposal, not a final theory of selfhood.

Its philosophical value is diagnostic.

The boundary between agent and environment can depend upon the problem being modelled.

A robot arm and target may form one control system for one task.

Another robot becomes part of the relevant environment.

The unit of action is relational.

---

This mirrors the reeds.

A self-model depends upon a world-model.

“I am threatened” requires a world containing threat.

“I am capable” requires actions that can alter outcomes.

“I am excluded” requires a social field.

The world-model also depends upon the self-model.

The same room appears dangerous, sacred, boring, or useful according to the kind of subject represented as inhabiting it.

Each pole confirms the other.

The self says:

This is happening to me.

The world says:

This is the kind of place in which that self exists.

The loop becomes stable enough to feel discovered rather than constructed.

---

Grasping hardens the loop.

A painful self-model predicts rejection.

Attention finds signs of rejection.

Action becomes defensive.

Others respond to the defensiveness.

Memory reconstructs previous events through the same frame.

The world increasingly resembles the world predicted.

The prediction gains evidence because it helped create the conditions from which evidence was sampled.

This is self-evidencing in a psychological register.

The loop does not need to be consciously chosen.

It only needs to remain coherent enough to reproduce itself.

---

Ñāṇananda describes dependent arising as a vortex maintained by preparations, tendencies, and appropriation.

Ignorance is not only missing information.

It is the activity of seeking stability, pleasure, and ownership inside a process that cannot supply them permanently.

The self is repeatedly rebuilt through:

mine;

for me;

against me;

what I must become.

The world becomes a field of objects capable of securing or threatening the owner.

The owner becomes the centre for which the world matters.

Each side spins the other into apparent solidity.

The reeds become a whirlpool.

---

This is why Buddhist liberation is not the destruction of functional perception.

The body still distinguishes food from stone.

Language remains.

Memory operates.

Appropriate action continues.

What weakens is the additional appropriation that forces every appearance to prove or endanger an enduring owner.

The world no longer needs to complete the self-model.

The self-model no longer needs to possess the world.

The reeds may continue functionally without being mistaken for independent substances.

Dependence becomes visible.

The claim of ownership loses precision.

---

Kashmir Śaivism agrees that the contracted ego is not the final subject.

It diverges at the ground.

For Ñāṇananda, analysis exposes reciprocal conditioned arising and the absence of a permanent owner.

For Abhinavagupta, the collapse of false limitation reveals consciousness’s universal reflexivity and freedom.

The world and finite self co-arise as manifestations within *citi*.

The ego is constructed.

The power of manifestation is real.

The Buddhist refuses to establish an intrinsically existing ground.

The Śaiva recognizes that ground as Śiva.

The shared diagnosis does not erase the metaphysical difference.

---

The scientific model reaches another edge.

Active inference can describe how self-model and world-model stabilize one another through prediction and action.

It can model precision, policy, and error.

It does not by itself determine whether the process is conscious.

Nor does it decide whether consciousness is emergent, intrinsic to organization, or fundamental to reality.

Enactivism gives the world-agent relation priority over internal representation.

It still faces the question of manifestness.

Why is the loop present from within rather than only describable from outside?

The reeds explain dependence.

They do not explain light.

---

The reciprocity also explains why facts alone rarely reorganize a world.

A fact enters through an existing field of salience. The person notices some implications and not others. The self-model determines which evidence feels credible; the world-model determines which changes in the self seem possible. New information can therefore be rejected without being consciously denied. It simply fails to become relevant.

Transformation often needs a new interaction, not another statement. A trusted relationship, unfamiliar action, or changed bodily state can make previously invisible evidence available. The world alters the self by offering another affordance. The self alters the world by becoming capable of seeing it.

---

Practice can work at the loop rather than attacking one pole.

Change the environment.

The self receives new evidence.

Change action.

The environment reveals new possibilities.

Change attention.

Different features become salient.

Change the body’s regulation.

The same signal loses some threat.

Change the story.

Memories enter another relation.

No single intervention controls the whole vortex.

But each alters how self and world support one another.

Freedom becomes the capacity to modify the loop that had presented itself as reality.

---

Meditation adds a more radical experiment.

Let the world appear.

Let the self-model appear.

Body here.

Sound there.

Thought about the sound.

Feeling of ownership.

All are present as events.

The practice does not need to decide immediately whether one universal awareness owns them or no owner can be found.

It first reveals construction.

The poles depend upon one another.

Neither stands alone.

The moment of seeing dependence is already a loosening of the vortex.

---

Place two bundles of reeds together.

Self and world hold each other upright through memory, action, value, and prediction.

The error is not that the structures function.

The error is believing either possesses the independent solidity it borrows from the other.

The world is not your invention.

The self is not a sealed spectator.

They are reciprocally stabilized poles inside one enacted field.

When one bundle stops demanding that the other prove it permanent, the structure does not necessarily collapse.

It becomes light enough to move.
