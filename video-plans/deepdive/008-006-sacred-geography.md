# Sacred Geography: The Eight Fields and the Body of the Yogī

**Tantrāloka 15.79–115ab | Lines 3961–4550**

## Overview

The selection of the place for sacrifice (sthānakalpana) reveals the Trika understanding of sacred geography. There are outer fields (kṣetra) — actual geographical locations — and inner fields — the body of the yogī. Both are real; both are maps of consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **83cd–93ab:** The eight primary fields (kṣetra) and eight secondary fields (upakṣetra) are enumerated — cremation grounds, river confluences, caves, and other liminal zones.
- **93cd–97ab:** These sacred sites are also located within the body — the cremation ground is the heart, the river is the flow of breath, the cave is the void of the cranial center.
- **102cd–104ab:** According to the Vīrāvalīhṛdaya, there are seven sacred places — linked to the seven levels of subjectivity.
- **107cd–115ab (line 4550):** Liberation comes by the blooming of the heart (hṛdayavikāsa), a pure mind, and freedom from thought constructs (nirvikalpa).

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Sacred space is consciousness, not location:** The outer field is powerful because it concentrates the same energies that are distributed in the body. A cremation ground is "sacred" because it confronts the practitioner with death — the dissolution of ego.

2. **The eight fields = eight bodies:** Just as there are eight forms (mūrti) of Śiva in the ablution section, there are eight outer fields that mirror the eight centers of the subtle body.

3. **Blooming of the heart (hṛdayavikāsa):** This is the key soteriological term. The heart (hṛdaya) is not the physical organ but the core of conscious being. "Blooming" is its expansion from contraction (saṅkoca) to fullness (vikāsa).

4. **Stimulation of emergence:** Sacred places are said to "stimulate the emergence of consciousness." This is because they are charged with the residual power (śeṣaśakti) of yogīs, Siddhas, and Yoginīs who have realized there.

## Outer and Inner Field

The practitioner first purifies the outer place (washing, smearing with cowdung, drawing the maṇḍala), but this outer purification is effective only because it mirrors the inner purification of the heart. Without the inner field, the outer is dead stone.
