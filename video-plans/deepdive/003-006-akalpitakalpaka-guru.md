# THE UNCREATED-CUM-CREATED TEACHER (AKALPITAKALPAKA GURU)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 51cd–59

---

### Core Teaching

The second type of teacher in Abhinavagupta's five-fold classification is the *akalpitakalpaka* — "the uncreated-cum-created." This teacher possesses spontaneous insight (like the *sāṁsiddhika*) but must develop it further through self-contemplation (*ātmabhāvanā*). His realization is thus both "uncreated" (arising from grace) and "created" (developed through practice). He knows the ultimate purport of scripture independently of another teacher but requires his own contemplative effort to fully stabilize his realization. This type admits of three degrees based on the intensity of grace received: intense, moderate, or weak. The *akalpitakalpaka* teacher can receive his "consecration" (*abhiṣeka*) through various practices — contemplation, meditation, japa, yogic sleep, vows, or oblations — without the intervention of a human teacher.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/51cd–59; Dyczkowski translation pp. 68–77, lines 5333–6032.

---

### Key Verse

**Sanskrit (verse 51cd–52ab):**
```
yas tu tadrūpabhāgātmabhāvanātaḥ paraṁ vinā |
śāstravit sa guruḥ śāstre prokto 'kalpitakalpakaḥ || 51–52ab ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 5335–5366):** "The teacher who has that nature (of a Spontaneously Enlightened teacher) and knows the (ultimate purport of the) scriptures by contemplating his own true nature (*ātmabhāvanā*) without (taking recourse to) another (teacher) is said in the scriptures to be one who is 'uncreated-cum-created' (*akalpitakalpaka*)."

---

### The Balance of Grace and Effort

Jayaratha explains (lines 5368–5420): "Again, (there may be a teacher) who has the nature of a spontaneously enlightened (teacher) but even so the insight (he possesses that has) arisen by itself is not completely full (and perfect) to that degree." By the power of his own contemplation, "in accord with the dictum 'I myself am the supreme soul, Śiva, the supreme cause'" (SVṬ 4/339c), he comes to know the scriptures.

He is called *akalpitakalpaka* because:
- **Uncreated** (*akalpita*): his insight is attained spontaneously by grace
- **Created** (*kalpaka*): he is formed by contemplating his own true nature, through the progressively developing experiential knowledge of scripture

---

### Three Degrees (Verse 52cd)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasyāpi bhedā utkṛṣṭamadhyamandādyupāyataḥ || 52cd ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 5388–5395):** "There are various types of the latter, according to whether the means (that is, the grace he has received,) is intense, moderate or weak."

The "*means*" is "the descent of the power (of grace this teacher has received)" (lines 5397–5399).

---

### Verse 53 — The Uncreated Consecration

**Sanskrit:**
```
bhāvanāto 'tha vā dhyānāj japāt svapnād vratād dhuteḥ |
prāpnoty akalpitodāram abhiṣekaṁ mahāmatiḥ || 53 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 5441–5449):** "A man of great intellect (and resolve) (*mahāmati*) (who is such by virtue of the practice of) creative contemplation (*bhāvanā*), meditation (*dhyāna*), the repeated recitation of mantra (*japa*), (yogic) sleep (*svapna*), (by observing a) vow (*vrata*) (or offering) oblations, receives (this) uncreated (*akalpita*) and noble consecration."

Jayaratha (lines 5451–5502) emphasizes: This consecration is "uncreated" because "it is not done by man, not because it is not done at all." The consecration is performed directly by the goddess. The teacher thus receives "the authority to transmit the knowledge of the scriptures even without having been initiated by a teacher."

---

### The Point of These Distinctions

Jayaratha explains the larger purpose (lines 5517–5532): "The important point Abhinava is making by these distinctions is that it is quite possible to develop spiritually in all these practices without having to be initiated. This is because progress is the result of Śiva's grace. Śiva is free to grace anybody at any time. It depends entirely on His own will to do so. Śiva is not constrained by any factors or conditions. Simply because a person has received initiation does not mean that Śiva must grace him with progress. Conversely, Śiva is not obliged to ignore those who have not taken initiation."

---

### The Śākta Initiation

Abhinavagupta discusses (lines 5550–5800) the various forms of initiation that the *akalpitakalpaka* teacher may undergo. These include:

1. **Śākta initiation** — initiation by the goddess directly, without human intermediary
2. **Initiation through dream** (*svapna-dīkṣā*) — the goddess appears in a dream and bestows consecration
3. **Initiation through insight** (*jñāna-dīkṣā*) — the spontaneous dawn of knowledge constitutes initiation
4. **Initiation through the teacher's grace** — initiated by a human teacher but with minimal ritual

Each corresponds to a different intensity of grace and degree of purification required.

---

### The Continuity with the Krama

The concept of an "uncreated" consecration — initiation directly by the goddess — connects this teaching to the Krama system. Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī, the Destroyer of Time, is the one who bestows this consecration. The *akalpitakalpaka* teacher is one who has received the "noble consecration" of the goddess herself (lines 5550–5600).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Middle Path**: The *akalpitakalpaka* represents the middle ground between spontaneous realization and gradual practice. Most practitioners who attain realization do so through a combination of grace and effort.

2. **The Possibility of Self-Consecration**: The teaching that one can receive consecration through one's own practice — without a human teacher — is radical. It asserts that Śiva's grace is not mediated exclusively through human institutions.

3. **The Uncreated Consecration is Real**: The "uncreated" consecration is not a metaphor. It is a genuine transformation of consciousness effected directly by the goddess. The teacher who has received it possesses real authority.

4. **The Basis of Spiritual Authority**: True authority comes from realization, not from ritual installation. The *akalpitakalpaka* teacher's authority rests on his direct knowledge of reality, even though he may lack formal initiation credentials.
