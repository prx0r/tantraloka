# Procedure for the Apprentice: Tattvaśuddhi and Mantra

**Tantrāloka 17.1–33 | Lines 2501–3000**

## Overview

Chapter 17 covers the detailed procedure (vidheyaprakriyā) for the apprentice. It begins with the fashioning of the three knots of the sacred thread (sūtrakḷpti) and then proceeds to the purification of the principles (tattvaśuddhi) — the systematic cleansing of each tattva from earth to Śiva.

## Key Verses

- **1cd–7ab (line 459):** Fashioning the three knots of the sacred thread — the three knots representing the three guṇas.
- **7cd–30 (lines 463–464):** The purification of the principles (tattvaśuddhi).
- **11cd–15 (lines 464–465):** Formation of the mantras that invoke the principles.
- **16–22ab (lines 466–467):** Establishing consciousness in the principles and their worship.
- **22cd–26ab (line 468):** The power of the semantics of mantra.
- **26cd–28ab (lines 468–469):** The function of mantra — to reveal the nature of the principle.
- **28cd–30ab (line 469):** Declaration of intent (saṅkalpa) should herald ritual action.
- **30cd–33 (lines 469–470):** Illumining the body of the disciple with breath and mantra.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Three knots = three guṇas:** The three knots in the sacred thread are sattva, rajas, and tamas — the three strands of prakṛti. By tying and untying them, the teacher demonstrates mastery over the guṇas.

2. **Tattvaśuddhi is not negation but purification:** Each tattva from earth to Śiva is "purified" — but this does not mean destroyed. It means the contraction (saṅkoca) that makes each tattva appear as limited matter is dissolved. The tattva is revealed in its true nature as a form of Śiva.

3. **Mantra as semantic power:** The power of mantra is not in its sound (dhvani) but in its meaning (artha). If I utter "earth" (pṛthivī) with the right mantra, the very principle of solidity in the disciple's body is revealed as the goddess Earth (Dharā) — a form of consciousness.

4. **Illumining the body:** The teacher "illumines" (dīpana) the disciple's body with the movement of the breath and mantra. The breath is the flow of consciousness; the mantra is its articulation. Together they transform the disciple's embodiment.

## The Declaration of Intent

The saṅkalpa ("I will perform this rite for the attainment of liberation") is not a formality. It is the act of WILL (icchā) that activates the entire ritual. Without saṅkalpa, the ritual is a corpse — with it, it is alive.
