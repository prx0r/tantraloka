# The Apprentice Initiation: Maṇḍala and Animal Sacrifice

**Tantrāloka 16.1–72 | Lines 1027–1300**

## Overview

The initiation of the spiritual son (putraka) begins with the drawing of the Triple Trident maṇḍala and culminates in the controversial rite of animal sacrifice (paśu). Abhinavagupta provides a philosophical defense of animal sacrifice that reinterprets it as the liberation of the animal's contracted consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **1–2 (lines 1050–1052):** The teacher promotes a regular initiate (samayin) to putraka status.
- **3–4 (lines 1086–1091):** Wheels (cakras) of 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32, and 64 spokes — the structure of the maṇḍala.
- **5–7 (lines 1110–1148):** Drawing the Triple Trident maṇḍala with three tridents and three goddesses.
- **28–36ab (lines 648–653):** Eight types of sacrificial animal.
- **36cd–47ab (lines 649–650):** Extracting the vital force of the animal with breath control.
- **50cd–52ab (line 650):** The teaching of the Tattvārthacintāmaṇi.
- **52cd–54ab (lines 651–652):** The heroic sacrificial animal.
- **54cd–62cd (lines 652–653):** Benefits of animal sacrifice.
- **63–70 (lines 653–654):** The best animal is reborn six times in higher states.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The maṇḍala as cosmos:** The Triple Trident maṇḍala with its wheels of deities is a representation of the 36 tattvas. By drawing it, the teacher creates a field of forces in which the disciple can be reborn.

2. **Animal sacrifice = liberation of the bound soul:** Abhinavagupta's shocking claim is that the animal (paśu) is actually the bound soul (paśu = "fettered one"). The "animal sacrifice" in the outer rite symbolizes the sacrifice of the ego. The literal animal is a stand-in for the disciple's own contracted consciousness.

3. **Extracting the vital force:** The teacher extracts the animal's prāṇa through breath control (uccāra) — this is not killing but liberating. The prāṇa is offered to the deities, and the animal is reborn in a higher state. The "death" is an initiation.

4. **The heroic animal:** The best type of animal is one that is "heroic" (vīra) — meaning one that does not fear death. This symbolizes the disciple who has conquered fear — the ultimate fetter.

## The Rebirth of the Animal

The teaching (63–70) that the best animal is reborn six times in increasingly higher states is a coded reference to the soul's ascent through the six paths (ṣaḍadhvan): kalā, tattva, bhuvana, varṇa, pada, mantra. Each "rebirth" is a purification of one path.
