# THE DIVINE ATTRIBUTES AND THE PURIFICATION OF THOUGHT (GUṆAŚODHANA)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 197–201

---

### Core Teaching

The Divine Attributes (*guṇa*) of Śiva — such as His transcendence beyond thought, permanence, majesty, and glory — are not mere qualities but are His very power (*śakti*). By meditating on any single attribute, one comes to rest in the possessor of all attributes, who is Śiva Himself. The purification of thought constructs through reflection on these attributes is the path of the Empowered Means.

**Line references:** TĀ 1/197–201; Dyczkowski translation pp. 342–347.

---

### Perceiving the Whole through Its Parts (Verse 197)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasmād yathā purasthe 'rthe guṇādyaṁśāṁśikāmukhāt |
niraṁśabhāvasaṁbodhas tathaivātrāpi budhyatām || 197 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Therefore, just as any object which is in front of one is perceived as a whole, by means of the partial (perceptions) of its qualities and the like, know that in the same way here also (the totality of reality can be grasped part by part)."

---

### Attributes as Power (Verses 198–200)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ata evāvikalpatvadhrauvyaprābhavavaibhavaiḥ |
anyair vā śaktirūpatvād dharmaiḥ svasamavāyibhiḥ || 198 ||
sarvaśo 'py atha vāṁśena taṁ vibhaṁ parameśvaram |
upāsate vikalpaughasaṁskārādye śrutotthitāt || 199 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Therefore, (there are) those who attend with reverence on the Supreme All-pervasive Lord, either in (His) entirety or part by part, by means of His (divine) attributes such as His (transcendence) beyond thought, permanence, majesty, glory or others."

Jayaratha: The attributes are not separate from Śiva — they are His power. By the intensity (*pāṭava*) of any one attribute manifesting repeatedly in thought, one enters the possessor of all attributes.

---

### Vidyādhipati's Testimony (Verse 201)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tvatsyarūpam avikalpam akṣajā
kalpane na viṣayīkaroti cet |
antarullikhitacitrasaṁvido
no bhaveyur anubhūtayaḥ sphuṭāḥ || 201 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation:** "(O Lord,) if (perception,) born of the senses, were not to render Your inconceivable nature into an object in the domain of conceptualization, the diverse perceptions that are sketched out within (consciousness) would not be clear experiences."

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Part Contains the Whole**: Each divine attribute contains all others because all are aspects of Śakti. Knowing one, one knows all.

2. **Thought as Purification**: The practice of thinking about Śiva's attributes is not intellectual speculation — it is a method to purify the flux of thought constructs (*vikalpaugha*) until the thought-free state dawns.

3. **The Three Moments of Perception**: The perception of an object follows three phases — pure awareness, analysis, and synthesis. The same structure applies to knowing Śiva through His attributes.

4. **Śiva Known by His Energies**: Just as fire is known by its heat, Śiva is known through His powers. The attributes are the channels (*mukha*) by which He becomes accessible.
