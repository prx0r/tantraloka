# 336 — What Are Tattvas?
**Tantrāloka 9.1–6** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
What is a tattva, and why does Abhinavagupta begin a chapter on causality with a definition of "thatness"?

## Core Teaching
A tattva is not merely a "principle" or "category." It is *thatness* (from *tat* + *tva*) — the one universal nature that pervades all members of a class. Verse 9.1 announces: *atha tattvapravibhāgo viṣṭarataḥ kathyate kramaprāptaḥ* — "Now its turn has come, the division of the principles will be explained at length."

## Key Verses

**9.2ab** — The supreme universal nature (*rūpa*), permeated without break by the Light of consciousness, shines within all worlds. It is Śiva, the one reality.

**9.2cd** — When diversity manifests from Śiva's abode by the aesthetic delight (*rasa*) of His freedom, the immanent nature that inheres in many ways is *tattva*.

**9.3** — Example: Earth principle extends from Kālāgnī's abode to Vīrabhadra's world because the power to sustain (*dhṛti*), solidity (*kāṭhinya*), and weight (*garimā*) manifest there.

**9.4cd–6ab** — Formal definition: A tattva is so-called because it extends (*tananāt*) like a universal: (1) throughout its own effect, (2) the multitude of properties, or (3) the group of perceivers similar to itself. Includes Earth through Nature, the individual soul, and Śiva. Does NOT extend to bodies or worlds.

## Threefold Extension
The definition operates on three registers:
1. **Throughout its own effect** — Earth in jar, potsherds
2. **Throughout the multitude of properties** — Guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas) as modifications of Nature
3. **Throughout the group of perceivers** — From contracted souls to Śiva

## Key Philosophical Moves
- A tattva is *anugāmin* — it inheres in and pervades its particulars
- The *one* tattva (supreme form) is Śiva Himself; the *thirty-five* tattvas are immanent forms
- Śiva's power (*Śakti*) consisting of thirty-five principles is His own deployment
- Tattvas are NOT mere conceptual categories — they are real generic natures grasped through their qualities

## Jayaratha's Commentary Highlights
- A tattva is what "shines undivided in various groups of things" and "serves as the cause to justify their being represented as belonging to one class"
- The Śaiva system adds eleven principles above the Sāṁkhya twenty-five
- Supreme Śiva is the thirty-sixth; Anākhyā Kālī the thirty-seventh and thirty-eighth

## Connection to Larger Argument
This definitional groundwork is essential because the causality discussion (9.7–40ab) depends on understanding that the same universal nature (*tattva*) persists through causal transformation — the seed and sprout share a common reality, which is consciousness itself.
