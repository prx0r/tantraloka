# 354 — The Five Coverings (Kañcukas)
**Tantrāloka 9.154cd–220ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Time, necessity, attachment, limited knowledge, limited agency — these five coverings are the architecture of the bounded self. Each one is a specific modality of how infinite consciousness becomes finite experience.

## Core Teaching
The *kañcukas* are not separate entities but five aspects of the single act of self-limitation. They arise from Māyā and structure the experience of the bounded soul.

## The Five in Detail

### 1. Kalā — The Force of Limited Agency (9.154cd–183)
- The power that enables the soul to act within limits
- Corresponds to *kriyāśakti* (power of action) in contracted form
- Makes possible the sense of "I am the doer" of limited actions

### 2. Vidyā — Limited Cognitive Subjectivity (9.191cd–193)
- The power of limited knowledge
- Corresponds to *jñānaśakti* (power of knowledge) in contracted form
- Enables cognition of objects but not of the true Self

### 3. Rāga — The Principle of Attachment (9.199cd–201ab)
- The driving force of desire
- Makes the soul seek fulfillment in objects outside itself
- The engine of karmic accumulation

### 4. Kāla — The Principle of Time (9.201cd–202ab)
- Divides the eternal now into past, present, future
- Makes the soul experience change, aging, death
- The framework within which karma bears fruit

### 5. Niyati — The Principle of Necessity (9.202cd)
- The law of karmic consequence
- Ensures that actions bear their fruits
- The principle of cosmic order that binds the soul to its karma

## Perception Within the Intellect (9.194–199ab)
A key sub-section: perception occurs within the intellect (*buddhi*), which is the first evolute of *prakṛti*. The *kañcukas* condition how perception happens:
- Kalā determines the scope of perceptible action
- Vidyā determines the range of perceptible objects
- Rāga determines what attracts perception
- Kāla determines when perception occurs
- Niyati determines the necessity of certain perceptions

## Māyā and the Coverings Working Together (9.203–205)
The coverings do not operate in isolation. They form an interlocking system:
- Time determines when karma bears fruit
- Necessity determines that it must bear fruit
- Attachment determines the quality of the experience
- Limited knowledge determines how it is understood
- Limited agency determines how the soul responds

## Two Accounts of the Sequence
- **According to the Śivatanu** (9.206–213): a specific sequence of emanation from the body of Śiva
- **The Sequence of Emanation** (9.214–220ab): the general order in which the coverings arise from Māyā

Both accounts are perspectives on the same process.

## Connection to Prakṛti (9.220cd–226)
Below the coverings lies *prakṛti* (primordial nature), with its three *guṇas*. While the *kañcukas* are subjective limitations (affecting the knower), *prakṛti* is the objective sphere (the known). Together, they constitute the entire field of impure experience.
