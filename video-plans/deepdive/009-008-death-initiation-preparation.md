# Death Initiation: Preparation and Severing

**Tantrāloka 19.1–23ab | Lines 516–540**

## Overview

The initiation for the immediate exit from the body (sadyaḥsamutkrama) is given to a dying person so that he attains Śiva at the moment of death. This is the most urgent and dramatic of all initiations — a race against time to liberate a soul before the body fails.

## Key Verses

- **1–3 (lines 518–519):** The initiation whereby a dying man attains Śiva at the moment of death.
- **4 (line 519):** The ascertainment of impending death (kālāvekṣā) — recognizing the signs that death is near.
- **5–9 (lines 521–523):** Qualifications for this initiation — the dying person must have at least some receptivity.
- **10–14 (lines 525–527):** The deposition of the razor (kṛpāṇyādinyāsa) that severs the vital points.
- **15–23ab (lines 527–530):** Severing the vital centers by the circulation of the breath (cāra) in the body.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Death as the final opportunity:** The moment of death is uniquely powerful because the soul is about to leave the body — the ultimate transition. If at that moment the soul can be directed to Śiva, liberation is instantaneous.

2. **The razor deposition:** The teacher deposits a "razor" (kṛpāṇa) — a mantra-form — that severs the soul's attachment to each vital center (marman). These are the points where prāṇa is most intensely bound to the body.

3. **Severing as liberation:** The "severance" is not violence but disentanglement. The soul is knotted to the body through its prāṇic attachments; the rasor-mantra cuts these knots so the soul can rise freely.

4. **Circulation of the breath (cāra):** The teacher circulates his own breath through the dying person's body, systematically withdrawing prāṇa from the limbs and directing it upward through the susumṇā.

## The Ascertainment of Death

The teacher must recognize the signs of approaching death (kālāvekṣā) — changes in the body's temperature, breath rhythm, and the progressive dissolution of sensory functions. If he waits too long, the soul may exit through a lower aperture and be reborn.
