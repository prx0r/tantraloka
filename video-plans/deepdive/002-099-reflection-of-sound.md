# Topic 99: The Reflection of Sound

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 99 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: TĀ 3/24-34ab

---

## 1. Core Teaching

Sound is reflected as echo (pratiśrutkā). Just as form is reflected in a mirror, sound is reflected in the sky or ether (ākāśa). The echo is a real reflection of sound — it is not an illusion but a genuine phenomenon demonstrating that the principle of reflection extends to all five senses.

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 3/24-34ab** (approximately lines 7642-8100): *itthaṁ pradarśite 'mutra pratibimbavartmaṇi | śabdasya pratibimbaṁ yat pratiśrutketi bhaṇyate*
Thus, in the path of reflection shown there, the reflection of sound is called the echo.

**TĀ 3/26** (lines 7647): *citratvāc cāsya śabdasya pratibimbaṁ mukhādivat*
Because sound is manifold, its reflection is like that of a face etc.

---

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
