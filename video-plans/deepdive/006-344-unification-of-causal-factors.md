# 344 — The Unification of the Causal Factors (Sāmagrī)
**Tantrāloka 9.30–35ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
How do clay, wheel, water, and potter converge to make a pot? What holds the *sāmagrī* together?

## Core Teaching
The *sāmagrī* (aggregate of causal factors) requires a unifying agent. Multiple factors cannot coordinate themselves. Only consciousness — specifically, Śiva's universal agency — can bring the causal factors together.

## The Problem of Coordination
No effect arises from a single cause. A pot requires:
- Material cause: clay
- Efficient cause: potter
- Instrumental causes: wheel, water, stick
- Formal cause: the potter's intention

These factors are separate, insentient, and have no intrinsic relation to each other. Something must coordinate them.

## The Solution
**9.30–35ab**: The unifying factor is the agent — but not the limited agent (potter) who is himself conditioned by the factors. Rather, it is Supreme Śiva who works through the potter, the clay, and all other factors simultaneously.

## The Reflexive Argument
If the potter were the real agent:
- He would need to control the clay (he cannot — it has its own nature)
- He would need to control his own body (he cannot fully)
- He would need to produce the pot by will alone (he cannot)

Therefore, the real agent is the one who CAN do all this: Śiva, whose will is immediately effective.

## Relation to the Perceptibility Matrix
This argument is crucial for Chapter 10: just as multiple causal factors converge in one effect, multiple perceivers converge on one object. The unity of the object across different perceptions (Sakala sees earth, Śiva sees consciousness) requires a universal subject.

## Jayaratha's Explanation
The *sāmagrī* "of its own accord" does not produce the effect. The effect arises when Śiva's consciousness, operating through the veil of *niyati*, brings the factors into relation. The potter's "choice" to make a pot is itself Śiva's choice.

## Philosophical Implications
- **Against atomism**: factors need unification, which only consciousness provides
- **Against mechanism**: no blind forces can coordinate themselves
- **For non-duality**: all causes are one in the universal agent
