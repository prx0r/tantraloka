# Supreme Śiva the Thirty-Seventh Principle
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

The thirty-sixth principle is Śiva as transcendent consciousness — pure I-consciousness beyond all objectivity. But for the purposes of instruction and meditation, this same reality is conceived as the thirty-seventh principle, called Supreme Śiva (Paraśiva) or Bhairava. The thirty-seventh is the immanent form of the transcendent: it is "the undivided nature of all things, beautiful with consciousness" (11.21cd-22ab). Abhinavagupta's innovation here is to provide a conceptual space where the absolute can be contemplated as both transcendent *and* immanent without contradiction.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verse 11.21cd-22ab — The Thirty-Seventh Principle

**Lines:** 2017–2023

> yat tu sarvāvibhāgātma svatantraṁ bodhasundaram || 21 ||
> saptatriṁśaṁ tu tat prāhus tattvaṁ paraśivābhidham |

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"They say that the thirty-seventh principle is called Supreme Śiva (Paraśiva). Free, (that Principle) is (immanent) as the undivided nature of all things (equally), and is beautiful with consciousness."

**Explanation:**
Note the three attributes:
1. **Sarvāvibhāgātma** — "the undivided nature of all things" — immanence without distinction
2. **Svatantra** — absolutely free, self-determining
3. **Bodhasundaram** — "beautiful with consciousness" — aesthetic delight of self-awareness

This is distinct from the thirty-sixth, which is purely transcendent (viśvottīrṇa). The thirty-seventh is the same reality considered as immanent — "the foundation of both realities (ubhayavastu)."

---

#### The Mālinīvijayavārtika (1/95-99ab) Quoted by Jayaratha

"This (God), being perfectly established in himself through a state of rest in a bliss replete (with all things), is denoted by many appropriate terms, such as, for instance, 'Bhairava'... And in this way (Śiva), who is essentially light, is higher than the thirty-seventh (principle)."

**Explanation:**
Even the thirty-seventh principle — Supreme Śiva as immanent totality — is not the final word. The absolute remains "higher" (paraḥ) than even this, as pure consciousness that can never be fully captured by any concept, even that of "immanent totality."

---

### Why a Thirty-Seventh Principle?

Jayaratha clarifies: philosophers who cannot accept that opposites (transcendence and immanence) coexist in one nature object that this is contradictory. To accommodate their limited understanding, the scriptures teach a thirty-seventh principle — the immanent aspect — as distinct from the thirty-sixth transcendent one. In reality, there is only one reality; the distinction is pedagogical (adhyāropāpavāda — superimposition and retraction).

### Narrative Arc

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** The problem: how can Śiva be both beyond the world and everything in the world? The 36th vs. 37th principles.

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** The thirty-sixth as viśvottīrṇa (transcendent). The thirty-seventh as sarvāvibhāgātma (immanent). Same reality, different perspectives.

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** The objection of the dualist philosophers and the pedagogical solution. Adhyāropa (superimposition) as teaching method.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** The thirty-seventh as Bhairava. Connection to the Mālinīvijayavārtika. Bhairava as the one who "shines forth" in all forms.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** The Trikasāra's alternative: Śakti as the thirty-seventh. Mālinī as Parā encompassing all 36 tattvas.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The thirty-seventh is a teaching device — a door that opens onto the formless. To transcend it is to enter the thirty-eighth.
