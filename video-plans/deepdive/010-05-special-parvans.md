# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Special Auspicious Times — Haiḍara, Ūrmikaula, and the Lunar vs. Solar Question

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 10, Chapter 28, Verses 36-59 (~lines 2270-2610)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
What is more important for determining the sacred calendar — the sun or the moon? Abhinava poses this question and resolves it with an appeal to his teacher Śambhunātha's authority. The answer illuminates the entire Tantric philosophy of time: the moon governs the inner, subjective dimension of consciousness, while the sun governs external, objective time.

## Part 1: The Haiḍaratantra's Special Parvans (0:45-3:00)
- Verses 36-47ab: Detailed catalog of special parvans for each month
- These are the viśeṣa (special) and viśeṣa-viśeṣa (special-special) grades
- Each is characterized by specific alignments of tithi (lunar day), nakṣatra (asterism), and vāra (weekday)
- Examples of special parvans:
  - Māgha: specific days for worship of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī
  - Phālguna: the Śivarātrī complex
  - Vaiśākha: days for the collective Ādiyāga
- Jayaratha quotes long passages from the Haiḍara to supplement Abhinava's condensed presentation
- The special-special parvan is one where the tithi, nakṣatra, and vāra all align in the most favorable configuration

## Part 2: The Ūrmikaulārṇava's Contribution (3:00-5:00)
- Verses 53-56ab: The Kālīkula perspective from the Ūrmikaulārṇava
- This text belongs to the Kālīkula tradition (the "Clan of Kālī")
- Different set of days, emphasizing the goddess-oriented practices
- The Ūrmikaula's parvans are more "left-hand" (vāmācāra) oriented
- They include specific days for Yoginī worship and the consumption of taboo substances
- Abhinava includes these to show the breadth of the tradition, even if his own focus is Trika Kaulism

## Part 3: The Great Debate — Lunar Day vs. Solar Day (5:00-7:30)
- Verses 47cd-49: Abhinava's own analysis
- The question: Is the lunar day (tithi) or the solar day (saura-dina) more important for determining a parvan?
- Jayaratha explains: Some hold that the solar day is primary because it determines the daily rhythm of worship
- Others argue for the lunar day because the moon governs the mind (manas) and therefore consciousness
- Abhinava's resolution: The **lunar day** is primary — and he learned this from his teacher **Śambhunātha**
- The reason: consciousness, which is "filled" during a parvan, is more directly connected to the lunar cycle
- The sun governs external action; the moon governs inner awareness
- This aligns with the Trika's emphasis on subjective realization over external ritual

## Part 4: The Bhairavakula's Confirmation (7:30-9:30)
- Verses 50-52ab: Quotation from the Trikabhairavakula
- This text confirms the primacy of the lunar day
- It adds that certain specific asterisms (nakṣatras) also have power
- The Bhairavakula text links the moon to the goddess and the sun to Bhairava
- Their union in the parvan produces the state of "filled consciousness"
- Abhinava uses multiple sources to triangulate the same truth — a hallmark of his synthetic method
- The verse also emphasizes that the teacher's instruction is essential: even the correct time cannot be determined by calculation alone

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The special parvans reveal a sophisticated Tantric calendar science. But more importantly, they show Abhinava's commitment to grounding practice in the inner dimension of consciousness. The moon wins over the sun because consciousness wins over external action. The next video explores what actually happens in the assembly on these sacred days — the cakracaryā.
