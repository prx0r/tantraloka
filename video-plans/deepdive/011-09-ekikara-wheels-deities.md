# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Ekīkāra — The Wheels of Yoginīs

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 11, Chapter 33, Verses 2-32 (~lines 7420-7800)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
Ekīkāra — "gathering together" or "making one" — is Chapter 33's theme. Here Abhinava presents the wheels (cakra) of Yoginīs: six-spoked, sixteen-spoked, twenty-four-spoked, and thirty-four-spoked wheels, each with its presiding powerholders and Bhairavas. These wheels are complete maps of consciousness, compressing the entire pantheon into unified, rotating mandalas.

## Part 1: The Six-Spoked Wheel (0:45-2:30)
- Verse 2 (~line 7420): The first and simplest wheel
- **The six spokes represent:**
  - The five senses + mind
  - The five elements + space
  - The five jñānendriyas + manas
- **Six Yoginīs:**
  - Each Yoginī presides over one spoke
  - They are the six powers of perception
- The wheel rotates:
  - Perception moves from center to periphery (creation)
  - Perception returns from periphery to center (dissolution)
- This is the wheel of **ordinary experience**
- Yoginīs at the periphery = the objects of sense
- The center = the perceiver

## Part 2: The Twenty-Four Spoked Wheel (2:30-5:00)
- Verses 3-5: Two groups of twelve
- **Group 1 (outer twelve):**
  - The twelve Kālīs (from the Krama system)
  - They are the phases of cognitive act
  - Creation, persistence, withdrawal, etc.
- **Group 2 (inner twelve):**
  - The twelve months = the year
  - The twelve signs of the zodiac
  - The twelve aspects of the sun
- The twenty-four spokes = the cycle of day and night
- The wheel turns through time, yet the center is timeless
- The powerholders of these wheels (verses 7cd-14ab):
  - Specific Bhairavas preside over each wheel
  - They are the "lords" (īśvara) of that cognitive configuration

## Part 3: The Sixteen and Thirty-Four Spoked Wheels (5:00-7:30)
- Verse 6-7ab: The sixteen-spoked wheel
  - The sixteen Yoginīs = the sixteen phases of the moon (kalā)
  - The moon = the mind (manas)
  - The sixteen phases = all possible mental states
  - The full moon = perfected consciousness
- Verses 14cd-17ab: The Bhairavas of the thirty-four-spoked wheel
  - The thirty-four spokes = the consonants (33) + bindu
  - Each consonant is a Bhairava
  - The Bhairavas are the lords of the phonemes
  - This wheel encompasses all of language
- The mantras of these wheels (verses 17cd-19):
  - Each wheel has a specific mantra
  - The mantra activates the wheel
  - Recitation of the mantra sets the wheel in motion

## Part 4: The Formation of Mālinī, Mātṛkā and Śabdarāśi (7:30-9:30)
- Verses 20-26: From the wheels arise the three goddesses of the alphabet
  - **Mālinī:** The garland of phonemes — the "encoded" alphabet used in mantras
  - **Mātṛkā:** The "mother" alphabet — the standard Sanskrit alphabet
  - **Śabdarāśi:** The "collection of sounds" — all possible sonic forms
- The development of the Ekaśīti (81) and other mantra-forms
- All mantras are permutations of these three alphabetic goddesses
- Verses 27-29: Fractions of measures and accents
  - The subtle variations of pronunciation
  - The power of duration, pitch, and stress
- Verses 30-32: All is contained in the three goddesses and Mātṛṣadbhāva
  - The entire universe of sound is captured in these wheels
  - To master the wheels is to master the universe

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
Ekīkāra shows that the Trika's pantheon is not a collection of separate deities but a unified, dynamic system — a rotating wheel of consciousness. Chapter 34, the shortest and most intense chapter, brings the entire Tantrāloka to its climax: svasvarūpe praveśa, entry into one's own true nature.
