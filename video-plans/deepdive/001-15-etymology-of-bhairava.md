# ETYMOLOGY OF BHAIRAVA — Sevenfold Meaning — Verses 94-105ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

The name "Bhairava" is explained through seven didactic etymologies (nirvacana), each revealing an aspect of His nature. These etymologies are not philologically precise but are meditative tools — each derivation from verbal roots unlocks a dimension of the Supreme Reality. The seven meanings cover: sustaining the universe, protecting the fearful, being born from the cry of fear, bestowing grace through fear-reflection, devouring time, mastering the senses and Yoginīs, and annihilating transmigration.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verses 1.94-105 — The Seven Etymologies

**Line ~19436** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
devo hy anvarthaśāstroktaiḥ śabdaiḥ samupadiśyate |
mahābhairavadevo 'yaṁ patir yaḥ paramaḥ śivaḥ || 94 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"(The nature of) God (deva) is taught by means of words stated in the scriptures (that serve as etymologies), the meanings of which are in accord with His nature. He is this great Bhairava, the Lord (Pati) and Supreme Śiva."

**Explanation:**
This introductory verse establishes the method: scriptural etymologies reveal the nature of God. The sevenfold meaning of "Bhairava" follows.

#### Verses 1.95-98 — The Seven Meanings

**Line ~19538** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit of the seven (summarized):**

A) **Bhi + rava**: He sustains (√bhṛ) the universe and is sustained by it; He is the resonance (rava) of reflective awareness.

B) **Bhī + rava**: He helps those frightened (bhī) of transmigration.

C) **Bhī + rava**: He is born from the cry (rava) of fear (bhīti) in the heart of the devotee.

D) **Bhaya + rava**: The reflection on fear (bhaya) is made lucid by His descent of power (śaktipāta).

E) **Bhera + va**: He devours time (bhera < bhā + īr) — He exhausts the principle of time.

F) **Bhī + rava**: He is the Master of the goddesses of the senses whose cry frightens contracted souls.

G) **Bhai + rava**: He annihilates (√bhaī) the course of transmigration.

**Dyczkowski Translation (for A):**
"A) He sustains the universe by nourishing and supporting it and is sustained by that. Again (He is called Bhairava) by virtue of His nature as the (cosmogonic) resonance (rava) (of consciousness) as His own reflective awareness."

**Explanation:**
The first etymology (from √bhṛ, to sustain, and √ru, to sound) is the most fundamental. Bhairava sustains all things and is the resonance of consciousness — the primal vibration (spanda) that is the substrate of existence.

#### Verses 1.99-105 — The Names Deva and Pati

**Line ~20080+** (Vol. 1)

The etymologies continue for the names Deva (God) and Pati (Lord). Deva derives from √div — to shine, to play, to protect. Pati means Master — the Lord who is the supreme sovereign (paramaḥ śivaḥ).

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On the didactic method (line ~19478+):** Jayaratha explains three types of etymology: grammatical analysis (vyutpatti), semantic analysis (nirukta), and those coherent with scriptural statements (sāmavāyika).

**On time-devouring (line ~19615+):** "'Bhairava' is derived from 'bhā' meaning 'star' and the root 'īr' to impel — together 'bhera' meaning time. The syllable 'va' is from √vai, to exhaust. Thus Bhairava is one who exhausts time."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the function of etymology (note 727, line ~19450+):** Sanderson is quoted: "For the unlearned, the name was adequately explained by its literal meaning ('The Terrible')... The nondualist tradition overlaid the basic meaning with more appropriate interpretations."

### Philosophical Context

The sevenfold etymology of Bhairava is not a linguistic exercise but a contemplative unfolding of the Absolute. Each meaning is a doorway into a dimension of Bhairava's nature: sustainer, protector, grace-giver, time-transcender, sense-master, and liberator. This method of multiple meanings is characteristic of Abhinava's synthetic approach.

### Visual Concepts

1. **Seven Meanings Wheel:** A seven-spoked wheel, each spoke labeled with one etymology, with Bhairava at the center.

2. **Devouring Time:** A figure of Bhairava consuming a clock-like wheel, representing the transcendence of temporal limitation.

3. **The Resonance (Rava):** Sound waves emanating from a central point, representing the primal vibration of consciousness.

### Narration Script

The name "Bhairava" is analyzed through seven didactic etymologies, each revealing an aspect of the Supreme Reality. He sustains the universe and is the primal resonance of consciousness. He protects those who fear transmigration. He is born from the cry of the devotee's heart. He devours time itself — exhausting the principle of temporal succession. He is the master of the senses and the Yoginīs. He annihilates the cycle of birth and death. These are not mere word-games — they are contemplative keys that unlock the nature of the Absolute.
