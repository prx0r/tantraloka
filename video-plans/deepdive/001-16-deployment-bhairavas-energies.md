# THE DEPLOYMENT OF BHAIRAVA'S ENERGIES — Verses 106cd-115ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

Śiva's three powers — Parā, Parāparā, and Aparā — manifest in four phases: creation (sṛṣṭi), persistence (sthiti), withdrawal (laya), and the Fourth (turya) that encompasses them. Thus they become twelvefold as the Twelve Kālīs. This is the foundation of the Krama system integrated into Trika. The Twelve Kālīs are the principal circuit of divine powers (mukhyaṁ śakticakram) in which Bhairava's nature is most fully manifest.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.106cd-107ab — Three Powers Become Twelve

**Line ~21074** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasya śaktaya evaitās tīsro bhānti parādikāḥ || 106 ||
sṛṣṭau sthitau laye turye tenaitā dvādaśoditāḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"(Śiva) has just these three powers, namely, (the Goddess) Parā (Supreme) and the rest. They manifest in (the phases of) creation, persistence, withdrawal and the Fourth (that both includes and transcends them). Thus (they) have arisen twelve-fold."

**Explanation:**
Each of the three goddesses operates in four phases — creation, persistence, withdrawal, and the Fourth (consciousness itself). This yields the twelve Kālīs of the Krama system. Parā as will, Parāparā as knowledge, and Aparā as action — each with four aspects.

#### Verses 1.108-115 — The Nature of the Twelve

**Line ~21265+** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit (excerpt from 1.108):**
```
sṛṣṭikālī sthitikālī saṁhārākhya tathaiva ca |
anākhyākhyā caturdhā syād ekaikā bhidyate tataḥ || 
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (from context):**
"Sṛṣṭikālī, Sthitikālī, Saṁhārākhyā, and Anākhyākhyā — each of the three becomes four in this way."

**Explanation:**
The twelve names are: Sṛṣṭikālī, Sthitikālī, Saṁhārākhyā, Anākhyākhyā (from Parā); and similar quadruples from Parāparā and Aparā.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On the Twelve Kālīs (line ~21111+):** "Abhinava begins his exposition of practice... by referring to the Twelve Kālīs, which he thus implicitly presents as the foundation of all Anuttara Trika practice."

**On Bhairava's plenitude (line ~21121+):** "The one Śakti as the Twelve Kālīs is Śiva's all-embracing plenitude, the perfection in all respects that is Who He is."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the Krama integration (note 779, line ~21158+):** Sanderson is quoted: "It is this set of twelve, rather than the three or four goddesses of the Trika proper, which Abhinavagupta declares to be 'the principal circuit of the divine Powers' in which Bhairava's nature is most fully manifest."

### Philosophical Context

The Twelve Kālīs represent the complete dynamism of consciousness. Every moment of perception involves creation (the appearance of an object), persistence (its maintenance in awareness), withdrawal (its disappearance), and the Fourth — consciousness itself, which transcends yet contains these three phases. The Krama system teaches that by meditating on this twelvefold structure, one attains liberation.

### Visual Concepts

1. **The Twelvefold Wheel:** A twelve-spoked wheel with the three goddesses at the center and the four phases on each spoke.

2. **The Four Phases:** An animated sequence showing creation (point appearing), persistence (point holding), withdrawal (point dissolving), and the Fourth (empty space containing all).

3. **Kālīs in Calligraphy:** The twelve names in Devanāgarī arranged in a circle.

### Narration Script

Śiva's three powers are not static — they pulse through four phases: creation, persistence, withdrawal, and the Fourth that encompasses them. Thus three become twelve — the Twelve Kālīs, which are the principal circuit of divine powers. Bhairava's nature is most fully manifest in this twelvefold dynamism. Every moment of experience follows this pattern: something arises, abides, falls away, and consciousness remains.
