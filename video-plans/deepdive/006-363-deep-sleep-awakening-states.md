# 363 — Deep Sleep, Awakening, and the States of Consciousness
**Tantrāloka 10.123cd–184** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
You wake from deep sleep — who woke? The answer reveals your level in the hierarchy of perceivers.

## Core Teaching
The states of consciousness — waking (*jāgrat*), dream (*svapna*), deep sleep (*suṣupti*), the fourth (*turya*), and beyond the fourth (*turīyātīta*) — correspond to the seven perceivers. Each perceiver experiences these states differently.

## Divisions of the Other Subjects (10.123cd–133ab)
Starting from thirteen divisions, the text shows how each level of perceiver:
- Relates to the states of consciousness
- Wakes from its own kind of sleep
- Experiences objectivity according to its level

## The Awakening of the Dissolution and Consciousness Deconditioned (10.133cd–140ab)
- **Pralayākala** — "wakes" from dissolution into a new cycle of existence
- **Vijñānākala** — "wakes" from the sleep of ignorance into knowledge
- **Mantra** — "wakes" from mantra recitation into mantric awareness

## Perceptibility as Potential (10.140cd–146ab)
Perceptibility is a potential that actualizes upon waking for those fit to perceive. During sleep, the potential remains dormant. This explains:
- Why the same room looks the same when you wake up
- Why a sage and an ordinary person waking in the same world see it differently

## The Subjective Response Varies (10.146cd–149)
Even waking from the same sleep, different perceivers:
- Have different objects of awareness
- Experience different qualities of awareness
- Perceive different worlds

## Primary and Secondary Varieties of Perceivers (10.150–166)
Each level has sub-types:
- **Primary**: the main form of the perceiver
- **Secondary**: variations due to karmic residue or specific conditions

## The Seven Perceivers and Their Powers (10.178–181)
A listing of the powers (*śaktis*) of each perceiver:
- Sakala → Icchā, Jñāna, Kriyā (in contracted form)
- Pralayākala → Icchā, Jñāna (no Kriyā during dissolution)
- Vijñānākala → Jñāna only (pure knowledge)
- Mantra → Mantra power
- Mantreśvara → Lordship power
- Mantramaheśvara → Great lordship power
- Śiva → Absolute freedom

## Own Nature and Conclusion (10.182–184)
The *svarūpa* (own nature) of each perceiver is ultimately consciousness itself. The differences are only in degree of self-recognition.

## Undivided Śiva's Five Aspects (10.185–186ab)
Even Śiva is spoken of as having five aspects (cognition, will, action, bliss, etc.) — but these are not real divisions. They are the teaching's concession to the student's limited perspective.

## Connection to the Vital Breath (10.186cd–205)
The entire fifteen-fold structure is now projected into the vital breath (*prāṇa*). The fifteen divisions correspond to phases of the breath cycle, making the ascent through perceivers a practical yogic technique.
