# Topic 94: Definition of the Purity of the Medium of Reflection

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 94 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: TĀ 3/8-9

---

## 1. Core Teaching

The teacher Utpaladeva has defined "purity" (nairmalya) — the capacity of an entity to reflect another — as its capacity to manifest in identity with itself, within itself, something different from it, without losing its own clearly apparent form. Only the Lord of Consciousness possesses this capacity in its primary form — in every respect, reflecting all types of sensations. All other reflective media (mirrors, water, etc.) are pure only partially, and by the Lord's specific intention.

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 3/8** (lines 5862-5868): *svasminn abhedād bhinnasya darśanakṣamataiva yā | atyaktasvaprakāśasya nairmalyaṁ tad gurūditam*
The teacher said that purity is the capacity to manifest in identity with itself, within itself, something different from it, without losing its own clearly apparent form.

**TĀ 3/9** (lines 5978-5984): *nairmalyaṁ mukhyam ekasya saṁvinnāthasya sarvataḥ | aṁśāṁśikātaḥ kvāpy anyad vimalaṁ tattadicchayā*
Only the Lord of Consciousness is endowed with purity in its primary form, in every respect. Anything else is pure only partially, by the Lord's specific intention.

## 3. Philosophical Context

This verse attributes the doctrine to Utpaladeva — the great teacher of the Pratyabhijñā school. Jayaratha tells us that the Doctrine of Reflection was first expounded by Utpaladeva in his commentary on his own Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā.

---

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
