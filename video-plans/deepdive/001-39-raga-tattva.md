# RĀGA TATTVA — The Principle of Attachment
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Rāga tattva is the 28th principle, the third of the five *kañcukas*. It is the principle of attachment — the power that creates the experience of desire, longing, and psychological dependency. In the bound soul, Rāga manifests as the sense of incompleteness: "I am not whole; I need something outside myself to be fulfilled." In reality, the Self is already full (*pūrṇa*). Rāga is the contraction of this fullness into the experience of lack. Jayaratha explains that Rāga is what binds the soul to objects, driving the cycle of pursuit, satisfaction, and renewed craving.

---

### Key Verse — The Goddess as Thirty-six Principles (TĀ 1.195)

**Sanskrit:**
```
yāvān ṣaṭtriṁśakaḥ so 'yaṁ yad anyad api kiñcana |
etāvatī mahādevī rudraśaktir anargalā || 195 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (Vol. 1, line ~29136):**
"As is this entire group of thirty-six (principles) and so too whatever else (may exist), such is the extent of the Great Goddess, Who is Rudra's power free of (all) restrictions."

**Explanation:**
Rāga, like all principles, is a form of the Goddess — but in its contracted mode, it appears as the driving force of saṃsāra. When recognized, it is reabsorbed into the unconditional fullness of consciousness.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On the kañcukas (Vol. 5, line ~21685): Jayaratha lists the five coverings as Necessity (Niyati), Time (Kāla), Attachment (Rāga), capacity for limited knowledge (Vidyā), and limited agency (Kalā). Rāga is the emotional dimension of limitation — the sense of attraction that binds the perceiver to objects of experience.

Jayaratha elsewhere explains that Rāga is the soul's misidentification of its own innate bliss (*ānanda*) with external objects. The soul seeks outside what it already is.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Experience of Lack**: Rāga is the ontological root of desire. The universal I-consciousness, being full, has no need. The contracted soul, by contrast, experiences itself as deficient — and Rāga drives it to seek completion through objects, relationships, and experiences.

2. **More Than Emotional Attachment**: Rāga is not merely psychological attachment but a metaphysical principle — the structure of consciousness that gives rise to the experience of desire. It colors all perception, making the world appear as a field of attractions and aversions.

3. **Reversal Through Recognition**: When the soul recognizes its own innate fullness as Śiva, Rāga dissolves. The energy of attachment is not destroyed but transformed — the longing for objects becomes the bliss of self-recognition (*vimarśānanda*).
