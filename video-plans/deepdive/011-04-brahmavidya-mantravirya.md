# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Brahmavidyā and the Three Vidyās — The Liberating Mantras

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 11, Chapter 30, Verses 62cd-123 (~lines 2260-2500)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
Among all the mantras of the Trika, the Brahmavidyā holds a special place. It is the mantra that directly awakens liberating knowledge (brahma-vidyā). Abhinava presents it in two forms — with parts (sakalā) and without parts (niṣkalā) — and also teaches the three Vidyās of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī, the supreme deity of the Krama system.

## Part 1: The Brahmavidyā With Parts (Sakalā) (0:45-3:00)
- Verses 64cd-66ab: The Brahmavidyā composed of distinct syllables
- This is the mantra used in the "Scale Initiation" (tulādīkṣā) of Chapter 20
- Its structure includes:
  - Invocations to the supreme reality
  - Seed-syllables of the Trika goddesses
  - Phrases that assert identity with Śiva
- The "parts" (syllables, words, phrases) are supports for meditation
- Each part corresponds to a specific reality level (tattva)
- Reciting the parts systematically purifies each level
- This form is for practitioners who still need support (ādhāra)

## Part 2: The Brahmavidyā Without Parts (Niṣkalā) (3:00-5:00)
- Verses 90cd-92: The formless Brahmavidyā
- This is the Brahmavidyā in its essence — without divisions
- It is simply the awareness "I am Bhairava" — without any articulated sounds
- This corresponds to:
  - The Śāmbhava level (divine means)
  - Pure awareness without mental constructs
  - Direct recognition (pratyabhijñā)
- The distinction between sakalā and niṣkalā:
  - Sakalā = path (the means)
  - Niṣkalā = goal (the realization)
- Both are essential: one works through the other
- The niṣkalā Brahmavidyā is the heart of the entire Trika teaching

## Part 3: The Mantras for Scale Initiation (5:00-6:30)
- Verses 91cd-98: Mantras for the tulādīkṣā
- **Mantra for fetching the sacrificial animal** (verses 91cd-92)
- **Mantra to paralyze Śākinī** (verses 93-94ab)
- **Bhairava's Heart Mantra** (verses 94cd-99):
  - A powerful protective mantra
  - Contains the essence of all Bhairava mantras
  - Used for initiation and for self-protection
- These mantras show the practical, magical dimension of the Trika
- They are not merely philosophical — they are effective powers

## Part 4: The Three Vidyās of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī (6:30-9:30)
- Verses 102-121ab: Three progressively higher Vidyās
- **First Vidyā (verses 107-110ab):** The external form
- **Second Vidyā (verses 110cd-115):** The intermediate form
- **Third Vidyā (verses 116-121ab):** The supreme form
- Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī is the supreme deity of the Krama system
- She is "beyond even Parā" (parātītā) of the Trika
- Her Vidyā is the most secret — Abhinava deliberately conceals it
- Jayaratha quotes the Devyāyāmala where it is given in code
- Abhinava's promotion of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī as the supreme deity tells us that her Vidyā was his main mantra, and so he kept it secret
- The three Vidyās take the practitioner from external worship through internal meditation to direct realization

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The Brahmavidyā completes the mantric repertoire of the Trika. From the three goddesses' Vidyās through the Brahmavidyā to Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī's secret Vidyās, the practitioner has a complete sonic path from duality to nonduality. The next chapter moves from mantras to their ritual embodiment: the Trika Maṇḍala.
