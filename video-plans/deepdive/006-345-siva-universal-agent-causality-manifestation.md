# 345 — Śiva Is the Universal Agent, Causality Is His Manifestation
**Tantrāloka 9.35cd–40ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Causality is not a law of nature — it is the grammar of divine freedom.

## Core Teaching
The entire causal process — from the seed's sprouting to the potter's crafting — is the single act of Śiva manifesting as the universe. Causality is not something that happens TO Śiva; it IS Śiva's own activity.

## Summary of the Section
The long argument from 9.7 to 9.40ab concludes:
- **9.35cd–36**: The relationship between cause and effect is the *prakāśa* (manifestation) of an entity in consciousness
- **9.37–38**: This manifestation occurs through the three modalities (internal, objective, external)
- **9.39–40ab**: Therefore, causality is the self-expression of free consciousness

## The Trika Doctrine of Causality
Unlike:
- **Sāṁkhya**: *satkāryavāda* — effect pre-exists in insentient *prakṛti*
- **Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika**: *asatkāryavāda* — effect is new, produced by conjunction of causes
- **Buddhist**: *pratītyasamutpāda* — dependent origination of momentary dharmas

Abhinavagupta's **ābhāsavāda**: all things appear (*ābhāsa*) as manifestations of consciousness. Causality is the rule-governed succession of these appearances, freely chosen by Śiva.

## The Freedom Within Causality
The "necessity" of cause-effect is not compulsion but:
- Self-consistency of divine nature
- The *niyati* (limiting principle) operating within the impure sphere
- The aesthetic delight (*rasa*) of Śiva's creative play

## Bridge to Chapter 9.40cd–48ab
Having established the nature of causality, Abhinava now examines varieties of causal sequences (the order of emanation of the *kañcukas* and principles), showing how different Śaiva scriptures describe the same process from different perspectives.

## Bridge to Chapter 10
Just as causality is the manifestation of one agent through multiple causes, perceptibility is the manifestation of one object to multiple perceivers. The same logic — unity within diversity — governs both chapters.
