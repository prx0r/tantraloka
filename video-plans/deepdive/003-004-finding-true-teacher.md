# FINDING A TRUE TEACHER (GURU-SATTVAVICĀRA)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 33–40ab

---

### Core Teaching

How does one find a true teacher? Abhinavagupta addresses this question within the framework of Śiva's grace (*śaktipāta*). Even one who has been deceived by false teachers and inferior doctrines can be led to a true teacher through the practice of Sound Reasoning (*sattarka*). The mechanism is not merely intellectual: Sound Reasoning *is* Pure Knowledge (*śuddhavidyā*), and Pure Knowledge *is* the will of the Supreme Lord. The descent of Śiva's power operates through the power called Jyeṣṭhā (the goddess of liberating knowledge), who makes the aspirant "propense to accept" a true teacher. This establishes that the nature of a true teacher is inseparable from the liberating knowledge he embodies — and that the search for a teacher is itself a manifestation of grace.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/33–40ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 43–50, lines 3673–4110.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verse 33 — The Danger of False Teachers

**Sanskrit:**
```
tenājñajanatākḷptapravādair yo viḍambitaḥ |
asadgurau rūḍhacit sa māyāpāśena rañjitaḥ || 33 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 3684–3697):** "Thus, he who is deceived by (inferior) doctrines contrived by the ignorant, and places his trust in a false teacher, is made to be attached (to them) by the snare of Māyā."

Jayaratha explains (lines 3699–3720): "He, a Vaiṣṇava or the like, has been deceived by the hordes of teachers such as Kapila with their inferior doctrines." The "snare of Māyā" is the power called Vāmā — the goddess who binds and deludes. In contrast, "one who trusts a true teacher has liberation (there in front of him) directly." The point is "established by its contrary counterpart (*vyatireka*)" — a logical argument from counterexample.

---

#### Verse 34 — Even the Deceived Can Be Led to a True Teacher

**Sanskrit:**
```
so 'pi sattarkayogena nīyate sadguruṁ prati |
sattarkaḥ śuddhavidyā sā cecchā parameśituḥ || 34 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 3731–3737):** "However, even he is led to a true teacher by (practicing) the Yoga of Sound Reasoning (*sattarkayoga*). Sound Reasoning is (not just talk), it is Pure Knowledge itself, and that is the will of the Supreme Lord."

Jayaratha comments (lines 3764–3782): "Even he, the Vaiṣṇava and the like, who has placed his trust in a false teacher, generates a sound intention by sound discriminative reasoning concerning reality, and then a true teacher is attained by Śiva's power." The expression "led to a true teacher" is found in MV 1/44d — it is one "who is penetrated by Rudra's power" who receives this grace.

Swami Lakshmanjoo explains (lines 3798–3814): the word *yuktiyukta* means both "sound and reasonable" and "engaged in deep meditation." Sound Reasoning is thus twofold: "engagement in rational thought centred on understanding the teachings" and "the insight of Pure Knowledge" which is the experience "I am all this and all this am I."

---

#### Verse 35 — Scriptural Authority: The Mālinīvijayatantra

**Sanskrit:**
```
śrīpūrvaśāstre tenoktaṁ sa yiyāsuḥ śivecchayā |
bhuktimuktiprasiddhyarthaṁ nīyate sadguruṁ prati || 35 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 3785–3790):** "Thus (the Lord) declares in the Mālinīvijayatantra: 'he (who) desires to go to a true teacher in order to attain worldly benefits (*bhukti*) and liberation (*mukti*) is led (to him) by Śiva's will.'"

Swami Lakshmanjoo comments (lines 3817–3822): "Bhoga is enjoyment of the universe. Mokṣa [liberation] means establishment in one's own Self. As long as there is no *bhukti* [worldly enjoyment], *mukti* [liberation] is not possible. There must be *bhoga* in *mokṣa* and *mokṣa* in *bhoga*. Desire is not excluded in Śaivism because it is the expression of Lord Śiva."

---

#### Verse 36 — Gradual Descent of Grace

**Sanskrit:**
```
śaktipātas tu tatraiṣa kramikaḥ saṅpravartate |
sthitvā yo 'sadgurau śāstrāntare vā satpathaṁ śritaḥ || 36 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 3846–3851):** "There (in that case), the descent of the power (of grace) (*śaktipāta*) is gradual (*kramika*) (for one who), having followed a false teacher or (some) other scripture, (subsequently) takes to the true path."

---

#### Verses 37–39 — Distinguishing True from False Teachers

Jayaratha establishes the criteria: a true teacher is one who has direct realization of the nondual reality, while a false teacher operates within the framework of duality. The nature of a *sadguru* is further developed in the classification of five types of teachers that follows (4/40cd–78ab). The essential point is that a teacher is known by the liberating knowledge he embodies — not by lineage, ritual status, or external marks.

---

#### Verse 40ab — The Transition to the Five Types

**Sanskrit:**
```
sa tāvat kasyacit tarkaḥ svata eva pravartate || 40 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 4113–4114):** "This (discriminative intuitive) reasoning (*tarka*) arises thus for some (spontaneously) by itself (*svata eva*)."

---

### The Two Goddesses: Vāmā and Jyeṣṭhā

Jayaratha explains (lines 3744–3747) that there are two aspects of Śiva's power:

1. **Vāmā**: Māyā, who binds and deludes, causing attachment to false teachers
2. **Jyeṣṭhā**: Pure Knowledge, who liberates and enlightens, leading to true teachers

"The stimulus brought about by sound discriminative reasoning to be propense to (accepting) a true teacher is that of Śiva's power itself" (lines 3833–3835).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Grace and Effort Are Not Opposed**: Sound Reasoning is not merely human effort — it is "Pure Knowledge itself," which "is the will of the Supreme Lord." The practice of discerning reflection is itself a manifestation of grace. The distinction between "human" and "divine" action collapses in the nondual perspective.

2. **The Guru as Embodied Knowledge**: The true teacher is defined by his realization, not by external credentials. The *Brahmayāmala* is quoted later (4/60ab): "Apart from the knowledge of (ultimate) reality, there is no other distinguishing characteristic (of a true teacher)" (lines 6037–6040).

3. **The Inclusivity of Grace**: Even one who has followed a false teacher can be led to the truth. Grace operates gradually (*kramika*) — "having followed a false teacher or some other scripture, (subsequently) takes to the true path" (4/36). No one is excluded from the possibility of liberation.

4. **The Integration of Bhukti and Mukti**: Worldly enjoyment and liberation are not opposed. The true path includes both — "desire is not excluded in Śaivism because it is the expression of Lord Śiva" (lines 3820–3822).
