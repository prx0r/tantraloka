# Daily Practice: The Sandhyā Worship

**Tantrāloka 26.29cd–51ab | Lines 742–753**

## Overview

The sandhyā worship (worship at the three junctures of the day) is the backbone of the Trika initiate's daily practice. Each juncture — dawn (prātaḥ), noon (mādhyāhna), and dusk (sāyaṁ) — is a threshold where the energies of creation, preservation, and dissolution are accessed.

## Key Verses

- **29cd–37ab (lines 742–744):** Worship at the junctures of the day.
- **37cd (line 744):** The regular worship on the sacrificial surface (sthaṇḍila).
- **37cd–51ab (lines 745–746):** Invocation (āvāhana) of the deities in sequence.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Sandhyā as temporal tīrtha:** Just as a physical tīrtha (ford, crossing place) is where the veil between worlds is thin, the sandhyā times are temporal tīrthas — moments when the transition between day and night mirrors the transition between the manifest and the unmanifest.

2. **Three goddesses of the day:** Dawn is presided over by Aparā (the immanent goddess of action), noon by Parāparā (the goddess of knowledge who bridges immanence and transcendence), and dusk by Parā (the transcendent goddess of will).

3. **The sthaṇḍila as the universe:** The sacrificial surface (sthaṇḍila) is purified, smeared with cowdung, and marked with lines of rice flour. It becomes a maṇḍala — a microcosm of the universe. Worship on it is worship of the cosmos.

4. **Invocation (āvāhana) as recognition:** The sequence of invocation — from the outermost deities to the innermost — is a progressive recognition of the divine presence in all things. It culminates in the invocation of the supreme Śiva in the heart.

## The Inner Meaning

The sandhyā worship is not about pleasing external deities but about aligning one's own consciousness with the cosmic rhythms. Dawn = the arising of awareness from sleep (deep sleep = the unmanifest). Noon = the full brilliance of conscious activity. Dusk = the withdrawal of awareness back into its source. The practitioner flows with this rhythm, never fighting it.
