# 337 — The Relationship Between Cause and Effect
**Tantrāloka 9.7–40ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Is causality a relation between dead objects, or the creative freedom of universal consciousness?

## Core Teaching
Abhinavagupta reframes causality entirely: the cause-effect relation is not an automatic mechanism of insentient things but the manifestation of Śiva's agency across different levels of contraction. The *siddhayogeśvarīmata* is cited as scriptural authority.

## Key Structure
The section divides into:
1. Supreme Śiva the Agent (8–10)
2. Refutation of Buddhist momentariness (11–12)
3. Seed and sprout: succession and continuity (13–17ab)
4. Succession and perception (17cd–20ab)
5. Succession as nature of the perceiver (20cd–22)
6. Nature of causal necessity (23–29)
7. Unification of causal factors / *sāmagrī* (30–35ab)
8. Śiva as universal agent, causality as His manifestation (35cd–40ab)

## Three Modalities of Manifestation
An entity manifests in three ways (lines 1755–1765):
1. **Internal** (*āntaratva*) — oneness with consciousness before perception
2. **Objective** (*grāhyatva*) — perceptible by mind alone (e.g., pleasure)
3. **External** (*bāhyatva*) — perceptible by both inner and outer senses (e.g., a jar)

## The Causal Argument
- The seed does NOT cease to exist when the sprout arises — they share a common reality
- Non-existence is not a real thing (*vastu*); it cannot "become" existent
- Causality is the relation between agent (*kartṛ*) and action (*karman*), not between dead objects
- The effect is "the sphere of the act of manifestation"

## Central Verse
**9.8ab**: *vastutaḥ sarvabhāvānāṁ karteśānaḥ paraḥ śivaḥ* — "In reality, the Lord, who is Supreme Śiva, is the agent of all things."

## Philosophical Stakes
- Refutes Sāṁkhya: insentient *pradhāna* cannot be an agent
- Refutes Buddhist: momentariness makes causation unintelligible
- Refutes Nyāya: the potter is not the real agent — Śiva works through natural law (*niyati*)
- Establishes Pratyabhijñā: causality is the self-expression of free consciousness

## Connection to Perceptibility (Chapter 10)
This analysis of causality as perception-dependent manifestation prepares the ground for Chapter 10's perceptibility matrix, where one object shines differently for seven perceivers.
