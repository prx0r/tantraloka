# 351 — Karma and the Seven Perceivers
**Tantrāloka 9.88cd–97ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Does karma apply equally to all beings? A worm and a sage both act, but do their actions carry the same weight?

## Core Teaching
Karma operates differently for each type of perceiver. The seven perceivers (*sapta pramātṛ*) — from the fully conditioned (Sakala) to Śiva Himself — experience karmic results according to their degree of contraction.

## The Seven Perceivers and Their Karma

| Perceiver | Karmic Status |
|-----------|---------------|
| **Sakala** | Fully bound — karma accumulates fully |
| **Pralayākala** | Meritorious karma only (in dissolution) |
| **Vijñānākala** | Karma does not adhere (knowledge burns it) |
| **Mantra** | Acts are mantric — no karmic residue |
| **Mantreśvara** | Lords of mantra — beyond karma |
| **Mantramaheśvara** | Great lords — pure will |
| **Śiva** | No karma — pure freedom |

## Key Insight
Karma is not an objective mechanism. It depends on the perceiver's degree of self-awareness. The more contracted the consciousness, the more real and binding the karma. The more expanded, the more karma dissolves into conscious action.

## The Recursive Loop
1. *Mala* (contraction) → sense of limited self
2. Limited self → desire (*rāga*)
3. Desire → action (*karma*)
4. Action → karmic residue (*saṃskāra*)
5. Residue → reinforces *mala*

This is the karmic recursion that Chapter 9 analyzes in detail (9.97cd–141).

## Bridge to the Nature of Karma (9.97cd–141)
Having established that karma varies by perceiver, Abhinava now examines the nature of karma itself: what is it? How does it stick? How is it removed?

## Connection to Chapter 10
The seven perceivers of Chapter 10 are the same seven discussed here. Chapter 9 establishes their relationship to karma; Chapter 10 establishes their relationship to perception. Together, they form a complete picture: the perceiver, karma, and perceptibility are three aspects of the same reality.
