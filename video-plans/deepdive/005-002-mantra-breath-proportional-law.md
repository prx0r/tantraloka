# Deep-Dive: The Arising of Mantras in the Breath — Proportional Law of Syllables

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7, verses 5cd–21ab  
**Catalog Entry:** #283  
**Lines:** 1196–1561

---

## Topic Overview

This is the mathematical heart of Cakrodaya. Abhinava lays out the proportional law governing how mantras of different syllable-lengths arise within the 21,600 daily breathing cycles. The key principle: one breathing cycle corresponds to one syllable of the mantra. Longer mantras arise proportionally fewer times per day.

### Verses 19cd–21ab (lines 1489–1502): Progressive Doubling

> krameṇettham idaṁ cakraṁ ṣaṭ kṛtvo dviguṇaṁ yadā
> tato 'pi dviguṇe 'ṣṭāṁśasyārdham adhyardham ekakam
> tato 'pi sūkṣmakuśalair ardhārdhādiprakalpane
> bhāgaṣoḍaśakasthityā sūkṣmaś cāro 'bhilakṣyate
> *"If one multiplies this cycle (of 108) by two, six times consecutively and then again by two, one arrives at a cycle that arises one and a half times plus one sixteenth (in twenty-four hours). The more subtle and expert yogis, dividing progressively in half, observe the increasingly subtle movement of the breath."*

The progression: 108 → 216 → 432 → 864 → 1,728 → 3,456 → 6,912 → 13,824 syllables. The 13,824-syllable mantra arises only once every 1.56 days. Further division yields cycles of 345,600 syllables (arising in 1/16th of a day), 691,200 (1/32nd), etc.

## Source: Yoginīkaula (lines 1346–1361, 1446–1460)

Jayaratha identifies the source as the Yoginīkaula. Abhinava has reworked and condensed it. Key citation from the Yoginīkaula (lines 1446–1460):

> *"The regular emergence of the breath takes place 21,600 times... When there are two syllables, 10,800... when three, 7,200... ending with: 'in the case of a cycle of 108 syllables, it is said to arise 200 times and bestows all accomplishments.'"*

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **One Breath, One Syllable:** The fundamental axiom — each breathing cycle corresponds to one syllable of mantra recitation. This is not arbitrary; breath and mantra share the same spanda-nature.

2. **Progressive Lengthening = Time Mastery:** By lengthening the mantra (more syllables per cycle), the yogi extends the duration of each breathing cycle, progressively mastering time itself.

3. **From 21,600 to 200:** The 108-syllable mantra arising only 200 times/day represents near-total mastery. The practitioner breathes only 200 times in 24 hours.

4. **Infinite Divisibility:** Beyond 108 syllables, the cycles become so long they arise less than once per day. The "subtle and expert" yogis perceive these extremely subtle movements of the breath.

5. **Mathematical Precision:** The figures are not approximate — Jayaratha explains they come directly from scripture. Some figures like 3,086 for 7 syllables are approximations (actual: 3,085.71).
