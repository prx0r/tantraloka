# The Absent Initiation: The Net Teaching and Examination

**Tantrāloka 21.25–61 | Lines 582–597**

## Overview

The "net teaching" (jālopadeśa) is the esoteric core of the absent initiation. The teacher weaves a net of mantras to capture the wandering soul of the dead person. Following this is the examination of the strength or weakness of the group of purifications (saṃskriyāgaṇe balābalavicāra).

## Key Verses

- **25–45 (lines 581–582):** The teaching concerning the net (jālopadeśa) — a detailed procedure for casting the net of mantras to catch the departed soul.
- **46–61 (lines 582–583):** Examination of the strength and weakness of the group of purifications — assessing whether the rites have been effective.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The net as the web of consciousness:** The "net" is the network of phonemes, mantras, and paths that constitute the universe. The teacher casts this net into the subtle realm where the departed soul wanders. The soul, entangled in the net of its own karma, is caught by the net of mantra and drawn upward.

2. **The soul after death:** According to Trika ontology, after death the soul enters a transitional state (preta) where it experiences the consequences of its karma. The net teaching intercepts this process and redirects the soul toward liberation.

3. **Balābalavicāra — the assessment of strength:** After performing the rites, the teacher examines whether the purifications are "strong" or "weak." This assessment can be made through various signs — dreams, intuitions, or subtle changes in the ritual materials.

4. **The power of intention:** The success of the absent initiation depends entirely on the teacher's power of intention (saṅkalpaśakti). His consciousness, expanded to encompass the universe, reaches the absent soul directly.

## The Limits of Rescue

Not every absent person can be rescued. The text specifies certain categories — those who died as apostates, those who committed the "five great sins," those who were enemies of Śaivism — may be beyond reach. But even this is qualified: with sufficient intensity of grace, no obstacle is absolute.
