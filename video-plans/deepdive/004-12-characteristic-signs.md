# 004-12: Characteristic Signs on the Path

- **Duration:** 10:00

## Key Verses (with line numbers)
- TĀ 5.101–112cd — pages 133–149
- Signs are described for each stage of ascent — lines 379–381
- The "characteristic signs" (liṅga) that mark progress — throughout section

## Central Thesis
The Characteristic Signs on the Path are objective markers of the yogi's progress through the stages of ascent. These include physical sensations, mental states, auditory phenomena, and visionary experiences that spontaneously arise as consciousness pierces through each level of the dvādaśānta.

## Script Outline

### 1. The Need for Landmarks (0:00–2:00)
- The yogic path is internal and subjective — objective signs confirm progress
- These signs prevent self-deception and provide encouragement — lines 379–381
- They are not goals in themselves but indicators of authentic development
- The signs correspond to each of the ten voids/states/abodes

### 2. The Progressive Signs (2:00–4:30)
- EARLY STAGES:
  - Sensations of lightness, levitation — line 10866
  - Trembling, shaking — line 10872
  - Joy, bliss — line 10863
- MIDDLE STAGES:
  - Visions of light, colours, deities
  - Auditory phenomena: sounds, music, unstruck resonance
  - The breath becomes increasingly subtle
- LATER STAGES:
  - Dissolution of the sense of separate identity
  - Experience of oneness with all things
  - Cessation of the distinction between internal and external

### 3. The Body's Transformations (4:30–6:00)
- Upward leap of the body — line 10866
- Shaking when residual traces dissolve — lines 10872–10875
- Stable realization when traces completely dissolve — lines 10899
- The body becomes transparent to consciousness
- "The Great Pervasion" is when the binding identification with the body ends — lines 3924–3927

### 4. The Inner Signs (6:00–8:00)
- The state of certainty (niścaya) becomes unshakeable
- Thought constructs (vikalpa) dissolve into pure awareness
- "Although he sees something, it is as if he does not see it" — lines 1641–1644
- The yogi's meditation (anusandhāna) becomes continuous
- Signs of the Fourth State: lucidity at the junction of states — lines 4084–4098

### 5. Avoiding Pitfalls (8:00–9:30)
- Signs can become attachments — the yogi must not grasp them
- The Spanda teaching: "when the sun and moon dissolve, the dull-minded yogi falls into deep sleep" — lines 4210–4213
- Maintaining awareness is essential through every stage
- The awakened yogi remains lucid, the dull one falls — lines 4212–4213

### 6. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- Signs are footprints on the path, not the destination
- They confirm the authenticity of the yogi's experience
- The final sign: no sign — the state where "meditation finds no direct application" — line 3969
