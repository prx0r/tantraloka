# The Nature of the Forces (Kalāsvarūpa)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

The five Forces (kalā) — Nivṛtti (Cessation), Pratiṣṭhā (Foundation), Vidyā (limited Knowledge), Śāntā (Tranquil), and Śāntyatītā (Beyond the Tranquil) — are the universal natures that pervade distinct groups of metaphysical principles (tattva). Unlike the tattvas themselves, which are specific ontological categories, the kalās are meta-categories: energies that extend through many principles simultaneously, binding them into functional groups for the purpose of initiation and yogic purification.

Abhinavagupta opens Chapter 11 by defining a Force as "the common universal nature that extends through its own given group of these principles, as distinct from other groups" (11.3). This definition mirrors the earlier definition of a tattva (9.4cd-6ab), but moves from the level of principle to the level of power (śakti). The kalās are not mere abstractions — they are the energies by which Śiva structures reality and by which the initiate ascends back to the source.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 11.2-3 — Definition of a Force

**Lines:** 1052–1063

> yathā pūrvoktabhuvanamadhye nijanijaṁ gaṇam |
> anuyat parato bhinnaṁ tattvaṁ nāmeti bhaṇyate || 2 ||
> tathā teṣv api tattveṣu svavarge 'nugamātmakam |
> vyāvṛttaṁ paravargāc ca kaleti śivaśāsane || 3 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"It was said before that a (metaphysical) principle (tattva) is said to be (the common universal) nature (rūpa) that extends through its own particular group of worlds, and is distinct from the other (principles). Analogously, according to Śaiva doctrine (śaivaśāsana), a Force (kalā) is said to be the (common) universal nature that extends through its own (given) group of these principles, as distinct from other groups."

**Explanation:**
The kalā is a *sāmānya* — a universal — that inheres across a set of tattvas. Just as "cow-ness" pervades individual cows and distinguishes them from horses, Nivṛtti (Cessation) pervades only the Earth principle, while Pratiṣṭhā pervades the group from Water to Nature (prakṛti). This structuring makes possible the abbreviated form of initiation known as kalādīkṣā, where purification proceeds by force-groups rather than individual tattvas.

#### Verse 11.4 — An Alternative View: Kalā as Subtle Power

**Lines:** 1088–1092

> kecid āhuḥ punar yā 'sau śaktir antaḥ susūkṣmikā |
> tattvānāṁ sā kalety uktā dharaṇyāṁ dhārikā yathā || 4 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Again, (from another point of view,) some say that this Force is that extremely subtle power that is present within the Principles, as is, for example, the sustaining power within the Earth Principle."

**Explanation:**
This alternative definition treats the kalā as an immanent *śakti* — the inner dynamism that sustains each group of principles from within. The two definitions (kalā as universal vs. kalā as subtle power) are ultimately equivalent, as Abhinava states in verse 5: "the essential point does not appear to differ from these two perspectives."

---

#### Verse 11.5 — Reconciliation of the Two Views

**Lines:** 1134–1140

> atra pakṣadvaye vastu na bhinnaṁ bhāsate yataḥ |
> anugāmi na sāmānyam iṣṭaṁ naiyāyikādivat || 5 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Here, (according to us,) the essential point (vastu) does not appear to differ from these two perspectives, because we do not maintain that this common nature (anugāmin) is a universal (sāmānya), as do, for example, the Naiyāyikas."

**Explanation:**
Abhinava rejects the Nyāya conception of a universal as a static, abstract category. For the Trika, the kalā is a *dynamic energy* — not a mere class-concept but a real power that operates within the principles. The two views differ only in emphasis: one foregrounds the kalā's pervasiveness, the other its immanent energy. Both describe the same reality.

---

#### Verse 11.6-7 — Convention Established by Śiva

**Lines:** 1160–1191

> anye vadanti dīkṣādau sukhasaṁgrahaṇārthataḥ |
> śivena kalpito vargaḥ kaleti samayāśrayaḥ || 6 ||
> kṛtaś ca devadevena samayo 'paramārthatām |
> na gacchatīti nāsatyo na cānyasamayodayaḥ || 7 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Others say that 'Force' is (just) a conventional term (samaya) that refers to a group (of principles) imagined (kalpita) by Śiva, so that the rites of initiation and the like may be performed easily in an abbreviated form. (6) (We reply that this is not so.) A convention established by the Lord of the gods does not become ultimately unreal. Thus, it is not false, (nor is it arbitrary, as) no other convention arises (that can replace it). (7)"

**Explanation:**
The objection that the kalās are merely convenient fictions is refuted: because the convention is established by Śiva himself, it participates in the real. It is not an arbitrary human construction but a divinely instituted taxonomy that corresponds to the actual structure of reality. The three criteria for a valid convention are: (1) it does not become unreal, (2) it is not false, (3) no other convention arises to replace it.

### Narrative Arc for the Video

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** The problem of many tattvas — how does one purify through 36 principles efficiently? The kalās as the answer: a grouping system that condenses the path.

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** The two definitions of kalā — universal nature (sāmānya) vs. immanent power (śakti). Both are correct. The reconciliation in verse 5. The rejection of the Nyāya abstract universal.

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** Are the kalās real or just convenient conventions? The objection and Abhinava's rebuttal. The authority of Śiva's institution.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** The five kalās introduced in outline — Nivṛtti (Earth), Pratiṣṭhā (Water to Prakṛti), Vidyā (Puruṣa to Māyā), Śāntā (up to Śakti tattva), Śāntyatītā (Śiva tattva). Their correspondence to the four cosmic eggs and the fifth which is beyond.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** Why this matters for practice — the kalādīkṣā (initiation by forces). How condensing the path makes liberation accessible.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The kalās as the bridge between ontology and soteriology. They are not just categories but energies of consciousness that the initiate must recognize and transcend.

### Key Commentarial Notes

- Jayaratha clarifies that the "subtle power" of verse 4 is called *kalayitrī* — "that which operates/measures" — because each Force measures out (kalana) its sphere of principles.
- The five kalās are also homologized with the five elements (earth, water, fire, air, ether) and with the five faces of Śiva (Sadyojāta, Vāmadeva, Aghora, Tatpuruṣa, Īśāna).
- The *Mālinīvijayottara* (2/49) is the source text for the four-egg schema that correlates with the first four kalās.
