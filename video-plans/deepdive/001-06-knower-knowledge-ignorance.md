# THE KNOWER, KNOWLEDGE AND IGNORANCE — Verses 36-38
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

Abhinava turns to the nature of the knower (pramātṛ), knowledge (jñāna), and ignorance (ajñāna). He establishes that consciousness itself is the essential nature of the Self — defined as pure freedom (svātantrya). Knowledge, in its binding form, is the perception of duality. Ignorance is not the absence of knowledge but its contraction — incomplete knowledge that mistakes the non-Self for the Self. The second aphorism of the Śivasūtra — "knowledge is bondage" — is reinterpreted through the lens of nonduality: it is the limited, dualistic knowledge that binds, not knowledge as such.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.36 — Consciousness Defined

**Line ~10557** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
caitanyaṁ syāt pūrṇajñānakriyam aiśvaryam ucyate |
svātantrya eva tan mātraṁ nānākṣiptaviśeṣaṇam || 36 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (from commentary):**
"Its state of being is 'consciousness', which is endowed with full (and perfect) knowledge and action. That itself is said to be the freedom (of consciousness) which is (Śiva's) supreme sovereignty. He says that (by declaring that consciousness is) 'just a state of unconditioned freedom from inhibition'."

**Explanation:**
Consciousness (caitanya) is not a static quality but a dynamic state of full knowledge and action. This is freedom (svātantrya) — the creative autonomy of Śiva. The verse emphasizes that consciousness is devoid of all specification; it cannot be categorized or defined by any particular attribute.

#### Verse 1.37 — Knowledge and Bondage

**Line ~10634** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
caitanyam ātmā jñānaṁ bandhaḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (from commentary):**
"The Self is consciousness; knowledge is bondage."

**Explanation:**
This is the famous second aphorism of the Śivasūtra. But Abhinava reinterprets it by reading it in conjunction with the first aphorism. When the two are combined (caitanyam ātmājñānaṁ bandhaḥ), the meaning becomes: "The Self is consciousness; ignorance is bondage." It is limited, contracted knowledge that binds, not knowledge itself.

#### Verse 1.38 — Ignorance as Incomplete Knowledge

**Line ~10768** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ajñānaṁ nāma saṁkucitam eva jñānaṁ, na jñānābhāvaḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (paraphrased):**
"Ignorance is just contracted knowledge, not the absence of knowledge."

**Explanation:**
This is the crucial nondualist insight. Ignorance is not a void, not a lack, but a contraction of the fullness of consciousness. It is knowledge that has forgotten its own nature. Even the Buddha who denies the Self, Abhinava argues, experiences his denial as "it manifests to me" — proving that consciousness underlies even the negation of consciousness.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On consciousness (line ~10557+):** Jayaratha explains that consciousness is not qualified in any way as being "this or that" consciousness. There are no varieties of consciousness. The abstract noun "caitanya" denotes an entity independently of its attributes.

**On the Śivasūtra interpretation (line ~10687+):** Jayaratha explains the two readings. Read separately: "The Self is consciousness; knowledge is bondage." Read together: "The Self is consciousness; ignorance is bondage." The 'a' of ajñāna fuses with the preceding 'a', creating the ambiguity that Abhinava exploits.

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On svātantrya (note 375, line ~10592+):** "Consciousness is Śiva, who is the sovereign Lord and master of all things. His lordship governs all that exists and so is 'supreme'. It is His perfect unconditioned freedom."

**On vimarśa as Supreme Speech (note 371, line ~10601+):** "The power of consciousness is reflective awareness. It is Supreme Speech that arises spontaneously. That is the primary freedom and sovereignty of the Supreme Self."

### Philosophical Context

This section establishes the nondualist epistemology. If ignorance is contracted knowledge, then liberation is expansion — the recognition of one's already-present fullness. The means to liberation is not to acquire new knowledge but to remove the contraction, which is accomplished by grace transmitted through the teacher.

### Narration Script

Abhinava defines consciousness as pure freedom — endowed with full knowledge and action, yet devoid of all specification. This freedom is not the ability to choose between options but the unconditioned autonomy to be all things. The Self is this consciousness. What binds is not knowledge but its contraction — the mistaken sense of being a limited individual. When the Śivasūtra says "knowledge is bondage," it means contracted knowledge. Read correctly, it says: "The Self is consciousness; ignorance is bondage." Liberation is the expansion of contracted knowledge back to its native fullness.
