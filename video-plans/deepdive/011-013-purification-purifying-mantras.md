# Purification and Purifying Mantras of the Anuttara Trika
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

Purification (saṁskāra) of the six paths proceeds through six stages, each employing a specific set of purifying mantras. The Anuttara Trika — the highest teaching of the Trika school — provides the mantras that purify the Path of the Forces (kalādhvan). Abhinavagupta details the application of these mantras in a graduated process that moves from the grossest to the subtlest levels. The purification is not merely ritual — it is the transformation of the disciple's phonemic, mantric, and cognitive structures.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verses 11.86-87ab — Six Stages of Purification

**Lines approximately 2050–2080:**

The six stages correspond to the six paths:
1. Purification of the **Phonemes** (varṇaśuddhi)
2. Purification of the **Mantras** (mantraśuddhi)
3. Purification of the **Padas** (padaśuddhi)
4. Purification of the **Forces** (kalāśuddhi)
5. Purification of the **Principles** (tattvaśuddhi)
6. Purification of the **Worlds** (bhuvanaśuddhi)

Each stage uses mantras specific to that path. The purification proceeds from the most subtle (phonemes) to the most gross (worlds), or vice versa depending on the tradition.

---

#### Verses 11.87cd-93ab — The Application of Purifying Mantras

**Lines approximately 2080–2150:**

The purifying mantras of the Anuttara Trika include:
- **SAUḤ** — the heart-mantra of the Trika, representing the absolute
- **KHPHREṀ** — the mantra of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī, the goddess who draws time into herself
- **HRĪṀ** — the heart-mantra of the Yoginīs
- The **Aghora mantra** for purification of the forces

These mantras are deposited (nyāsa) on the body of the disciple, on the sacrificial ground, and in the consciousness of the initiate. The teacher who performs the purification must himself be identified with the mantras — he must be the mantra in consciousness.

---

### The Process in Practice

1. The teacher meditates on the supreme reality as the mantra
2. He "touches" the disciple with the mantra — transmitting its energy
3. The six paths within the disciple are purified one by one
4. Each path is resolved into the next higher one
5. The culmination is the resolution of all paths into the supreme consciousness

---

#### The Role of the Anuttara Trika

The "Anuttara Trika" refers to the highest triad: Parā, Parāparā, and Aparā — the three goddesses who are the energies of consciousness. Their mantras are the most powerful purifiers because they are the energies of the absolute itself. The Anuttara — the "unsurpassed" — is the supreme reality from which all mantras arise and into which they dissolve.

Jayaratha explains that these mantras are not merely recited but *become* the consciousness of the practitioner. The purification is not the removal of something impure but the recognition that the paths were always pure — the mantras reveal what is already the case.

### Narrative Arc

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** Purification is the heart of initiation. But what is being purified? The six paths that constitute the disciple's cognitive structure.

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** The six stages of purification. Each path requires its own mantra. The progression from phonemes to worlds.

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** The purifying mantras of the Anuttara Trika. SAUḤ, KHPHREṀ, HRĪṀ — the energy of the absolute.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** Nyāsa — deposition of mantras on the body. The body becomes the path. The teacher as the living mantra.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** The resolution of paths into consciousness. Purification as recognition, not cleansing.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The six stages are the ascent from the many to the one. The mantras are the vehicle, and the destination is the mantra's own source.
