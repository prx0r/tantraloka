# 004-20: Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī — The Three Goddesses of Time

## Central Thesis
Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā, and Raudrī are the three goddesses who govern the movement of time within the breath. They correspond to the three fundamental energies of will, knowledge, and action, and their interplay creates the flow of time. Understanding them is the key to mastering the temporal means (Kālopāya).

## Script Outline

### 1. Opening: The Triad of Time (0:00–1:30)
- The three goddesses are the powers of Śiva that regulate time
- Vāmā = the power of will (icchā)
- Jyeṣṭhā = the power of knowledge (jñāna)
- Raudrī = the power of action (kriyā)
- They are not separate deities but three aspects of one consciousness

### 2. Vāmā — The Power of Will (1:30–3:00)
- Vāmā governs the impulse to create and emit — lines 428
- She is the forward flow of time — the urge to manifest
- In the breath: the impulse to exhale, the outward movement
- Associated with creation (sṛṣṭi)
- "Vāmā" also means "left" — the left channel (iḍā) aspect

### 3. Jyeṣṭhā — The Power of Knowledge (3:00–4:30)
- Jyeṣṭhā governs the sustaining and knowing of manifestation — lines 428
- She is the awareness that accompanies time
- In the breath: the retention (kumbhaka), the stillness within movement
- Associated with persistence (sthiti)
- "Jyeṣṭhā" means "eldest" — she precedes and contains all

### 4. Raudrī — The Power of Action (4:30–6:00)
- Raudrī governs the withdrawal and dissolution — lines 428
- She is the reverse flow of time — the urge to merge back
- In the breath: the impulse to inhale, the inward movement
- Associated with withdrawal (saṁhāra)
- "Raudrī" means "fierce" — she consumes all back into consciousness

### 5. The Movement of the Breath as Their Play (6:00–8:00)
- TĀ 6.60: "All depend on Time; its nature is the vital breath" — lines 19687–19688
- The three goddesses spin the wheel of breath
- Their interplay creates the 21,600 daily cycles — lines 3789–3792
- Uccāra = the movement of the breath = the activity of the three goddesses — lines 430
- The yogi who understands their play is liberated

### 6. Liberation Through the Three (8:00–9:30)
- By recognizing Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī in each breath, the yogi transcends their play
- The three merge into the fourth: the supreme goddess Parā
- Parā is beyond the three times (past, present, future)
- "Liberation is attained by the revelation of this entire process" — commentary lines ~19690s

### 7. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- Vāmā, Jyeṣṭhā, Raudrī are the heartbeat of time
- To know them is to know the secret of temporal existence
- Their union in Parā is the breath's ultimate rest

## Visual Suggestions
- The three goddesses as three faces of Kālī
- Their movement in the breath: creation (out), persistence (hold), dissolution (in)
- The wheel of time with three spokes
- The integration into Parā as the fourth, the centre of the wheel
