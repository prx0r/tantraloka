# THE TWELVE KĀLĪS — OVERVIEW (KĀLĪCAKRA)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 146cd–173ab

---

### Core Teaching

The Twelve Kālīs are the central revelation of the Krama system embedded within the Tantrāloka. They are not merely goddesses to be worshipped externally but are the twelve phases of consciousness (*saṃvitkrama*) through which the one Goddess of Consciousness — Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī, the Destroyer of Time — manifests, sustains, and withdraws all reality. Arranged in three groups of four, they govern the spheres of objectivity (*prameya*), the means of knowledge (*pramāṇa*), and subjectivity (*pramātṛ*), culminating in the Inexplicable (*anākhya*) state that transcends all three. The entire cycle is a "Wheel" (*cakra*) with twelve spokes, corresponding to the twelve months, twelve suns, twelve vowels, and the twelve phases of every cognitive act.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/146cd–173ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 195–252, lines 14450–18120.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verse 146cd — The Goddess as Time

**Sanskrit:**
```
devī cāpi prameye 'pi māsarāśyādirūpataḥ || 146cd ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 14561–14564):** "The goddess also resides (within the sphere of) objectivity in the form of the (twelve) months (of the year), the signs of the zodiac and the like."

Jayaratha explains (lines 14622–14637): The cycle of the twelve Kālīs is "the great cycle of time — *kālacakra*." The Year has been "from Vedic times a symbol of Totality embodied in the great God of the Śatapathabrāhmaṇa, Prajāpati."

---

#### Verse 147 — Consciousness Manifests as Both Internal and External

**Sanskrit:**
```
ata eṣā sthitā saṁvid antarbāhyobhayātmanā |
svayaṁ nirbhāsyatatrānyad bhāsayantīva bhāsate || 147 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 14647–14654):** "Thus, even as this abiding (unchanging) consciousness, having made itself manifest as both internal and external reality, it manifests as though it is making manifest something there (within itself) which is different (from itself)."

Although "supreme consciousness alone manifests, and nothing else at all can manifest apart from that," by "the power of its own innate freedom, it makes the subject etc. manifest as if it were separate from it, and so it pours out twelve-fold" (lines 14693–14696).

### The Structure of the Twelve Kālīs

The twelve Kālīs are divided into three groups of four, each governing one of the three cognitive spheres:

#### I. The Four Kālīs of Objectivity (*Prameya*) — Verses 148–151

| Kālī | Function | Cognitive Phase |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| 1. **Sṛṣṭikālī** | Emanation of objectivity | Object arises in consciousness |
| 2. **Raktakālī** | Attachment/coloring | Object persists through sensory delight |
| 3. **Sthitināśakālī** | Destruction of persistence | Object begins to withdraw |
| 4. **Yamakālī** | Restriction/inexplicable | Object in indefinable state |

#### II. The Four Kālīs of the Means of Knowledge (*Pramāṇa*) — Verses 152–163

| Kālī | Function | Cognitive Phase |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| 5. **Saṁhārakālī** | Withdrawal of objectivity | Emergence of means of knowledge |
| 6. **Mṛtyukālī** | Death/persistence of means | Means of knowledge stabilizes |
| 7. **Rudrakālī/Bhadrakālī** | Withdrawal of means | Means of knowledge withdraws |
| 8. **Mārtaṇḍakālī** | The Sun/inexplicable of means | Means of knowledge in indefinable state |

#### III. The Four Kālīs of Subjectivity (*Pramātṛ*) — Verses 164–173ab

| Kālī | Function | Cognitive Phase |
|------|----------|-----------------|
| 9. **Paramārkakālī** | Supreme Sun — emanation of subject | Subject emerges |
| 10. **Kālāgnirudrakālī** | Fire of Time — persistence of subject | Subject persists |
| 11. **Mahākālakālī** | Great Time — withdrawal of subject | Subject withdraws |
| 12. **Mahābhairavaghoracaṇḍakālī** | Horrific Kālī of Great Bhairava | Inexplicable state of subject |

---

### The Process of Consumption

Each group of four Kālīs follows the same pattern (line 14450–14503):

1. **Emanation** (*sṛṣṭi*) — generation of the sphere
2. **Persistence** (*sthiti*) — stabilization of the sphere
3. **Withdrawal** (*saṁhāra*) — retraction of the sphere
4. **The Inexplicable** (*anākhya*) — indefinable state beyond the three

This is the "four-fold" structure: "as each one, one at a time, has three natures, and in the three, is four-fold" (MVV 1/935–942, quoted at lines 14518–14549). The Kālīs "eat up" or "consume" each sphere — objectivity is consumed into the means of knowledge, means into subjectivity, and subjectivity into the pure consciousness of Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī.

---

### The Solar Wheel

The twelve Kālīs are explicitly identified with the twelve suns of the year (lines 14515–14549). Abhinavagupta's *Mālinīvijayavārtika* (1/935–942) states:

"Consciousness is twelve-fold in all the fields of emanation and the rest equally, as each one, one at a time, has three natures, and in the three, is four-fold."

The "Wheel with twelve spokes operates (in this way) all the time. The occurrence of (the twelve) months, the signs of the zodiac and the like, are just its gross state" (lines 14541–14544).

---

### The Purpose of the Teaching

Swami Lakshmanjoo explains (lines 14551–14620): "How can the Śaiva yogi on the plane of duality, which is Māyā and said to be that of subject and object associated with the means of knowledge, spontaneously attain the plane on which his own nature, which is the state of the supreme subject, be directly perceived?" The answer: the Twelve Kālīs show how the yogi can experience the supreme even within duality.

The ultimate goal is *nirvyuthānasamādhi* — "contemplation without emergence (from it)" (line 14620), where "the yogi's (inner state of) contemplation and (that of his) emergence from it become one" — called "Cosmic Bliss" (*jagadānanda*), considered "much more excellent than all the other planes of bliss" (lines 14614–14618).

---

### Connection to the Krama System

Jayaratha states (line 14701–14702) that Abhinavagupta "implicitly declares that this system (i.e. Anuttara Trika) is closely related to the Krama system (*kramanayasodara*)." The Krama system (also called Mahānaya, Kālīnaya, Devīnaya, Kramadarśana, and Atinaya) is one in which Kālī as Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī is "the Goddess of Consciousness Who is the perpetually active power of consciousness that 'measures out,' 'manifests' or 'emits' the streams of perceptions that constitute all things" (lines 11414–11417).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Cosmic Cognition**: Every act of perception recapitulates the entire cosmic process. The twelve phases are not just metaphysical categories — they are the structure of every moment of awareness.

2. **The Inexplicable as the Fourth**: Each group culminates in an *anākhya* (inexplicable) state that transcends the triad of emanation, persistence, and withdrawal. This "fourth" is not a separate state but the encompassing ground of the other three.

3. **Time as Goddess**: Kālī (from *kāla* = time) is the Goddess who both manifests and consumes time. The wheel of the year, the months, and the zodiac are her gross forms. To understand the Kālīs is to understand the nature of time itself as the pulsation of consciousness.

4. **The Identity of Krama and Trika**: Far from being a digression, the exposition of the Twelve Kālīs demonstrates the convergence of the Krama and Trika systems within Abhinavagupta's synthesis.
