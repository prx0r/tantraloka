# 348 — The Five Pure Principles
**Tantrāloka 9.49cd–55ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Above Māyā's realm of limitation, five principles of pure consciousness structure the experience of liberated beings. What are they, and how do they differ from the impure tattvas?

## Core Teaching
The five pure principles (*śuddhatattva*) — Śiva, Śakti, Sadāśiva, Īśvara, and Śuddhavidyā — constitute the pure path (*śuddhādhvan*). They are not "coverings" but progressive unfoldings of the I-consciousness.

## The Five Pure Principles

### 1. Śiva Tattva
Pure I-consciousness (*aham*). The "I" without any "this." The first pulsation of self-awareness.

### 2. Śakti Tattva
Reflexive power — the "I-ness" (*ahantā*) that makes self-awareness possible. The dynamic aspect of the static Śiva.

### 3. Sadāśiva Tattva
The "I-This" (*aham-idam*). Consciousness begins to turn toward objectivity while still grounded in subjectivity. *I* am this.

### 4. Īśvara Tattva
The "This-I" (*idam-aham*). Objectivity becomes prominent, but still grounded in the I. This *is* I.

### 5. Śuddhavidyā Tattva
Balanced awareness — the "I-This" without predominance of either subject or object. Dual and non-dual simultaneously.

## The Five Pure Subjects (9.53cd–55ab)
Corresponding to the five principles are five types of perceivers who experience freedom while remaining embodied:
1. **Vijñānākala** — those whose impurity is only the *āṇava mala* (individuality)
2. **Mantra** — knowers of mantra
3. **Mantreśvara** — lords of mantra
4. **Mantramaheśvara** — great lords of mantra
5. **Śiva** — the supreme perceiver

These pure subjects anticipate the seven perceivers of Chapter 10.

## Why Six Causes Are Not Tattvas (9.55cd–59)
The six causes (*sṛṣṭi, sthiti, saṃhāra, anugraha, vilaya, bhakti* or similar) are not tattvas because they are functions (*kriyā*) of consciousness, not categories of being. This distinction maintains the ontological purity of the tattva system.

## The Pure Path vs. Impure Path
The pure path (Śiva through Śuddhavidyā) is the realm of non-dual experience. Below Māyā begins the impure path, where dualistic experience predominates. The transition at Māyā is the most crucial pivot in the entire ontological scheme.
