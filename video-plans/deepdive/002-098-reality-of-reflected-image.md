# Topic 98: The Reality and Nature of the Reflected Image: The Mirror Teaching

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 98 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: TĀ 3/21-23

---

## 1. Core Teaching

The reflection in a mirror is real — not an illusion. Abhinava refutes the view that reflections are unreal. The face reflected in the mirror is my own face. It is a true reflection, not a mistake. Similarly, the world reflected in consciousness is real. This distinguishes Trika from Advaita Vedānta (which sees the world as illusory) and Buddhist Yogācāra (which sees it as a mistaken projection).

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 3/21-23** (approximately lines 6451-6650): *yathā hi darpaṇe rūpaṁ satyam eva prakāśate*
Just as the form in a mirror manifests as real.

## 3. Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the reality of reflections** (lines 5195-5203): "For the Buddhist idealists, the mirror example serves to illustrate that the world is just an unreal appearance. However, Abhinava, following Utpaladeva, takes care to stress that the reflections are real. The face reflected in the mirror is my own. It is a true reflection, not a mistake.

---

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
