## Central Thesis
The Ten Voids (daśaśūnya) are the ten levels of emptiness experienced by the yogi as consciousness withdraws from objectivity and ascends through increasingly subtle states. They are not nihilistic voids but plenitudes of consciousness where objectivity has ceased, revealing the luminous nature of awareness itself.

## Script Outline

### 1. Opening: The Paradox of the Void (0:00–1:30)
- In Trika Śaivism, the void is NOT nothing — it is the plenitude of consciousness — lines 3592–3625
- "That which is not void is called the Void" — SYT 4/292cd-293ab — lines 3592–3596
- Kṣemarāja: the "Nonbeing" meant here is the principle of consciousness (cittattva) — lines 3621–3625
- The void is the emptiness of deep sleep, which is the ground of all experience

### 2. The Ten Voids in the Breath's Ascent (1:30–4:00)
- The ten voids correspond to stations along the dvādaśānta axis — lines 372–373
- As the breath moves from Heart to End of Twelve, it passes through increasingly subtle voids
- Each void marks the dissolution of a level of objectivity
- Śūnyapramātṛ: the perceiver in the state of emptiness (deep sleep) — lines 3633–3635, 16839

### 3. The Void Beyond Void (4:00–6:00)
- Śūnyātiśūnya: "the void beyond voids" — line 16581
- This is the supreme void where all voids merge into consciousness itself
- "The place of rest for those yogis who have attained the highest" — lines 16640
- Beyond this is the pure light of consciousness — lines 16423–16425

### 4. Experiencing the Void (6:00–8:00)
- The yogi's experience: all objective content dissolves — lines 4136–4156
- Only alert awareness remains in "unfathomably deep emptiness" — lines 4136–4139
- This is not sleep — it is lucid emptiness, the Fourth State (turīya) — lines 4084–4098
- The transition between waking and sleep is the gateway — lines 4106–4115

### 5. The Void in the Six Blisses (8:00–9:30)
- Nijānanda is the bliss of resting in pure emptiness — lines 3440–3469
- Nirānanda emerges from that emptiness as the first movement of breath — lines 3678–3688
- The ten voids are the ten stages of emptying that prepare the six blisses
- Each bliss transcends a level of void

### 6. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- The Ten Voids are not absences but the progressive self-emptying of consciousness
- This emptying reveals the plenitude (pūrṇatā) that was always present
- The supreme void is the Heart itself, which is "full of all things"
