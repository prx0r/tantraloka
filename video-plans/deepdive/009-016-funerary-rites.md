# Funerary Rites (Antyeṣṭi)

**Tantrāloka 24.1–24ab | Lines 682–696**

## Overview

The funerary rites (antyeṣṭi) for a Trika initiate are radically different from conventional Hindu death rites. Since the initiate has already "died" to his former self through initiation, his physical death is merely the final shedding of an already-transcended body. The rites focus not on propitiating the ancestors or ensuring a good rebirth but on confirming the soul's already-attained union with Śiva.

## Key Verses

- **2–4, 6–11ab (line 685):** The qualified recipient (adhikārin) — who gets these rites and who does not.
- **5, 11cd–16 (lines 686–687):** The rite of passage (saṃskāra) — the body is treated as a form of Śiva.
- **11cd–17ab (lines 688–689):** The procedure according to the Kulagahvara Tantra.
- **17cd–20 (lines 692–693):** The procedure according to the Dīkṣottara.
- **21–24ab (lines 696–697):** The purpose of the funerary rite (tatprayojanam).

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The initiate has already died:** Initiation (dīkṣā) is the "first death" — it kills the fettered soul (paśu) and gives birth to the Śiva-soul (śiva). Physical death is merely the second death, the dissolution of the body that was already seen through.

2. **No mourning, no ancestors:** For the Trika initiate, the conventional śrāddha (ancestral offering) is inappropriate. The deceased is not a "departed ancestor" (pitṛ) but a liberated soul (śivayogin). Offerings are made to HIM, not for HIM.

3. **The body as Śiva's mūrti:** The corpse is cremated with mantras that identify it with Śiva's eight forms. The funeral pyre is the fire of consciousness consuming the last vestige of contracted embodiment.

4. **The purpose: no purpose:** The funerary rite does not "accomplish" anything for the deceased — he is already liberated. The rite is for the living — it confirms their understanding and completes their relationship with the departed.

## The Antyeṣṭi as Celebration

The tone of this chapter is notably un-mournful. The death of a Trika initiate is a homecoming, not a loss. The rites are described with the same detail as the initiation rites because they are, in essence, the same thing — a transition from one state of Śiva to another.
