# Topic 92: The Light of Consciousness Within Which Everything is Reflected

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 92 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: TĀ 3/1-4

---

## 1. Core Teaching

The Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) begins with the Doctrine of Reflection (Pratibimbavāda). All that is objectively perceived — outer objects, sensory perceptions, inner cogitations — shines within consciousness as reflections in a mirror. Unlike reflections in a physical mirror that require an external prototype (bimba), the reflections within consciousness are produced by the inherent power of the mirror of Bhairava's consciousness itself.

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 3/1** (lines 5314-5321): *prakāśamātraṁ yat proktaṁ bhairavīyaṁ paraṁ mahaḥ | tatra svatantratāmātraṁ adhikaṁ pravivicyate*
The pure Light expounded in the previous chapter is Bhairava's supreme glory. Anything more one may discern there is only His freedom.

**TĀ 3/2** (lines 5387-5394): *yaḥ prakāśaḥ sa sarvasya prakāśatvaṁ prayacchati*
The Light is that which illumines all things and makes them manifest.

**TĀ 3/3** (lines 5465-5474): *ato 'sau parameśānaḥ svātmavyomany anargalaḥ | iyataḥ sṛṣṭisaṁhārāḍambarasya pradarśakaḥ*
Thus the Supreme Lord, free of restraints, exhibits in the Sky of His own nature the great clutter of emission and withdrawal.

**TĀ 3/4** (lines 5510-5522): *nirmale makure yadvad bhānti bhūmijalādayaḥ | amiśrās tadvad ekasmiṁś cinnāthe viśvavṛttayaḥ*
Just as earth, water and the rest manifest reflected in a stainless mirror without mixing, so do all modalities of things within the one Lord of Consciousness.

## 3. Jayaratha's Commentary

**On the meaning of "prakāśamātra"** (lines 5370-5384): "Anuttara is the pure Light of consciousness alone, because that is its predominant nature. The Light is not devoid of reflective awareness. The freedom of the Light is the agency of the act of manifestation, making all things manifest by its own free will."

## 4. Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the Doctrine of Reflection** (lines 5178-5219): "Abhinava begins his presentation of the Supreme Means with an exposition of the Doctrine of Reflection. This maintains that all that is objectively perceived shines within consciousness at one with it as do reflections with the mirror in which they are reflected. But while reflections in a mirror are those of an original object outside the mirror, these reflections are produced by the inherent power of the mirror of Bhairava's consciousness."

**On Swami Lakshmanjoo's explanation** (lines 5340-5351): "'The Doctrine of Reflection is meant for advanced yogins. The theory teaches them how to be aware in their daily activities, while talking, while walking, while tasting, while touching, while hearing, while smelling. While they are doing all these various actions, they see that all of these actions move in their Supreme Consciousness.'

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
