# Full Oblations and Conjunction with the Supreme

**Tantrāloka 17.78cd–122 | Lines 3501–4000**

## Overview

The three full oblations (pūrṇāhuti) are the climax of the putraka initiation. Each oblation is a complete offering of everything — the universe, the ego, all karma — into the fire of consciousness. After the third, the disciple is conjoined to the supreme principle.

## Key Verses

- **78cd–82 (line 486):** The first full oblation — conjoining to the principle of Śiva.
- **83–85ab (line 488):** The second full oblation.
- **85cd–91 (line 490):** The third full oblation — complete stabilization.
- **92–97ab (line 492):** Initiation for worldly benefits (bhogadīkṣā) by means of a full oblation.
- **97cd–99ab (line 493):** Initiation into the disembodied form of Śiva — beyond all principles.
- **99cd–102 (lines 494–495):** Emission of the pure principles by full oblation.
- **103–111 (lines 495–496):** The pure states of the principles — what each tattva IS when freed from contraction.
- **112–115ab (lines 496–497):** The senses bound and liberated.
- **115cd–117 (line 497):** Purification of principles with Trika mantras.
- **118–122 (lines 497–498):** The various paths and their required oblations.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Three oblations = three bodies:** The three full oblations correspond to the purification of the gross (sthūla), subtle (sūkṣma), and causal (kāraṇa) bodies. When all three are offered, nothing is left to bind.

2. **Bhogadīkṣā vs. mokṣadīkṣā:** Even those who seek worldly enjoyment can receive a full oblation — but it is directed to a specific Lord of a world (bhuvaneśvara) rather than to Śiva without parts. The same rite, different intention, different result.

3. **Disembodied form of Śiva:** The initiation into the formless Śiva (niṣkala) is for those who desire liberation without remainder. They are conjoined to the principle beyond all principles — the 37th (or 38th) tattva.

4. **Pure states of the principles:** A principle "purified" is not destroyed but seen from the divine perspective. Earth remains earth, but it is experienced as the goddess Dharā, not as dead matter. The senses remain senses, but they operate as powers of consciousness, not as instruments of bondage.

## The Oblation as Non-Return

The key concept is "conjunction" (yojanā). The disciple is "yoked" (the root YUJ — same as yoga) to ever-higher planes until he is yoked to Śiva. At that point, there is no possibility of falling back — because there is nowhere else to go.
