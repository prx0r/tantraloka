# THE SUPREME GOAL IS ŚIVA — VERSES 293cd–294ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 2, Chapter 3

---

### Core Teaching

The final verses of Chapter 3 serve as both its conclusion and its summation. Abhinavagupta declares that all forms of worship, even those belonging to lower means yet to be explained in subsequent chapters, ultimately find their fulfillment in the realization that "the supreme goal is Śiva." There is no plurality of goals corresponding to the plurality of means — every spiritual path, at whatever level it operates, converges on the same end. The chapter closes with the colophon verse announcing that the supreme insight into one's own nature (ātmavedana) pertaining to the Divine Means has been explained at length. The identity of the aspirant with the goal is the final word: the seeker is the sought, the means is the end.

**Line references:** TĀ 3/293cd–294; Dyczkowski translation pp. 410–411, lines 29615–29679.

---

### All Means Culminate in Śiva (Verses 293cd–294ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
bhāvinyo 'pi hy upāsās tā atraivāyānti niṣṭhitim |
etanmayatvaṁ paramaṁ prāpyaṁ nirvarṇyate śivam ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "(All forms of) worship, even those in the future, (ultimately develop and) become well established (and fulfilled) here (in the profound) realization that: 'the supreme goal, which is such, is Śiva.'"

Jayaratha explains: "The future forms of worship" refers to the teachings of the Individual (āṇava) and Empowered (śākta) Means that Abhinavagupta will expound in subsequent chapters. These lower means do not lead to a different goal — they give access to one another, progressively refining the practitioner's understanding until they culminate in the Supreme Means. As Swami Lakshmanjoo puts it: "All means lead one to the other, finally culminating in Śāmbhavopāya."

The word "here" (atra) means in this realization — the recognition that the supreme goal is "of this nature" (etanmaya), that is, identical with Śiva. Nothing is possible without attaining this state of rest. Thus, although there are many means, the goals are not many. All paths, whether they know it or not, are moving toward the same recognition.

Abhinavagupta cites his own earlier statement (1/227ab): "One should not imagine that there is any difference here in the fruit which is consciousness (attained by applying oneself to the various means of realisation)."

---

### The Concluding Verse (Verse 294cd)

**Sanskrit:**
```
iti kathitam idaṁ suviṣṭaraṁ paramaṁ śāmbhavam ātmavedanam ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "So, we have explained at length this, the supreme insight into one's own nature pertaining to the Divine (śāmbhava) Means."

This single half-verse closes the chapter. The phrase "supreme insight into one's own nature" (paramaṁ śāmbhavam ātmavedanam) encapsulates the entire teaching: the Divine Means is not a ritual, a meditation technique, or a philosophical system — it is the direct knowledge (vedana) of oneself (ātman) as Śiva. This knowledge has been explained "at length" (suviṣṭaram), meaning that everything from verse 1 of Chapter 3 to this concluding verse has been leading to this single point.

The chapter colophon follows: "The third chapter called 'the Revelation of the Divine Means' is complete."

---

### Jayaratha's Closing Remarks

Jayaratha's commentary on the chapter ends with a personal note of remarkable beauty. He describes himself as:

"A certain Jayaratha who, pouring out with the enthusiasm of the penetration of Śiva consciousness, is the veritable oneness of the savour of the unique juice (rasa) of aesthetic rapture."

This self-description is significant. Jayaratha presents his commentary not as a dry scholarly exercise but as a spontaneous outpouring (ullāsa) of the same realization that the chapter describes. His words are themselves a manifestation of the Śāmbhava state — the unique rasa of nondual consciousness finding expression in language.

---

### The Unity of Means and Goal

The concluding teaching of Chapter 3 resolves a tension present throughout the chapter. The Supreme Means appears to be a means (upāya) to attain Śiva, but if Śiva is already one's own nature, what is there to attain? The answer is that the Śāmbhava "means" is not a means in the ordinary sense — it is the recognition that there is no distance between the seeker and the sought. As Abhinavagupta stated in verses 2/35–37 (Topic 87), for one firmly established in the supreme reality (anuttara), there is no means, no goal, no liberation, no bondage.

Yet the teaching of means is not false — it is a compassionate concession to those who experience themselves as separate. The Supreme Means is the threshold where the means consumes itself, leaving only the natural state. The verse "the supreme goal is Śiva" marks this threshold: the goal is not something to be obtained but the nature of the one who seeks.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Unity of All Means**: The plurality of spiritual paths does not imply a plurality of goals. All means — whether Individual, Empowered, or Supreme — converge on the same realization: Śiva. The difference is only in the speed and directness of the approach.

2. **The Means that Consumes Itself**: The Supreme Means is unique in that it is a means that, when fully entered, reveals itself to be the goal. It is the ladder that is kicked away upon arrival — or rather, the recognition that there never was a ladder.

3. **Ātmavedana**: The chapter's final word is ātmavedana — knowledge of the Self. This is not conceptual knowledge (jñāna in the sense of information) but direct, experiential recognition. It is the same as pratyabhijñā — the recognition of one's identity with Śiva.

4. **The End of Chapter 3 as a Beginning**: Chapter 3 is the heart of the Tantrāloka's teaching on means. The remaining seven chapters elaborate the lower means and the details of practice, but the summit has already been reached. Everything that follows is, in a sense, commentary on what has already been revealed.
