# 358 — The Seven Perceivers as Powers of Consciousness
**Tantrāloka 10.6–18** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
The seven perceivers are not just cognitive types — they are the powers (*śaktis*) of consciousness itself, arranged in a hierarchy of increasing expansion.

## Core Teaching
Each perceiver is defined by two factors: (1) the level of reality they perceive, and (2) the degree of self-awareness they possess. The hierarchy is a gradient of contraction and expansion.

## The Trika Goddesses (10.7cd–9ab)
The three highest perceivers — Mantra, Mantreśvara, Mantramaheśvara — correspond to the three goddesses of the Trika:
- **Parā** (supreme) — corresponds to Mantramaheśvara
- **Parāparā** (supreme-non-supreme) — corresponds to Mantreśvara
- **Aparā** (non-supreme) — corresponds to Mantra

These three goddesses are also the three poles of perception:
- **Subject** (*pramātṛ*) — Mantramaheśvara
- **Means** (*pramāṇa*) — Mantreśvara
- **Object** (*prameya*) — Mantra

## The Fourteen Divisions Pertaining to Earth (10.9cd–12ab)
Using Earth as the example: the Earth principle has a fourteen-fold division (7 perceivers × 2 = perceiver + power), plus its own nature (*svarūpa*) as the fifteenth. This structure applies to every tattva.

## The Ascent and Elimination of Ignorance (10.12cd–18)
The progressive ascent:
1. **Sakala** — identifies with the body; experiences the world as external
2. **Pralayākala** — turns inward; experiences the self as separate from the body
3. **Vijñānākala** — recognizes the self as consciousness
4. **Mantra** — knows the mantric nature of reality
5. **Mantreśvara** — lords over the mantric universe
6. **Mantramaheśvara** — abides in pure I-consciousness
7. **Śiva** — is consciousness itself

## Elimination of Ignorance
At each stage, the ignorance (*avidyā*) of the previous stage is eliminated:
- Sakala doesn't know the self → Pralayākala knows the self but not as consciousness
- Pralayākala doesn't know consciousness → Vijñānākala knows it but not as non-dual
- Vijñānākala doesn't know unity → Mantra knows unity but not as one's own self
- etc.

## The Seven Śaktis
Corresponding to the seven perceivers are seven powers:
1. **Icchā** (will) — Sakala
2. **Jñāna** (knowledge) — Pralayākala
3. **Kriyā** (action) — Vijñānākala
4. **Mantratā** (mantra-hood) — Mantra
5. **Mantreśatā** (mantra-lordship) — Mantreśvara
6. **Mantramaheśatā** (great mantra-lordship) — Mantramaheśvara
7. **Svātantrya** (absolute freedom) — Śiva

## Connection to Perceptibility (10.19–78ab)
Having established the perceivers, Abhinava now argues that perceptibility (*vedyatva*) is not a quality of objects but a function of the perceiver's level. The same object is "perceptible" differently to each perceiver.
