# 365 — The Waking, Dream, Deep Sleep, and Fourth States
**Tantrāloka 10.227cd–309** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
Waking, dreaming, sleeping — and beyond. The states of consciousness are not three but five, and each corresponds to a level of the seven perceivers.

## Core Teaching
The five states (*avasthā*) — waking (*jāgrat*), dream (*svapna*), deep sleep (*suṣupti*), the fourth (*turya*), and beyond the fourth (*turīyātīta*) — are analyzed from a Kaula perspective. Each state is a mode of the perceiver, not a property of the object.

## The Waking State (10.231–246)
- **Sakala's waking**: perception of external objects through the senses
- **Pralayākala's waking**: awareness of the subtle body
- **Vijñānākala's waking**: awareness of consciousness itself
- **Mantra's waking**: mantric awareness pervading all experience
- **Mantreśvara's waking**: lordship over perception
- **Mantramaheśvara's waking**: pure I-consciousness in activity
- **Śiva's waking**: the entire world as His own light

## The Dream State (10.247–248ab)
Dream is not unreal — it is a different mode of perception:
- Internal objects appear as if external
- The perceiver is partially withdrawn from the body
- Higher perceivers have "dreams" that are visions of subtle realities

## Waking in the Dream State (10.248cd–250ab)
Lucid dreaming: within the dream, one can become aware that one is dreaming. This corresponds to the Vijñānākala level within the dream state.

## Dreaming Within Dreaming (10.250cd–257ab)
Infinite regress of dreams: dreams within dreams, each with its own perceiver. This illustrates the nested structure of consciousness — each level contains the potential for lower levels.

## Deep Sleep (10.257cd–264ab)
Deep sleep is NOT unconsciousness:
- It is the Pralayākala state: all objectivity dissolved
- Consciousness remains, but without content
- The "seed" of waking experience remains dormant

## The Fourth State — Turya (10.264cd–277)
Beyond the three ordinary states:
- Awareness that persists through all three states
- The witness (*sākṣin*) that is never absent
- Corresponds to Vijñānākala and above
- In turya, the three states are seen as modifications of the one consciousness

## Beyond the Fourth — Turīyātīta (10.278–287)
The state beyond even the witness:
- No distinction between witness and witnessed
- Pure non-dual consciousness
- Corresponds to Śiva Himself
- This is the goal of the entire practice

## The States of the Seven Perceivers (10.288–294ab)
Each perceiver has all five states:
- Sakala's deep sleep = death-like unconsciousness
- Pralayākala's deep sleep = cosmic dissolution
- Śiva's deep sleep = the universe held in potential
- etc.

## The Five States and the Fifteen Divisions (10.301cd–309)
Mapping the five states onto the fifteen-fold structure:
- Waking corresponds to the fully deployed fifteen divisions
- Dream corresponds to partial withdrawal
- Deep sleep corresponds to dissolution into svarūpa
- Fourth corresponds to recognition of unity
- Beyond fourth corresponds to the non-division of Śiva

## The Seven Perceivers from Another Point of View (10.294cd–301ab)
Alternative mapping: each state of each perceiver can be analyzed in terms of:
- The perceiver itself
- The means of perception
- The object perceived
- The nature of the experience
- The degree of self-awareness

## Conclusion of Chapter 10
The final verses (up to 10.310) summarize: "Thus we have explained the Path of the Principles at length." The seven perceivers, their fifteen-fold division, their states of consciousness, their projection into the breath — all are facets of the one reality: consciousness freely manifesting as the universe.
