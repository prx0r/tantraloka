# 353 — The Nature of Māyā
**Tantrāloka 9.142–154ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Māyā is often translated as "illusion." But for Abhinavagupta, it is something far more precise and powerful: the very power by which the one becomes many.

## Core Teaching
Māyā is not unreal — it is the real power (*śakti*) of Śiva to contract His infinite nature. It is the principle of limitation that makes the universe of duality possible.

## Key Verses and Topics

**9.142–147abc**: Māyā is the power that creates the sense of separation. It is the "knot" (*granthi*) that binds consciousness to the body.

**9.147cd–151ab — Māyā as the Night**: Māyā is like night — it veils the true nature of things. But the same night that obscures also reveals (the stars, the moon). Māyā both hides Śiva and makes the world appear.

**9.151cd–154ab — Māyā as a Principle**: Māyā is a tattva — the twenty-sixth principle counting from Earth upward. It is the first principle of the impure path.

## Māyā in Trika vs. Vedānta

| Aspect | Vedānta | Trika |
|--------|---------|-------|
| Status | Unreal (*mithyā*) | Real power (*śakti*) |
| Relation to Brahman | Adventitious | Intrinsic |
| Function | To veil | To contract |
| Removal | Knowledge alone | Knowledge + Grace |

## The Five Functions of Māyā
1. **Vikṣepa** — projection of multiplicity
2. **Āvaraṇa** — veiling of unity
3. **Throwing** the soul into the cycle of birth and death
4. **Creating** the *kañcukas* (coverings)
5. **Sustaining** the dualistic universe

## Māyā and the Coverings
From Māyā emerge the five *kañcukas*:
- Kāla (Time)
- Niyati (Necessity)
- Rāga (Attachment)
- Vidyā (Limited Knowledge)
- Kalā (Limited Agency)

These are the five modalities of Māyā's limiting power.

## Beyond Māyā
Above Māyā lie the five pure principles (Śiva through Śuddhavidyā). These constitute the *śuddhādhvan* — the pure path where duality does not operate. Liberation is the soul's return *through* Māyā to this pure realm.

## Connection to the Kañcukas (9.154cd–220ab)
Having established Māyā's nature, Abhinava now examines each of the five coverings in detail, showing how Māyā's single limiting power manifests as five distinct modalities of bondage.
