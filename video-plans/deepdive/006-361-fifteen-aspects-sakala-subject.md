# 361 — The Fifteen Aspects of the Fully Conditioned Subject
**Tantrāloka 10.97cd–109** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
The Sakala — the fully conditioned perceiver — has fifteen aspects. Why fifteen? Because the perceiver, his power, their object, and the means of perception form a complex but enumerable structure.

## Core Teaching
The fifteen-fold division captures the complete structure of any perceptual act at the Sakala level: 7 perceiver-aspects, 7 power-aspects, and 1 "own nature" (*svarūpa*) that is the object as it is in itself.

## The Fifteen Divisions (10.103–104)
For the Sakala perceiver (Earth through Prakṛti):
- **7 perceivers** (*pramātṛ*): from the most contracted to the most expanded at that level
- **7 powers** (*śakti*): the corresponding energies of each perceiver
- **1 own nature** (*svarūpa*): the object as it is, untouched by subject-object division

Total = 15

## The Fifteen Listed
From the text (10.103–104):

1–7: The seven perceivers in their Sakala mode
8–14: The seven powers that enable perception
15: The svarūpa — the pure object-nature before it is colored by perception

## Practical Application in Meditation
When the Yogin meditates on Earth principle:
1. First, he perceives Earth as object (svarūpa)
2. He then becomes aware of himself perceiving (perceiver)
3. He becomes aware of his power of perception (śakti)
4. He realizes these three are one movement of consciousness
5. This dissolves the fifteen into thirteen (Pralayākala level)

## The Thirteen Aspects (10.105–107)
At the Pralayākala level:
- The perceiver and power of the lowest level merge
- Two divisions are "lost" — folded into the higher perspective
- 15 → 13

## The Eleven Aspects (10.108–109)
At the Pralayākala dissolution level:
- Further folding: 13 → 11
- The perceiver, power, and means of knowledge increasingly unify

## The Telescoping Effect
Each higher perceiver:
1. Subsumes the previous perceiver as an object
2. Folds the previous power into its own power
3. Experiences greater unity of subject and object

## The Goal
At the level of Śiva (10.110–113ab):
- 15 → 3 (Śiva, Śakti, and their unity)
- Finally → 0 (non-duality, beyond all division)

## Connection to Self-Luminous Śiva (10.113cd–117ab)
The final stage: Śiva is not an object of perception. He is self-luminous (*svaprakāśa*). The entire fifteen-fold structure is His own self-manifestation, and when perceived as such, it dissolves into His light.
