# The External Fire Sacrifice

**Tantrāloka 15.366–450ab | Lines 13701–14500**

## Overview

The external sacrifice (bahiryāga) is the visible complement to the inner visualization. It includes taking possession of the field (kṣetragraha), worship of Earth and Gaṇeśa, installation of the jars, and the elaborate fire sacrifice (agnikārya) with its purifications, libations, oblations, and the preparation of the sacrificial pap (caru).

## Key Verses

- **366–374ab:** Taking possession of the field and the five products of the cow (pañcagavya).
- **374cd–377:** Worship of Earth and Gaṇeśa.
- **378–390:** Worship of the weapon (astra) and the jars.
- **391–420:** The fire sacrifice — purification of the fire pit, the ten purifying rites (saṃskāra), the fire's rites of passage, the nine oblations.
- **421–438:** The libation to the fire (tarpaṇa).
- **427–431ab:** The full oblation (pūrṇāhuti).
- **434cd–438:** Inner ascent through the tattvas by inner conjunctions.
- **439–446:** Preparation and consumption of the sacrificial pap.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The fire as consciousness:** The fire pit (kuṇḍa) is the mouth of the goddess; the fire is the energy of consciousness that consumes all offerings. Oblations are not items burned but dualities dissolved.

2. **Ten purifying rites = saṃskāras:** The fire itself undergoes the ten life-cycle rites (saṃskāra) from impregnation to marriage — because the fire is a person, a form of Agni, who must be purified to serve as the mouth of the gods.

3. **The sacrificer must not be stingy:** Vāmā's power binds the miser. The spirit of sacrifice is generosity — the willingness to offer everything. To hold back is to contract.

4. **Inner ascent through oblations:** Each oblation (ājya, caru, etc.) corresponds to a tattva. The sacrificer ascends through the 36 principles as he offers. The full oblation (pūrṇāhuti) at the end is the offering of the entire universe — and of the ego itself.

## The External as Support

The external sacrifice is never an end in itself. It is a "support" (ādhāra) for the inner contemplation. For one established in non-dual awareness, the external rite is a celebration; for one still ascending, it is a ladder.
