# INJUNCTIONS DO NOT APPLY TO THE SUPREME DEITY — Verses 125-133
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

The Supreme Lord, who is one's own Self, cannot be the object of a Vedic or scriptural injunction. An injunction impels one to action to attain something not yet attained. But the Self is always already attained — it is ādisiddha, "proved from the very beginning." One cannot be commanded to attain what one already is. Even Vedic injunctions to worship deities are ultimately aimed at the Self, though they use intermediate forms. The Self is the implicit recipient of all worship, the thread that strings the garland of all deities together.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.125-126 — The Self is Not Attained by Injunction

**Line ~22867** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit (approximate):**
```
svātmā na vidhibhājako vidher aviṣayatvāt |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (from commentary, line ~23010+):**
"The nature of one's own Self, which is consciousness, (never) becomes the field of operation (viṣaya) of injunction, because it cannot be attained (by following) injunctions. As long as (something) is not attained, it is a field of operation of injunction, and one's own Self is never unattained, because it is (always and) everywhere manifest as accomplished (and proved to exist) from the very beginning (ādisiddha)."

#### Verse 1.127 — The Three Aspects of Injunction

**Line ~23028** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
vidhir niyogas tryaṁśā ca bhāvanā codanātmikā |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"An injunction (vidhi) is (essentially), a command (niyoga) that impels (to action) (codanā) (as laid down according to the Mīmāṁsā in its standard) execution (bhāvanā) in three aspects."

**Explanation:**
Abhinava analyzes the Mīmāṁsā theory of injunction to show that it cannot apply to the Self. An injunction has three aspects: the command, the execution, and the impulsion. All three presuppose something to be attained that is not yet present. The Self is always present.

#### Verses 1.128-133 — The Self as the True Object of All Worship

**Line ~23250+** (Vol. 1)

**Dyczkowski Translation (from commentary):**
"The Self is the ultimate object of sacrifice, even though it is not named directly as a recipient. The various deities, different and separate from the Self, are necessarily the object of injunction. But present in all the deities that are mentioned, implicit within them and constantly mentioned, is our own Self, which by its very nature manifests all things and shines permanently as the continuous idea and sentiment of 'I'."

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On ādisiddha (line ~23015+):** Jayaratha explains that the Self is ādisiddha — proved from the very beginning. It is not an object to be attained but the ever-present subject.

**On the Bhagavadgītā (line ~22789+):** Jayaratha quotes extensively from the Gītā to show that even those who worship other deities are worshipping the Self, though they do so "without prior injunction" — without the conscious recognition that the deity is their own nature.

### Philosophical Context

This section is Abhinava's critique of Vedic ritualism (Mīmāṁsā). The Mīmāṁsā holds that liberation is achieved through the performance of Vedic injunctions. Abhinava argues that the Self, being ever-present, cannot be the object of an injunction. This establishes that liberation is not the result of action but of recognition (pratyabhijñā). Ritual can be a means — but only if understood as a form of knowledge, not as a mechanical cause of liberation.

### Visual Concepts

1. **The Already Attained:** A figure searching everywhere for something they already hold in their hand — representing the Self as ādisiddha.

2. **The Thread of Self:** A garland of deities with a thread labeled "Self" running through all of them.

3. **Injunction vs. Recognition:** Two panels — one showing a command ("Do this!"), the other showing a recognition ("You already are this").

### Narration Script

Injunctions command us to attain what we do not yet have. But one's own Self is not an object to be attained — it is the ever-present subject, proved from the very beginning. You cannot be commanded to attain what you already are. The Vedic injunctions to worship various deities are effective not because they create the Self but because, even in dualistic worship, the Self is the implicit recipient. It is the thread that holds the garland of all deities together. Liberation comes not from performing actions but from recognizing the Self that is already present.
