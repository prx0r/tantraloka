# Sensations Spanning the Tattvas: The Sensory Ascent
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

Among the five sensations (smell, taste, sight, touch, sound), each corresponds to a specific element and a specific Force. But crucially, these sensations do not all operate within the same sphere. Smell, taste, and sight are limited to the impure path — they operate up to the level of Māyā. Touch, however, extends beyond Māyā into the pure path. Sound, the subtlest sensation, reaches up to the supreme Light of consciousness. This graded extension of sensation provides a map for spiritual ascent: by refining one's sensory awareness, one ascends through the Forces.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verses 11.29-32 — The Sensory Hierarchy

**Lines approximately 1600–1670:**

The text presents a hierarchy of sensory extension:
- **Gandha (smell)** — associated with Earth and Nivṛtti; limited to the Earth principle
- **Rasa (taste)** — associated with Water and Pratiṣṭhā; extends through the water principle
- **Rūpa (sight/form)** — associated with Fire and Vidyā; extends through the fire principle up to Māyā
- **Sparśa (touch)** — associated with Air and Śāntā; breaks through the boundary of Māyā into the pure path
- **Śabda (sound)** — associated with Ether and Śāntyatītā; extends to the supreme Light

The underlying principle: as the element becomes more subtle (Earth → Water → Fire → Air → Ether), its corresponding sensation extends further. Sound and Ether are the most subtle, bordering on consciousness itself.

---

#### The Threshold of Māyā

The critical transition is between sight (rūpa) and touch (sparśa). Sight is confined within the sphere of Māyā — it operates within duality, where subject and object are separate. Touch breaks through this boundary: touch requires proximity, which implies a partial overcoming of the subject-object gap. Sound goes even further: it is vibration, which is the very nature of consciousness (spanda).

---

#### Practical Correlates

This sensory hierarchy has practical implications for meditation:
- **Gross sensory objects** (smell, taste, sight) are supports for lower forms of meditation
- **Touch** can be used in more subtle meditations, particularly those involving the vital breath (prāṇa), which is tactile
- **Sound** is the support for the highest meditations — mantra meditation, listening to unstruck sound (anāhata), and absorption in the supreme vibration
