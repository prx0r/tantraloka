# THE ŚĀMBHAVA MASTER — VERSES 291–293ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 2, Chapter 3

---

### Core Teaching

If the Supreme Means transcends all ritual practice, how can a teacher who has realized this state guide others? Abhinavagupta addresses this question by describing the Śāmbhava master — one who is established in the Supreme Means (or, at its deepest level, in No Means). Such a teacher graces disciples spontaneously through mere presence — a look or a word is sufficient for those who are fit recipients of this highest grace. For disciples who are not yet ready for the Supreme Means, the same teacher skillfully employs the diverse means of initiation (dīkṣā) appropriate to their level. The Śāmbhava master thus embodies perfect freedom: established beyond all conventions, yet able to operate within them for the sake of others.

**Line references:** TĀ 3/291–293ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 408–410, lines 29469–29533.

---

### The Teacher Established Herein (Verses 291cd–292ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
parānugrahakāritvam atrasthasya sphuṭaṁ sthitam |
yadi tādṛg anugrāhyo daiśikasyopasarpati ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "The teacher who is established herein evidently graces anyone who approaches him, if he is fit to be graced in this way."

Jayaratha explains: "Herein" (atra) means in the Supreme Means — or, more precisely, in No Means, which is the culmination of the Śāmbhava state. Such a teacher does not need to perform rituals or give elaborate teachings. Merely looking at the disciple or speaking with him is enough to transmit realization.

The phrase "if he is fit to be graced in this way" (tādṛg anugrāhya) refers to one who possesses the capacity to practice the Supreme Means — that is, one who has received the most intense descent of grace (tīvra śaktipāta). Such a disciple achieves the goal within himself spontaneously upon contact with the master, "like the flame of a lamp that has arisen from another flame."

### The Skilful Teacher's Adaptability (Verses 292cd–293ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
athāsau tādṛśo na syād bhavabhaktyā ca bhāvitaḥ |
taṁ cārādhayate bhāvitādṛśānugraheritaḥ ||
tadā vicitraṁ dīkṣādividhiṁ śikṣeta kovidaḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Now if (the disciple) is not such, (but even then,) purified by devotion to Rudra (Bhava) and impelled by (the thought that) there will be such grace in the future, worships that (teacher), then the skilful (learned teacher) should impart initiation and (whatever other) wonderfully diverse (practice the disciple is fit to receive)."

Jayaratha elaborates: If the disciple does not possess the capacity for the Supreme Means because the most intense descent of grace has not taken place in him, but he is nevertheless "purified by devotion to Rudra" (bhavabhaktyā bhāvitaḥ) — this devotion being the first sign of a descent of grace, according to the Mālinīvijayottaratantra (2/14ab) — then the teacher must adapt.

The term "skilful" (kovida) is significant: the Śāmbhava master, though himself established in the nondual state, possesses the discernment to recognize the level of the disciple and employ the appropriate means. He may use the wonderfully diverse (vicitra) forms of initiation, mantra, and ritual practice that belong to the Individual (āṇava) or Empowered (śākta) Means, gradually leading the disciple toward the Supreme.

Jayaratha cites the principle: "The teacher should not disregard his duty to rescue his disciple from the world of transmigratory existence when the time comes to perform the rite through which the disciple is initiated." Even a teacher established in the highest state must compassionately descend to lower means when necessary.

### The Freedom of the Śāmbhava Master

Although Abhinavagupta does not describe the Śāmbhava master's external behavior in detail here, the implications are clear from the broader context of Chapter 3 and the earlier discussion of the liberated soul in Topic 88 (verses 2/33–36):

1. **Spontaneity**: The Śāmbhava master acts spontaneously (svacchanda), without deliberation or calculation. His grace flows naturally, like light from the sun.

2. **Conventionlessness**: He is free from all conventions — social, religious, or ritual. Yet he may observe them when doing so serves the disciple's welfare.

3. **Nondual Vision**: He sees no distinction between himself and others, between sacred and profane, between means and goal. This nondual vision is itself the grace that transforms those who come into his presence.

4. **Compassionate Adaptability**: Though established in the highest, he does not abandon those who cannot receive the highest. He meets them where they are, using the means appropriate to their capacity.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Grace Through Presence**: The Śāmbhava master demonstrates that at the highest level, teaching is not a matter of instruction but of transmission. The master's mere presence, saturated with the recognition of nondual consciousness, awakens the same recognition in the fit disciple.

2. **The Hierarchy of Grace**: The distinction between the disciple who is fit for the Supreme Means and the one who is not reflects the Trika's teaching on the levels of grace (śaktipāta). The most intense grace (tīvra) leads directly to the Supreme Means; milder grades require progressive practice.

3. **The Teacher as Universal Resource**: The Śāmbhava master is not limited to one method. His freedom includes the freedom to employ limitation — to use dualistic practices for those who cannot yet receive the nondual teaching. This is the paradox of the liberated teacher: completely free, yet completely available.

4. **Devotion as the First Sign**: Jayaratha's citation of the Mālinīvijaya reminds us that devotion to Śiva (bhavabhakti) is itself a sign of grace. Even the disciple who cannot directly enter the Supreme Means is already being drawn by the power of Śiva.
