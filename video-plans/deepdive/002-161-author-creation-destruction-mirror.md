# "I AM THE AUTHOR OF CREATION AND DESTRUCTION IN THE MIRROR OF MY CONSCIOUSNESS" — VERSES 280cd–288ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 2, Chapter 3

---

### Core Teaching

This passage is the culminating declaration of the Supreme Means (śāmbhavopāya) practitioner. Abhinavagupta presents the threefold realization that defines the Śāmbhava state: all phenomena are born from me, reflected in me, and not separate from me. The yogi directly experiences himself as the author of creation (sṛṣṭi), persistence (sthiti), and destruction (saṁhāra) — not as a cosmic agent separate from the universe, but as the consciousness in which the entire cosmic drama arises and dissolves like reflections in a mirror. The mirror metaphor reaches its completion here: consciousness is not merely the medium of reflection but the active power that projects, sustains, and withdraws all phenomena within itself.

**Line references:** TĀ 3/280cd–288ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 401–407, lines 28975–29371.

---

### The Threefold Divine Means (Verses 280cd–281ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
matta evoditam idaṁ mayy eva pratibimbitam |
mad abhinnam idaṁ ceti tridhopāyaḥ sa śāmbhavaḥ ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "All this is born from me", "it is reflected in me" and "is not separate from me" — three-fold is this Divine (Śāmbhava) Means.

Abhinavagupta distills the entire teaching of the Supreme Means into this single threefold realization. The pronoun "I" (mat) denotes pure awakened consciousness (bodha), not the empirical ego. Everything arises *from* consciousness, appears *in* consciousness as a reflection, and is ultimately *not different from* consciousness. These three aspects correspond to creation, persistence, and withdrawal — but here they are experienced simultaneously as the unified play of one's own awareness.

### The Link with Spanda (Verses 281cd–282ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
sṛṣṭeḥ sthiteḥ saṁhṛteś ca tad etat sūtraṇaṁ kṛtam |
yatra sthitaṁ yataś ceti tad āha spandaśāsane ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "That (reflective awareness thus) forges this link (which contains implicitly within itself and connects together) creation, persistence and withdrawal. As is said in the Spanda teachings: 'that in which all this creation is established and from whence it arises . . .'"

Abhinavagupta invokes the authority of the Spanda teachings (Spandakārikā 1/2) to show that this realization is not his invention but the core of the entire Śaiva nondual tradition. The Spanda (divine vibration) is the pulse of consciousness that simultaneously generates, sustains, and reabsorbs the universe.

### Lordship is Consciousness Itself (Verses 282cd–283ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
etāvataiva hy aiśvaryaṁ saṁvidaḥ khyāpitaṁ param |
viśvātmakatvaṁ cety anyal lakṣaṇaṁ kiṁ nu kathyatām ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "This (creativity) itself declares the supreme lordship of consciousness, and is its cosmic nature. Pray tell what else could be its characterising feature?"

Jayaratha explains: The capacity of consciousness to bring about creation, persistence, and withdrawal is not a secondary quality — it is its very essence. Those who conceive of the Absolute as inactive or indeterminate (like the Vedāntins' Brahman) miss the dynamic, creative nature of supreme consciousness. The lordship (aiśvarya) of consciousness is precisely its power to manifest as all things while remaining one with them.

### The Three Insights of the Liberated Yogi (Verses 283cd–286ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
svātmany eva cidākāśe viśvam asmy avabhāsayan |
sraṣṭā viśvātmaka iti prathayā bhairavātmatā ||
ṣaḍadhvajātaṁ nikhilaṁ mayy eva pratibimbitam |
sthitikartāham asmīti sphuṭeyaṁ viśvarūpatā ||
sadoditamahābodhajvālājaṭilatātmani |
viśvaṁ dravati mayy etad iti paśyan praśāmyati ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "I, manifesting the universe in my own Self, the Sky of Consciousness, am the creator who is all things" — by this insight one becomes Bhairava. "The entire cosmic order is reflected within me alone, I am the agent of persistence!" — (with this insight) this (one's own) cosmic nature becomes evidently apparent. "The universe melts away into me, whose nature is the scorching fire of the great, perpetually active consciousness" — seeing thus, one becomes peaceful.

Jayaratha explains these three insights as corresponding to the three phases of cosmic process:

1. **Creation insight** ("I am the creator who is all things"): The yogi sees the universe arising from his own consciousness as its source. This insight is not a conceptual affirmation but a direct realization that transforms one's identity into Bhairava himself.

2. **Persistence insight** ("I am the agent of persistence"): The yogi recognizes that the entire cosmic order — the six paths (ṣaḍadhva) of reality — is reflected within him and sustained by his awareness. His cosmic nature (viśvarūpatā) becomes self-evident.

3. **Dissolution insight** ("The universe melts away into me"): The yogi experiences all phenomena dissolving into the fire of his own perpetually active consciousness (sadodita mahābodha). This dissolution is not annihilation but the recognition of the universe's true nature as consciousness, which brings profound peace (praśāmyati).

### The Fire that Burns the Dream of Saṁsāra (Verses 286cd–287ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
anantacitrasadgarbhasaṁsārasvapnasadmanaḥ |
ploṣakaḥ śiva evāham ity ullāsī hutāśanaḥ ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "The Fire (of consciousness) that pours forth (enflamed with the awareness that): 'I am indeed Śiva, who burns the mansion of the dreams of transmigratory existence with its endless and wonderfully diverse (worlds, which are its) wombs of existence.'"

Jayaratha comments: Transmigratory existence (saṁsāra) is like a mansion of dreams — vivid while it lasts but without substance. The fire of Śiva-consciousness burns this mansion completely, leaving no trace. Even the latent traces (vāsanās) of past experiences are consumed in this realization. The phrase "I am indeed Śiva" (śiva evāham) is the mantra-like affirmation that fuels this inner fire.

### The Unified Vision and the Fourth State (Verses 287cd–288ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
jagat sarvaṁ mattaḥ prabhavati vibhedena bahudhā
tathāpy etad rūḍhaṁ mayi vigalite tv atra na paraḥ |
tad itthaṁ yaḥ sṛṣṭisthitivilayam ekīkṛtivaśād
anaṁśaṁ paśyet sa sphurati hi turīyaṁ padam abhitaḥ ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "The entire universe with (all its) diverse (aspects) emerges out of me (in many ways, and is also established here in the same way within me (in my pure consciousness), from which (all duality) has fallen away and nothing else (remains)." He who sees (the cycle of creation and destruction) in this way without parts because creation, persistence and withdrawal has been unified (and so) has penetrated the Fourth state (of pure, divine consciousness), shines radiantly (as supreme consciousness).

Jayaratha explains: The yogi who sees creation, persistence, and withdrawal as the unified activity of his own consciousness — without division into separate phases — has penetrated the Fourth state (turīya). This is not a state among other states but the transcendental ground of waking, dreaming, and deep sleep. Such a yogi "shines radiantly" (sphurati) as supreme consciousness itself, the Inexplicable (anākhya).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Threefold Realization**: The Śāmbhava Means is not a practice to be done but a recognition to be stabilized. Its three aspects — emergence from me, reflection in me, non-separateness from me — correspond to creation, persistence, and withdrawal experienced as one's own conscious activity.

2. **The Mirror Completed**: The mirror metaphor, developed throughout Chapter 3, reaches its fulfillment. Consciousness is not a passive mirror but the active source, sustainer, and reabsorber of all reflections. The yogi does not merely see reflections — he knows himself as the mirror that creates what it reflects.

3. **Lordship is Creativity**: Against absolutist views that conceive the Absolute as inactive, Abhinavagupta insists that creativity (the capacity to manifest, sustain, and withdraw the universe) is the defining characteristic of supreme consciousness. Without this dynamic power, consciousness would be inert and lifeless.

4. **The Fourth State**: The turīya is the culmination of the Śāmbhava practice. It is not a mystical trance but the natural state of one who has unified the three phases of cosmic process within the recognition "I am Śiva."
