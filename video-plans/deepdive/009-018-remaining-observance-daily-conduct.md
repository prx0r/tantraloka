# The Remaining Observance: Daily Conduct

**Tantrāloka 26.1–29ab | Lines 726–738**

## Overview

The "remaining observance" (śeṣavṛtti) is the daily practice that the initiate must follow after initiation for the rest of his life. The term "remaining" (śeṣa) refers to what remains of one's karmic residue after initiation — the prārabdha karma that must still be lived through. The daily practice ensures that this residue is lived consciously and exhausted without creating new bonds.

## Key Verses

- **2 (lines 728–729):** The purpose of the remaining observance — to exhaust residual karma.
- **3–20 (lines 730–731):** The teacher instructs the disciple in daily conduct, including ritual and mantra.
- **21–25ab (lines 734–735):** Mantras are powerless if drawn from a book — they must be received from a teacher.
- **25cd–29ab (lines 736–737):** The teacher imparts Trika mantras in secret — the transmission is oral, from heart to heart.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Śeṣa — the remainder:** After initiation, the "three bonds" (past, present, future karma) are destroyed — except for the prārabdha karma that has already begun to bear fruit in this life. This residual karma is the śeṣa. It must be "lived through" (bhuj) but not "added to" (sañcita).

2. **Mantra must be received:** A mantra read from a book is "dead" — it has no power (mantras te 'pi nirvīryāḥ). A mantra received from a living teacher in the chain of transmission is "alive" because it carries the śakti of the lineage.

3. **Oral transmission:** The Trika mantras are imparted in secret because their power is not in the text but in the transmission. The teacher speaks the mantra into the disciple's ear with his own breath and awareness — this is the "mouth-to-mouth" (āsya-āsya) transmission.

4. **Daily conduct as continued purification:** The daily rites are not separate from the path — they ARE the path. Each day, the initiate re-purifies the paths (tattva, mantra, etc.) that were purified in initiation, because the residual karma tends to re-obscure them.

## The Teacher's Ongoing Role

The teacher continues to guide the disciple for the rest of his life. The śeṣavṛtti is not a set of rules but a living relationship. The disciple checks his experiences with the teacher, receives clarification of doubts, and deepens his understanding through practice.
