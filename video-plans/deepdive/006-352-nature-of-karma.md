# 352 — The Nature of Karma
**Tantrāloka 9.97cd–141** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
What is karma, actually? A bank account of merits and demerits, or something more intimate to consciousness?

## Core Teaching
Karma is not a substance or a thing but a specific configuration of consciousness — a trace (*vāsanā*) that conditions future experience. It exists only as a modality of awareness, not as an independent entity.

## Key Arguments

### What Karma Is Not
- Not a physical substance
- Not an independent force
- Not a record kept by a cosmic judge

### What Karma Is
- A *saṃskāra* (latent impression) in consciousness
- A pattern of self-limitation
- The trace left by past acts of identification

## The Three Types of Karma
1. **Sañcita** (accumulated) — all past karma not yet begun to bear fruit
2. **Prārabdha** (begun) — karma that has begun to manifest as current life
3. **Āgāmin** (future) — karma being created by current actions

## How Karma Operates
Karma operates through the *kañcukas*:
- **Kāla** — determines when karma bears fruit
- **Niyati** — determines the necessity of karmic consequence
- **Rāga** — determines the desire that drives karmic action
- **Vidyā** — determines the knowledge that shapes karmic choice
- **Kalā** — determines the agency that executes karmic action

## The Destruction of Karma
Karma cannot be destroyed by more karma (action). It can only be destroyed by:
1. **Knowledge** (*jñāna*) — recognizing the true nature of the self
2. **Grace** (*śaktipāta*) — the descent of Śiva's power
3. **Initiation** (*dīkṣā*) — ritual purification through mantra

## Karmic Recursion
The most subtle point: karma creates the sense of self, and the sense of self creates more karma. This recursion is only broken from outside the system — by grace.

## Connection to Māyā (9.142–154ab)
Karma operates within the field of Māyā. Without Māyā's limiting power, karma could not function. Understanding Māyā is therefore essential to understanding how karma binds — and how it can be released.
