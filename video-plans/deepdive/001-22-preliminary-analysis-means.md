# Preliminary Analysis of the Means: Will, Knowledge and Action
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 140-149

---

### Core Teaching

The one reality is pure consciousness, which from different points of view is equally the Absolute, Deity and the Self. The means to realization are divided into four categories: No Means (Anupāya), the Divine Means (Śāmbhavopāya — based on will), the Empowered Means (Śāktopāya — based on knowledge), and the Individual Means (Āṇavopāya — based on action). These three correspond to Śiva, Śakti and Nara. The Lord reveals His form to some in complete fullness and to others part by part, successively.

---

### The Lord Reveals by Degrees (Verse 140)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
ataḥ kaṁcit pramātāraṁ prati prathayate vibhuḥ |
pūrṇam eva nijaṁ rūpaṁ kaṁcid aṁśāṁśikākramāt || 140 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Thus, while the Pervasive Lord (vibhu) reveals His own complete form (in all His fullness) to a certain perceiver (pramātṛ), (He does so) to some (other) part by part, successively."

Jayaratha explains: To 'a certain perceiver' — by the rapidly heightening gradation of the intense modality of the decrease of obscuration. To 'another perceiver' — part by part, successively, in accord with the gradation ranging from slow onwards, in which the obscuring covering decreases.

---

### Supreme and Inferior Knowledge (Verse 141)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
viśvabhāvaikabhāvātmasvarūpaprathanaṁ hi yat |
aṇūnāṁ tat paraṁ jñānaṁ tadanyad aparaṁ bahu || 141 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The (full and complete) experience of one's own nature as the one Being of all beings is the supreme knowledge (of Deity) individual souls possess. Inferior and multiple is that (knowledge) which is other than that."

Jayaratha: The 'one Being' is the fundamental Being which is the supreme light of consciousness of all phenomena. That alone is supreme knowledge — the direct experience by means of the dynamic modality of consciousness free of thought constructs (avikalpavṛtti).

---

### The Means Defined (Verses 142-149)

**Verse 142:** Knowledge is divided into wonderfully diverse modalities — the direct means (Śāmbhavopāya) and means to that direct means (Śāktopāya and Āṇavopāya).

**Verse 143:** These means are of many kinds: direct or mediated, in all respects or part by part, with or without interposed obstacles.

**Verse 144:** The means based on knowledge is not ignorance but knowledge itself; the supreme means is subtler still — the power of will (icchā).

**Verse 145:** The distinction between means and goal is an error inherent in the gross state of knowledge — the power of action itself is the sole cause of both bondage and liberation.

**Verse 146:** That which manifests clearly in the first moment of perception within self-awareness is the direct means called the will (icchā) — the Divine Means.

**Verses 147-148:** Just as an entity manifests clearly for those with wide-open eyes without mental effort, so does Śiva's nature manifest to elevated souls. For others, the means based on knowledge (Śāktopāya) operates through repeated deliberation and developing conviction.

**Verse 149:** The means based on action (kriyopāya) utilizes external entities fashioned by conceptualization as its means. The difference between means is not in their goal, which is liberation.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary Insights

- The three means correspond to the triadic nature of reality: Śiva (will), Śakti (knowledge), Nara (action)
- No Means (Anupāya) is not mere negation but the supreme summit of the Divine Means
- The first moment of perception (prathamikālocana) — free of thought constructs — is the domain of the Divine Means
- Thought constructs operate in the second moment, giving rise to the Empowered and Individual Means
- Action is 'gross knowledge' — the outpouring of manifestation as the duality of subjects and objects

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Unity of Means and Goal**: Knowledge and liberation are not related as cause and effect; liberation is the experience of one's own nature, which is knowledge itself.

2. **Hierarchy of Means**: The higher means (Divine) is immediate, based on will; the middle (Empowered) is based on knowledge and conceptual purification; the lower (Individual) is based on external action. Yet all lead to the same liberation.

3. **Freedom of Consciousness**: Śiva's grace determines which means operates — it is not a matter of personal choice.

4. **Inclusion**: The Divine Means includes the other two as its basis; all means ultimately rest in it.
