# THE PURPOSE OF THE BOOK AND ITS INSPIRATION — Verses 14-21
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

Having invoked the deities and teachers, Abhinava states the purpose of the Tantrāloka. There are many liturgical manuals (paddhati) for various currents of scripture, but none for the Anuttara Trika — the highest realization of nondualist Śaivism. The Tantrāloka fills this gap. But it is more than a ritual manual: it is the Light of the Tantras and the Light on the Tantras — both the illumination that comes from the Tantras themselves and the illumination that makes them understood. Abhinava writes for the sake of future generations who will practice this path.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.14 — The Gap in the Traditions

**Line ~6333** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
santi paddhatayaś citrāḥ srotobhedeṣu bhiyasā |
anuttaraṣaḍardhārthakrame tv ekāpi nekṣyate || 14 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Many and various are the liturgical manuals (paddhati) (in use) in the diverse currents (of scripture). But there is not even one to be seen for the procedures (krama) of the Anuttara Trika."

**Explanation:**
The diverse currents of scripture are the five (or six) streams of Śaiva revelation. Each has its paddhatis — manuals that explain how to perform the rituals. But for the Anuttara Trika — the highest, most secret teaching — no such manual exists. The Tantrāloka will supply this lack.

#### Verse 1.15 — The Tantrāloka as a Complete Guide

**Line ~6525** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ato guruprasādāci śrīmatkalyāṇavāridheḥ |
āmnāyaikarasāsvādatṛptāśeṣamanīṣibhiḥ || 15 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Therefore, by the grace of my teacher, the venerable Kalyāṇa, who is an ocean (of compassion), (this work is written by me) whose mind is satisfied by the (undivided) taste (ekarasa) of the savour of the entire tradition (āmnāya)."

**Explanation:**
Abhinava acknowledges that his qualification to write this work comes from the grace of his teacher Kalyāṇa. His mind is "satisfied by the single taste" of the entire tradition — he has penetrated to the essence of all scriptures.

#### Verse 1.16 — The Book Defined

**Line ~6788** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ataś cātra samāmnāyaḥ śivāmnāyamayo mahān |
tantrālokābhidhāno ‘yaṁ paddhatiḥ kathyate mayā || 16 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"And so the great compilation (samāmnāya) consisting of (the essence of) the Śaiva tradition (śivāmnāya), called the Tantrāloka, is here taught by me as a liturgical manual (paddhati)."

**Explanation:**
The Tantrāloka is defined as a "great compilation" of the essence of the Śaiva tradition. The title means both "the Light of the Tantras" and "the Light on the Tantras." It is at once a liturgical manual (paddhati) and a comprehensive philosophical exposition.

#### Verse 1.17 — The Structure of the Work

**Line ~6826** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
atra tatroktamāndaiḥ svair vakṣyamāṇaiś ca hetubhiḥ |
vistārāt saṁkṣepataś ca siddhāntaḥ sthāpyate mama || 17 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Here (in this work), by reasons (hetu) stated elsewhere and by those that will be stated, (my) established conclusion (siddhānta) is set forth both in extenso and in brief."

**Explanation:**
Abhinava will draw from many sources — from the Tantras, from Pratyabhijñā philosophy, from the Spanda school — and present his synthesis both in detail (in the body of the text) and in summary (in the Tantrasāra). 

#### Verses 1.18-21 — The Five Currents and the Sixth

**Line ~7100** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit (excerpt):**
```
srotasāṁ tu samāmnāyaḥ pañcadhā vai vinirmitaḥ |
ūrdhvaṃ srotaḥ samākhyātaṃ tiryag adhaś ca sarvaśaḥ || 18 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The compilation of the currents (of scripture) (srotas) has been made five-fold. The upper current, the oblique (current) and the lower (current) are described in full."

**Explanation:**
The Śaiva scriptures are classified into five currents: the upper (Siddhānta from Īśāna's face), the right and left (from the other faces of Śiva), and the oblique and lower. Above all these is the sixth — the Kaula current from the Yoginī's mouth — which is the essence of the others.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On verse 14 (line ~6358+):** Jayaratha explains the nature of a paddhati — it is a text that enables performance of rituals by arranging in order the instructions dispersed throughout the Tantras. The Tantrāloka is such a manual, but it is more: it is a compendium of the essence of the entire Trika teaching.

**On the six currents (line ~7140+):** Jayaratha elaborates the five currents: the upper (Siddhānta), the right (Dakṣiṇa/Bhairava Tantras), the left (Vāma), the oblique, and the lower. Above these is the Kaula current, which is the essence of all. This is the Anuttara Trika.

**On the author's qualification (line ~6528+):** Jayaratha explains that Abhinava's mind is "satisfied by the single taste" because he has realized the nondual essence of all scriptures — the one taste of consciousness.

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On paddhati (note 201, line ~6358+):** "The Tantrāloka is not a paddhati in the narrow sense... Rather it builds on the basis of the Mālinīvijayottara a theory-rich account of the vision of the Trika's teaching of the nature of ultimate reality and the means to realization."

**On the title (note to verse 16, line ~6792+):** "The title means both 'the Light of the Tantras' and 'the Light on the Tantras' — both the illumination that comes from the Tantras themselves and the illumination that makes them understood."

### Philosophical Context

The purpose of the Tantrāloka is explicitly stated: it is a manual (paddhati) for the Anuttara Trika — the highest teaching — which had no manual. But it is also far more: it is a synthesis of all Śaiva knowledge, integrating the six currents of scripture, the Pratyabhijñā philosophy, the Spanda teachings, and the Kaula tradition into a unified system. The work is written for future generations who will not have access to a living teacher.

### Visual Concepts

1. **Five Currents Diagram:** Animated streams of light flowing from five faces of Śiva, with a sixth stream emerging above all five.

2. **The Light of the Tantras:** A book that glows from within, radiating light, with the title "Tantrāloka" inscribed in gold.

3. **The Single Taste:** A single point of light reflecting in multiple scriptures, showing the "one taste" (ekarasa) of all traditions.

### Narration Script

The purpose of the Tantrāloka is twofold. First, to provide a complete manual for the Anuttara Trika. Second, to synthesize all Śaiva knowledge into a single system. Abhinava draws from the six currents of scripture, the Pratyabhijñā, the Spanda school, and the Kaula tradition, presenting them as expressions of a single truth. The Tantrāloka is both "the Light of the Tantras" — the illumination the Tantras themselves provide — and "the Light on the Tantras" — the illumination that makes them understood. It is written for future generations, to ensure that the teaching survives.
