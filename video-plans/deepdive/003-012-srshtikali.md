# SṚṢṬIKĀLĪ — THE KĀLĪ OF CREATION AND EMANATION OF OBJECTIVITY
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verse 148

---

### Core Teaching

Sṛṣṭikālī is the first of the Twelve Kālīs, governing the initial phase of consciousness in which objectivity first begins to emerge. Before any perception, consciousness is "pure" (*śuddhā*) — unsullied by any fixed form of subject or object. Then, by its innate freedom, the Goddess of Consciousness becomes "eager to manifest" (*bhāsanotsukā*) and "ideates emanation" (*sṛṣṭiṁ kalayate*). This first moment is not yet a fully formed object but a faint trace (*āsūtrita*) of externality — the initial intention to emit objectivity. Swami Lakshmanjoo describes it as the "first flow of consciousness" in which perception is free of thought constructs, devoid of notions of name and form.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/148; Dyczkowski translation pp. 199–204, lines 14703–15013.

---

### The Goddess in Her Initial State

Jayaratha explains (lines 14767–14856): "Before it manifests in that way, it is 'pure,' that is, uncoloured by any fixed form, whether subject or object or any of the rest." This supreme "goddess of consciousness" is called Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī (the Attractress of Time), who shines "perpetually in this form, greedy to devour the stain of the procession of time."

The phrase "eager to manifest" indicates that "by the power of Her innate freedom" she contemplates phenomena as if faintly indicated externally — "like the object of one's desire at the instant the desire arises" (lines 14886–14893). In Pratyabhijñā terms, this corresponds to the level of Sadāśiva — the reflective awareness "I am all this universe," which is one with consciousness and not yet externally manifest.

---

### Swami Lakshmanjoo's Explanation

Swami Lakshmanjoo describes (lines 14794–14834) the moment of Sṛṣṭikālī in simple perceptual terms:

"When I perceive anything by means of (the sensations) of sound, touch, form, taste and smell, I am attracted towards it. In the first moment, I experience the manifestation of that entity only in a form free of thought constructs. This manifestation free of thought constructs is called 'the initial perception' (*prāthamikālocana*). When consciousness is directed towards an entity and the object begins to be perceived, then in this first flow (of consciousness), consciousness only perceives the nature of the object free of thought constructs devoid of (any) notion of (its) name and form."

In that state "the perceiver has no conceptual (*savikalpa*) knowledge such as 'I am seeing, I am speaking.'" All that manifests is "the nature of consciousness, free of thought constructs and beyond description."

---

### The Kālī's Function: Ideation (*Kalana*)

The verb *kalayate* (from √kal) carries five meanings: "casting forth, knowledge, enumeration, movement and sound" (lines 14771–14775). In Sṛṣṭikālī's case, the primary meaning is "casting forth" (*kṣepa*) — the initial projective impulse of consciousness toward objectivity.

This is the "wave" (*ūrmi*) of the Ocean of Energy (*kaulārṇava*) — the first pulsation of consciousness that contains within itself both the unfolding (*unmeṣa*) and withdrawal (*nimeṣa*) of the universe (lines 14922–14991).

---

### Scriptural Quotations

**Devīpañcaśatika (DP 5/23cd–25ab, lines 14917–14919):**
"That energy (*kalā*) whose glorious power (*vibhava*) is all things, and capable of serving as the instrument of emanation, inwardly attains tranquillity, is known as Sṛṣṭikālī."

**Kramastotra (lines 14922–14991, from Cidgaganacandrikā):**
"I constantly worship Sṛṣṭikālī, Who is the wave of dense bliss of the Ocean of Energy, and contains inwardly both the unfolding of the universe and its withdrawal."

---

### The Paradox of the First Kālī

Jayaratha notes (lines 14937–14950) that Sṛṣṭikālī is paradoxically said to "destroy emanation." This apparent contradiction is resolved when we understand that "the Kālīs are all aspects of the one Kālī, who is engaged in consuming everything by withdrawing it into herself." Emanation and destruction are two sides of the same process: "emanation follows in the wake of destruction. Each new moment emerges from the emptiness of the cessation of the previous one."

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Primal Moment of Perception**: Sṛṣṭikālī represents the first moment of any cognitive act — the pure, non-conceptual awareness of an object before it is named or categorized. This is the ground from which all subsequent cognitive phases unfold.

2. **Freedom as the Ground of Manifestation**: The "eagerness" to manifest is not a lack or compulsion but the expression of innate freedom (*svātantrya*). Consciousness becomes objective not because it must, but because it wills to.

3. **The Seed of All Kālīs**: Sṛṣṭikālī contains within herself the entire cycle of twelve. As the first wave of the ocean of energy, she initiates the pulsation that unfolds as the full wheel of time and consciousness.
