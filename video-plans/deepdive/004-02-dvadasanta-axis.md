## Central Thesis
The dvādaśānta ("End of the Twelve") is the vertical axis of yogic practice in the Tantrāloka — a series of twelve finger-breadths spanning from the heart centre up through the crown of the head and extending into the space above. Along this axis, the breath moves, the six blisses unfold, the ten voids are experienced, and the entire cosmic time-cycle is inscribed.

## Script Outline

### 1. Opening: What Is Dvādaśānta? (0:00–1:30)
- Literally "End of the Twelve" — the twelve finger-breadths measuring the yogic body
- Three dvādaśāntas: heart-to-crown (inner), crown-to-upper-space (outer), and the supreme beyond — lines 8657, 34318
- Not merely anatomical — a metaphysical axis of consciousness ascent — lines 11231, 11442
- The breath travels from heart to dvādaśānta and back — line 19687–19688

### 2. The Measurement System (1:30–3:00)
- Body measured in sets of twelve finger-breadths — line 8657
- Eight sets from soles to crown = 96 fingers for physical body
- One set below feet, one above head = subtle body extension = 108 fingers total — line 6470s
- The "basic" dvādaśānta is the genital centre — commentary on 5/55
- The upper dvādaśānta is above the crown — line 11442

### 3. The Breath's Journey Along the Axis (3:00–5:00)
- Prāṇa (exhalation) rises from heart to dvādaśānta — lines 3680–3682
- Apāna (inhalation) descends from dvādaśānta to heart — lines 3692–3696
- HAṀ and SAḤ: the two poles of the unstruck sound — lines 3705–3708
- The heart is the source of all breath movement — lines 3656–3662
- "The uttered breath above and the inhaled jīva below, Parā who is emission is uttering forth" — VBH 24, lines 3705–3708

### 4. The Dvādaśānta in Chapter 5 Practice (5:00–6:30)
- Hṛdayoccāra: the utterance from the heart to the dvādaśānta — lines 4398–4418
- The Trident (triśūla) projected along the axis — lines 4673–4683
- Lambikā (uvula) as the crossroads of breath ascent — lines 4811–4832
- The End of the Twelve as the supreme plane of emission — lines 6470s

### 5. The Dvādaśānta in Chapter 6 Practice (6:30–8:00)

- The sixfold path in the breath from heart to dvādaśānta — lines 417–418

### 6. The Dvādaśānta as the Supreme Void (8:00–9:30)
- Śūnyātiśūnya (void beyond void) — line 16581
- The ten voids experienced along the dvādaśānta axis — lines 372–373
- The dvādaśānta as the place where time dissolves — lines 180–185
- "Established in Emptiness, he is thereafter devoid of all" — line 16721

### 7. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- The dvādaśānta is the central axis of the entire Āṇavopāya and Kālopāya systems
- It microcosmically contains all cosmic geography, all time, and all states of consciousness
- "One should cast one's mind into the End of the Twelve in any way and wherever, every moment" — VBH 51, line 6729
