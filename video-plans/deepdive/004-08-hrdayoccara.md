## Central Thesis
Hṛdayoccāra — the Utterance from the Heart — is the practice of tracing the breath's movement from the Heart centre up through the body's vertical axis to the End of the Twelve. It integrates the six blisses with the Trident maṇḍala projected into the subtle body, using the seed-syllable SAUḤ as the vehicle of ascent.

## Script Outline

### 1. What Is Hṛdayoccāra? (0:00–1:30)
- Hṛdaya = Heart, uccāra = utterance/upward motion — lines 4444–4449
- The Heart is not a cakra but "the one all-embracing reality which pulses like a heart" — lines 4437–4442
- The passage of the breath from Heart to End of Twelve IS the utterance — lines 4444–4449
- The six breaths (prāṇa, apāna, udāna, vyāna, samāna, prāṇanā) — lines 4475–4479

### 2. The Perfect Rest (1:30–3:00)
- "The perfect rest there is the abiding state which is the Absolute (Anuttara)" — lines 4415–4418
- This rest can be achieved through Hṛdayoccāra — line 4401–4404
- The same power is present in all centres from Heart to End of Twelve — lines 4467–4474
- No real difference for one who has departed from the Heart — lines 4534–4541

### 3. The Stick of the Vital Breath (3:00–5:00)
- Prāṇadaṇḍa: Kuṇḍalinī straightened like a stick — lines 4744–4753
- "Kuṇḍalinī should be awakened by the teacher, like a snake struck by a stick" — lines 4749–4753
- Equalizing prāṇa and apāna — lines 4673–4683, 4755–4760
- The breath enters suṣumnā (the Middle Abode) — lines 4808–4811
- The Lambikā (uvula) as the crossroads of ascent — lines 4811–4832

### 4. The Trident Projection (5:00–7:00)
- The three channels (iḍā, piṅgalā, suṣumnā) unite at the Trident — lines 4679–4683
- The powers of will, knowledge, and action on the Trident's tips — lines 4679–4683
- The Square (catuṣpatha) below the prongs = the Pure Knowledge Principle — lines 4795–4804
- The Trident's shaft extends through the 36 tattvas — lines 4522–4528
- SAUḤ pervades from the genital region up through the crown — lines 4520–4528

### 5. The Six Breaths and Six Blisses United (7:00–9:00)
- The six forms of the vital breath as universal and particular — lines 4552–4562
- The six breaths produce the six blisses as progressive expansion — lines 4556–4562
- "This is the reality born of the utterance of the six breaths within the abode of one's own essential nature" — lines 4467–4474
- Both the bliss progression and the mantra utterance reach the same goal: Anuttara — lines 4415–4418

### 6. Conclusion (9:00–10:00)
- Hṛdayoccāra is the master practice integrating breath, mantra, and maṇḍala
- The Heart is both the starting point and the goal — the alpha and omega of the breath's journey
- "Once having attained that Goddess, what is not attained?" — line 4730–4731
