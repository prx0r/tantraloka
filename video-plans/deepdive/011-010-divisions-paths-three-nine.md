# Divisions of the Paths: Three, Nine, and Six
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

The six paths (ṣaḍadhvan) — Forces (kalā), Principles (tattva), Worlds (bhuvana), Phonemes (varṇa), Mantras (mantra), and Padas (pada) — can be variously divided for different purposes. Abhinavagupta teaches a division into three principles (subject, means, object), into nine principles (the eight prakṛtis plus puruṣa), and the standard six paths. Each division serves a different pedagogical function, and the number of possible initiations is correspondingly countless.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verses 11.34cd-36 — The Division into Three Principles

**Lines approximately 1680–1710:**

> trayaḥ prāptā dhāmabhedā upāyānāṁ vasādataḥ |
||

The three principles correspond to the three subjective states:
1. **Puruṣa** — the individual subject, the experiencer
2. **Prakṛti** — fundamental nature, the matrix of objectivity
3. **Śiva** — the absolute subject, pure consciousness

These three correspond to the three upāyas (means): Āṇava (Individual), Śākta (Empowered), and Śāmbhava (Divine).

---

#### Verses 11.37-38ab — The Division into Nine Principles

> navadhā tu prakṛtyaṣṭapuruṣābhyāṁ prakalpanāt |
||

The nine principles are the eight "natures" (prakṛtis) plus puruṣa. The eight prakṛtis are: the five elements (Earth through Ether) plus the three internal organs (buddhi, ahaṁkāra, manas). This ninefold division is used for certain types of initiation (navātman dīkṣā).

---

#### Verses 11.38cd-42ab — Countless Initiations

Because the six paths can be combined, subdivided, and applied in various ways, the number of possible initiations is countless. Each initiate may be purified through a unique combination of paths. The teacher, through his insight, determines which path or combination is appropriate.

---

#### Verse 11.42cd-43 — The Six Paths: Three Objective, Three Subjective

**Lines approximately 1750–1770:**

> trayo 'rthādhvānas trayaś ca pramāṇādhvāna īritāḥ |
||

The six paths divide into:

**Objective (artha-adhvan):**
1. **Bhuvana** (Worlds) — the realm of locations and cosmological levels
2. **Tattva** (Principles) — the 36 ontological categories
3. **Kalā** (Forces) — the 5 energies that group the principles

**Subjective (pramāṇa-adhvan):**
4. **Varṇa** (Phonemes) — the 50 letters of the Sanskrit alphabet as units of consciousness
5. **Mantra** — the articulated forms of divine awareness
6. **Pada** — the "words" or divisions of mantras

The objective paths correspond to what is known (prameya); the subjective paths correspond to the means of knowing (pramāṇa). Together, they constitute the entire sphere of experience.

### Narrative Arc

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** Why so many divisions? The six paths, three principles, nine principles — what is the purpose of this taxonomic abundance?

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** The three principles (puruṣa, prakṛti, śiva) as the most condensed formulation. Each corresponds to a means of realization.

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** The nine principles for initiation. The eight prakṛtis plus puruṣa. The navātman dīkṣā.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** The six paths as the complete system. Objective vs. subjective paths. Purification operates on both.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** Countless initiations — why the teacher's discernment is essential. No single formula works for everyone.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The divisions are not arbitrary — they correspond to real structures of consciousness. To know the paths is to know the architecture of liberation.
