# 338 — Supreme Śiva the Agent of All Things
**Tantrāloka 9.8–10** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Who really makes the pot — the potter, or something working through him?

## Core Teaching
Only the Supreme Lord, whose body is consciousness alone, is truly an agent. All apparent agency of limited beings is a contracted reflection of Śiva's universal agency, operating through the covering of *niyati* (natural law).

## Key Verses

**9.8cd**: *asvatantrasya kartṛtvaṁ nahi jātūpapadyate* — "It is totally impossible for agency to belong to that which is not free."

**9.9ab**: *svatantratā ca cinmātravapuṣaḥ parameṣituḥ* — "Freedom belongs only to the Supreme Lord, whose body is consciousness alone."

**9.9cd**: *svatantraṁ ca jaḍaṁ ceti tad anyonyaṁ virudhyate* — "The autonomous and the insentient are mutually contradictory."

**9.10ab**: *jāḍyaṁ pramātṛtantratvaṁ svātmasiddhim api prati* — "The insentient condition is a state of dependence on the autonomous subject even for the attainment of its own existence."

**9.10cd**: *na kartṛtvād ṛte cānyat kāraṇatvaṁ hi labhyate* — "There is no other cause apart from the agency of the subject."

## The Potter Argument
- The potter depends on clay, wheel, water, and countless factors
- His intention cannot alone produce the pot — clay resists becoming cloth
- The potter's arrogation "I made this" is itself Śiva's power functioning through him
- Real agency = freedom (*svātantrya*) = self-luminosity (*svaprakāśatā*)
- Insentience = dependence on another for illumination

## Three Key Philosophical Moves
1. **Agency requires freedom** — Pāṇini 1/4/54 defines *kartā* as *svatantra* (independent)
2. **Freedom requires consciousness** — insentient things have no autonomy
3. **Only Śiva is pure consciousness** — therefore only He is truly an agent

## Jayaratha on Pāṇini
The potter's "false arrogation" of personal agency arises from identifying with the insentient body. The freedom to act is solely associated with consciousness.

## Connection to Karma and Mala
This argument grounds the later discussion of karma (9.88cd–141): karmic impressions cannot bind a free agent. Bondage is only apparent, arising from misidentification with the limited body-mind.
