# Liṅga Worship: The Many Kinds of Liṅga

**Tantrāloka 27.1–29 | Lines 772–781**

## Overview

The final chapter of Volume 9 discusses the worship of the liṅga (liṅgārcā). The liṅga is not merely a phallic symbol but a "sign" (liṅga = "mark" or "emblem") of the unmanifest Śiva. Remarkably, the highest Trika mantras should NOT be used for external liṅgas — they are reserved for the inner liṅga of consciousness. The chapter covers the installation of deities into liṅgas and even into skulls.

## Key Verses

- **2 (line 772):** The many kinds of liṅga — from svayambhu (self-manifested) to mānava (man-made).
- **2–8 (lines 773–774):** Higher secret mantras should not be used externally — they are for the inner liṅga.
- **9–20ab (lines 775–776):** Installation of a deity into a liṅga or iconic representation.
- **20cd–21ab (lines 778–779):** A deity can be installed and worshipped in a skull (kapāla).
- **21cd–29 (lines 781–782):** The characteristics and features of a skull suitable for worship.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The liṅga as sign of the signless:** The liṅga points to that which has no liṅga — the unmanifest Śiva. Its worship is the worship of the formless through form. The "inner liṅga" is the heart where Śiva is directly known.

2. **Higher mantras are for the inner worship:** The supreme mantras — Parā Vidyā, Parāparā Vidyā, Aparā Vidyā — are not to be used for external liṅga pūjā. They are too powerful, too intimate. They are the heart of the inner worship (antarārca).

3. **Worship in a skull:** The worship of deities in a human skull is the most transgressive form of liṅga worship. The skull is the "container" of consciousness during life; after death, it becomes a vessel for the divine presence. This practice confronts the practitioner with mortality and transcendence simultaneously.

4. **The installation (pratiṣṭhā) is a consecration of consciousness:** Installing a deity in a liṅga is not bringing a god into a stone but awakening the consciousness that has always been there — the stone is already Śiva, but the installation makes this apparent.

## The Inner-Outer Dialectic

The liṅga chapter completes the arc of Volume 9. The outer worship of the liṅga is for those who need a support; the inner worship of consciousness is for those who have realized. But even the outer liṅga, when worshipped with the right awareness, leads to the inner.
