# THE INITIAL INVOCATION — VERSE 1
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verse 1

---

### Core Teaching

The Tantrāloka opens with a maṅgalaśloka — an auspicious invocation that is simultaneously a biographical confession and a complete summary of Trika metaphysics. Abhinavagupta invokes his parents, Vimalā and Narasiṁhagupta, as the human embodiments of Śiva and Śakti. From their union — the *yāmala* (united form) of the divine couple — arises his own Heart (*hṛdaya*), which he identifies with the supreme reality: the body (*kula*) of the nectar (*amṛta*) of the Absolute (*anuttara*), pulsating as the emission (*visarga*) of the universe. This single verse encodes the entire Trika system: the polarity of Śiva and Śakti, their dynamic union, the nature of consciousness as self-emissive light, and the identity of the author's innermost being with the ultimate reality. To understand this verse is to hold the key to the entire Tantrāloka.

**Line references:** TĀ 1/1; Dyczkowski translation pp. 2–30, lines 960–1099.

---

### The Verse

**Sanskrit:**
```
vimalakalāśrayābhinavasṛṣṭimahā jananī |
bharitatanuś ca pañcamukhaguptarucir janakaḥ |
tadubhayayāmalasphuritabhāvavisargamayaṁ |
hṛdayam anuttarāmṛtakulaṁ mama saṁsphuratāt || 1 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "(My) mother, Vimalā, is (joyous with) the glory (of the festival of my birth), the ever new (*abhinava*) creation, her foundation the pure (*vimala*) energy (of the Moon). (My) father (Narasiṁhagupta) is the hidden (protected) (*gupta*) light (*ruci*) with five faces and his body is full. May the body (*kula*) of the nectar (*amṛta*) of the Absolute (*anuttara*), my Heart, the emission (*visarga*), which is the vibrant (emissive) state of the united form (*yāmala*) of both of them (my father and mother, Śiva and Śakti), pulsate radiantly!"

---

### The Two Levels of Meaning

This verse operates on two levels simultaneously — the literal/biographical and the metaphysical:

**The Biographical Level:** Abhinavagupta names his biological parents. His mother Vimalā ("the Pure One") is described as rejoicing in her son's birth, her foundation being the pure energy (the Moon, *kalā*). His father Narasiṁhagupta ("Lion-hidden") is described as having a full body, his light hidden/protected (*gupta*) by five faces — a reference to his restraint of the five senses. The verse prays that Abhinavagupta's heart — born from the union of these two — may pulsate radiantly.

**The Metaphysical Level:** The "mother" is Vimalā — the pure energy of the Moon, identified with Śakti as the power of creation (*sṛṣṭi*). Her "ever new creation" (*abhinavasṛṣṭi*) is the perpetual emergence of phenomena within consciousness. The "father" is Śiva, whose "full body" (*bharitatanu*) signifies his all-encompassing nature, and whose "five faces" represent the five powers of consciousness (*cit, ānanda, icchā, jñāna, kriyāśakti*). Their united form (*yāmala*) is the dynamic union of Śiva and Śakti — *prakāśa* and *vimarśa* — from which arises the Heart (*hṛdaya*). This Heart is *anuttarāmṛtakula* — the family/collectivity (*kula*) of the nectar of the Absolute — the very essence of nondual consciousness pulsating as the emission (*visarga*) of the universe.

---

### The Heart as the Supreme Reality

Abhinavagupta identifies his own Heart with the supreme reality. This is not a statement of ego but of recognition (*pratyabhijñā*). The Heart is the *yāmala* — the union of Śiva and Śakti — experienced as the pulsation (*sphurattā*) of I-consciousness. It is:

- **Anuttara** — the Absolute beyond which nothing is higher
- **Amṛta** — the nectar of immortality, consciousness in its undying self-presence
- **Kula** — the body or family, the totality of all energies gathered together
- **Visarga** — the emission, the creative outpouring that is both the act and the result of self-manifestation

The prayer "mama saṁsphuratāt" ("may my Heart pulsate radiantly") is not a request for something new to happen but a recognition of what already is — the eternal pulsation of consciousness that is the reality of all things.

---

### The Significance of the Maṅgalaśloka

As Jayaratha explains, it is a well-established practice to begin a Sanskrit treatise with an auspicious verse (*maṅgalaśloka*) praising the author's deity to remove obstacles. Here, Abhinavagupta's deity is the Heart itself — the union of Śiva and Śakti. This verse heads all his works on Anuttara Trika (except the brief Tantravaṭadhānikā), indicating its centrality.

The verse functions as:
1. **A Benediction** — invoking blessings for the successful completion of the work
2. **A Summary** — encapsulating the entire Trika metaphysics in a single verse
3. **An Authorization** — establishing the author's credentials through his lineage (both biological and spiritual)
4. **A Homage** — honoring his parents as embodiments of the divine

---

### The Name "Abhinavagupta"

The verse famously contains a hidden signature: *abhinava* (ever new) and *gupta* (hidden/protected) — the two components of Abhinavagupta's name. The "ever new creation" of the mother and the "hidden light" of the father combine to form the name of the author, who is himself the living union of these two principles. This is not mere wordplay but a profound statement: Abhinavagupta presents himself as the embodiment of the Trika teaching — the living *yāmala* of Śiva and Śakti.

As Dyczkowski notes, a reduced version of this verse appears at the end of Abhinava's earliest surviving work, the *Bhagavadgītārthasaṁgraha*, suggesting he arrived at its final form gradually.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Heart as the Absolute**: The *hṛdaya* is not a physical organ but the core of consciousness — the point where subject and object, Śiva and Śakti, are recognized as one. It is the reality of one's own being.

2. **The Biographical as Metaphysical**: Abhinavagupta's biological parents are transparent to the divine. Vimalā is Śakti; Narasiṁhagupta is Śiva. Their union is the *yāmala* that generates all reality.

3. **Visarga as the Key**: The emission (*visarga*) is the central dynamic of the Absolute — the self-outpouring of consciousness that is both the universe and its transcendence. It is the state of absolute potential that is also perpetual actualization.

4. **Self-Blessing**: Unlike standard *maṅgalaślokas* that praise an external deity, Abhinava praises his own Heart as the supreme reality. This is consistent with the nondual vision: there is nothing higher than one's own consciousness.
