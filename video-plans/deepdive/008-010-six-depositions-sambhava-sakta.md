# The Six Depositions: Śāmbhava and Śākta

**Tantrāloka 15.232cd–276ab | Lines 6101–6700**

## Overview

After the purification of the body and breath, the practitioner performs the "special depositions" (viśeṣanyāsa). These install the six male (Śāmbhava) and six female (Śākta) mantric energies onto the body, transforming it into the divine couple (yāmala) of Bhairava and Bhairavī.

## Key Verses

- **239cd–246:** The six depositions — six aspects of the mantra-body are installed.
- **247–248ab (lines 597–598):** The Śāmbhava deposition — six male mantras placed on head, face, heart, genitals, feet, and all limbs.
- **248cd–251ab:** The Śākta deposition — six corresponding female energies.
- **251cd–256:** The depositions with Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī Kālī — she who "attracts time."
- **257–261ab:** The sixfold deposition in relation to the six states of consciousness.
- **267cd–275ab:** Purifying the thought of identity with Śiva attained in consciousness free of thought.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Male and female as prakāśa and vimarśa:** The Śāmbhava (male) depositions correspond to the light of consciousness (prakāśa); the Śākta (female) correspond to its reflective awareness (vimarśa). Together they form the yāmala — the non-dual couple.

2. **Six states of consciousness:** Waking, dream, deep sleep, the Fourth (turīya), Beyond the Fourth (turīyātīta), and the state of the Transmental (unmanā). Each deposition corresponds to one state, indicating that the mantra-body spans ALL states.

3. **The burning of confinement (261cd–267ab):** After the depositions, the practitioner's sense of limitation (saṅkoca) is burned away, and the pure emanation of omniform Śiva arises. This is the transformation from aṇu (contracted soul) to Śiva.

4. **Freedom from thought:** The final refinement (267cd–275ab) purifies even the thought "I am Śiva" — because that thought is still a vikalpa. True identity is nirvikalpa — without mental construction.

## The Six Limbs

The six limbs (ṣaḍaṅga) — heart, head, topknot, armor, eye, weapon — are the standard structure of mantra bodies in Tantric Śaivism. Each limb corresponds to a divine attribute (aiśvarya). Together they constitute the "body of mantras" (mantradeha) that the worshipper becomes.
