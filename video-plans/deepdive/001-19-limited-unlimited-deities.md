# LIMITED AND UNLIMITED DEITIES AS FORMS OF THE LORD — Verses 122cd-124
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

The Lord of the Universe, Bhairava Who is Consciousness, is worshipped in two modes: with limitation (sāvaccheda) by those who desire worldly benefits, and without limitation (niravaccheda) by those who seek liberation. But even the limited form is vitalized by the unlimited supreme form. Those who worship other deities are ultimately worshipping the same Lord, though without the conscious understanding that He is their own Self. The Self is implicitly present in all worship as the thread that strings the garland of deities together.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.122cd-123ab — Two Forms of Worship

**Line ~22594** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasmād viśveśvaro bodhabhairavaḥ samupāsyate || 122 ||
avacchedānavacchidbhyāṁ bhogamokṣārthibhir janaiḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Therefore, the Lord of the universe, the Bhairava Who is Consciousness, is worshipped by those who desire worldly benefits or liberation, with a limited form or without limitation, respectively."

**Explanation:**
The same Bhairava is approached differently according to the aspirant's goal. The one seeking worldly benefits worships a limited form — a specific deity with specific attributes. The one seeking liberation worships without limitation — as the formless consciousness that is all forms.

#### Verse 1.123cd — The Bhagavadgītā Reference

**Line ~22629** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ye 'py anyadevatābhaktā ity ato gurur ādiśat || 123 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The teacher (Kṛṣṇa) has taught this (in a verse of the Bhagavadgītā) that begins with (the words): 'those also who are devotees of other deities.'"

**Explanation:**
Abhinava cites Bhagavadgītā 9/23: "Those who worship other deities worship Me alone, but without prior injunction." He interprets "without prior injunction" to mean they do not recognize that the deity they worship is their own Self.

#### Verse 1.124 — All Worship is of Consciousness

**Line ~22724** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ye bodhād vyatiriktaṁ hi kīñcid yājyatayā viduḥ |
te 'pi vedyaṁ viviñcānā bodhābhedena manvate || 124 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Even those who consider something separate from consciousness to be an object of worship, they too — (rightly) distinguishing the object of worship — think of it as not different from consciousness."

**Explanation:**
Even dualistic worship is ultimately worship of consciousness. The dualist may think he worships a deity separate from himself, but the deity's reality is consciousness. The distinction is not in the object but in the understanding.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On limited and unlimited (line ~22602+):** "A 'limited form' is the condition of a rite in which it is governed by a fixed procedure such as a visualization. What is otherwise is 'without limitation'."

**On the Gītā interpretation (line ~22678+):** Abhinava comments that those who sacrifice with limited desires "themselves put limitations on what they could achieve because of the weakness of their mind."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the Bhagavadgītā passage (note 846, line ~22710+):** "The immediate sense of avidhipūrvaka is 'not according to proper procedure'... Abhinava prefers to understand this expression as 'without prior injunction' — meaning without the recognition that the Self is the ultimate recipient of worship."

### Philosophical Context

This section establishes the nondualist theory of worship. All worship, whatever its form, is ultimately worship of one's own Self. The difference between worldly and liberating worship is not the deity but the understanding. The limited forms are not false — they are real manifestations of consciousness, but they are limited by the worshipper's understanding. Liberation comes when worship is recognized as the Self's self-recognition.

### Narration Script

Bhairava as Consciousness is worshipped in two ways: with limitation by those who seek worldly benefits, without limitation by those who seek liberation. But even limited worship is effective because the limited form is vitalized by the unlimited supreme form. When Kṛṣṇa says in the Gītā that those who worship other deities worship Him alone, Abhinava explains this to mean that the Self is always the true recipient of worship — present in every deity like the thread that holds a garland together.
