# 346 — Varieties of Causal Sequences
**Tantrāloka 9.40cd–48ab** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Different scriptures describe the order of creation differently. Which one is right? All of them — because the same reality can be traversed in different directions.

## Core Teaching
Abhinavagupta presents multiple Śaiva scriptural accounts of the order of emanation, especially of the *kañcukas* (coverings). These differences are not contradictions but reflect different perspectives on the same process.

## Scriptural Sources Compared

### Mālinīvijayottara (Trika)
Sequence: Māyā → Kāla → Niyati → Kalā → Vidyā → Rāga
(This is Abhinava's preferred order)

### Kiraṇatantra (Siddhānta)
Sequence: Pradhāna → Puruṣa → Rāga → Impure Knowledge → (Kāla+Niyati as couple) → Kalā → Māyā

### Svāyambhuvāgama (Siddhānta)
Sequence: Māyā → Kāla → Niyati → Kalā → Vidyā

### Mātaṅgapārameśvara
Sequence: Puruṣa → Niyati → Kāla + Rāga → Vidyā → Kalā

### Sarvajñānottara
Sequence: Puruṣa → Rāga → Vidyā → Kalā → Kāla → Māyā
(Notably omits Niyati)

## Why the Differences?
1. **Emanation vs. Withdrawal** — sequences differ depending on whether you describe the descent (emanation) or ascent (withdrawal)
2. **Level of Discourse** — different perceivers experience the sequence differently
3. **Scriptural Context** — each Āgama teaches what is appropriate for its audience

## Key Insight
The *kañcukas* are not temporal stages but logical layers of contraction. They can be enumerated in any order because they arise simultaneously as the five aspects of the one act of self-limitation.

## Jayaratha's Note
"The commentators explain that the principle of Necessity is called Well Established (Saṁsthā) because it establishes well, that is, induces the individual souls into having their particular worldly experiences as a consequence of their karma."

## Connection to Mala (9.62–88ab)
These varying sequences of the coverings are all descriptions of how the one impurity (*mala*) — the contraction of consciousness — manifests as multiple limiting factors. The variety of sequences reflects the variety of viewpoints on this contraction.
