# THE SPONTANEOUSLY ENLIGHTENED TEACHER (SĀṀSIDDHIKA / AKALPITA GURU)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 40cd–51ab

---

### Core Teaching

The highest type of teacher is the *sāṁsiddhika* ("spontaneously enlightened") or *akalpita* ("uncreated") guru — one for whom discriminative reasoning arises by itself (*svata eva*), independently of any teacher, scripture, or practice. This teacher is "enlightened by himself" (*svapratyayātmaka*), having received the most intense descent of Śiva's grace through No Means (*Anupāya*). His consciousness is completely free of impurities, and he embodies the perfect plenitude of realization. Abhinavagupta enumerates five types of teachers in total, descending from this highest to the lowest, based on the intensity of grace received and the extent to which purification of thought was needed.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/40cd–78ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 49–93, lines 4068–7135.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verses 40cd–41ab — The Definition

**Sanskrit:**
```
sa ca sāṁsiddhikaḥ śāstre proktaḥ svapratyayātmakaḥ |
ākhyātā vinā sa bhavati guror anapalāpinaḥ || 41 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 4114–4122):** "And such a one is called in the scriptures 'Spontaneously Enlightened' (*sāṁsiddhika*), and his nature is such that he is enlightened by himself (*svapratyayātmaka*) (without instruction)."

Jayaratha explains (lines 4124–4136): This reasoning "arises in some cases by itself, that is, independently of the causes well known to all, such as a teacher's instruction." However, "it is not without a cause in all respects, because in actual fact the Supreme Lord's descent of the power (of grace) arises as an alternative instrumental cause." The name "accords with its etymological meaning" — one who is "fully accomplished" (*samyak siddha*) by his very nature or from his own nature (*svabhāvataḥ*).

---

#### The Three Kinds of Siddhis in the Siddhānta

Jayaratha (lines 4190–4238) quotes the *Mṛgendratantra* (10/26–29) to contextualize *sāṁsiddhika* within the Siddhānta framework of three types of accomplishment:

1. **Sāṁsiddhika** — "The quality of those whose minds are energized by special traces of Dharma" that "shines even after death, when there is no body"
2. **Vainayika** — attained through discipline
3. **Prākṛta** — natural, worldly

The *sāṁsiddhika* teacher's attainment is "by knowledge, liberation; by dispassion, the dissolving away of Prakṛti; by mastery, one is without obstacles" (lines 4235–4238).

---

#### Verse 42 — Three Grades Based on Grace

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasyāpi bhedā utkṛṣṭamadhyamandādyupāyataḥ || 42 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 5389–5393):** "There are various types of the latter, according to whether the means (that is, the grace he has received,) is intense, moderate or weak."

---

#### Verses 44cd–47ab — Pure Knowledge of Sound Reasoning

Abhinavagupta clarifies (lines 5346–5356): Chapter Four "deals with the Empowered Means, which is based on the activation of the power of knowledge. This is set in operation in accord with the developing descent of Śiva's power of grace. As it grows, the innate intelligence (*pratibhā*) of consciousness comes to the fore, thus incrementing the capacity for intuitive, creative and discerning insight, i.e. Sound Reasoning (*tarka*). It is this which, when well developed, is the cardinal characteristic of true teachers."

The highest kind of teacher "is graced by such an intense form of it that he is spontaneously enlightened" (lines 5355–5356).

---

#### The Sāṁsiddhika and the Siddhānta Tradition

Jayaratha notes (lines 4177–4184) that the concept of a spontaneously enlightened teacher "finds its scriptural basis in the Bhairava and Kaula Tantra." The Siddhāntas, which "generally do not promote the possibility of liberation in this life (*jīvanmukti*)," nonetheless maintain "moderate links to it" through the concept of *siddhis* (accomplishments). The *sāṁsiddhika siddhi* is the highest of three types, "practically amounting to a form of liberation."

---

#### The First Two Types of Teacher

Abhinavagupta's five-fold classification (lines 4068–7135) from highest to lowest:

| Type | Nature | Characteristics | Verses |
|------|--------|-----------------|--------|
| 1. **Sāṁsiddhika** (Spontaneously Enlightened) | *Akalpita* (Uncreated) | Reasoning arises by itself; no teacher needed | 40cd–51ab |
| 2. **Akalpitakalpaka** (Uncreated-cum-created) | Mixed | Has spontaneous insight but needs purification through self-contemplation | 51cd–59 |
| 3. **Kalpitākalpita** (Created-cum-uncreated) | Mixed | Needs teacher initiation but has genuine realization | 69cd–70ab |
| 4. **Kalpita** (Created) | Lower | Formed entirely by teacher/scripture; limited realization | 70cd–72ab |
| 5. **Akalpita** (uncreated — a second type) | Pure | Different from #1; spontaneously free of all constructs | 73cd–78ab |

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Possibility of Spontaneous Liberation**: The *sāṁsiddhika* teacher demonstrates that liberation can occur without external means — purely by Śiva's grace. This is "No Means" (*Anupāya*) realization, the highest form of grace (lines 5506–5510).

2. **Grace as the Determining Factor**: All distinctions between teachers are ultimately based on "the descent of the power (of grace)" (*śaktipāta*). The *sāṁsiddhika* has received the most intense descent, while others require progressively more mediation through practice and instruction.

3. **Hierarchical Nondualism**: The classification of teachers into grades does not compromise nondualism. Even the lowest teacher possesses genuine realization — the difference is in degree of clarity and completeness, not in kind.

4. **The Teacher as Category of Grace**: The guru is not merely a human figure but a category of divine grace. Abhinavagupta's teaching emphasizes that the teacher's authority derives entirely from the degree of Śiva's self-revelation within his consciousness.
