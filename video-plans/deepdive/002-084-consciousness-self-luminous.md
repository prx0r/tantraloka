# Topic 84: Consciousness is Self-luminous, Practice Cannot Be a Means

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 84 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: 8-14

---

## 1. Core Teaching

No practice associated with action (kriyāyoga) can serve as a means to realize consciousness, because consciousness is self-luminous (svaprakāśa). Action depends on consciousness; consciousness depends on nothing. All means, external or internal, depend on consciousness. Even meditation (bhāvanā) and attention (avadhāna) cannot serve as direct means on Bhairava's supreme path. Those who seek to discern reality with any means are "fools who make use of a firefly to perceive the sun" (TĀ 2/14).

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 2/8** (lines 1835-1859): *tatra tāvat kriyāyogo nābhyupāyatvam arhati | sa hi tasmāt samudbhūtaḥ pratyuta pravibhāvyate*
No practice associated with action can serve as a means; on the contrary, action is born from consciousness.

**TĀ 2/9** (lines 1892-1901): *jñaptāv upāya eva syād iti cej jñaptir ucyate | prakāśatvaṁ svaprakāśe tac ca tatrānyataḥ katham*
If action were the means to consciousness — consciousness is illumination, and that luminosity shines within what is self-luminous; how can it arise from anything else?

**TĀ 2/10** (lines 2042-2054): *saṁvitattvaṁ svaprakāśam ity asmin kiṅ nu yuktibhiḥ | tadabhāve bhaved viśvaṁ jaḍatvād aprakāśyakam*
What use are arguments to establish that consciousness is self-luminous? If it were not, everything would be unconscious and could not be made manifest.

**TĀ 2/11** (lines 2151-2163): *yāvān upāyo bāhyaḥ syād āntaro vāpi kaścana | sa sarvas tanmukhaprekṣī tatropāyatvabhāk katham*
All means whatsoever, external or internal, depend on consciousness — how can they serve as a means to it?

**TĀ 2/12** (lines 2224-2268): *tyajāvadhānāni nanu kva nāma dhatse 'vadhānaṁ vicinu svayaṁ tat | pūrṇe 'vadhānaṁ na hi nāma yuktaṁ nāpūrṇam abhyeti ca satyabhāvam*
Abandon attention! Where do you apply it? Attention to the full makes no sense; the not-full cannot attain authentic existence.

**TĀ 2/13** (lines 2377-2385): *tenāvadhānaprāṇasya bhāvanādeḥ pare pathi | bhairavīye kathaṅkāraṁ bhavet sākṣādupāyatā*
How can meditation, whose life is attention, serve as a direct means on Bhairava's supreme path?

**TĀ 2/14** (lines 2400-2406): *ye 'pi sākṣādupāyena tadrūpaṁ praviviñcate | nūnaṁ te sūryasaṅvittyai khadyotādhitsavo jaḍāḥ*
Even those discerning reality with the direct means are fools using a firefly to perceive the sun.

## 3. Jayaratha's Commentary

**On self-luminosity** (lines 1903-1918): "An insentient entity is such that its nature is not self-luminous; its light is that of another. What is sentient is self-luminous by nature. If it were illumined by another, it would be insentient."

**On attention** (lines 2362-2375): "Attention is a state of concentration grounded in a specific object. Thus it is based on duality and incapable of attaining oneness. Where even attention is useless, what to say of meditation?"

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
