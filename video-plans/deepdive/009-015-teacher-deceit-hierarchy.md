# Teacher: Deceit, Hierarchy, and the Adept

**Tantrāloka 23.44cd–103 | Lines 652–678**

## Overview

This section addresses the difficult realities of the teacher-disciple relationship: what to do when a disciple proves deceitful, the hierarchy of teachers, and the consecration into the state of an adept (sādhaka). The teacher has the power to remove knowledge from a deceitful disciple — an astonishing claim about the nature of knowledge as something that can be "withdrawn."

## Key Verses

- **44cd–64 (lines 652–653):** The removal of knowledge from a deceitful disciple (haraṇaṁ mateḥ) and the signs of his obscuration.
- **70–71 (lines 658–659):** Better to follow an inferior doctrine than to give up a superior one after receiving it.
- **72–75 (line 662):** Only the teacher who made the disciple fall can elevate him again.
- **76–77 (line 665):** A Trika master is the highest — if he dies, take refuge in another.
- **78–89ab (lines 667–668):** Disqualifying defects in teacher and disciple.
- **89cd–96 (lines 668–669):** Those who are graced may remove their own obscuration to some degree.
- **97–101ab (line 674):** The categories of deceitful teachers.
- **101cd–103ab (lines 677–678):** The consecration into the state of an adept (sādhakatva).

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Knowledge can be withdrawn:** The claim that a teacher can "remove knowledge" (haraṇaṁ mateḥ) from a deceitful disciple is paradoxical — how can knowledge, once known, be unknown? The answer lies in the nature of knowledge as participation in the teacher's consciousness. The teacher can withdraw his presence, and the disciple's understanding collapses.

2. **The hierarchy of teachers:** Not all teachers are equal. A Trika master who has realized non-duality is higher than any teacher who has not — regardless of lineage, fame, or apparent accomplishments.

3. **Self-remediation of obscuration:** Even a fallen or obscured disciple can recover through grace (śaktipāta). The descent of grace is not a one-time event but a continuous possibility. Śiva's freedom is not constrained by past mistakes.

4. **The state of an adept (sādhaka):** The consecration into sādhakatva is the empowerment to perform rituals for others. This is below the level of teacher (ācārya) but above the level of simple initiate (samayin).

## The Karmic Logic of Teaching

The teacher-disciple relationship is governed by subtle karmic bonds. Only the teacher who initiated a disciple and then saw him fall can redeem him — because the original initiation created an ontological connection that no other teacher can replicate.
