# Daily Worship and the Hymn to Inner Worship

**Tantrāloka 26.29cd–76 | Lines 740–768**

## Overview

The daily rite (nityavidhi) includes worship at the junctures of the day (sandhyopāsana), invocation of deities (āvāhana), libation (tarpaṇa), and the magnificent "Hymn to Inner Worship" (antarārca-stotra) — a poetic expression of the non-dual vision where all worship is recognized as the self-worship of consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **29cd–37ab (lines 742–744):** Worship at the three junctures of the day (sandhyā) — dawn, noon, and dusk.
- **37cd (line 744):** The regular worship on the sacrificial surface (sthaṇḍile).
- **37cd–51ab (lines 745–746):** Invocation (āvāhana) of the deity.
- **51cd–54ab (line 753):** Libation (tarpaṇa) to the deities, teachers, and Siddhas.
- **54cd–55ab (line 755):** Worship with whatever expands consciousness — the substance is secondary to the awareness.
- **55cd–57 (lines 756–759):** Rites are fixed for those who seek worldly benefits, not for those who aspire to liberation.
- **58–61 (lines 762–763):** Ritual action aimed at oneness (abheda), born of bliss (ānanda).
- **62cd–65 (lines 764–765):** Hymn to Inner Worship.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Sandhyā as thresholds:** The three junctures (sandhyā) are liminal times when the veil between worlds is thin. Dawn = creation (sṛṣṭi), noon = maintenance (sthiti), dusk = dissolution (saṃhāra). Each juncture corresponds to one of the three goddesses.

2. **Invocation = recognition:** "Āvāhana" (invocation) is usually understood as "calling" the deity to be present. In the Trika, it is redefined as the recognition that the deity is ALREADY present — invocation is acknowledging what has always been the case.

3. **Whatever expands consciousness:** The substance used for worship (wine, water, flowers, etc.) is less important than whether it expands consciousness (cittavistāraka). If a substance provokes contraction, it should not be used. If it provokes expansion, it is sacred.

4. **The Hymn to Inner Worship (verses 62cd–65):** This short hymn is one of Abhinavagupta's most beautiful compositions. It declares that the true worshipper, the act of worship, and the worshipped are one — the inner sacrifice is the self-recognition of consciousness.

## Freedom Within Form

The daily rite is both structured and free. The structure (sandhyā, āvāhana, tarpaṇa, etc.) provides a framework; the freedom is in the awareness with which each act is performed. For the liberation-seeker, the outer form may eventually be dropped — but for those still bound by residual karma, the form is the path.
