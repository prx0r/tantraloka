# OBSCURED AND UNOBSCURED CONSCIOUSNESS — Verses 134-139
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

Consciousness veils itself and becomes present on the plane of the insentient. It is half-veiled and half-revealed in all living beings from the gods down to plants. This is the mystery of manifestation: the one consciousness, free and unconditioned, voluntarily contracts into the limited forms of sentient and insentient existence. But even in its contracted state, consciousness never ceases to be consciousness — its objective appearance is a quality of itself, and its shadow cannot cover it.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.134-135ab — The Self-Veiling of Consciousness

**Line ~23677** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
so 'yam ātmānam āvṛtya sthito jaḍapadaṁ gataḥ |
āvṛtānāvṛtātmā tu devādisthāvarāntagaḥ || 134 ||
jaḍājaḍasyāpy etasya dvairūpyasyāṣṭi citratā |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"This (consciousness) veils itself, and having done so, is present on the plane of the insentient. Half-veiled and half-revealed, it is present (in living beings) starting from the gods right down to plants. Each of these two forms (of consciousness), sentient and insentient, is wonderfully diverse."

**Explanation:**
Consciousness is not limited by obscuration — it obscures itself. The entire range of existence, from the highest gods to the most insentient matter, is consciousness in varying degrees of self-veiling. The "half-veiled, half-revealed" state is the condition of all embodied beings.

#### Verse 1.135cd-136ab — Freedom of Consciousness

**Line ~23804** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tasya svatantrabhāvo hi kiṁ kiṁ yan na vicintayet || 135 ||
tad uktaṁ triśiraḥśāstre saṁbuddha iti veīti yaḥ |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"He (the Lord of Consciousness) is free. What may He not conceive? That is said in the Triśiraḥśāstra, (which declares that) one who knows (this wonderful diversity) is well awake (saṁbuddha)."

**Explanation:**
Because consciousness is free, it can conceive any form. The diversity of existence is not a limitation of consciousness but an expression of its freedom. The one who knows this is truly "awake" — he recognizes that all forms are the play of his own consciousness.

#### Verse 1.136cd-137 — Objectivity as a Quality of Consciousness

**Line ~23865** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
jñeyabhāvo hi ciddharmas tacchāyācchādayen na tām || 136 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Objectivity is a quality of consciousness (cit); its shadow cannot cover it."

**Explanation:**
This is the nondualist answer to the problem of the object world. Objectivity is not something outside consciousness — it is a quality (dharma) of consciousness itself. The world of objects is the "shadow" of consciousness, but a shadow cannot cover its source. Consciousness remains untouched by the objects that appear within it.

#### Verses 1.138-139 — The Conclusion of the Introduction

**Line ~23950+** (Vol. 1)

**Dyczkowski Translation (summary):**
Thus the introduction to the Tantrāloka concludes. The Lord of Consciousness, free and self-luminous, veils and reveals Himself through the entire range of existence. The means to realize Him are determined by the degree of this self-veiling. The following chapters will expound these means.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On the self-veiling (line ~23700+):** Jayaratha explains the process: "Initially, the Supreme Lord, wishing to create the universe, illumines it within Himself in such a way that it is not separate from Himself. This is the first creation (ādisarga). Subsequently, by His own innate freedom, He causes the endless series of manifestations of subject and object."

**On the variety of forms (line ~23780+):** The variety of insentient things is due to differing words and entities; the variety of sentient beings is due to the hierarchy of bondages.

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On ādisarga (note to verse 134, line ~23754+):** "Jayaratha explains that initially, the Lord who is pure consciousness, wishing to create the universe, illumines it within Himself in such a way that it is not separate and distinct from Him. This is the first creation."

### Philosophical Context

These verses conclude the introduction (upodghāta) to the Tantrāloka. They summarize the entire nondualist position: consciousness alone exists; it manifests as all things by its own freedom; the diversity of existence is not a fall but a play; obscuration is self-imposed and can be self-removed by recognition. The following chapters open with the exposition of the means to realization (upāya).

### Visual Concepts

1. **Self-Veiling Animation:** A luminous sphere gradually covered by translucent veils, yet still visible through them — representing the half-veiled half-revealed state.

2. **Shadow Cannot Cover Source:** A figure casting a shadow on a wall, but the shadow cannot cover the figure itself — illustrating verse 136.

3. **The Awakened One (Saṁbuddha):** A figure with open eyes, surrounded by diverse forms that emanate from and return to the figure's heart.

### Narration Script

The introduction to the Tantrāloka concludes with the teaching of obscured and unobscured consciousness. The one consciousness, by its own freedom, veils itself and becomes present as the entire range of existence — from the highest gods to insentient matter. Every being is consciousness half-veiled and half-revealed. But objectivity is a quality of consciousness itself — its shadow cannot cover it. Even in the most contracted state, the light of consciousness shines. The one who knows this is truly awake. The introduction has established the nature of bondage and liberation, the dynamics of knowledge and ignorance, and the structure of Śiva's freedom. With this foundation, Abhinava will now expound the means to realize this truth.
