# Śiva is All His Powers
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 156-160

---

### Core Teaching

Liberation is nothing but the full experience of one's own nature, and the nature of the Self is pure consciousness. Śiva, who is universal consciousness, is Himself all His powers — will, knowledge, action and the rest. These powers are not attributes of Śiva separate from Him; they are Śiva Himself, conceived differently according to their functions. Unlike the Vaiśeṣika view (Kaṇāda), where the Self is a substratum possessing qualities, in the Śaiva view, consciousness itself is the power of creative freedom.

---

### Liberation is the Experience of One's Own Nature (Verses 156-157)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
mokṣo hi nāma naivānyaḥ svarūpaprathanaṁ hi saḥ |
svarūpaṁ cātmanaḥ saṁvin nānyat tatra tu yāḥ punaḥ || 156 ||
kriyādikāḥ śaktayas tāḥ saṁvidrūpādhikā nahi |
asaṁvidrūpatāyogād dharmiṇaś cānirāpaṇāt || 157 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Liberation is nothing but the (full and complete) experience (prathana) of one's own nature. And the nature of the Self is (pure) consciousness. Indeed, there is nothing else there. Moreover, nor are those (various) powers of action and the rest other than the conscious nature, because, indeed, it is unreasonable (to maintain that anything else can exist) there that is not consciousness, and (also) because (the scriptures) do not describe (any underlying ground of these powers) that possesses (them as its) attributes. (In other words, Śiva, Who is universal consciousness, is Himself all His powers)."

---

### Refutation of the Substratum View (Verses 158-159)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
pārameśvaraśāstre hi na ca kāṇādadṛṣṭivat |
śaktīnāṁ dharmarūpāṇām āśrayaḥ ko 'pi kathyate || 158 ||

tataś ca dṛkkriyecchādyā bhinnāś cec chaktayas tathā |
ekaḥ śiva itīyaṁ vāg vastuśūnyaiva jāyate || 159 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Unlike Kaṇāda's view, in the Supreme Lord's scripture, there is not said to be any substratum of the powers (to which they are related) as its attributes (that are separate from it in themselves)." (158)

"Moreover, if the powers of will, knowledge, action and the rest were to be (independent and) separate from (their underlying ground), the statement (vāc) 'Śiva is one' would become devoid of (any) reality." (159)

Jayaratha: The Vaiśeṣikas maintain that the Self is the permanent substratum of sensations and volitions. Pleasure, pain, desire, aversion, the will, etc., are considered to be independently existing qualities of the Self. Here, by contrast, each power is the one absolutely free consciousness, differentiated by particular limiting adjuncts.

---

### Consciousness is Freedom (Verse 160)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
tasmāt saṁvittvam evaitat svātantryaṁ tat tad apy alam |
vivicyamānaṁ bahvīṣu paryavasyati śaktiṣu || 160 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Therefore, consciousness itself is this (power of creative) freedom, which (according to the measure in which) it is conceived (vivicyamāna) as this or that (power) results in many powers."

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary Insights

- The relationship between Śiva and His powers is not one of substance and attribute but of identity-in-difference
- Consciousness is supreme, transcendent and free; due to limiting adjuncts it appears as particular powers
- The powers arise when consciousness is conceived (vivicyamāna) in specific ways — the same freedom manifests as will, knowledge, and action
- Jayaratha quotes the earlier verse (1/69): "Just as there is no difference between fire's capacity to burn and the fire itself, similarly, what difference is there between the forms of God?"
- The doctrine of nonduality (advaya) would be refuted if powers were truly separate from Śiva

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Identity of Power and Possessor**: Śiva does not possess powers as a king possesses subjects; He is the powers themselves. The distinction is merely conceptual, not real.

2. **Freedom as the Essence**: Consciousness is not static awareness but dynamic freedom (svātantrya). This freedom is the power to create, sustain and withdraw the universe.

3. **Refutation of Dualism**: The Vaiśeṣika model of substance-attribute is rejected. Śiva's oneness would be compromised if powers were independent realities.

4. **Nondual Soteriology**: Because liberation is the experience of one's own nature as consciousness, and consciousness is all powers, liberation includes the fullness of all divine attributes.
