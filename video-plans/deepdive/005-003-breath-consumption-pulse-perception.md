# Deep-Dive: Breath Consumption, the Pulse of Perception, and the Eternity of Consciousness

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7, verses 21cd–38ab  
**Catalog Entry:** #284  
**Lines:** 1563–2676

---

## Topic Overview

This is the philosophical core of Chapter 7. Having established the proportional law of mantra cycles, Abhinava now explains why this practice leads to liberation. The key insight: breath and perception share the same pulsation (spanda) of consciousness. When the breath is consumed (prāṇagrāsa), time is consumed (kālasaṁkarṣaṇa), and consciousness stands revealed as one, full, and eternal.

---

## Key Verses

### Verses 21cd–22ab (lines 1573–1578)
> evaṁ prayatnasaṁruddhaprāṇacārasya yoginaḥ
> krameṇa prāṇacārasya grāsa evopajāyate
> *"In this way, the movement of the vital breath of the yogi whose breathing has been controlled with effort is progressively consumed (grāsa)."*

### Verses 22cd–23ab (lines 1711–1722)
> prāṇagrāsakramāvāptakālasaṁkarṣaṇasthitiḥ
> saṁvid ekaiva pūrṇā syāj jñānabhedavyapohanāt
> *"The state in which time is dragged into and consumed within consciousness obtains in consonance with the process by which the breath is consumed. Consciousness is one alone and perfectly full, by setting aside the relative distinctions of discursive conceptual knowledge."*

### Verses 23cd–24ab (lines 1726–1737)
> tathā hi prāṇacārasya navasyānudaye sati
> na kālabhedajanito jñānabhedaḥ prakalpate
> *"When a new movement of the breath does not arise, the duality of knowledge generated by temporal distinctions is no longer conceived."*

### Verse 24cd (lines 1777–1782)
> saṁvedyabhedānna jñānaṁ bhinnaṁ śikharivrttavat
> *"Perception is not divided by the multiplicity of the objects of knowledge, as is the case with a person standing on the peak of a mountain who sees a town below as a single undivided whole."*

### Verse 25ab (lines 1895–1899)
> kālas tu bhedakas tasya sa tu sūkṣmaḥ kṣaṇo mataḥ
> *"It is time that divides up perception, and that in its most subtle form is considered to be an instant (kṣaṇa)."*

### Verse 25cd (lines 1907–1913)
> saukṣmyasya cāvadhir jñānaṁ yāvat tiṣṭhati sa kṣaṇaḥ
> *"The limit of temporal subtlety is 'the instant', which is the duration of a moment of perception."*

### Verse 28 (lines 2107–2158)
> sā ced udayate spandamayī tatprāṇagā dhruvam
> bhaved eva tataḥ prāṇaspandābhāve na sā bhavet
> *"If this union and separation which is the pulsation of consciousness arises, it must certainly be present within the movement of the vital breath, and so, were the pulse of the vital breath not to exist, that also would not."*

### Verses 29–31 (lines 2200–2340): Consciousness survives the cessation of breath
> *"The absence of that does not entail the absence of consciousness. That remains just as it is, the same cognitive consciousness... In reality, time does not even touch consciousness."*

### Verses 30cd–31ab (lines 2306–2318)
> ata ekaiva saṁvittir nānārūpe tathā tathā
> vindānā nirvikalpāpi vikalpo bhāvagocare
> *"Thus, the one consciousness, knowing in this way and that the multiple form of the objects of knowledge, although essentially devoid of ideation, is itself ideation within the sphere of phenomenal existence."*

### Verses 31cd–32ab (lines 2333–2357)
> spandāntaraṁ na yāvat tad uditaṁ tāvad eva saḥ
> tāvān eko vikalpaḥ syād vividhaṁ vastu kalpayan
> *"Until another pulse of cognitive consciousness arises, that representational ideation abides alone as a single unified whole, conceiving the diversity of manifest reality."*

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **Prāṇagrāsa = Kālasaṁkarṣaṇa:** The consumption of the breath is simultaneously the consumption of time. Breath and time are co-extensive — when one ceases, both cease.

2. **Two Phases of Perception:**
   - **Nirvikalpa (nonconceptual):** Immediate, direct, undivided — the first moment
   - **Vikalpa (determinate):** Conceptual labeling, temporal succession — the second moment
   - The gap between pulses of perception is consciousness itself

3. **Time as the Divisor of Perception:** Objects of knowledge do not divide consciousness — time does. Each perception is a whole unto itself, bounded by its arising and ceasing.

4. **The Pulse (Spanda) of Consciousness:** The union and separation of senses, object, and perceiver is the pulsation of consciousness itself, which is present within the vital breath.

5. **Consciousness Beyond Time:** When breath ceases, time ceases, and consciousness stands revealed as untouched by temporal distinctions — neither eternal nor non-eternal in conventional terms.

6. **Refutation of Buddhist Momentariness (verses 32–36):** Abhinava argues that Buddhist momentariness cannot account for unified perception or daily commerce (vyavahāra).

---

## Swami Lakshmanjoo's Explanation (lines 1606–1701)

The progressive stages of cakrodaya involve the piercing (vedha) of six cakras — from mūlādhāra through navel, heart, throat, palate, and ājñā — until the eight yogic powers arise and the yogi abides in the Supreme Abode.
