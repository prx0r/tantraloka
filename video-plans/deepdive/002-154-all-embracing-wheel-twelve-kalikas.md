# THE ALL-EMBRACING WHEEL OF THE TWELVE KĀLIKĀS
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 2, Chapter 3, Verses 250cd–259ab

---

### Core Teaching

The twelve forms of reflective awareness that emerge from the six root vowels (A, Ā, I, Ī, U, Ū) conjoined with the power of action (E, AI, O, AU, Bindu, Visarga) constitute the ultimate Wheel of Energies (*mukhyaśakticakra*) — the Twelve Kālikās. These are not merely phonetic categories but the fundamental phases of consciousness itself, encompassing all cognitive acts. From these twelve alone emerge every possible configuration of energy, from the simplest wheel of one spoke to the thousand-spoked wheel. Abhinavagupta identifies them with the central teaching of the Krama system, integrating them directly into his Trika synthesis. The Goddess Parā, the one syllable SAUḤ, is at the center of this wheel, surrounded by the twelve as her Yoginīs.

**Line references:** TĀ 3/250cd–259ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 373–381, lines 27007–27511.

---

### The Emergence of the Twelve (Verses 250cd–251ab)

**Line references:** 27007–27135.

The six root vowels arise as the powers of will (*icchāśakti*, A), sovereignty (*aiśvarya*, Ā), knowledge (*jñāna*, I and Ī), and the subtle states of subjectivity (U and Ū). Their complementary counterparts — the diphthongs E, AI, O, AU together with Bindu (the Point) and Visarga (Emission) — collectively constitute the power of action (*kriyāśakti*). Because the power of action contains and presupposes the powers of will and knowledge, these six emerge *from* the six root vowels, penetrating one another to become twelve.

**Sanskrit (250cd–251ab):**
```
āsām eva samāveśāt kriyāśaktitayoditāt |
saṁvido dvādaśa proktā yāsu sarvaṁ samāpyate ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "These (six forms of) consciousness emerge as the power of action, and so, by penetrating (*samāveśa*) (into one another), are said to be twelve forms of consciousness. Within them everything is concluded (and contained)."

Jayaratha explains that these twelve forms of consciousness, "arranged within the supreme subject as the twelve phases of the process of perception, are absorbed progressively into consciousness." Because there is no reality apart from consciousness, everything is brought to completion and satisfaction within them.

### The Principal Wheel of Energies (Verses 251cd–253ab)

**Line references:** 27136–27194.

These twelve constitute the God of gods' principal Wheel of Energies:

**Sanskrit (251cd–252ab):**
```
etāvad devadevasya mukhyaṁ tac chakticakrakam |
etāvatā devadevaḥ pūrṇaśaktiḥ sa bhairavaḥ ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Such is the God of the gods' principal Wheel of Energies, by virtue of which the God of the gods is Bhairava, Who is endowed with all power (*pūrṇaśakti*)."

The name *Kālikā* is derived from the root *kal*, which carries multiple meanings: resounding, projecting, emitting, enumerating, and knowing. Jayaratha explains:

"Thus, in accord with the meanings of the root, the Kālīs, that is, those very Kālikās ('Little Kālīs') 'project' (*kalayanti*), that is, reflect (*parāmṛśanti*), 'emit' (*kṣipanti*), that is, create all things by emitting them out of themselves (*visṛjanti*), and withdraw them back (*saṁharanti*), that is, 'enumerate' them (*gaṇayanti*)."

**Sanskrit (252cd–253ab):**
```
parāmarśātmakatvena visargākṣepayogataḥ |
iyattākalanāj jñānāt tāḥ proktāḥ kālikāḥ kvacit ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "These (powers) are in some places (in the scriptures) called Kālikās, because their nature is reflective awareness, and because they project, emit, enumerate and know (all things)."

### The Central Goddess and Her Twelve Yoginīs (Verses 253cd–254ab)

**Line references:** 27194–27350.

The Trikasāra scripture describes the goddess Parā as the one syllable SAUḤ, Bhairava's own Self, surrounded by twelve Yoginīs:

**Sanskrit (253cd–254ab):**
```
śrīsāraśāstre cāpy uktaṁ madhya ekākṣarāṁ parām |
pūjayed bhairavātmākhyāṁ yoginīdvādaśāvṛtām ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "It is also said in the venerable Sāra scripture that one should worship the (Goddess) Parā, Who is the one syllable (SAUḤ), and is called Bhairava's Self, surrounded by twelve Yoginīs."

Jayaratha notes that the difference between 'Kālikās' and 'Yoginīs' is merely a difference in name, not in reality. These twelve forms of consciousness are essentially each of the twelve forms of reflective awareness corresponding to the twelve fertile vowels (omitting the four neuter vowels ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ). In the Trikasāra's ritual: "That seed is conjoined with the twelve vowels, in due order, and these are in this way the goddesses."

### The Wheels of Energy: From One to a Thousand Spokes (Verses 254cd–257ab)

**Line references:** 27350–27416.

From these twelve energies alone emerges every possible configuration of the wheel of energies:

**Sanskrit (254cd–257ab):**
```
tābhya eva catuhṣaṣṭiparyantaṁ śakticakrakam |
ekārataḥ samārabhya sahasrāraṁ pravarṭate ||
tāsāṁ ca kṛtyabhedena nāmāni bahudhāgame |
upāsāś ca dvayādvaitavyāmiśrākārayogataḥ ||
śrīmattraiśirase tac ca kathitaṁ vistarād bahu |
iha no likhitaṁ vyāsabhayāc cānupayogataḥ ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "From (just) these (twelve energies) alone emerges (every) wheel of energies, right up (to the full complement of) sixty-four. (So too the wheel) with one spoke up to that of a thousand spokes. They have many names in the scriptures, according to their various functions. The ways they are worshipped are also various, according to whether the form of their worship is dual, nondual or mixed. This has been explained at length in the venerable Traiśirasa (Triśirobhairavatantra), but not written here for fear of going into too many details, and also because it would serve no purpose to do so."

The twelve Kālikās are thus the fundamental matrix from which all energy configurations — from the simplest unity to the most complex thousand-fold manifestation — are derived.

### The Threefold Nature: Aghorā, Ghorā, Ghoratarā (Verses 257cd–259ab)

**Line references:** 27416–27511.

Abhinava integrates the Twelve Kālīs with the Trika goddesses by classifying them into three types — Aghorā (Not Fierce), Ghorā (Fierce), and Ghoratarā (Most Fierce) — each operating in the four phases of creation, persistence, withdrawal, and the unconditioned fourth:

**Sanskrit (257cd–259ab):**
```
tā eva nirmalāḥ śuddhā aghorāḥ parikīrtitāḥ |
ghoraghoratarāṇāṁ tu sotṛtvāc ca tadātmikāḥ ||
sṛṣṭau sthitau ca saṁhāre tadupādhitrayātyaye |
tāsām evaṁ sthitaṁ rūpaṁ bahudhā pravibhajyate ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Those same (powers), stainless and pure, are called Aghorā. They are established in creation, persistence and withdrawal, as well as in the passing away of these three limiting adjuncts, by generating the (powers) Ghorā and Ghoratarā, that are of their (same) nature. Their nature, which abides thus, is divided in many ways."

Jayaratha explains: the three types of energies operating in the four phases become twelve. As there are innumerable energies of each type, they generate countless energies in this way. The "passing away of the three limiting adjuncts" uncovers the state of pure unconditioned consciousness called the Inexplicable (*Anākhya*), which is the source and resting place of the entire cycle.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Primacy of the Twelve**: All wheels of energy, from one to sixty-four to a thousand spokes, derive from the twelve fundamental Kālikās. They are the irreducible phases of cognitive consciousness.

2. **Krama-Trika Synthesis**: By identifying the Twelve Kālīs — a distinctively Krama teaching — with the Trika goddess Parā and her Yoginīs, Abhinava demonstrates the unity of these traditions. The Krama system is not a foreign element but the very heart of his Anuttara Trika.

3. **The Vowel Matrix**: The twelve fertile vowels are not arbitrary symbols but the phonetic embodiment of the phases of consciousness. The four neuter vowels (ṛ, ṝ, ḷ, ḹ) are excluded because they represent states of arrested or introverted awareness that do not participate in the cognitive-emission cycle.

4. **The Inexplicable as Ground**: The threefold operation of creation, persistence, and withdrawal is grounded in the fourth — the Inexplicable (*anākhya*) — which is the unconditioned consciousness that both generates and consumes the cycle.

5. **SAUḤ as Center**: The seed-syllable SAUḤ — S (being) + AU (Śiva-Śakti union) + Ḥ (visarga, emission) — is the supreme mantra of Parā, the central goddess around whom the twelve Kālikās revolve as her attending Yoginīs.

### Connection to Chapter Four

As Jayaratha notes, the Twelve Kālīs "will be discussed at length in the fourth chapter dealing with the Empowered Means." There, they appear as the *Kālīcakra* — the Wheel of the Twelve Kālīs — governing the spheres of objectivity, means of knowledge, and subjectivity. The present treatment in Chapter Three serves as the doctrinal foundation, establishing the Kālīs as the energies of reflective awareness within the Supreme Means, while Chapter Four develops their practical application within the Empowered Means.
