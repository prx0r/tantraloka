# Topic 86: The Light is Undefinable Anuttara Beyond Deity, Meditation and Ritual

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 86 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: 23cd-33ab

---

## 1. Core Teaching

The Light of No Means consciousness is neither power (śakti) nor possessor of power (śaktimān), neither object of meditation nor meditator, neither object of worship nor worshipper, neither mantra, object of mantra, nor mantrin. It is not initiation, the initiator, or the initiate. It cannot be defined by any of the four ontological alternatives (catuṣkoṭi): existence, non-existence, both, or neither. It is Anuttara — the Absolute that is no "thing" at all.

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 2/23cd-26ab** (lines 3039-3057): *naiṣa śaktir mahādevī na paratrāśrito yataḥ || na caiṣa śaktimān devo na kasyāpy āśrayo yataḥ | naiṣa dhyeyo dhyātrabhāvān na dhyātā dhyeyavarjanāt || na pūjyaḥ pūjakābhāvāt pūjyābhāvān na pūjakaḥ | na mantro na ca mantryo 'sau na ca mantrayitā prabhuḥ || na dīkṣā dīkṣako vāpi na dīkṣāvān maheśvaraḥ*
This is not power, the Great Goddess, nor the possessor of power. It is not meditator or object of meditation, not worshipper or worshipped, not mantra, mantrin, or object of mantra, not initiation, initiator, or initiate.

**TĀ 2/27cd-29ab** (lines 3184-3209): *na san na cāsat sadasan na ca tan nobhayojjhitam || durvijñeyā hi sāvasthā kim apy etad anuttaram*
Neither existence nor non-existence, neither both nor neither — that state is hard to know. What could this Anuttara be?

**TĀ 2/29cd-30ab** (lines 3333-3357): *asattvaṁ cāprakāśatvaṁ na kutrāpy upayogitā || viśvasya jīvitaṁ satyaṁ prakāśaikātmakaś ca saḥ*
What does not exist and does not manifest is of no use anywhere. Real existence is the life of the universe, one with the Light.

## 3. Jayaratha's Commentary

**On the rejection of ritual categories** (lines 3061-3100): "Power exists having associated itself with something else — the possessor of power. This Light is one because it does not depend on anything else to generate duality... The same applies to meditation, mantra, initiation — all depend on duality between agent and object."

## 4. Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the four ontological alternatives** (lines 3234-3248): "The four possible ontological alternatives (catuṣkoṭi) define every phenomenal entity. Nāgārjuna used this to show all things are empty. Abhinava understands that only the Absolute is indefinable in these terms. It cannot be defined as some particular 'thing', for that would imply relative distinction.

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
