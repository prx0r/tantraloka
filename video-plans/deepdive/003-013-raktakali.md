# RAKTAKĀLĪ — THE RED KĀLĪ OF ATTACHMENT AND PERSISTENCE
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 149–150a

---

### Core Teaching

Raktakālī is the second of the Twelve Kālīs, governing the phase in which objectivity persists through sensory attachment. After the initial impulse of emanation (Sṛṣṭikālī), consciousness becomes "extroverted" (*bahirmukhī*) and "colours" (*rañjanā*) the aspects of reality through the circle of its sensory functions. The word *rakta* means both "red" and "attached" — this Kālī is the goddess of sensory delight, passion, and the aesthetic coloring that makes objects persist in awareness. She is the phase where the perceiver becomes engaged with objects through the senses, and the pleasure of this engagement sustains the objective world.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/149–150a; Dyczkowski translation pp. 204–209, lines 15058–15378.

---

### Key Verse

**Sanskrit:**
```
tathābhāsitavastvaṁśarañjanāṁ sā bahirmukhī |
svavṛtticakreṇa samaṁ tato 'pi kalayanty alam |
sthitir eṣaiva bhāvasya || 149–150a ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 15060–15074):** "Then that (same consciousness) is extroverted, and capable of doing so (*alam*), activates (a sense of delight that) colours (*rañjanā*) the (various) aspects of reality made manifest in this way, together with the circle of its own (sensory) functions. This alone is the state of persistence of (phenomenal) existence."

---

### The Mechanism of Sensory Coloring

Jayaratha explains (lines 15076–15210): After Sṛṣṭikālī, "that supreme consciousness itself, at one with the subject, is extroverted; that is, rests on the plane of the means of knowledge by (the power of) its own (innate) freedom." The "circle of its own (sensorial) functions" is "the perception of form and the rest associated with the eyes etc."

The process unfolds as follows (lines 15121–15183):

1. Consciousness activates the senses, which perceive their objects (form, sound, etc.)
2. The senses are "coloured" by their objects — the means of knowledge takes on the quality of the object
3. Through the means, the subject also becomes "coloured" by the object of knowledge

Crucially, this coloring takes place "in the modality of perception which is free of thought constructs (*avikalpavṛttyā*)" — it is a generic, aesthetic delight before conceptualization. The activation (*kalayati*) here corresponds to the second meaning of √kal: "knowledge" (line 15180).

---

### The Meaning of "Red"

The word *rakta* has three overlapping meanings (lines 15223–15230):

1. **Red** — the colour of blood and passion
2. **Attached** — the emotional engagement with objects
3. **Coloured** — from *rañjana*, meaning "to make manifest" through aesthetic delight

Thus Raktakālī is the Kālī "Who governs and makes manifest the attachment to the objects of the senses, due to which we pay attention to them, so that their manifestation persists within the field of the senses."

---

### Swami Lakshmanjoo's Commentary

Swami Lakshmanjoo explains (lines 15090–15115): When the same supreme consciousness becomes "propense to perceive phenomena such as a jar or a cloth and becomes extroverted, it enters the state of persistence." The goddess is "coloured (*rakta*), that is, delighted by the aggregate of the objects of the senses."

"When objects are perceived, this supreme goddess experiences the (pleasing) attachment (*rakti*), which is the enjoyment of the objects of the senses, as a state free of thought constructs."

He quotes Utpaladeva (Śivastotrāvalī 13/8): "May we be mad with inebriation even as we drink constantly from the goblets of all phenomena, the alchemical potion which is the wine of your worship through the mouth of each one of the senses."

---

### Scriptural Quotations

**Devīpañcaśatika (DP 5/29–30ab, lines 15212–15216):**
"That energy which has arisen inwardly, that cannot be grasped by the eye, nor is it present in any of the senses, and (although) devoid of (phenomenal) qualities and free of ego, colours (with attachment the entire) circle of the universe, should be known to be Raktakālikā."

**Kramastotra (lines 15219–15277):**
"I bow to Raktakālī (the Red Kālī) Whose form is all things, She Who is reddened (with passion) when the (period of cosmic) dissolution ends (and creation begins), She Who is attached to drinking the essence (*rasa*) of the blood of the most excellent Heroes, who offer it with great delight to the Circle of the Mothers."

---

### The Blood Offering and the Senses

Jayaratha interprets the reference to blood esoterically (lines 15287–15342): The "most excellent Heroes" (*vīra*) are the senses, which unite with their objects. The "essence of their blood" is "the reflective awareness which is the essence of the energies of the senses." This is offered "to the Circle of Mothers" — the goddesses of the senses. Raktakālī is thus "the modality of the reflective awareness of consciousness that is engaged in sensory perception and, 'passionately attached to it,' ultimately receives this offering."

---

### The Discrepancy in Order

The sequence of the Kālīs differs between texts. In the Devīpañcaśatika and Kramastotra, Raktakālī is the *fourth* Kālī (after Sṛṣṭi, Sthiti, and Saṁhāra). In the Tantrāloka, she is placed *second* — after Sṛṣṭi and before Sthitināśa. Jayaratha explains (lines 15457–15500): "In order to hide (the correct) sequence of consciousness stated in the scripture, it is taught in a laconic and disordered way." Abhinavagupta has reordered the Kālīs to conceal the ritual procedure (*pūjākrama*) while revealing the sequence of consciousness (*saṁvitkrama*).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Sensory Delight is Spiritual**: The engagement of the senses with their objects is not a fall from grace but a phase of consciousness. The "inebriation" of sensory experience is the wine of the goddess's worship.

2. **Persistence Through Attachment**: Objects persist in consciousness not because of their intrinsic reality but because the perceiver is attached to them. Raktakālī reveals that the "binding" force of attachment is also the means by which the world is experienced as real.

3. **Aesthetic Consciousness**: The term *rañjana* (coloring) links this Kālī to the aesthetic theories of Abhinavagupta. The delight of sensory experience is a form of *rasa* — aesthetic relish — which is itself a manifestation of consciousness.
