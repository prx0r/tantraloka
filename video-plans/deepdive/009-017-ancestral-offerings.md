# Ancestral Offerings (Śrāddhakḷpti)

**Tantrāloka 25.1–29 | Lines 698–724**

## Overview

The fashioning of ancestral offerings (śrāddhakḷpti) is the Trika reinterpretation of the ancient Vedic rite of feeding the ancestors. Abhinavagupta transforms it from a ritual of propitiation into a meditation on the nature of consciousness beyond death. The ancestor is not a hungry ghost to be fed but a manifestation of Śiva to be recognized.

## Key Verses

- **2cd–4ab (line 701):** The purpose of the rite and the times when it should be performed.
- **4cd–9 (lines 704–707):** Identifying the fettered aspect with the experiencer — thereby becoming Śiva.
- **10 (line 709):** No procedure is necessary for the deceased who already knows reality.
- **11–12cd (lines 713–715):** The days of the teacher's enlightenment and birth are sacred days (parvan).
- **13–14ab, 19ab–21ab (lines 718–719):** The procedure for the channels of the breath (nāḍīvidhi).
- **14cd–18 (lines 719–720):** The worship of the assembly when performing śrāddha for the ancestors.
- **21cd–29 (lines 723–724):** The bestowal of worldly benefits (bhoga) and liberation (mokṣa).

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The ancestor is oneself:** The "ancestor" (pitṛ) to whom offerings are made is not an external being but a dimension of one's own consciousness — the contracted aspect that experiences mortality. By making the offering, one liberates that aspect within oneself.

2. **Identifying with the experiencer:** The key practice (4cd–9) is to identify the fettered, mortal aspect of oneself with the eternal witness (pramātṛ). When this identification is made, one becomes Śiva — and the ancestor is simultaneously liberated.

3. **The teacher's days as sacred:** The day of the teacher's enlightenment and his birth are more sacred than any calendrical holiday. They are "parvan" — junctures of power where the veil between worlds is thin.

4. **Nāḍīvidhi — the channel procedure:** The offering to ancestors is made through the channels of the breath (nāḍī). The practitioner offers the fruits of his prāṇāyāma to the ancestors, transforming them into Śiva through the energy of his life-breath.

## No Rite for the Knower

Those who have realized the truth do not need to perform śrāddha — not because they neglect their ancestors but because they already see that all beings, including the departed, are already one with Śiva. Their very state of being IS the offering.
