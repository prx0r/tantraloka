# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Common Auspicious Days According to the Haiḍaratantra

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 10, Chapter 28, Verses 25cd-35 (~lines 2070-2270)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
After Abhinava's innovative phenomenological analysis, he grounds his teaching in scripture — specifically the Haiḍaratantra. This source text provides the authoritative framework for the six kinds of parvans, from ordinary-ordinary to special-special, each with specific ritual fruits. The hierarchy of parvans mirrors the hierarchy of spiritual competence.

## Part 1: The Haiḍara's Sixfold Classification (0:45-3:00)
- Verses 25cd-31ab (~line 2131): The Haiḍara's teaching on equality in the Kula
- Verse 26: "yasya yad dhr̥daye devi vartate daiśikājñayā | mantro yogaḥ kramaś caiva pūjanāt siddhido bhavet"
- Whatever mantra, yoga, and liturgy is present in a disciple's heart at the teacher's command becomes accomplished through worship on these days
- Verse 27: Kulācāra (Kaula conduct) is necessary for worship aimed at siddhi and liberation
- Verse 30: All rites — including śaktiyāga, subjugation, attraction, and killing — are effortlessly accomplished on parvan days
- Six kinds: sāmānya-sāmānya (ordinary-ordinary), sāmānya-viśeṣa (ordinary-special), and so on up to viśeṣa-viśeṣa (special-special)

## Part 2: The First Two Grades of Parvans (3:00-5:30)
- Verses 31-33 (~line 2194):
  - The first day and fifth day of the month are called "śrīdina" (glorious days)
  - Ordinary-ordinary (sāmānya-sāmānya): the 4th, 8th, end of the world (14th?) — both fortnights
- Verses 34-35:
  - Ordinary-special (sāmānya-viśeṣa): specific days for specific purposes
- Jayaratha's commentary elaborates on the gradation:
  - The most common days are accessible to all initiates
  - The special days require greater purity and competence
- The gradation is not just external — it corresponds to the depth of the practitioner's realization

## Part 3: The Monthly Cycle of Special Parvans (5:30-7:30)
- Eleven parvans are listed for each month except Aśvayuja
- The sequence covers the entire year, mapping specific lunar days to specific ritual purposes
- Each month's parvan has a distinct presiding deity and specific fruit
- The alignment of lunar day (tithi) with lunar asterism (nakṣatra) and weekday (vāra) creates windows of maximum ritual efficacy
- These are the "special" (viśeṣa) parvans, as distinct from the common ones
- Jayaratha quotes the Haiḍara extensively here, providing the missing scriptural background

## Part 4: The Inner Meaning — Parvans in the Breath (7:30-9:30)
- Abhinava constantly relates external time to the inner breath cycle (from Chapter 6)
- The 14th lunar day corresponds to the pause at the end of exhalation
- The 8th corresponds to the pause at the end of inhalation
- The full moon corresponds to the fullness of the breath at the heart center
- Worship on these days is meaningful only if one also attends to the inner parvan in the breath
- The six grades of parvans correspond to six levels of yogic attainment:
  1. Ordinary: waking consciousness
  2. Special: dream consciousness
  3. More special: deep sleep
  4. Ordinary-special: the fourth state (turiya)
  5. Special-ordinary: turiyātīta (beyond the fourth)
  6. Special-special: absolute Bhairava consciousness

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The Haiḍara's classification reveals that Tantric ritual is not a one-size-fits-all system. Different times suit different practitioners, and even within the same tradition, there is a graded path. The next video explores the even more special parvans and the crucial question: is the lunar day or the solar day more important?
