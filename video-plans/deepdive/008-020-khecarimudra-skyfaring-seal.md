# Khecarīmudrā: The Seal of the Skyfaring Goddess

**Tantrāloka 15.361–362 | Lines 568–573**

## Overview

Khecarīmudrā — the "Seal of the Skyfaring Goddess" — is the yogic practice par excellence of the Trika. It is mentioned only briefly in Volume 8 (two verses) but is developed extensively in Chapter 32 of the Tantrāloka. It is the mudrā that closes the door to samsāra and opens the door to the void of supreme consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **361–362 (lines 569–571):** *khecarī mudrayā devi sarvān saṁśodhya mārgaṇān / brahmāṇḍe yāty anāyāsaṁ paraṁ tattvaṁ vicakṣaṇaḥ* — "By the Seal of the Skyfaring Goddess, having purified all paths, the wise one goes effortlessly through the cosmic egg to the supreme principle."

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Khe = void, carī = moving:** Khecarī means "she who moves in the void (kha)." The void is not nothingness but the empty space of consciousness — the womb (yoni) from which all manifestation arises and into which it dissolves.

2. **The seal that seals:** Mudrā means "seal" — it seals the practitioner's awareness into the supreme, preventing leakage into objective consciousness. It is the final "closure" (vimudraṇa) that locks the door of the senses.

3. **Skyfaring = freedom from support:** Just as a bird flies in the sky without any support, the yogī in Khecarīmudrā abides in consciousness without any support (nirālamba). This is the state of the jīvanmukta — liberated while living.

4. **The tongue and the palate:** In its physical form, Khecarīmudrā involves turning the tongue back to seal the upper palate. This blocks the flow of breath and redirects prāṇa into the central channel (susumṇā). But the true Khecarīmudrā is the mental state of resting in the void.

## Khecarī as the Supreme Mudrā

Khecarīmudrā is the "king of mudrās" because it directly accomplishes what all other practices aim at — the cessation of mental fluctuation (cittavṛtti-nirodha) and the establishment in one's true nature (svarūpa-sthāna). It is both the means and the end.
