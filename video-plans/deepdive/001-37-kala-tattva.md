# KĀLA TATTVA — The Principle of Time
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Kāla tattva is the 30th principle, the first of the five *kañcukas* (coverings) that emanate from Māyā. It is the principle of time — the power that fragments the eternal now of Śiva-consciousness into past, present, and future. In the nondual view, time is not an independent dimension but a contraction of the Lord's eternal presence (*nitya-samvit*). Jayaratha explains that Kāla, along with the other kañcukas, arises to obscure the universal I-consciousness of the bound soul, creating the experience of sequence, change, and finitude.

---

### Key Verse — The Five Principles of Limitation (TĀ 1.189cd)

**Sanskrit:**
```
aśuddhiśuddhyamānatvaśuddhitastu mitho 'pi tat || 189 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation on the Impure Path (Vol. 1, note ~28880):**
"The six above the individual soul are related to its limitations. These are its capacity for limited action (kalā) and knowledge (vidyā), attachment (rāga), time (kāla) and the law of causal and karmic necessity (niyati). They are considered to be aspects or energies of Māyā."

**Explanation:**
Although not named directly in TĀ 1.189, the kañcukas are the impure principles — fragments of the one I-consciousness. Kāla is the first of these, the segmentation of the eternal into moments.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On the kañcukas (Vol. 5, line ~21667): Jayaratha quotes the Svacchandatantra: "A group of five principles (Kāla, Niyati, Rāga, Vidyā, Kalā) is called an 'obscuring covering' (kañcuka). Each has arisen separately (for each soul) and is present there along with the individual soul's impressions."

Kāla is the principle that makes the soul experience succession — the feeling of time passing, of aging, of delay between desire and fulfillment.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Time as Contraction**: The eternal *nityaprakāśa* (ever-shining light) of Śiva is without sequence. Kāla tattva contracts this into serial time — past, present, future. The soul under Kāla experiences life as a timeline rather than an eternal now.

2. **First Kañcuka**: Time is the most fundamental limitation. Before the soul can experience limited agency (Kalā) or knowledge (Vidyā), it must first be placed within a temporal framework. Kāla is thus the first obscuration of the universal consciousness.

3. **Transcendence in Practice**: The Krama system explicitly works with Kāla — the goddess Kālasaṃkarṣiṇī (the Destroyer of Time) consumes temporal succession, returning the yogi to the eternal present. The twelve Kālīs are the phases of time reabsorbed into consciousness.
