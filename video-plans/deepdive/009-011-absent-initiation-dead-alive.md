# The Absent Initiation: For the Dead and the Faraway

**Tantrāloka 21.1–24 | Lines 566–582**

## Overview

The initiation of those absent (pārokṣīdīkṣā) is for persons who are not physically present — whether alive but far away, or already dead. The teacher can initiate an absent person through a simulacrum (a drawing, a doll, or a bundle of cloth) that stands in for the disciple.

## Key Verses

- **2–5 (lines 569–571):** The procedure for the dead or living absent person.
- **6–15 (lines 571–575):** Those to whom this rescue initiation can be imparted — even those who have died without initiation.
- **16–18ab (lines 575–576):** The media through which the absent disciple can be made present — a drawing, a effigy, the mind alone.
- **22cd–24 (lines 576–577):** Placing the absent disciple in a simulacrum — the teacher consecrates an object as the "body" of the absent person.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Presence is not physical proximity:** The teacher's consciousness, when identified with Śiva, transcends space and time. The "presence" required for initiation is not the presence of the physical body but the presence of the teacher's intent (saṅkalpa) and the disciple's receptivity (which continues after death in a subtle form).

2. **Rescue of the dead:** The most remarkable claim is that the dead can be initiated. This is based on the understanding that the soul (puruṣa) survives death and continues in a subtle body (liṅgaśarīra). If that soul can be reached through mantra and directed to Śiva, it can still be liberated.

3. **The simulacrum as the body of mantra:** An image or object becomes the "body" (vigraha) of the absent person through the power of the teacher's deposition (nyāsa). The teacher deposits the paths onto the simulacrum as he would onto a living body.

4. **The net teaching (jālopadeśa):** The "net" is the web of mantras that the teacher casts to catch the wandering soul. Like a fisherman's net, it gathers the soul from wherever it has strayed.

## Universal Salvation

The pārokṣīdīkṣā represents the ultimate extension of the Trika's soteriological optimism. No soul is beyond reach — not the dead, not the faraway, not even those who died without any spiritual practice. Śiva's grace, channeled through the teacher, can reach anyone, anywhere, anytime.
