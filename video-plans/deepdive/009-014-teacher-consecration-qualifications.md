# Teacher Consecration: Qualifications and the True Guru

**Tantrāloka 23.1–44ab | Lines 622–650**

## Overview

The consecration of a teacher (abhiṣecana) is the formal empowerment of a qualified initiate to transmit the Trika teachings. Abhinavagupta provides an extensive list of qualifications required of a teacher — and an equally extensive list of defects that disqualify one. The true teacher is not made by ritual alone but by the perfection of knowledge.

## Key Verses

- **2–7 (lines 625–626):** Qualities required of a teacher — knowledge, compassion, mastery of mantra, purity, patience.
- **8–10 (lines 627–628):** Defects of a false teacher — anger, greed, lust, inconsistency with scripture.
- **11–12ab (line 629):** The true teacher — one who has realized his identity with Śiva.
- **13cd–16ab (lines 630–631):** Teachers that should be avoided.
- **17cd–20ab (lines 635–636):** The creation of a teacher (ācāryakaraṇam) — the actual consecration rite.
- **20cd–22ab (lines 637–638):** Knowledge is the sole sign of initiation — not birth, not external marks, not office.
- **22cd–23ab (lines 639–640):** The seven sacrifices — seven forms of worship the teacher must perform.
- **31–32 (lines 646–647):** The teacher's mantra observance (tadvratam).
- **33–44ab (lines 649–650):** The yoga of the vow of the observance of the Vidyā.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Knowledge, not office, makes a teacher:** The most radical claim of this chapter: "Knowledge is the sole sign of initiation" (jñānaṁ liṅgam). A person may have all the ritual qualifications, external marks, and lineage credentials — but without the direct realization of non-duality, he is not a teacher.

2. **The seven sacrifices:** The teacher must perform seven kinds of sacrifice — to the deities, to the teacher, to the scriptures, to the assembly of initiates, to the ancestors, to the elements, and to all beings. These seven exhaust the dimensions of offering.

3. **The yoga of the Vidyā:** The teacher's observance is not external rule-following but the continuous abiding in the awareness of the Vidyā — the supreme knowledge "I am Śiva." This is the only vow that cannot be broken.

4. **The creation of a teacher:** The consecration rite (abhiṣecana) involves the teacher pouring water from the sacred jars over the disciple's head while reciting the Vidyās. This "coronation" is the formal authorization to teach — but it works only if the disciple has already realized the truth.

## The Teacher's Two Bodies

The true teacher has two bodies: the physical body (sthūlaśarīra) through which he interacts with disciples, and the "body of mantra" (mantradeha) which is his identity with Śiva. It is from the second body that he teaches. The first body is merely its instrument.
