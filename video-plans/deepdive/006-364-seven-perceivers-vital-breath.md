# 364 — The Seven Perceivers in the Vital Breath
**Tantrāloka 10.186cd–227ab** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
The hierarchy of perceivers is not just philosophy — it is a living reality in every breath you take.

## Core Teaching
The fifteen divisions of the perceivers are projected into the phases of the vital breath (*prāṇa*). By attending to the breath in meditation, the Yogin can ascend through the perceiver-levels in a single cycle of respiration.

## The Projection into the Vital Breath (10.186cd–205)
Each phase of the breath corresponds to a level of perception:
- **Inhalation** (*pūraka*) — Sakala level: drawing in the external world
- **Retention** (*kumbhaka*) — Pralayākala: suspension of external awareness
- **Exhalation** (*recaka*) — Vijñānākala: releasing identification
- **Void between breaths** — Śiva level: pure consciousness

The fifteen aspects of the Sakala are distributed across the breath's movement through:
- 12 fingers' distance (the standard measure of breath travel)
- The three internal channels (iḍā, piṅgalā, suṣumṇā)
- The five prāṇas (prāṇa, apāna, samāna, udāna, vyāna)

## Penetration into Śiva (10.206–209)
In the first two of the sixteen phases of perception, the Yogin penetrates into Śiva and attains the Goddess of Intuition (*Pratibhā*). This happens:
- At the very beginning of inhalation
- At the very end of exhalation
- In the gap between breaths

## Arising of Other Perceivers (10.210–211)
The other perceivers arise in subsequent moments:
- From the Śiva state emerge Mantramaheśvara
- Then Mantreśvara
- Then Mantra
- Then Vijñānākala
- Finally, Sakala and the full fifteen-fold perception

## Accomplishments by Attending to Moments (10.212–218ab)
By attending to the *moments* (*kṣaṇa*) of the breath cycle and closing the gap between subject and object:
- The Yogin gains *siddhis* (accomplishments)
- The distinction between perceiver and perceived dissolves
- The breath becomes the vehicle of liberation

## Interiority and Exteriority (10.218cd–219ab)
Interiority = expansion of consciousness (*vikāsa*). Exteriority = contraction (*saṅkoca*). The breath moves between these two poles.

## Śiva Only Apparently Distant (10.219cd–220ab)
Śiva is not far away. He is the closest thing — the witness of the breath itself. When the Yogin traces the breath back to its source, he finds Śiva.

## Consciousness Everywhere Full (10.220cd–224ab)
Consciousness is:
- **Full** (*pūrṇa*) — nothing is outside it
- **Emergent** (*udita*) — manifesting as the breath and perception
- **Devoid of succession** (*akrama*) — never moving, even while appearing as the breath

## Mantreśas as Instruments of Consciousness (10.224cd–225ab)
Even the higher perceivers (Mantreśa, etc.) are instruments of consciousness. Only Śiva is the user.

## The Lord of Khecarīcakra (10.225cd–227ab)
This is the state of the Yogin who has ascended through all perceivers — he becomes the Lord of the Sky-faring Wheel (*Khecarīcakra*), moving freely through all levels of consciousness.

## Connection to the States of Consciousness (10.227cd–288)
Having established the breath as the vehicle of ascent, Abhinava now examines the states of consciousness (waking, dream, sleep, fourth, beyond fourth) from a Kaula point of view, showing how each state corresponds to a level of the perceivers and a phase of the breath.
