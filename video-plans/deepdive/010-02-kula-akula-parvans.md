# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Kula and Akula Parvans — The Two Cycles of Sacred Time

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 10, Chapter 28, Verses 10-18ab (~lines 1457-1820)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
Time in the Trika Kaula tradition is not linear — it is a living, conscious cycle. The Kula and Akula parvans correspond to the waxing and waning moon, the bright and dark fortnights, and ultimately to two modes of consciousness: the expansive and the contractive. Understanding these two cycles is the key to harmonizing ritual with cosmic rhythm.

## Part 1: The Eight Kula Mothers and Their Lunar Days (0:45-3:30)
**From the Yogasaṁcāra (verses 10-14ab)**
- The eight Mothers preside over specific lunar days (tithis):
  - Māheśī: 14th (*abdhīndu* = ocean + moon) and 7th (*muni* = sage)
  - Brāhmī (belongs to Brahmā's family): 1st (*pratipad*) and 15th (*pañcadaśī*)
  - Kaumārī: 6th (*rasa* = sentiment) and 3rd (*vahni* = fire)
  - Vaiṣṇavī: 4th (*abdhi* = ocean) and 12th (*akṣīndu* = eyes + moon)
  - Aindrī: 5th (*astra* = weapon) and 13th
  - Vārāhī: 9th (*randhra* = cavity) and 11th (*rudra*)
  - Caṇḍī (Cāmuṇḍā): 8th (*vasu* = eight) and 2nd (*akṣi* = eye)
  - Yogeśī: 10th and shares 8th with Caṇḍī
- "Whatever lunar day one is born on, that is his family deity" — the Kula day is one's spiritual birthday

## Part 2: Akula Parvans — The Reversed Cycle (3:30-5:30)
- Verse 14ab: "anyaiś cākulaparvāpi vaiparītyena lakṣitam" — Akula days are characterized "in reverse"
- Two interpretations:
  1. **Metaphorical reversal (Jayaratha):** Akula days are metaphorically called Kula — like a lover's "anger" that is really love
  2. **Structural reversal:** The same Mothers preside but on corresponding dark-fortnight days, in reverse order
- Kula = bright fortnight (waxing moon, expansion of consciousness)
- Akula = dark fortnight (waning moon, contraction)
- The privative prefix A- in Akula does not mean "not auspicious" — Akula days are equally sacred, just operating in the withdrawl mode

## Part 3: The Etymology of Parva/Parvan (5:30-7:30)
- Verses 15-18ab (~line 1686-1693): Detailed etymology from Haiḍara and Bhairavakula
- Two derivations:
  - From *parva* (root √pṝ = to fill): "parva pūraṇa ity eva"
  - From *pR* (also = to fill/protect) with *-van* suffix: "parva tatpūraṇād iti"
- The word appears in two forms: *parva* (neuter, ending in -a) and *parvan* (ending in -n)
- "A parvan is so called because consciousness is filled (by it)"
- Time related to the vital breath (taught in Chapter 6) generates a certain consciousness when moving through its cycles
- A parvan is the particular time when "the plenitude of one's own consciousness is attained"

## Part 4: The Mahāvākya — Time is Consciousness (7:30-9:30)
- This is not mere ritual astrology — it's a full-blown philosophy of time
- Time is the movement of the vital breath (prāṇa)
- The breath cycles (Chapter 6) encode all cosmic cycles: day/night, month, year, zodiac
- A parvan is when these cycles align to produce a "fullness" of consciousness
- The eight Mothers are not external goddesses but the eight modalities of cognitive energy
- Worship on these days is worship of one's own expanded consciousness
