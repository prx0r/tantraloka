# From the 36th to the 38th Principle and Back
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

The three principles — 36th (transcendent Śiva), 37th (immanent Supreme Śiva), and 38th (undefined pure consciousness) — are not three different realities. They are the same absolute reality considered from three perspectives: as utterly transcendent, as immanent totality, and as the unobjectifiable subject that remains when even immanence is conceptualized. The oscillation between them is not a metaphysical confusion but a pedagogical method: by moving through these viewpoints, the practitioner arrives at direct realization that transcends all viewpoints.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verse 11.23cd-24 — The Thirty-Sixth Shines in the Heart

**Lines:** 2186–2198

> na cānavasthā hy evaṁ syād dṛśyatāṁ hi mahātmabhiḥ || 23 ||
> yad vedyaṁ kiñcid ābhāti tatkṣaye yat prakāśate |
> tat tattvam iti nirṇītaṁ ṣaṭtriṁśaṁ hṛdi bhāsate || 24 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"This does not entail in this way an infinite regress. Let the wise consider the matter thus. When whatever manifests as an object (of perception) becomes one with consciousness, and so ceases (to be such), the Principle that shines within the Heart (of consciousness) is said to be the thirty-sixth."

**Explanation:**
The resolution: when all objectivity — including the objectified forms of the 37th and 38th — dissolves into consciousness, what remains is the 36th, shining directly in the Heart. This is not a regress but a return. The 36th is not one term in a series but the ground of the series itself.

---

#### Verse 11.25ab — The Two Alternatives

**Lines:** 2244–2247

> tat kiṁ na kiñcid vā kiñcid ity ākāṅkṣāvaśe vapuḥ |

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"When someone who is in the grips of the need (to know) what (this reality could be), whether it is just nothing or something of some kind..."

**Explanation:**
The mind, trying to grasp the absolute, oscillates between two poles: "Is it nothing?" (the void of the nihilist) or "Is it something?" (the object of the realist). Both are incorrect. The absolute is neither a thing nor the absence of things.

---

#### Verse 11.25cd-26ab — Consciousness, Bliss, Freedom

The answer to the dilemma: the absolute is "consciousness, bliss and freedom" — not a thing that can be known as an object, but the very awareness in which knowing occurs.

---

#### Verse 11.26cd-27 — The Thirty-Eighth Becomes the Thirty-Seventh Again

**Lines:** 2343–2350

> tac cāpi kalpitavedyatvaṁ yatra bhāti sa cinmayaḥ || 26 ||
> aṣṭātriṁśattamaḥ so 'pi bhāvanāyopadiśyate |
> yadi nāma tataḥ saptatriṁśa eva punar bhavet || 27 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"(In the case) where that also manifests as an object fashioned (by the imagination, ultimate reality) which is (pure) consciousness (would be) the thirty-eighth (principle). If, for the purpose of meditation (bhāvanā), instruction is given about this also, it would again become the thirty-seventh."

**Explanation:**
The fluidity is explicit: whenever the 38th is taught (made an object of instruction), it becomes the 37th. Whenever the 37th is contemplated as mere object, its subject becomes the 38th. This is not a flaw but the very mechanism of spiritual pedagogy — the absolute cannot be pinned down.

---

#### Verse 11.28 — The Attributes Are the Same

**Lines:** 2376–2382

> avibhāgasvatantratvacinmayatvādidharmatā |
> samaiva vedyīkaraṇaṁ kevalaṁ tv adhikaṁ yataḥ || 28 ||

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The attributes (of the thirty-seventh and the thirty-eighth principles) are the same. They are both (pure) consciousness which is free and undivided etc. They differ only in the manner in which they are (conceptually) objectivized (vedyīkaraṇa)."

**Explanation:**
The 37th and 38th share identical qualities. The only difference: one is object, the other is subject in a given act of contemplation. When you meditate on the absolute, it is the 37th; when you *are* that awareness, it is the 38th. The difference is entirely in the direction of attention.

### Narrative Arc

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** The three principles are not a ladder of higher realities but a single reality viewed from three angles.

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** The infinite regress problem. If every object needs a subject, doesn't the 38th need a 39th?

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** The solution: when all objects dissolve, the 36th shines directly. The Heart is self-luminous, requiring no external witness.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** The oscillation between 37th and 38th. Teaching makes the absolute an object; realization is the subject.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** The same attributes, different direction. Vedyīkaraṇa (conceptual objectivization) as the only difference.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The three principles are a dialectic that returns to itself. The goal is not to reach the 38th but to recognize that you *are* it.
