# THE ASSEMBLY OF TWELVE GODDESSES — Conceptual Teaching
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

The Assembly of Twelve Goddesses is the full manifestation of Bhairava's power as the Twelve Kālīs. They are not separate goddesses but aspects of the one Śakti, the reflective awareness of Bhairava. This assembly is the foundational structure of Trika practice — the maṇḍala of energies that the practitioner enters through meditation and ritual. The Twelve encompass the entire range of consciousness: the subject (Parā), the means (Parāparā), and the object (Aparā), each in its four phases.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### The Twelve as the Supreme Circuit

**Line ~21076** (Vol. 1)

**Dyczkowski Translation (from commentary):**
"Consciousness shines in its plenitude by the realisation that it is this all-encompassing totality of all its powers. And the powers of the Lord are numberless... everything is summated in just three of them. The venerable Supreme power, by means of which the Supreme Lord sustains, perceives and makes manifest this entire reality."

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On the Twelve Kālīs (line ~21145+):** Jayaratha explains that Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī (the Attractress of Time) embraces everything in the domain of objectivity, the means of knowledge, and the perceiver. She is the "Inexplicable" (Anākhya) because she cannot be defined either as sequential process nor as other than it.

**On Bhairava's plenitude (line ~21180+):** "Abhinava implicitly declares in a passage in Tantrasāra that this one power is the ultimate of all the major traditions, named in various ways."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the significance of the Twelve (note 779):** Sanderson: "It is this set of twelve, rather than the three or four goddesses of the Trika proper, which Abhinavagupta declares to be 'the principal circuit of the divine Powers' in which Bhairava's nature is most fully manifest."

### Philosophical Context

The Twelve Goddesses represent the complete actuality of consciousness — all its powers, phases, and dimensions. To realize them is to realize the fullness of Bhairava. This is why Abhinava places this teaching at the foundation of practice: the aspirant begins by recognizing the twelvefold structure of his own consciousness.

### Visual Concepts

1. **Maṇḍala of Twelve:** A circular diagram with twelve goddess figures arranged around a central Bhairava, each with distinct colors and attributes.

2. **The Four Phases in Each Domain:** Three concentric circles (subject, means, object) each divided into four quadrants (creation, persistence, withdrawal, fourth).

### Narration Script

The Assembly of Twelve Goddesses is the plenitude of Bhairava's power — the complete manifestation of consciousness in all its phases and dimensions. Each goddess is an aspect of the one Śakti. They encompass the subject who perceives, the means by which he perceives, and the object he perceives — each operating through the four moments of creation, persistence, withdrawal, and the transcendent Fourth. Together they are the Twelve Kālīs — the principal circuit of divine powers in which Bhairava's nature is most fully manifest.
