# 362 — Self-Luminous Śiva and the Remaining Subjects
**Tantrāloka 10.110–123ab** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
At the apex of the seven perceivers sits Śiva — not as a perceiver among perceivers, but as the very light in which all perceiving happens.

## Core Teaching
Śiva is self-luminous (*svaprakāśa*). He is never an object of perception. Even when the scriptures speak of Śiva, they are making an object of instruction — the real Śiva is beyond all objectification.

## The Remaining Subjects (10.110–113ab)
After Sakala (15 divisions), Pralayākala (13), and Vijñānākala (11):
- **Mantra**: 9 divisions
- **Mantreśvara**: 7 divisions
- **Mantramaheśvara**: 5 divisions
- **Śiva**: 3 divisions (Śiva, Śakti, and their identity)

But Śiva's "3" is really non-division — it is the trace of distinction that allows the teaching itself.

## Self-Luminous Śiva (10.113cd–117ab)
Key argument: Śiva cannot be perceived as an object because:
1. He is the subject of all subjects
2. He is the light in which everything appears
3. If He could be objectified, He would not be the Supreme

## The Supreme Lord Makes Śiva an Object of Instruction (10.117cd–120ab)
A paradox: the scriptures speak of Śiva as if He were an object. This is:
- Not a description of Śiva-as-He-is
- A pedagogical device to lead the student to direct recognition
- Like pointing at the moon — the finger is not the moon

## Śiva Is Not Objective and Is Undivided (10.120cd–123ab)
The section concludes: Śiva has no real divisions. The fifteen → thirteen → eleven → nine → seven → five → three progression is:
- For the Yogin's practice, not a description of reality
- A ladder that is kicked away when the goal is reached
- The final "3" dissolves into the non-dual

## The Pedagogical Paradox
The supreme Lord "makes Śiva into an object of instruction" — this is the purpose of the entire perceptibility matrix. The teaching is a finger pointing to what cannot be taught.

## Connection to Deep Sleep and Awakening (10.123cd–140ab)
From the teaching about Śiva, Abhinava now turns to the states of consciousness (waking, dream, deep sleep, fourth, beyond fourth) — showing how they correspond to the levels of the perceivers. Deep sleep, for example, corresponds to the Pralayākala state where objectivity is dissolved.
