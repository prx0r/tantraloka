## Central Thesis
Kālopāya — the Temporal Means — is the practice of realizing the identity of time and the vital breath. By projecting cosmic time-cycles (days, nights, months, years, planetary periods) into the breath, the yogi discovers that all time is within the breath, and the breath is within consciousness. Time thus becomes the means to transcend time.

## Script Outline

### 1. Opening: What Is Kālopāya? (0:00–1:30)
- The fourth upāya, following Śāmbhava, Śākta, and Āṇava
- Kāla = time; Kālopāya = the means that works through time
- The breath is both time (when it flows) and timeless (when it rests) — lines 15540–15560
- Based primarily on Svacchandatantra chapter 7 — lines 212–213

### 2. Time and the Goddess Kālī (1:30–3:30)
- Kālī is the supreme goddess of time — lines 409–411
- The origin of the breath is also the origin of time — lines 409–411
- "All phases of creation depend on Time, and its nature is the vital breath" — lines 19687–19688
- The 12 signs of the zodiac are Kālī's forms — from 4/146cd
- She embodies the Year (saṁvatsara) — lines 212–213

### 3. The Two Times (3:30–5:30)
- Maitri Upaniṣad teaching: two forms of Brahman — time and the timeless — lines 15540–15560
- Time begins with the sun; the timeless is prior to the sun
- The breath participates in both: flowing = time; resting = timeless
- The dvādaśānta axis: the End of Twelve is where time dissolves

### 4. The Breath as Cosmic Clock (5:30–7:30)
- The breath's measure: 36 fingers from Heart to nasal End of Twelve — lines 19770–19780
- 21,600 breaths per day and night — lines 3789–3792
- Each breath = a mini time-cycle containing all cosmic periods
- The planets, constellations, and gods are projected into the breath — lines 432–438
- By mastering the breath, the yogi masters time

### 5. Time as Means (7:30–9:00)
- The insight: time is not external — it is the pulse of consciousness
- The movement of the breath (uccāra) reveals the true nature of emanation, persistence, and withdrawal — lines 19687–19688
- Liberation is attained by understanding the entire temporal process — commentary lines ~19690s
- The "tying of the topknot" (sikthabandha) — the gathering of all times into one point

### 6. Conclusion (9:00–10:00)
- Kālopāya transforms time from a binding cycle into a liberating practice
- The breath is the loom on which the fabric of time is woven
- To know the breath is to know time; to know time is to transcend it
