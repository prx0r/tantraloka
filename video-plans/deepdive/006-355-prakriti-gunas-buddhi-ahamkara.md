# 355 — Prakṛti, Guṇas, Buddhi, and Ahaṃkāra
**Tantrāloka 9.220cd–252** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Below the subjective coverings lies the objective sphere — nature (*prakṛti*), its three qualities (*guṇas*), the intellect (*buddhi*), and the ego (*ahaṃkāra*). These are the building blocks of our everyday experience of the world.

## Core Teaching
The impure universe below Māyā follows the Sāṁkhya model, but reinterpreted in a non-dual framework. Prakṛti is not separate from consciousness — it is consciousness in its contracted, object-oriented mode.

## Pradhāna — Fundamental Nature (9.220cd–221ab)
Prakṛti, also called *pradhāna* (the principal), is the root of all material existence. In Trika:
- It is not an independent principle (as in Sāṁkhya)
- It is the power of Śiva to appear as the objective world
- It is the *ābhāsanaśakti* — the power of manifestation

## The Three Guṇas (9.221cd–226)
1. **Sattva** — purity, light, harmony, knowledge
2. **Rajas** — activity, passion, motion
3. **Tamas** — inertia, darkness, ignorance

The guṇas are not things but tendencies of consciousness in its contracted state. They combine in varying proportions to create all objective phenomena.

## Buddhi — The Intellect (9.227–229)
Buddhi (*mahat* in Sāṁkhya) is the first evolute of prakṛti:
- It is the seat of determination (*adhyavasāya*)
- In its pure form, it discriminates reality from appearance
- In its impure form, it creates the sense of separate self

## Ahaṃkāra — The Principle of the Ego (9.230–232)
Ahaṃkāra is the "I-maker":
- **Sāttvika ahaṃkāra** — produces the five sense organs and five organs of action
- **Rājasa ahaṃkāra** — powers the other two and produces the mind (*manas*)
- **Tāmasa ahaṃkāra** — produces the five subtle elements (*tanmātras*)

## The Inner and Outer Senses (9.233–246)
The senses are instruments of consciousness, not mere physical organs:
- **Jñānendriyas** (5 sense organs): hearing, touch, sight, taste, smell
- **Karmendriyas** (5 organs of action): speech, grasp, gait, excretion, generation
- **Antaḥkaraṇa** (inner organ): *manas* (mind), *buddhi* (intellect), *ahaṃkāra* (ego), *citta* (mind-stuff)

## Impure Knowledge and Limited Agency (9.246–252)
Below the *kañcukas* and within the sphere of prakṛti:
- Knowledge is always partial and mediated
- Agency is always constrained
- The soul mistakes these limitations for its true nature

## Connection to the Elements (9.253–314)
From the tāmasa ahaṃkāra emerge the five subtle elements (*tanmātras*): sound, touch, form, taste, smell. From these, the five gross elements (*mahābhūtas*): ether, air, fire, water, earth. This is the complete ontological descent into materiality — the endpoint of the impure path.
