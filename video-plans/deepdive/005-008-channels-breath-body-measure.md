# Deep-Dive: The Channels of the Breath and the Measure of the Body

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7, verses 65cd–70  
**Catalog Entry:** #289  
**Lines:** 4029–4128

---

## Topic Overview

The final section of Chapter 7 details the physical and energetic basis of the breath cycles: the channels (nāḍīs), the measure of the body, and how the 21,600 daily breaths are distributed across the body's subtle anatomy. This grounds the preceding esoteric teachings in the concrete reality of the yogi's body.

---

## Key Verses

### Verses 65cd–70 (lines 4029–4128)

The number of breaths in a day and night is 21,600. This is because the subtle body has 72,000 channels (nāḍīs), each of which represents a specific energetic pathway. The precise measurement of the breath's movement — from the Heart to the End of the Twelve (dvādaśānta) — is given.

The breath moves through:
- **Suṣumnā:** The central channel
- **Iḍā:** The left channel
- **Piṅgalā:** The right channel
- **72,000 subsidiary nāḍīs**

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **21,600 Breaths as Cosmic Measure:** This number (derived from 216 × 100, or 24 hours × 60 minutes × 15 breaths/minute) is the baseline for all breath-based practice.

2. **The End of the Twelve (Dvādaśānta):** The breath extends from the Heart (hṛdaya) to 12 finger-breadths outside the nose — this is the full measure of each exhalation.

3. **The Nāḍī System:** The 72,000 channels of the subtle body are the highways of prāṇa. Mastery of the breath is mastery of the entire nāḍī system.

4. **From Heart to Dvādaśānta:** The Heart is the origin of the breath; the End of the Twelve is its terminus. The still point between breaths is where consciousness rests in itself.
