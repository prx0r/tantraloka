# The Theology of Yogic Dreaming

**Tantrāloka 15.478cd–498ab | Lines 19862–21000**

## Overview

The examination of dreams (svapna-vicāraṇa) is grounded in a sophisticated theology of dreaming that spans the Trika system. Dreams are not "less real" than waking — they are experiences in a different modality of consciousness, one in which the mind's creative power (sṛṣṭiśakti) operates without external constraints.

## Key Philosophical Framework

1. **Svapnasvātantrya — freedom in dreaming:** The Vijñānabhairava teaches that by meditating on the breath and entering the heart, one attains "freedom in dreaming" (svapnasvātantrya). This is the ability to maintain awareness within the dream state and to exercise creative will there.

2. **Dreaming as a real state (avasthā):** The Trika recognizes four main states of consciousness: waking (jāgrat), dream (svapna), deep sleep (suṣupti), and the Fourth (turīya). Each state is a field of experience, not an illusion. Dream objects are as real as waking objects — they are both projections of consciousness.

3. **The teacher's dream is authoritative:** Because the teacher's consciousness is more expanded (vikasita), his dreams are more revelatory. His awareness penetrates the dream state more deeply and sees the karmic patterns more clearly.

4. **Diagnostic dreaming:** The content of dreams reveals the state of the disciple's purification. Auspicious dreams (riding elephants, drinking wine with deities) indicate progress; inauspicious dreams (falling, drowning, being chased) indicate residual obstructions.

## Dream Signs (verses 487–491)

**Auspicious signs (line 20040–20044):**
- Pleasing women (hṛdyā strī)
- Drinking wine (madyapāna)
- Eating raw meat (āmamāṃsasya bhakṣaṇam)
- Drinking blood (raktapāna)
- Decapitation (śiraścheda) — the cutting off of the ego!
- Riding mountains, elephants, horses

**Inauspicious signs and remedy:**
- A homa of 108 oblations with the Weapon (astra) mantra

## The Philosophical Role of Dreaming

Dreaming demonstrates the creative power of consciousness in the absence of external objects. This proves that the mind (manas) is not merely a receiver of sensations but a creator of realities. When this creative power is recognized as one's own, the dreamer becomes free — and this freedom prepares him for the greater freedom of liberation.
