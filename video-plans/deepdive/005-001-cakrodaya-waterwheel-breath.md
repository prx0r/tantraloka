# Deep-Dive: The Nature of Mantras and the Waterwheel of Breath (Cakrodaya Introduction)

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7 (Cakrodaya), verses 1–5ab  
**Catalog Entry:** #282  
**Lines:** 294–1176 (esp. 1005–1176)

---

## Topic Overview

This opening section of Chapter 7 introduces the supreme secret (paramarahasya) of the cycles of mantras arising within the flow of the breath. Abhinavagupta frames the practice as the "effortful" (yatnaja) counterpart to the spontaneous (ayatnaja) emergence of time cycles in the breath taught in Chapter 6. The central analogy is the waterwheel (ghaṭīyantra / araghaṭṭacakra) — a single exertion drives multiple machines simultaneously.

---

## Key Verses

### Verse 1 (lines 1012–1014)
> atha paramarahasyo 'yaṁ cakrāṇāṁ bhaṇyate 'bhyudayaḥ
> *"Now we will expound this supreme secret, which is the emergence of the cycles (of Mantras in the flow of the breath)."*

Sets the stage: this is a secret teaching, reserved for advanced practitioners.

### Verses 2ab (lines 1021–1024)
> ity ayatnajam ākhyātaṁ yatnajaṁ tu nigadyate
> *"The (spontaneous) effortless emergence (of time within the flow of the breath) has been discussed; we will now talk about the (voluntary) one that is the result of effort."*

Marks the transition from Chapter 6 (spontaneous, effortless) to Chapter 7 (effortful, willed practice).

### Verses 2cd–3ab (lines 1073–1080)
> bījapiṇḍātmakaṁ sarvaṁ saṁvidaḥ spandanātmatām
> vidadhat parasaṁvittāv upāya iti varṇitam
> *"All (Mantras) consisting of seed syllables (bīja) and syllabic groups (piṇḍa) are the dynamic pulsing nature (spandanātmatā) of consciousness. Thus, (a Mantra) is said to serve as a means to attain Supreme Consciousness."*

**Core doctrine:** Mantras are not arbitrary sounds — they are the very pulsation (spanda) of consciousness within the flow of breath.

### Verses 3cd–5ab (lines 1136–1149)
> yathāraghaṭṭacakrāgraghaṭīyantraughavāhanam
> ekānuṣaṁdhiyatnena citraṁ yantrodayaṁ bhajet
> ekānuṣaṁdhānabalāj jāte mantrodaye 'niśam
> tanmantradevatā yatnāt tādātmyena praṣīdati
> *"A waterwheel moves a series of machines for raising water located in front of it, and so possesses the varied emergent activity of the machines by its single, unified exertion. Similarly, when Mantras arise by the strength of a single continuous application (of attention and intention), the deity of those Mantras, because it is at one with them, becomes propitious by virtue of that exertion."*

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **Effortful vs. Effortless (Yatnaja vs. Ayatnaja):** Chapter 6 dealt with the spontaneous projection of cosmic cycles onto the natural breath (21,600 breaths/day). Chapter 7 introduces voluntary lengthening — the practitioner deliberately extends each breathing cycle.

2. **The Waterwheel Analogy (Araghaṭṭacakra):** Just as a single waterwheel drives multiple water-raising machines simultaneously, the single-pointed effort (ekānuṣaṁdhāna) of mantra practice in consonance with the breath produces multiple effects — the mantra arises, the deity is propitiated, and ultimately supreme consciousness is attained.

3. **Mantra as Spanda:** Verses 2cd-3ab establish that mantras are not mere linguistic constructs but the dynamic pulsation of consciousness itself. This grounds mantra practice in the metaphysics of Spanda.

4. **Stages of Penetration:** The commentary (lines 1087–1094) explains that mantras first induce penetration into Śakti's essential nature (śāktāveśa), then successively into Śiva.

5. **The Supreme Secret:** Abhinava calls this paramarahasya — indicating that the correlation of mantra cycles with breath cycles is a highly esoteric teaching meant for advanced adepts (sādhakas).

---

## Swami Lakshmanjoo's Explanation (lines 984–999, 1025–1052)

Swami Lakshmanjoo explains Cakrodaya as involving two aspects:
- **Cakrabhid** — the various cycles of mantra
- **Mantrabheda and Vidyābheda** — various mantras and vidyās

The practice begins with the natural cycle of 21,600 breaths/day. The yogi progressively lengthens each breath — doubling, then tripling, etc. — until breathing only 24 times in 24 hours. Stage six involves effortful practice (yatnajopāsana); stage seven is effortless (ayatnajopāsana).
