# MĀYĀ TATTVA — The Power of Limitation
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Māyā tattva is the 31st principle — the power of cosmic limitation that contracts universal consciousness into individual, finite awareness. In the Śaiva nondual tradition, Māyā is not illusion (as in Advaita Vedānta) but *śakti* — the Lord's own power of self-concealment (*tirodhāna*) by which the one consciousness appears as many. It is the boundary between the pure path (Śuddhavidyā and above) and the impure path (all principles below Māyā). As Jayaratha explains, Māyā is of three forms: as the Knot (*granthi*), the Cavity (*bila*), and the Cave (*guhā*), representing gross, subtle, and supreme aspects.

---

### Key Verse — The Impure and Pure Distinguished (TĀ 1.189cd)

**Sanskrit:**
```
aśuddhiśuddhyamānatvaśuddhitastu mitho 'pi tat || 189 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"They are also mutually (related and distinguished) as (the Person spans) the impure (principles), (Knowledge spans those) in the process of being purified, and (Power spans the one that) is pure."

**Explanation:**
Māyā is the first principle of the impure path — the gateway to duality. The Person spans from Earth up to Māyā, meaning Māyā is the highest impure principle. Below it, all is *aśuddha* (impure), ruled by duality.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On TĀ 1.189 (line ~28683): Jayaratha states that the Person (*puṁ*) is "impure" because the principles up to Māyā that he pervades consist of duality (*bheda*). Māyā is thus the summit of the impure — the finest veil between the one and the many.

On Māyā's three forms (Vol. 5, line ~21652): Jayaratha describes Māyā as the Knot, the principle, and the energy (Śakti). The Knot is the obscuring power, the principle is the ontological ground of limitation, and the energy is the active force of contraction.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Māyā as Śakti, Not Ignorance**: Unlike the Advaitin view of Māyā as inexplicable illusion, Śaiva nondualism sees Māyā as a real power of the Lord — His *svātantrya* to appear as the finite. It is not a mistake but a purposeful self-limitation for the sake of cosmic experience.

2. **The Five Kañcukas**: From Māyā arise the five coverings (*kañcukas*) — Kāla (time), Niyati (necessity), Rāga (attachment), Vidyā (limited knowledge), Kalā (limited agency). These are the instruments of Māyā's contraction, fragmenting the one I-consciousness into the finite self.

3. **Threshold Principle**: Māyā is the most important boundary in the 36 tattvas. Everything above it is *śuddha* (pure), everything below is *aśuddha* (impure). The yogi must pierce through Māyā to attain liberation — a crossing known as *māyāgranthi-bheda*.
