# THE FIVE SENSES OF KNOWLEDGE (JÑĀNENDRIYAS) — Śrotra, Tvak, Cakṣus, Jihvā, Ghrāṇa
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

The five *jñānendriyas* (senses of knowledge) are principles 16–20 in the tattva hierarchy. They are the instruments by which the soul perceives the objective world: hearing (*śrotra*), touch (*tvak*), sight (*cakṣus*), taste (*jihvā*), and smell (*ghrāṇa*). They arise from the sāttvika aspect of Ahaṃkāra and correspond to the five *tanmātras* (subtle elements) which are their objects. In Abhinavagupta's nondual framework, the senses are not passive receptors but active powers of consciousness — the means by which the one subject perceives itself as objective form.

---

### Key Verse — The Impure Principles (TĀ 1.189cd)

**Dyczkowski notes (Vol. 1, line ~28873):**
"They are sound (śabda) – hearing, form (rūpa) – sight, taste (rasa) – tongue, touch (sparśa) – skin, and smell (gandha) – nose."

**On the evolution from Ahaṃkāra (line ~28838):**
"The principles below mind are emanated by the ego (ahaṁkāra)."

**Explanation:**
Each sense corresponds to a subtle element and a gross element. The senses are the cognitive interface between the inner self and outer reality.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On the senses (Vol. 6, line ~16677): Jayaratha explains that the five senses of knowledge arise from the sāttvika aspect of Ahaṃkāra. Each sense is a specific power of consciousness that grasps a particular quality of the objective world — hearing grasps sound, touch grasps tactility, sight grasps form, taste grasps flavor, smell grasps odor.

Jayaratha notes that the senses are not material but subtle — they are the cognitive capacities of the experiencing subject, not the physical organs (ear, skin, eye, tongue, nose). The physical organs are the supports (*ādhiṣṭhāna*), the senses themselves are the powers (*śakti*).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Active Powers, Not Passive Receptors**: In the Śaiva idealist view, the senses are not "windows" through which an external world enters the mind. They are the outgoing activities of consciousness by which the subject constructs its world. Perception is a creative act.

2. **Correlative Structure**: Each sense is paired with a tanmātra (subtle element) and a mahābhūta (gross element): hearing/śabda/ākāśa, touch/sparśa/vāyu, sight/rūpa/tejas, taste/rasa/āpas, smell/gandha/pṛthivī. This is the five-fold matrix of embodied perception.

3. **Reabsorption in Practice**: The yogi withdraws the senses from their objects (*pratyāhāra*), then reabsorbs each sense into its tanmātra, the tanmātrā into Ahaṃkāra, and so upward. The process of liberation reverses the descent.
