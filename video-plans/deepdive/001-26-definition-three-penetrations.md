# Definition of the Three Penetrations
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 167-170

---

### Core Teaching

The three-fold manner of means to realization — Divine (Śāmbhava), Empowered (Śākta), and Individual (Āṇava) — corresponds to three forms of penetration (samāveśa) into Śiva consciousness. These are defined in the Mālinīvijayottara Tantra (Śrīpūrva). The Śāmbhava penetration arises for one who does not think of anything, by intense awakening. The Śākta penetration is attained by thinking of reality with the mind alone, without utterance of Mantra. The Āṇava penetration involves uccāra, karaṇa, dhyāna, varṇa, and sthānakalpanā.

---

### The Three-Fold Manner Taught by the Lord (Verse 167)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
tad etat trividhatvaṁ hi śāstre śrīpūrvanāmani |
ādeśi parameśitrā samāveśavinirṇaye || 167 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"This three-fold manner (of means to realisation) was taught by the Supreme Lord in the scripture called Śrīpūrva (i.e. the Mālinīvijayottara) in the course of explaining (the forms) of penetration (samāveśa) (into and by the supreme state)."

The word āveśa (penetration) literally means 'entry' — commonly denoting possession by supernatural beings. Here it is transformed into a liberating realization: the penetration of individual consciousness by Śiva's energy.

---

### The Three Penetrations Defined (Verses 168-170)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
akiñciccintakasyaiva guruṇā pratibodhataḥ |
utpadyate ya āveśaḥ śāmbhavo 'sāvudīritaḥ || 168 ||

uccārarahitaṁ vastu cetasaiva vicintayan |
yaṁ samāveśam āpnoti śāktaḥ so 'ṭrābhidhīyate || 169 ||

uccārakaraṇadhyānavarṇasthānaprakalpanaiḥ |
yo bhavet sa samāveśaḥ samyag āṇava ucyate || 170 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The penetration (āveśa) that arises for one who does not think of anything, by an intense (guruṇā) awakening (prabodhataḥ), is said to be the one pertaining to Śiva (śāmbhava)." (168)

"The penetration one attains whilst thinking of (the nature of) reality (vastu) with the mind alone, without (having to practice) the utterance of Mantra (uccāra) (or the like), is here called Empowered (śākta)." (169)

"The penetration that takes place by (the practice) of the utterance (of Mantra and concentration on the movement of the breath) (uccāra), the instruments (of Yoga) (karaṇa), visualization (dhyāna), (the cycling of) phonemes (in the breath) (varṇa) (as the subtle practice of Mantra), and the formation of supports (sthānakalpanā) (for ritual concentration), is rightly said to be Individual (āṇava)." (170)

### Jayaratha's Commentary Insights

- The word āveśa is a distinguishing feature of Kaula traditions — the transformative experience of penetration by Śiva's energy
- Grace is experienced as a 'descent of power' (śaktipāta) that enlightens and expands individual consciousness
- Abhinava reverses the order of the verses from the MV — he presents Śāmbhava first so that practitioners may aim for the highest goal
- The three penetrations are also understood as types and levels of initiation (dīkṣā)
- Śāmbhava penetration can occur by the teacher's glance or touch, or even without a teacher through spontaneous insight
- The instruments of Yoga (karaṇa) are defined from the Trisirobhairava: practice based on awakened consciousness, of seven types

### The Three Penetrations in Practice

| Penetration | Means | State | Basis |
|-------------|-------|-------|-------|
| Śāmbhava (Divine) | No thought, intense awakening | Śāmbhavāvastha | Will (icchā) |
| Śākta (Empowered) | Thinking with mind, no Mantra | Śāktāvastha | Knowledge (jñāna) |
| Āṇava (Individual) | Mantra, Yoga, ritual supports | Āṇavāvastha | Action (kriyā) |

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Penetration as Liberation**: Samāveśa is the entry of Śiva's consciousness into the individual soul, expanding contracted awareness into universal consciousness.

2. **Hierarchy of Practice**: The higher means (Śāmbhava) is immediate and free of effort; the lower (Āṇava) requires external supports. Yet all culminate in the same state.

3. **Grace and Effort**: The Śāmbhava penetration depends on intense grace; the Āṇava involves systematic practice. Both are expressions of Śiva's freedom.

4. **The Precedence of the Divine**: Abhinava reverses the scriptural order to emphasize that the Divine Means is the ground and goal of all practice.
