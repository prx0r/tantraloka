# Deep-Dive: The Cycles of Mantra Within the Breath According to the Yoginīkaula

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7, verses 41–52  
**Catalog Entry:** #286  
**Lines:** 2800–3271

---

## Topic Overview

Abhinava now quotes directly from the Yoginīkaula, the primary scriptural source for the Cakrodaya teaching. This section covers the practical distribution of mantra parts within the breath cycles, the classification of mantras eligible for breath-synchronization, and the concluding summary of the entire Cakrodaya exposition.

---

## Key Verses

### Verse 41 (lines 2805–2814)
> padamantrākṣare cakre vibhāgaṁ śaktitattvagam
> padeṣu kṛtvā mantrajño japādau phalabhāg bhavet
> *"Having distributed the unit of measure of a breathing cycle present in the principle of the power (of the vital breath) in the parts within the cycle of phonemes, Mantras or parts thereof, he who knows Mantra enjoys the fruits of the repetition of Mantra and the rest."*

### Verses 42cd–44 (lines 2881–2935)
> itthaṁ śaktisthitā mantrā vidyā vā cakranāyakāḥ
> padapiṇḍasvarūpeṇa jñātvā yojyāḥ sadā priye
> nityodaye mahātattve udayasthe sadāśive
> ayuktāḥ śaktimārge tu na japtāś codayena ye
> te na siddhyanti yatnena japtāḥ koṭiśatair api
> *"The Mantras, Vidyās and the Lords of the Cycles established in the power of the vital breath should be known, in accord with their nature, as parts of Mantra and syllabic groups. They should always be applied, once known in this way. Those that are not applied on the path of the power, this great reality that is perpetually emergent, established in the emergence of the breath and ever benevolent, are not successful even if repeated laboriously one thousand million times."*

### Verse 45 (lines 2988–2993)
> mālāmantreṣu sarveṣu mānaso japa ucyate
> upāṁśur vā śaktyudayaṁ teṣāṁ na parikalpayet
> *"It is said that in the case of all long garland-like Mantras (and Vidyās), the repetition should be mental or whispered. One should not apply the emergence of power (the breath procedure) to them."*

### Verses 46–48: Long Mantras and the Dviśatika (lines 3020–3100)
He who knows the division of mantra, vidyā, and cycles, and lacks nothing (in knowledge), is the Lord of the Cycles. Such a yogi, focused on the path of the vital breath, should be worshipped like Śiva.

### Verses 49–52ab: Calculation of Remainders

The text details elaborate calculations for breathing remainders. For example, a cycle of 192 syllables arises 112½ times, with 96 cycles remaining. Multiplying 16 × 16 yields 256 sixteenth-parts, each of 84 cycles, with 96 remaining (3/8 more).

### Verse 52cd (lines 3265–3271)
> eṣa cakrodayaḥ proktaḥ sādhakānāṁ hitāvahaḥ
> *"This, the emergence of the cycles that benefits adepts, has been taught."*

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **Śaktitattva as Vital Breath:** The power (śakti) in this context is the vital breath (prāṇaśakti). "Established in power" (śaktistha) means recited in consonance with the breath.

2. **Garland-Mantras Are Exempt:** Long garland-like mantras (mālāmantra) with many syllables should be recited mentally or whispered, not with the breath-procedure. This distinguishes two classes of mantra practice.

3. **The Central Abode (Madhyadhāman):** Success comes from being conjoined to the Central Abode — the suṣumnā channel where the breath moves in balance between inhalation and exhalation.

4. **Scriptural Precision:** The calculations with remainders (e.g., 96 cycles remaining, 3/8 parts) demonstrate that these are not theoretical — they are drawn directly from the Yoginīkaula.
