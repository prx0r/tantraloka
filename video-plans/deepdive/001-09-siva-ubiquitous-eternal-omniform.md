# ŚIVA UBIQUITOUS, ETERNAL AND OMNIFORM — Verses 59cd-66ab
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

Because Śiva is independent (anapekṣa) and the master (vaśin), spatial, temporal, and formal processes cannot restrict Him. Space cannot divide Him (He is vibhu — ubiquitous). Time cannot sequence Him (He is nitya — eternal). Form cannot contain Him (He is viśvākṛti — omniform). These three divine attributes are not qualities added to Śiva but intrinsic aspects of His nature as the Light of consciousness. They manifest as the wonderful variety of all things, both sentient and insentient, which are nothing but the Light appearing as all forms.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.59cd-60ab — Śiva's Freedom from Limitation

**Line ~15167** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
anapekṣasya vaśino deśakālākṛtikramāḥ |
niyatā neti sa vibhur nityo viśvākṛtiḥ śivaḥ || 59cd-60ab ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"For one who, independent and the master (of all things, transcends them,) spatial, temporal and formal processes are not restricted (niyata) (by Karma and natural law), and so He is Śiva Who is ubiquitous, eternal and omniform."

**Explanation:**
Space, time, and form condition all limited existence. But Śiva is not subject to these because He is the consciousness in which they appear. Jayaratha explains that space cannot divide Śiva because He is all-pervasive; time cannot sequence Him because He is eternal; form cannot contain Him because He is all forms.

#### Verse 1.60cd-61ab — Manifestation of All Things

**Line ~15197** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
vibhutvāt sarvago nityabhāvād ādyantavarjitaḥ |
viśvākṛtitvāc cidacittadvaicitryāvabhāsakaḥ || 60cd-61ab ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"By virtue of His ubiquity, He is all-pervading. As He is eternal, He is free of beginning and end. As He is omniform, He manifests the wonderful variety of things, both sentient and insentient."

**Explanation:**
This verse makes explicit the relationship between Śiva's attributes and the manifest world. Because He is ubiquitous, He pervades all. Because He is eternal, He transcends temporal succession. Because He is omniform, the entire range of existence — from sentient beings to insentient matter — is His appearance.

#### Verse 1.61cd-62 — The Six Forms of Śiva

**Line ~15238** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
tato 'sya bahurūpatvam uktaṁ dīkṣottarādike || 61 ||
bhuvanaṁ vigraho jyotiḥ khaṁ śabdo mantra eva ca |
bindunādādisaṁbhinnaḥ ṣaḍvidhaḥ śiva ucyate || 62 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Thus, His pluriform nature is taught, for example, in the Dīkṣottara. Śiva, conjoined with the Point (bindu) and Sound (nāda) etc., is said to be of six kinds, as World (bhuvana), (Iconic) Form (vigraha), Light (jyotiṣ), Space (kha), (Unstruck) Sound (śabda) and Mantra."

**Explanation:**
The Dīkṣottara teaches that Śiva appears in six forms corresponding to levels of manifestation: as the world itself, as iconic forms worshipped in temples, as light, as the space of consciousness, as unstruck sound, and as mantra. This pluriformity does not compromise His unity — it is the expression of His omniform nature.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On spatial, temporal and formal limitation (line ~15175+):** Jayaratha explains: "Space, time and form cannot divide up Śiva, Who is the Light of consciousness and independent of (all) else, because (space and the rest) are conditioned by thought constructs based on unity and diversity (bhedābheda)."

**On the Kāmikāgama reference (line ~15108+):** Jayaratha cites the Kāmika: "What was taught in the first chapter to be the supreme abode's own vitality is the own nature of everything... it is self-luminous; it is not an object of a means of knowledge that is other than its own self-awareness."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the attributes (note 364, line ~15204+):** "Śiva is consciousness, which is eternal, ubiquitous and omniform — unaffected by time, undivided by space, without a fixed form because everything is a form of the Light of consciousness."

### Philosophical Context

This section establishes the three fundamental attributes of the Absolute that will recur throughout the Tantrāloka: omnipresence (vibhutva), eternity (nityatva), and omniformity (viśvākṛtitva). These are not accidents of Śiva's nature but define His relationship to the world: He is simultaneously transcendent (free of space-time-form) and immanent (appearing as all space-time-forms).

### Visual Concepts

1. **Three Attributes Animation:** Three interlocking circles labeled Space, Time, Form, with a radiant center that passes through all three undiminished.

2. **Six Forms of Śiva:** A hexagonal mandala with six sections — World, Icon, Light, Space, Sound, Mantra — each containing a visual symbol.

3. **The Independent Lord:** A figure seated in a lotus, with arrows representing space, time, and form approaching but dissolving before reaching Him.

### Narration Script

The Supreme Lord is independent — He depends on nothing. This means that space, time, and form do not restrict Him. Space cannot divide Him, so He is all-pervasive. Time cannot sequence Him, so He is eternal. Form cannot contain Him, so He is all forms. These are not three separate attributes but three aspects of His one nature as the Light of consciousness. The entire universe — every world, every being, every sound, every thought — is His manifestation. He appears as world, as iconic form, as light, as space, as unstruck sound, and as mantra. His pluriformity is the play of His freedom.
