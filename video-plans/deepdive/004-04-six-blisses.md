## Central Thesis
The Six Blisses (ṣaḍānanda) map the progressive expansion of consciousness through the five forms of the vital breath plus their cosmic synthesis. Each bliss corresponds to a specific breath-phase (exhalation, inhalation, retention, rising, pervasion) and a corresponding level of subjectivity. The sequence moves from individual subjectivity in deep sleep (Nijānanda) to universal cosmic consciousness (Jagadānanda).

## Script Outline

### 1. Opening: The Architecture of Bliss (0:00–1:30)
- The Six Blisses: Nijānanda, Nirānanda, Parānanda, Brahmānanda, Mahānanda, Cidānanda, Jagadānanda — lines 3440–3491
- Unlike the MVV version with four blisses, Abhinava adds Brahmānanda and reformulates — lines 3476–3491
- Associated with the five vital breaths plus the universal synthesis — lines 4475–4479
- The Blisses are the "Utterance of the Principle of the Vital Breath" — lines 3477–3478

### 2. Nijānanda — Innate Bliss (1:30–2:30)
- The initial state: "established in the Heart, the pure subjective aspect alone" — lines 3466–3469
- The bliss of deep sleep, but lucidly experienced — lines 3637–3654
- Nijānanda is not counted as a separate stage in TĀ (it's the base state) — lines 3485–3491
- The yogi rests in pure emptiness (śūnyatāmātra) — line 3457

### 3. Nirānanda — Bliss of Stillness (2:30–4:00)
- Emerges when prāṇa (exhaled breath) rises from heart to End of Twelve — lines 3512–3514
- Associated with the means of knowledge (pramāṇa) — lines 3678–3688
- "The Bliss of Stillness, which is considered to be that of the individual perceiver" — lines 3686–3688
- Connected to exhalation (recakakrama) — lines 3682–3683

### 4. Parānanda — Bliss of the Other (4:00–5:30)
- Arises when apāna (inhaled breath) descends — lines 3516–3523
- Associated with the object of knowledge (prameya) — lines 3690–3696
- "Satisfied by drinking the plenitude of the infinite aspects of objectivity" — lines 3520–3523
- Connected to inhalation (pūrakakrama) from End of Twelve to Heart — lines 3755–3756

### 5. Brahmānanda — Bliss of Brahman and Mahānanda — Great Bliss (5:30–7:00)
- Brahmānanda: arises in breath retention (kumbhaka), on the plane of samāna — lines 3526–3530, 3758–3775
  - Objects fuse into unitary consciousness — lines 3762–3768
  - "Equal because he has made all objectivity his own" — line 3767–3768
- Mahānanda: arises in udāna (rising breath) — lines 3532–3537, 3777–3820
  - "Devouring the differentiated manifestation of means and object of knowledge" — lines 3532–3534
  - The 21,600 breaths are consumed in the fire of udāna — lines 3789–3792

### 6. Cidānanda — Bliss of Consciousness (7:00–8:00)
- The Great Pervasion (mahāvyāpti) free of limitation — lines 3844–3902
- Associated with vyāna (pervasive breath) — lines 3850–3851
- "Not augmented with any insentient thing" — lines 3851, 3883–3885
- Pervades from Kalā down to Earth — lines 3891–3894

### 7. Jagadānanda — Cosmic Bliss (8:00–9:30)
- "Where there is no limitation at all" — line 3966
- "Shines radiantly everywhere" — lines 3966–3967
- "Fed by the Supreme Nectar of freedom" — line 3968
- "Wherein meditation finds no direct application" — line 3969
- Taught by Śambhunātha — line 3970
- This is the state of Anupāya (No Means) — lines 4002–4003
- Both introverted and extroverted samādhi become one — lines 4250–4265

### 8. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- The Six Blisses form a complete curriculum of contemplative ascent
- Each breath becomes a vehicle of expanding conscious awareness
- The final state, Jagadānanda, is the permanent recognition of the universe as one's own consciousness
