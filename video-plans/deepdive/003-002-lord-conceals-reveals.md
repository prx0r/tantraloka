# THE LORD CONCEALS AND REVEALS HIS OWN NATURE
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 8–12

---

### Core Teaching

How can consciousness, which is essentially the subjective perceiver, become an object of its own awareness? If it were to become an object, would it not become insentient like any other object? This epistemological paradox is addressed in verses 8–12. Abhinavagupta's answer is radical: consciousness is "full of all things" (*nirbharā*) by its very nature. Nothing needs to be added or removed. The appearance of an "unclear" state is the play (*krīḍā*) of the Supreme Lord, who "conceals His own nature" through His unsullied freedom (*svātantrya*). This self-concealment is Māyā — not an illusion layered over reality, but the Lord's own power of contracting His infinite fullness into apparent limitation. The removal of that duality is the very act of reflective awareness.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/8–12; Dyczkowski translation pp. 10–14, lines 1190–1480.

---

### Key Verses

#### Verse 8 — The Objection: Consciousness Cannot Be an Object

**Sanskrit:**
```
nanu saṃvit parāmraṣṭrī parāmarśamayī svataḥ |
parāmṛśyā kathaṁ tāthārūpyasṛṣṭau tu sā jaḍā || 8 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1196–1203):** "Surely, (someone may object that this cognitive) consciousness (*saṃvid*) is a reflecting perceiver that is itself (*svataḥ*) (intrinsically and spontaneously) reflective awareness. How can it be an object of awareness? If it were to be emitted in that form (it would be) insentient (not consciousness)."

Sanderson's translation (lines 1214–1217): "Surely consciousness is itself awareness. It is that which carries out acts of representation. How then, could it be itself the object of such representation? If it were to assume such a character it would no longer be consciousness."

---

#### Verse 9 — The Response: Consciousness is Full by Nature

**Sanskrit:**
```
ucyate svātmasaṃvittiḥ svabhāvād eva nirbharā |
nāsyām apāsyaṁ nādheyaṁ kiñcid ity uditaṁ purā || 9 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1223–1231):** "It is said that consciousness, which is one's own (authentic) self (*svātmasaṃvitti*), is by its very nature full (of all things) (*nirbharā*). It was said before that there is nothing at all that needs be subtracted from it or added to it."

Jayaratha explains (lines 1233–1258): "consciousness is the form of one's own (authentic) self because there is nothing separate from it on which it depends. It is (spontaneously) by itself full (of all things), that is to say, it craves for nothing else." Because it is "ever manifest (*nityodita*), there is nothing at all within consciousness that could be 'unclear' etc., that 'needs be removed' or that is 'supremely clear' etc. that (needs be) 'added to it.'" This is confirmed by Anupāya teaching from Chapter 2: "(No practice) associated with action can serve there as a means (to realisation)" (line 1246–1248, quoting TĀ 2/8ab).

---

#### Verse 10 — The Lord's Play of Self-Concealment

**Sanskrit:**
```
kiṁ tu durghaṭakāritvāt svācchandyān nirmalād asau |
svātmapracchādanakrīḍāpaṇḍitaḥ parameśvaraḥ || 10 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1296–1307):** "However, by virtue of His unsullied freedom, which can accomplish the impossible, the Supreme Lord is adept in (playing) the game of concealing His own nature."

**Line references:** 1296–1307. The phrase *durghaṭakārin* literally means "one who can do what is very difficult (to do)" (line 1315). This verse is quoted in the *Mahārthamañjarī* (p. 51), where Maheśvarānanda explains (lines 1318–1346): "Even a potter who is engaged in making a pot possesses Śiva's nature with all its powers. However, because no descent of the power (of grace) etc. has taken place, due to not knowing that, he has the notion that he is merely a potter." The key point: "The specific nature of the Supreme Lord that is universally accepted is that He is always engaged in the five acts (creation etc.). The Supreme Lord, being free, conceals His own nature and manifests through Māyā."

---

#### Verse 11 — Māyā as the Covering of Self-Awareness

**Sanskrit:**
```
māyā tv ācchadanaṁ tasya yad anāvṛtavigrahaḥ |
yadātmācchādanaṁ nāma svavīryopārjanaṁ kila || 11 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1350–1395):** "Māyā is the covering of (the All-pervasive Lord), although (in reality) His form is unconcealed. This covering of His own nature means the acquisition of His own power."

Jayaratha explains (lines 1404–1430): "Māyā, which is the power of Śiva, covers Śiva's all-pervasive nature just as a 'cloud (formed) from the pure radiance of the sun, the source of heat, covers the sun.'" This covering is an exercise of the Lord's freedom: "It is by the Lord's own will that He becomes such as this, not because He is forced by anything else." The covering is *real* in its operation but *not ultimately real* in the sense that it limits the Lord — He remains free even in the act of self-limitation.

---

#### Verse 12 — The Removal of Duality is Reflective Awareness

**Sanskrit:**
```
evam asya prabhor asya dvaitam ittham ihoditam |
taddvayāpāsanenāyaṁ parāmarśa udāhṛtaḥ || 12 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1477–1480):** "Duality is said to be the manifestation of this Great Lord in this way. The removal of that duality is said to be this reflective awareness."

"The removal of that duality takes place by attending to the (essential) oneness (*abheda*) of the effect and (its) cause (that is, universal manifestation and Lord Śiva)" (lines 1482–1485).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Paradox of Self-Knowledge**: Consciousness knowing itself is not like a subject knowing an object. It is the self-luminous (*svaprakāśa*) nature of consciousness that makes all knowledge possible. When consciousness appears "unclear," this is not a diminution of its nature but a play (*krīḍā*) of self-concealment.

2. **Māyā as Divine Play**: Unlike Advaita Vedānta where Māyā is an inexplicable (*anirvacanīya*) principle of illusion, here Māyā is the Lord's own power of self-concealment. It is "the acquisition of His own power" (line 1350–1395) — a positive expression of freedom, not a veil of ignorance.

3. **Two Levels of Reflective Awareness**: The form of reflective awareness in the Divine Means is "the pure all-encompassing awareness of 'I'" (lines 1506–1516). The Empowered Means operates in the sphere of "both unity and duality" — a choice between them. Sound Reasoning (*sattarka*) is the form of reflective awareness that removes duality.

4. **Nothing to Add, Nothing to Remove**: The liberated state is not a transformation of consciousness but a recognition of what it always already is. "Full to overflowing with all things, it lacks nothing, and, perfect in all respects, it is free of all defects that could possibly require removal or remedy" (lines 1281–1285).
