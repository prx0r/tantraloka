# 357 — Introduction to the Seven Perceivers
**Tantrāloka 10.1–5** | Chapter 10: Tattvabheda (Perceptibility Matrix)

## Hook
One world. Seven perceivers. Each sees the same object — but each sees it completely differently.

## Core Teaching
This chapter reveals the "sacred secret found only in Trika scriptures" (10.1cd): the division of principles into fifteen aspects, corresponding to seven types of perceivers who range from the fully conditioned (Sakala) to Śiva Himself.

## The Opening Verse
**10.1cd**: *ucyate trikaśāstraikarahasyaṁ tattvabhedanam* — "The Division of the Principles, which is a sacred secret found only in the Trika scriptures, will now be taught."

## The Seven Perceivers

| # | Perceiver | Level | Divisions |
|---|-----------|-------|-----------|
| 1 | **Sakala** | Fully conditioned (Earth through Prakṛti) | 15 |
| 2 | **Pralayākala** | Deconditioned by dissolution | 13 |
| 3 | **Vijñānākala** | Deconditioned by knowledge | 11 |
| 4 | **Mantra** | Knower of mantra | 9 |
| 5 | **Mantreśvara** | Lord of mantra | 7 |
| 6 | **Mantramaheśvara** | Great lord of mantra | 5 |
| 7 | **Śiva** | Supreme perceiver | 3 |

## Key Verses 10.3–5
**10.3**: Principles from Earth up to Root Nature (Prakṛti) are divided into fifteen, according to their powers and possessors.

**10.4**: Kalānta (the end of Kalā) has thirteen; Pralayākala has eleven; Māyā similarly has nine; Vijñānākala has seven.

**10.5**: Mantras are five-fold; Mantreśvaras three-fold; Śiva, whose nature is consciousness as dense uninterrupted light, has no divisions.

## The Hidden Structure
These are not seven different people but seven levels of the same perceiver. The Yogin ascends through them in meditation, "telescoping" lower perceivers into higher ones. At each level:
- A previous perceiver becomes the object of the next
- The means of knowledge are folded into the subject
- Consciousness becomes increasingly expansive

## The Fourteen + One = Fifteen
For each perceiver, there are:
- The perceiver (*pramātṛ*)
- The power/energy (*śakti*) — 7 pairs = 14
- Plus the "own nature" (*svarūpa*) = 15 total

## Source and Tradition
This teaching comes from the *Mālinīvijayottara* (2/1–46ab). Abhinava calls it the "one secret of Trikaśāstra" — the most excellent disclosure of a fundamental teaching.

## Connection to Meditation
The ascent through perceivers is an actual yogic practice: by successively objectifying each level of subjectivity, the Yogin reaches the non-dual state of Śiva.
