# The Kaula Ablution and the Wine Sacrifice

**Tantrāloka 15.66–78 | Lines 3767–3960**

## Overview

The Kaula ablution and the subsequent five purifications with wine constitute one of the most controversial and philosophically dense sections. Here, the transgressive substances of Kaula practice — wine, meat, and other "forbidden" items — are reinterpreted as direct stimuli to the expansion of consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **66–68:** The Kaula ablution, where the worshipper looks at wine in a vessel used by heroes (vīra), transforms it into Śiva and offers libation to the assembly of deities within the body and breath.
- **69–76:** The five purifications with wine (pañcagavya of a different order) — each corresponds to a level of consciousness.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Wine as ānandadravya:** Wine is called the "substance that imparts bliss" (ānandadravya). In Kashmiri Śaivism, bliss (ānanda) is the very nature of consciousness (cidānanda). The Kaula practitioner uses wine not for intoxication but to access the bliss-structure of reality.

2. **The transgressive as sacramental:** Substances normally prohibited (madya, māṃsa, mudrā, matsya, maithuna) become offerings precisely because they provoke the ego's contraction. By offering WHAT IS FORBIDDEN, the practitioner shatters the duality of pure/impure that sustains the fettered state.

3. **Five purifications = five energies:** The five purifications with wine correspond to the five energies of Śiva (cit, ānanda, icchā, jñāna, kriyā). Each sip is a re-identification with a dimension of divine consciousness.

4. **Looking as transformation:** The crucial act is looking (dṛṣṭi) at the wine. The gaze itself transforms the substance — this is the power of vimarśa (reflective awareness) to re-cognize any object as Śiva.

## Inner Kaula vs. Outer License

Abhinavagupta is careful to distinguish between the internal Kaula sacrifice (which is contemplative) and mere outward indulgence. The true "wine" is the bliss of consciousness; the true "meat" is the ego offered to the fire of awareness. Extreme left-hand practice is for those of highest capacity.
