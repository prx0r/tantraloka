# The Three Goddesses and Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī

**Tantrāloka 15.319–365 | Lines 12901–13700**

## Overview

On the Trident Throne, the three goddesses — Parā, Parāparā, and Aparā — are worshipped with their respective Bhairavas. At the apex stands Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī, the Attractress of Time, who draws all temporal succession into the eternal present. This section culminates with Khecarīmudrā — the Seal of the Skyfaring Goddess — which is the yogic practice of ranging freely in the void of supreme consciousness.

## Key Verses

- **319–320 (lines 547–548):** The superiority of the Trika school over others — because it worships the totality of reality, not fragments.
- **321–323ab (lines 550–551):** Worship of the three goddesses on the Trident Throne.
- **323cd–330ab (lines 553–555):** Each goddess sits with her Bhairava — Parā with Ratiśekhara, Parāparā with Bhairavasadbhāva, Aparā with Navātman.
- **330cd–335ab (lines 556–557):** The goddess in the centre — Parā — is the supreme energy.
- **335cd–338 (lines 558–559):** Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī, who extracts time from the cosmos.
- **339–345 (lines 560–561):** The seven perceivers along the Trident Throne.
- **359–360:** The inner oblation (antarhoma).
- **361–362 (line 569):** Khecarīmudrā — the seal of the skyfaring goddess.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The Triad as the whole:** Parā (transcendent consciousness), Parāparā (the bridge of reflective awareness), and Aparā (immanent nature) together constitute the totality of the real. No aspect of reality is excluded.

2. **Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī's function:** Time (kāla) is the principle of succession — one moment after another. Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī "attracts" (saṁkarṣa) time back into the eternal present. She does not destroy time but deconstructs its sequentiality, revealing that all moments are simultaneous in the light of consciousness.

3. **Seven perceivers along the axis:** The seven levels of subjectivity (pramātṛ) from the bound soul (sakala) up to Śiva are distributed along the Trident. Each is a degree of self-awareness.

4. **Khecarīmudrā:** The "sky-faring seal" — khecara means "moving in the void (kha)." This mudrā is the yogic practice of turning awareness back on itself, ranging freely in the space of consciousness. It is the seal that closes the door to samsāra.

## The Inner Oblation

The final offering (359–360) is not of ghee or rice but of the entire objective universe into the fire of consciousness. This is the ultimate sacrifice — the recognition that all objectivity is consumed in the light of the subject.
