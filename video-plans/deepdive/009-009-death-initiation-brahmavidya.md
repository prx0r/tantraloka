# Death Initiation: The Brahmavidyā and Self-Liberation

**Tantrāloka 19.23cd–55 | Lines 540–560**

## Overview

The Brahmavidyā (knowledge of Brahman/Śiva) is the central mantra of the death initiation. It can be recited by the teacher, by a fellow initiate, or, remarkably, by the dying person himself. The final verses teach that a dying man — if sufficiently awakened — can impart the initiation to himself.

## Key Verses

- **23cd–31ab (line 534):** The procedure for the Brahmavidyā — the mantra that effects the exit from the body.
- **31cd–39 (lines 537–538):** The Brahmavidyā recited by a fellow initiate to liberate the dying person.
- **40–46 (lines 542–543):** The liberating awakening of the dying man — he is "awakened" (prabodha) to his true nature.
- **51–55 (lines 543–544):** The dying man can impart the initiation to himself — if he has the knowledge, no external teacher is needed.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The Brahmavidyā as the sword of knowledge:** The Brahmavidyā (the mantra SAUḤ or the longer form) is the "knowledge of Brahman" that cuts through the last vestiges of ignorance. Its recitation at the moment of death is the final stroke of gnosis.

2. **The fellow initiate as proxy:** If the teacher is not present, a fellow initiate (sabrahmacārin) can recite the Brahmavidyā for the dying person. The power of the mantra does not depend on the reciter's status but on the truth it embodies.

3. **Self-initiation at death:** This is one of the most radical teachings — a man of knowledge can release himself at death without any external ritual. His own recognition (pratyabhijñā) at the moment of death is sufficient. This echoes the earlier teaching that knowledge alone is initiation.

4. **The liberating awakening:** The dying person is "awakened" (prabodha) — called out of his identification with the dying body into the realization "I am Śiva, I am not this body, I am not dying." This awakening, even for a moment, is enough.

## The Ultimate Non-Dependency

The entire death initiation chapter demonstrates that at the boundary of life and death, only consciousness matters. Ritual, mantra, and teacher are supports for those who need them — but the one who KNOWS needs nothing. His knowledge IS the path and the goal.
