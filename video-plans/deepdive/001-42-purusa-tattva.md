# PURUṢA TATTVA — The Contracted Experiencer
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Puruṣa tattva is the 25th principle — the individual soul, the contracted experiencer. It is the *ahaṁ* (I-sense) at the core of each living being, the witness-consciousness (*sākṣin*) that has been limited by the five kañcukas and entangled in Prakṛti. In the Sāṁkhya system, Puruṣa is the 25th principle (counting from Earth), and Āgamic Śaivism adds eleven more above it. Abhinavagupta adopts this Sāṁkhya framework but reinterprets it: Puruṣa is not a plurality of distinct souls but the one consciousness appearing as many through Māyā's limitation.

---

### Key Verse — The Trika Triad: Person, Knowledge, Power (TĀ 1.187cd-188ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
etat tattvāntare yat puṁvidyāśaktyātmakaṁ trayam || 187 ||
ambhodhikāṣṭhājvalanasaṁkhyair bhedair yataḥ kramāt |
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"This is so because the three, namely, the Person (Nara), Knowledge (Vidyā) and Power (Śakti), that are amongst these (thirty-six) metaphysical principles (and span them), are of four, ten and three different kinds, respectively."

**Explanation:**
The Person (*puṁ*) spans the principles from Earth to Māyā — it is the principle of subjectivity within the impure path. Puruṣa is the "experiencer" of the lower 24 principles.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On TĀ 1.187 (line ~28554): Jayaratha explains that the Person is of four kinds: Conditioned (Sakala), Dissolution Deconditioned (Pralayākala), Consciousness Deconditioned (Vijñānākala), and Pure (Śuddha). These are four levels of the soul's contraction or purification.

On TĀ 1.189 (line ~28683): The Person is "impure" because the principles it pervades (Earth to Māyā) consist of duality (*bheda*). The Puruṣa is the experiencer who has forgotten his identity with Śiva.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Sāṁkhya Heritage**: Puruṣa is the 25th principle of the Sāṁkhya system — the pure witness, inactive, distinct from Prakṛti. Abhinavagupta accepts this but adds: Puruṣa is ultimately Śiva — the same consciousness, only contracted. Liberation is not *kaivalya* (isolation) but recognition of one's true nature as Śiva.

2. **Multiple Souls**: Unlike the one Śiva, Puruṣa appears as many — each bound soul is a Puruṣa. This multiplicity is real at the empirical level but ultimately a play of Māyā within the one consciousness.

3. **Puruṣa in the Tattva Hierarchy**: Puruṣa sits just below the five kañcukas. Above it are the principles of limitation; below it are Prakṛti and the evolutes of Nature. The soul's journey upward is the reabsorption of each limitation until Puruṣa recognizes itself as Śiva.
