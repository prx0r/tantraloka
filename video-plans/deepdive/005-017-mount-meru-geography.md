# Deep-Dive: Mount Meru and the Cosmic Geography of the Upper Egg

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 8, verses 43cd–67  
**Catalog Entries:** #298–303  
**Lines:** 7201–8000+

---

## Topic Overview

Above the netherworlds, at the center of the upper half of the Egg of Brahmā, stands Mount Meru — the axis mundi, the golden mountain that is the abode of the gods. This section describes Meru's dimensions, shape, surrounding cities, mountains, and the nine regions (varṣas) of Jambudvīpa.

---

## Key Verses

### Mount Meru (verses 43cd–45ab, lines 7600–7644)

Meru is the king of mountains, made of gold, 84,000 leagues high, 16,000 leagues deep, and 16,000 leagues wide at the base. Its shape is described as octagonal in the middle, round above, square below, with a peak shaped like a concave plate (śarāva). It is a Bhairava Liṅga, and its base (pīṭhikā) is the earth itself.

### The Cities Surrounding Meru (verses 45cd–58ab, lines 7700–7900)

Eight cities of the Lokapālas (guardians of the directions) encircle Meru on a circular terrace:
- **East:** Amarāvatī (Indra)
- **Southeast:** Tejovatī (Agni)
- **South:** Saṁyamanī (Yama)
- **Southwest:** Kṛṣṇāṅgārā (Nirṛti)
- **West:** Śuddhavatī (Varuṇa)
- **Northwest:** Gandhavatī (Vāyu)
- **North:** Mahodayā (Kubera/Soma)
- **Northeast:** Yaśasvinī (Maheśa/Īśāna)

### The Four Mountains (verses 58cd–62ab)
- **Mandara** (east)
- **Gandhamādana** (west)
- **Vipula** (south)  
- **Supārśva** (north)

### Nine Regions of Jambudvīpa (verses 62cd–67)
1. **Ilāvṛta** (center, around Meru)
2. **Ketumāla** (west)
3. **Bhadrāśva** (east)
4. **Kuru** (north)
5. **Hiraṇyamaya** (north of Kuru)
6. **Ramyaka** (north of Hiraṇyamaya)
7. **Hari** (south)
8. **Kinnara** (south of Hari)
9. **Bhārata** (south of Kinnara)

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **Meru as Axis Mundi:** Meru is the center of the cosmos, the vertical axis connecting the heavens, earth, and netherworlds.

2. **Meru as Bhairava Liṅga:** The mountain's three sections (square, octagonal, round) correspond to Brahmā, Viṣṇu, and Rudra — the three aspects of the divine.

3. **Jambudvīpa as the Known World:** The continent of Jambudvīpa is the central landmass of the Earth, with Bhārata (India) as the southernmost region where karma is accumulated most intensely.

4. **The Hierarchy of Regions:** Each region has specific characteristics — Ilāvṛta is the land of the gods, Bhārata is the land of karma and liberation.
