# Initiation by Śiva's Hand

**Tantrāloka 15.450cd–498ab | Lines 14501–15400**

## Overview

After the fire sacrifice, the teacher gives the disciple the vision of deities in the maṇḍala and then performs the "Initiation by Means of Śiva's Hand" (śivahastavidhi) — the culminating act where the teacher, acting as Śiva, places his hand on the disciple's head and transmits the liberating energy.

## Key Verses

- **450cd–458:** The vision of deities in the maṇḍala — the disciple sees the gathered deities in the maṇḍala before him.
- **459–471ab:** The initiation by Śiva's hand — the teacher places his hand on the disciple's head (or on the flower the disciple holds) and introduces him to the supreme plane.
- **471cd–474ab:** The four types of initiates — from regular initiates to teachers.
- **474cd–476:** Who can become a teacher? Only one who has received this transmission.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Śiva's hand as grace incarnate:** The teacher's physical hand is not merely a hand — it is the vehicle of śaktipāta (the descent of grace). The touch transmits energy because the teacher has become Śiva through his own realization.

2. **Vision of the maṇḍala:** Before receiving the touch, the disciple sees the deities in the maṇḍala — this vision is the first direct experience of the divine forms as real presences, not mere symbols.

3. **The four types of initiates:** (1) Samayin — regular observer of the Rule; (2) Putraka — "spiritual son" entitled to higher knowledge; (3) Sādhaka — adept who can perform rituals for others; (4) Ācārya — teacher qualified to initiate others.

4. **The transmission is irreversible:** Once Śiva's hand has touched a soul, even if the person later transgresses, the seed has been planted. It may lie dormant but can never be destroyed.

## The Yoga of Sleeping

The section on the bed-preparation (śayyākḷpti) is remarkable — the teacher sleeps in yogic awareness, and the quality of his dreams becomes diagnostic for the disciple's future. Initiation continues even in sleep.
