# Examination of Disciple Competence

**Tantrāloka 15.18–38ab | Lines 1831–3229**

## Overview

People are variously competent (18–19). The teacher must examine the disciple to determine the quality of the grace that has descended upon him (śaktipāta) and question him about his desires — worldly enjoyment or liberation. This determines the entire form the initiation will take.

## Key Verses

- **18 (line 2014):** *dīkṣāyogyā yogayogyā iti śrīkairaṇe vidhau* — "Some are suited for initiation, others for yoga, others for knowledge." (Kiraṇa Tantra)
- **20 (lines 2123–2125):** *sa ca prāguktaśaktyanyatamapātapavitritam / parīkṣya pṛṣṭvā vā śiṣyaṁ dīkṣākarma samācaret* — "After examining the disciple to ascertain which descent of grace has purified him, or questioning him, the teacher should perform initiation."
- **30 (lines 2858–2859):** The disciple with seed (sabīja) enjoys yogic powers in this body, then goes to the plane where the teacher conjoins him.
- **31–32 (lines 2915–2918):** The initiation without seed (nirbīja) severs all three bonds — past, present, and future karma — and liberates immediately.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Grace has degrees:** The descent of power (śaktipāta) is not binary — it admits of intensities (tīvra, madhya, manda). The teacher's diagnostics determine which layer of the onion to peel.

2. **Seed and no-seed:** The "seed" (bīja) is the set of lifelong observances (samaya) that structure the initiate's post-initiation life. Sabīja initiation requires the disciple to actively maintain the Rule; nirbīja initiation burns everything at once — the "Immediate Bestower of Nirvāṇa" (sadyonirvāṇadā).

3. **Lokadharmin vs. Śivadharmin:** The worldly-dharmin seeks enjoyment even in the spiritual path; the Śiva-dharmin seeks only liberation. The teacher must not impose liberation on one who craves experience, nor withhold it from one who is ready.

4. **The teacher's responsibility:** The teacher must personally fund the initiation if the disciple is poor. Even water and dūrva grass suffice — but the teacher must never disdain the disciple, for this would violate Śiva's command.

## The Ontology of Vāsanā

The teacher assumes a mental disposition (vāsanā) that matches the disciple's need. This is not pretense but participation — the teacher enters the disciple's reality level to lift him from within.
