# ĪŚVARA TATTVA — The This-I
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Īśvara tattva is the 33rd principle, the second phase of the subject-object relation within the pure path. It is defined as "This am I" (*idam aham*) — a state in which objectivity (*idam*) has become prominent while subjectivity (*aham*) recedes. If Sadāśiva is the cosmic "I am This," Īśvara is "This is I." The emphasis shifts from the subject to the object, though both are still experienced as one consciousness. In the phonemic scheme, Īśvara corresponds to Ū — the long Ū that extends outward into form.

---

### Key Verse — The Pervasion of Knowledge (TĀ 1.188cd-189ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
puṅvidyāśaktisaṁjñaṁ yat tat sarvavyāpakaṁ yataḥ |
avyāpakebhyastenedaṁ bhedena gaṇitaṁ kila || 188 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"The three called the Person, Knowledge and Power pervade the entire (series of thirty-six metaphysical principles). Thus, they have been reckoned apart from the other (principles), which are not pervasive."

**Explanation:**
Knowledge (Vidyā) pervades from Śuddhavidyā to Sadāśiva. Īśvara sits between these — closer to objectivity than Sadāśiva, but still within the purified sphere. It is the "Lord" (*īśvara*) aspect of consciousness, where the universe is seen as the Lord's body.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On TĀ 1.189 (line ~28688): Jayaratha explains that the principles above Māyā (Śuddhavidyā, Īśvara, Sadāśiva) consist of *bhedābheda* (unity-in-diversity). Īśvara is the level at which the object-side of this unity begins to predominate. The perceivers at this level are the *Mantreśvaras* — lords of mantra who experience the universe as their own manifestation but with the object now clearly discerned.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Object-Side of Unity**: Īśvara tattva is where objectivity first becomes conspicuous within the pure path. The "This" is no longer a faint trace but a distinct pole, yet it remains recognized as one's own Self. This is the *parāparā* (supreme-cum-inferior) state.

2. **Cosmic Lordship**: Unlike the later impure principles where objectivity constrains subjectivity, Īśvara's objectivity is freely assumed. The Lord (*īśvara*) is not bound by the object but holds it as His own manifestation — the universe as divine body.

3. **Phonemic Correspondence**: Īśvara corresponds to Ū (long Ū), the vowel that extends outward. It completes the movement begun with Ī (Sadāśiva): Ī = subjectivity extending outward, Ū = objectivity extending inward to meet the subject.
