# The Philosophy of Animal Sacrifice in the Trika

**Tantrāloka 16.28–70 | Appendix to Ch. 16 (Netratantra Ch. 20) | Lines 648–792**

## Overview

The animal sacrifice (paśubandha) in the Trika is one of the most misunderstood and controversial topics. Abhinavagupta provides a radical reinterpretation: the "animal" is primarily the fettered soul (paśu), and the "sacrifice" is the liberation of that soul from its contracted state. The actual animal is symbolic — its death re-enacts the death of the ego.

## Key Verses

- **28–36ab:** Eight types of sacrificial animal — from the buffalo to the goat — each corresponding to a tattva or impurity.
- **36cd–47ab:** Extracting the vital force of the animal with the breath — the teacher absorbs the animal's prāṇa into his own.
- **50cd–52ab:** The Tattvārthacintāmaṇi teaching — the animal is the bound soul.
- **52cd–54ab:** The heroic (vīra) animal — one that does not fear death.
- **54cd–62cd:** The benefits of animal sacrifice — purification of the corresponding tattva.
- **63–70:** The best animal is reborn six times in higher states before final liberation.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The animal = the fettered soul:** The Sanskrit word paśu means both "animal" and "fettered soul" (the "beast" bound by the pāśa). The animal brought to sacrifice is a stand-in for the disciple's own contracted consciousness.

2. **Extracting prāṇa = liberating consciousness:** The teacher extracts the animal's vital force not by killing but by yogic absorption. He merges the animal's prāṇa with his own and offers it to the deity. This is not destruction but transformation.

3. **Eight animals = eight bodies:** The eight types of animal correspond to the eight forms of Śiva (Earth, Water, Fire, Air, Space, Moon, Sun, Self). Sacrificing the "earth-animal" purifies the earth-element in the disciple's body.

4. **Rebirth six times:** The animal that is sacrificed with proper mantra and awareness is reborn six times in successively higher states, each time closer to liberation. These six births correspond to the six paths (ṣaḍadhvan).

## The Ethical Dimension

Abhinavagupta clearly teaches that the outer animal sacrifice is optional and for those of lower capacity. The inner sacrifice — offering the "animal" of the ego — is the true meaning. The literal killing of animals is neither required nor recommended for those who understand the inner teaching.
