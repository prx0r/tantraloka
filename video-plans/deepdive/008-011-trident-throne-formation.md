# The Trident Throne: Cosmic Axis of Consciousness

**Tantrāloka 15.297cd–318 | Lines 12130–12900**

## Overview

The Trident Throne (triśūlāsana) is the most architecturally elaborate visualization in the Tantrāloka. It is a vertical axis extending from the Foundation Energy (ādhāraśakti) below the navel up through the Knot of Māyā to the three prongs where the three goddesses — Parā, Parāparā, and Aparā — reside. This throne is nothing less than the total structure of reality (tattva, kalā, bhuvana, mantra, pada, varṇa) mapped onto the practitioner's body.

## Key Verses

- **297cd–299 (lines 12154–12159):** Deposit the Energy of the Foundation (HRĪṀ), Earth (KṢLĀṀ), the Ocean of Wine (KṢVIṀ), and Fire (VAṀ) — the spherical base shaped like an āmala fruit.
- **300–301 (lines 12181–12184):** The shaft (Ananta) extends up to the uvula; the Knot of Māyā sits above, containing dharma, jñāna, vairāgya, aiśvarya, adharma, ajñāna, avairāgya, and anaiśvarya.
- **302 (line 12187):** Above the Knot is the square pedestal of Pure Knowledge (śuddhavidyā), with three coverings (chadana).
- **303ff:** The three prongs rise above the pedestal, each culminating in a lotus where a goddess sits with her Bhairava.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The Throne as ontology:** The Trident Throne is a map of the 36 tattvas, from earth at the bottom to Śiva at the top. The practitioner does not merely visualize this — he INSTALLS it within his own body, becoming the axis mundi.

2. **The Knot of Māyā:** The granthi (knot) is the point where pure creation becomes impure. It is the "night" (niśā) of māyā — the principle of limitation. The eight qualities of the intellect (dharma, adharma, etc.) are its contents.

3. **The three goddesses as the three energies:** Parā (supreme) = icchā (will), Parāparā (supreme-non-supreme) = jñāna (knowledge), Aparā (non-supreme) = kriyā (action). Or alternatively: Parā = transcendence, Parāparā = the bridge, Aparā = immanence.

4. **Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī — she who attracts time:** Above the three prongs, Kālasaṁkarṣiṇī stands as the attractress of time itself. She draws all temporality back into the timeless present of Bhairava.

## The Body as Temple

The Trident Throne is the inner temple. Every outer maṇḍala, every fire altar, every stone liṅga is an externalization of this inner reality. The practitioner who has built the Trident Throne within his own body carries the cosmos within him.
