# SOUND REASONING (SATTARKA) — THE AXE THAT SEVERS DUALITY
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 13–16

---

### Core Teaching

Sound Discriminative Reasoning (*sattarka*) is not mere logical argumentation. It is the intuitive discerning insight that recognizes the identity of the perceiver and the perceived. Abhinavagupta compares it to a "fully sharpened axe" that severs the root of the tree of harmful duality. Arising from the intellect sanctified by contact with Pure Knowledge (*śuddhavidyā*), *sattarka* is both a process of purification and the culminating insight itself. It operates as the reverse of Māyā — where Māyā uses the freedom of consciousness to generate duality, *sattarka* uses that same freedom to grasp the truth of oneness. The classic formulation of this insight is: "I am this" (*aham idam*) and "this am I" (*idam aham*).

**Line references:** TĀ 4/13–16; Dyczkowski translation pp. 14–22, lines 1500–1720.

### Key Verses

#### Verse 13 — The Axe of Sound Reasoning

**Sanskrit:**
```
durbhedapādapasyāsya mūlaṁ kṛntanti kovidāḥ |
dhārārūḍhena sattarkakuṭhāreṇeti niścayaḥ || 13 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1552–1624):** "It is certain (*niścaya*) that the learned sever the root of this tree of harmful duality (*durbheda*) (which is hard to fell) with the fully sharpened axe of sound discerning reasoning (*sattarka*) (that has attained the highest level)."

Jayaratha explains (lines 1626–1704): "Duality consisting of subject and object etc. is the sole cause of bondage and so is 'harmful.' It is (like) a tree because it is hard to uproot." Sound Reasoning is "different from other (lower forms of) reasoning" — it is the one that "has attained the supreme eminence (*koṭi*) of insight." Emerging "from the intellect sanctified by (its) contact with the aspect (of consciousness) which is Pure Knowledge," it brings about "the recognition of one's own (true) nature."

The "root" severed is "ignorance (*akhyāti*)" — severed "in such a way that this (tree which is the) ignorance of full (all-encompassing) and supreme pure consciousness may not arise again" (lines 1644–1646).

---

#### Verse 14 — From Reasoning to Contemplation

**Sanskrit:**
```
sa eva bhāvanāyogaḥ parām arhati saṃpadam |
yāvad asphuṭarūpeyaṁ saṃvit sphuṭatarā bhavet || 14 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1707–1719):** "That (very Sound Reasoning), which is the discipline of contemplation (*bhāvanāyoga*), attains the supreme state (when) this (initially) unclear consciousness becomes more clear."

Jayaratha comments (lines 1720–1740): "Sound Reasoning (*sattarka*) when developed to its most excellent (<-> degree), is the discipline of contemplation (*bhāvanā*), of which the fruit is the supreme state." The *Kālīkulakrama* (line 1724) is quoted: "Discernment is classified as twofold — that which is contingent on scripture and that which is based on Yogic insight." Both lead to the same goal.

---

#### Verse 15 — The Qualities of One Who Reasons Soundly

**Sanskrit:**
```
avipralambhī satkāryavicāraikaparo yataḥ |
na kiñcid api jānāti tena yuktyā na mucyate || 15 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1742–1765):** "He who is honest (i.e. does not deceive himself), solely intent on examining (the nature of) reality, (knows well that) he does not know anything (about his true Śiva nature). Thus because he is endowed with this (kind of) reasoning, he is not (just) liberated — (he is the Supreme Lord Himself)."

Jayaratha explains: such a person "has not even the slightest trace of 'I know something' with regard to reality, because the essential nature of the Supreme Lord cannot be known as an object." Paradoxically, it is this honest confession of ignorance that constitutes liberation — for it is the recognition that the Self cannot be objectified, and "He whose nature is such that he is not (just) liberated (anymore, because he is now the Supreme Lord) — he is the Supreme Lord, the Supreme Bhairava" (lines 1769–1783).

---

#### Verse 16 — The Lord's Nature is Sound Reasoning

**Sanskrit:**
```
ity āhuḥ śivayoginaḥ svahṛdayākhyānena nānāvidhaṁ |
jātijñānavivarjitāś ca satataṁ bhāvāśraye na sthitāḥ |
dhyānādhyānaparaṁparāparigate citte samāveśitaṁ |
tattvaṁ tattvavido vidanti śivayor dharmāṅganānām api || 16 ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation (lines 1801–1829):** "Thus the Śivayogis, by (this) revelation of their own heart, speak variously. (They) are free of (the limitation of) class and (limited) knowledge and so are never settled in a state of reliance on phenomenal existence. (Their) consciousness, (by the power of the practice of Sound Reasoning), enters into a succession of meditations and deeper meditations, and so the knowers of reality know the reality of Śiva and His Energies."

### The Nature of Sattarka as Śuddhavidyā

Swami Lakshmanjoo explains (KNP notes, summarized in lines 1598–1614): The classic formulation of the experience at this level is the insight: "I am this" (*aham idam*) and "this am I" (*idam aham*). There is a "common base (*sāmānyādhikaraṇya*) of consciousness on which subject ('I') and object ('this') rest, equally balanced as aspects of the same consciousness, like 'the evenly weighted scales of a balance.'"

*Sattarka* has a two-fold quality: it is both "the process of the 'purification of thought' that culminates in the insight which sustains it and underlies it," and the insight itself. This dual nature is essential: "Where it not so there would be no possibility of thought, reason or insight" (lines 1605–1614).

### The Six Levels of Clarity Connected to Sattarka

Although *sattarka* is presented as the method of purification, it maps directly onto the six levels of clarity taught in verses 4–5:

1. **Unclear** (*asphuṭa*) — initial engagement with the teaching
2. **About to become clear** (*sphuṭatābhāvin*) — dawning comprehension
3. **Becoming clear** (*prasphuṭan*) — insight developing
4. **Clear** (*sphuṭita*) — stable understanding
5. **More clear** (*sphuṭatara*) — deepening realization
6. **Supremely clear** (*sphuṭatama*) — perfect identification with Śiva

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Beyond Dry Logic**: *Sattarka* is distinguished from the "dry logic" (*śuṣkatarka*) of the Nyāya school. It is "sound or true reasoning focused on the highest reality and truth — *sat* in the highest sense" (lines 1560–1562). The *Śivasūtra* (1/18) declares: "Right discernment is the knowledge of the Self" (line 1563).

2. **Sound Reasoning as Grace**: The progression from unclear to clear notions is not purely intellectual. "Sound Reasoning is Pure Knowledge itself, and that is the will of the Supreme Lord" (4/34). It operates through the descent of Śiva's power (*śaktipāta*) and grows as the innate intelligence (*pratibhā*) of consciousness comes to the fore (lines 5346–5356).

3. **The Reversal of Māyā**: Māyā induces the error of duality. *Sattarka* is "the application of that same freedom that grasps the truth of oneness" (lines 1583–1587). Subject and object persist but not as separate realities — they are known as aspects of a single consciousness.

4. **Epistemological Humility**: The paradox of verse 15 — "he does not know anything" — underscores that the Supreme Lord "cannot be known as an object." True knowledge is the recognition that the Self is not an object of knowledge but the very light by which all things are known.
