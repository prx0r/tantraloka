# Topic 95: Māyā and Pure Knowledge; Śiva as Prototype and Reflection

## Catalog Reference
- **Topic**: 95 of 161
- **Section**: Volume 2 — Chapter 3: Paropāya (The Supreme Means)
- **Verses**: TĀ 3/10-15

---

## 1. Core Teaching

Śiva manifests as both the prototype (bimba) and the reflection (pratibimba). The entire universe shines through this relationship. Māyā is the principle of limitation that makes the one appear as many. Pure knowledge (śuddhavidyā) is the recognition that the reflection is not other than the prototype. The world is a real reflection within consciousness — not an illusion, but not independent either.

## 2. Key Verses

**TĀ 3/10-15** (approximately lines 6036-6150): *vibhāti varado bimbapratibimbadṛśākhile*
The Boon-giver (Śiva) shines in all things through the relation of prototype and reflection.

## 3. Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the refutation of Sāṁkhya** (lines 5277-5331): "Abhinava systematically refutes the Sāṁkhya view that consciousness is reflected in the intellect. The problem is that Prakṛti and its products are insentient (jaḍa), quite the opposite of consciousness. Abhinava accepts the mechanism of perception as the Sāṁkhya describes it, but insists that it is only possible if consciousness itself is all the reality levels and that they are essentially its energies.

## 5. Philosophical Context

This teaching is part of Abhinavagupta's systematic exposition of the Supreme Means (Śāmbhavopāya) in Chapter 3 of the Tantrāloka. It integrates the Doctrine of Reflection with the alphabet mysticism of the Tantric traditions, showing how the structures of language embody the structures of consciousness.

## 6. Visual Concepts

- **The mirror of consciousness** with all phenomena reflected within it
- **The fifty letters** arranged as a maṇḍala of energies
- **AHAṀ** as the encompassing cycle of self-awareness
- **The five-fold bindu** as the pentad of divine activities
- **The four levels of speech** from transcendent silence to uttered sound
