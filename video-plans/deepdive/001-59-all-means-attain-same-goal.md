# ALL MEANS ATTAIN THE SAME GOAL (SARVOPĀYA EKAPHALA)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 225cd–228ab

---

### Core Teaching

Although the three means (Śāmbhava, Śākta, Āṇava) appear distinct — one free of thought, one operating through thought, one dependent on outer supports — they all lead to the same goal: the recognition of one's identity with Śiva. The difference is not in the destination but in the speed and manner of the journey. Even the most dualistic practice culminates in thought-free consciousness.

**Line references:** TĀ 1/225cd–228ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 366–368.

---

### The Validity of Knowledge Free of Thought (Verse 227cd–228ab)

**Sanskrit:**
```
vikalpāpekṣayā yo 'pi prāmāṇyaṁ prāha tanmate |
tadvikalpakramopāttanirvikalpapramāṇatā ||
```
**Dyczkowski Translation:** "Even he who says that the (assessment of) the validity of knowledge (free of thought constructs) depends on thought, in that view (also), the validity of knowledge that is free of thought constructs is attained by means of a series of thoughts."

Jayaratha: Even the Buddhist Vaibhāṣikas who hold that the validity of nondiscursive knowledge depends on discursive thought must admit that the nondiscursive state is attained through a progressive series of thought constructs. This applies equally to the three means.

---

### The Progressive Refinement

The three means correspond to three levels of the subject's engagement:

1. **Śāmbhavopāya**: The pure subject knows itself directly — instantaneous
2. **Śāktopāya**: The subject refines its thought constructs — progressive, mental
3. **Āṇavopāya**: The subject uses outer supports — progressive, physical

---

### One Consciousness, Three Approaches

Jayaratha explains: The difference is not in the reality attained but in the intensity and directness of the means. The power of grace (*śaktipāta*) flows through all three. A practitioner on the Āṇava path who recites mantras with devotion arrives at the same Śiva-consciousness as one who rests in thought-free awareness.

---

### The Example of the Jeweler (Later in the Text)

In verses 228cd–229ab, Abhinava gives the example of a jeweler who does not know a gem's value at a glance. He must deliberate, compare, test — yet finally arrives at the same certainty as the expert who knew instantly. The three means are like this: different speeds, same destination.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Unity of Liberation**: There is not one liberation for the Śāmbhava practitioner and another for the Āṇava practitioner. Liberation is one — the recognition of one's identity with Śiva.

2. **No Hierarchy of Value**: The Āṇavopāya is not "inferior" — it is simply slower. All means are valid expressions of Śiva's grace.

3. **Grace Flows Through All**: The power of grace descends according to the capacity of the recipient. The means adapt to the practitioner, not the other way around.

4. **Practical Inclusivity**: Abhinava's vision is radically inclusive. Every genuine spiritual path, whether it uses mantra, ritual, thought, or pure awareness, leads to the same goal.
