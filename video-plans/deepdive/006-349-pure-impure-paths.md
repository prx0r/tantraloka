# 349 — The Pure Path and the Impure Path
**Tantrāloka 9.55cd–61** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
Between the realm of pure consciousness and the world of suffering lies a single pivot: Māyā. Understanding the two paths is the key to the entire tattva system.

## Core Teaching
The 36 tattvas divide into two fundamental paths: the pure (*śuddha*) path of 5 principles (Śiva through Śuddhavidyā) and the impure (*aśuddha*) path of 31 principles (Māyā through Earth). The transition at Māyā is the "knot" (*granthi*) that binds consciousness to limitation.

## Verse 9.60 — The Pure Path
The pure path consists of the five principles beginning with Śiva. These are beyond time, necessity, attachment, limited knowledge, and limited agency. Beings who abide here experience non-duality.

## Verse 9.61 — The Impure Path
The impure path is emitted by Aghoreśa (Śiva's fierce aspect). It comprises:
- Māyā (the power of limitation)
- The five *kañcukas* (coverings)
- Puruṣa (individual soul)
- Prakṛti (nature) and its evolutes
- The 25 Sāṁkhya tattvas

## The Function of Māyā
Māyā is not an illusion in the Vedāntic sense. It is the power of Śiva to contract His infinite nature into finite forms. The "knot of Māyā" (*māyāgranthi*) is the point where universal consciousness becomes individual consciousness.

## Why the Pure/Impure Distinction Matters
1. **For soteriology**: liberation means ascending from impure to pure, then transcending even the pure path
2. **For epistemology**: the pure principles are known through recognition (*pratyabhijñā*); the impure through ordinary perception and inference
3. **For ritual**: initiation (*dīkṣā*) purifies the soul through all levels of both paths

## Connection to Mala and Karma
The impure path is where *mala* (impurity) and karma operate. The pure path is where they are absent. Liberation (*mokṣa*) is the soul's return through the impure path to its natural state on the pure path.

## Jayaratha's Explanation
"Aghoreśa" — Śiva in his non-terrifying aspect — emits the impure universe out of his own freedom. The impure is not a fall but a deliberate self-limitation, serving both bondage and the possibility of liberation.
