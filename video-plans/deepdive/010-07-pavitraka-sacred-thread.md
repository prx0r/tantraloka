# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Pavitraka — The Sacred Thread Rite

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 10, Chapter 28, Verses 112-186ab (~lines 3300-4100)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
The sacred thread (pavitraka) is one of the most important occasional rites in Trika Kaulism. Abhinava presents three distinct versions from three authoritative texts — the basic procedure, the Kularatnamālā version, and the Triśirobhairavatantra version — showing both the common structure and the sectarian variations of this essential observance.

## Part 1: The Basic Procedure (0:45-3:00)
- Verses 112-127ab: The elementary form of the pavitraka rite
- The thread is made of cotton, wool, or kusa grass, depending on the purpose
- **Structure:** Three knots are tied, each corresponding to one of the Trika goddesses (Parā, Parāparā, Aparā)
- The thread is consecrated with the seed-syllables of the three goddesses
- The number of threads and knots varies:
  - 3 threads for basic observance
  - 5 threads for siddhi purposes
  - 7 threads for liberation seekers
- The thread is worn around the wrist (right for men, left for women)
- The rite is performed at specific times: 4 times, 3 times, twice, or at least once a year
- Quotation from Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa: "He who does not fashion the sacred thread four times... is without fruit"

## Part 2: The Kularatnamālā Version (3:00-5:00)
- Verses 127cd-146: A more elaborate version
- This text provides additional seed-mantras for the knots
- The thread is consecrated with the Navātmā mantra
- The colors of the threads have specific meanings:
  - White = purity (sattva)
  - Red = energy (rajas)
  - Yellow or black = stability (tamas)
- The rite includes offerings to the teacher, the deities, and the Yoginīs
- The thread is not just worn — it is worshipped as a manifestation of the goddess's power

## Part 3: The Triśirobhairavatantra Version (5:00-7:00)
- Verses 146-186ab: The most detailed version
- This text specifies:
  - The exact measurements of the threads
  - The specific mantras for each knot (using the Mālinī code)
  - The number of strands for different castes and levels of initiation
  - The occasions when the thread should be changed
- Additional elements:
  - The thread is anointed with sandalwood paste and saffron
  - The thread is offered to the deities before being worn
  - The rite concludes with a homa (fire offering)
  - The teacher receives a special thread as guru-dakṣiṇā

## Part 4: The Symbolic Meaning (7:00-9:30)
- The pavitraka is not merely a ritual object — it is a "binding" (bandhana) of the initiate's commitment
- The three threads represent:
  1. The three goddesses (Parā, Parāparā, Aparā)
  2. The three śaktis (icchā, jñāna, kriyā)
  3. The three guṇas (sattva, rajas, tamas)
  4. The three times (past, present, future)
- The knots represent the binding of the three impurities (āṇava, māyīya, kārma)
- Wearing the thread is a constant reminder of one's initiation and commitment
- The thread is "the vessel that carries the vitality of mantra"
- Without it, ritual action is incomplete: "pavitrakaṁ samastādhyaparipūrṇatvabhāvanāt"

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The pavitraka rite exemplifies Abhinava's method: presenting a common ritual in multiple versions from different source texts, showing its variations while preserving its essential meaning. The thread literally "ties" the initiate to the tradition. The next video examines the remaining occasional rites and the worship on teachers' birth and death days.
