## Central Thesis
The Instruments of Yoga (karaṇa) are the bodily postures and physical practices that constitute the outermost, most objective layer of Āṇavopāya. They are the farthest from consciousness but equally valid as means, serving those whose purification requires physical engagement before subtler practices become accessible.

- Outer practice is necessary when inner purification is insufficient — lines 854–859
- The body is the "aggregate" (samghāta) — the collection of elements — lines 353–354
- Ritual procedures anchor the mind in physical action
- The "vitality" (vīrya) of consciousness must infuse all outer practices — lines 155cd–160
- Without vitality, practices are like a eunuch or corpse — lines 155cd–160

### 4. The Hierarchy of Practice (6:00–8:00)
- The highest yogi progresses by "crossing over" stages — line 155cd–160
- The dull intellect must proceed in order — line 155cd–160
- Karaṇa prepares the body for subtler breath practices
- Breath practices prepare for intellectual meditation
- Meditation leads to direct recognition

### 5. The Vitality Principle (8:00–9:30)
- All practices depend on the vitality of consciousness (vīrya) — lines 155cd–160
- "If the conception of this means, whether internal or external, is devoid of the vitality of consciousness, it is as powerless as a eunuch" — lines 155cd–160
- The vitality is the recognition of one's identity with Śiva
- Outer form without inner recognition is mechanical and ineffective

### 6. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- Karaṇa are the most accessible entry point for embodied practitioners
- They are not ends in themselves but supports for recognition
- Even the body is a form of consciousness when rightly understood
