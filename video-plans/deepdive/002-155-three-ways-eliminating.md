# THREE WAYS OF ELIMINATING CREATION, PERSISTENCE AND DESTRUCTION
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 2, Chapter 3, Verses 259cd–260ab

---

### Core Teaching

Having described the twelve Kālikās and their operations across creation, persistence, and withdrawal, Abhinavagupta now turns to the ultimate question: how does one transcend these limiting adjuncts (*upādhi*) entirely? The form that transcends them is of two types: either the limiting adjuncts never emerge at all (*anullāsa*), or they cease to be (*praśama*). The first corresponds to the state of absolute latency — the "waveless lake" of the supreme principle at the initial phase (*prākkoṭi*), where no differentiation has yet stirred. The second corresponds to the destruction of adjuncts that have already arisen. This distinction frames two radically different paths to liberation: the rare state of primordial non-emergence and the more practically relevant process of cessation through specific means.

**Line references:** TĀ 3/259cd–260ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 381–382, lines 27584–27605.

### The Form That Transcends Limiting Adjuncts (Verses 259cd–260ab)

**Line references:** 27584–27605.

**Sanskrit:**
```
upādhyatītaṁ yad rūpaṁ tad dvidhā guravo jaguḥ |
anullāsād upādhīnāṁ yad vā praśamayogataḥ || 259cd-260ab ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:** "The teachers have said that the form which transcends (these) limiting adjuncts is of two types, according to whether limiting adjuncts do not emerge, or due to (their) cessation (*praśama*)."

**Explanation:** The qualification "the teachers have said" invokes the authority of the Kaula and Krama lineages. The two alternatives address fundamentally different spiritual situations: one in which limitation has never arisen (the state of Śiva in his primordial purity), and one in which limitation has arisen and must be dissolved.

---

### Anullāsa: The Non-Emergence of Limiting Adjuncts

The first type, *anullāsa* (non-emergence), is the prior non-existence (*prāgabhāva*) of the limiting adjuncts. In the initial phase (*prākkoṭi*), the supreme principle rests in itself like a waveless lake (*niṣprakampahrada*). Limiting adjuncts only arise from that state when, by its own freedom (*svātantrya*), consciousness assumes a state propense to externalization (*bahirmukhatā*). This non-emergence has no beginning but can come to an end when the impulse toward manifestation stirs.

Jayaratha explains that this alternative, while doctrinally meaningful, is "completely discarded" for practical purposes, because what is required for bound souls is the passing away of adjuncts that have *already* arisen. The path of *anullāsa* belongs to Śiva alone, not to the embodied practitioner.

---

### Praśama: Cessation of Limiting Adjuncts

The second type, *praśama* (cessation), is the subsequent non-existence (*pradhvaṁsābhāva*) of limiting adjuncts — the state attained after creation, persistence, and withdrawal have been dissolved. Unlike *anullāsa*, this cessation has a beginning (when the process of dissolution begins) but no end (once liberation is attained).

This *praśama* is further divided into two kinds — peaceful cessation (*śānti*, *madhurapāka*) and violent digestion (*haṭhapāka*) — which Abhinavagupta develops in the following verses. The present verse thus establishes the framework: the transcendence of limiting adjuncts is possible through either primordial non-manifestation or active cessation, and the latter admits of both gradual and sudden approaches.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Two Models of Liberation**: The *anullāsa* model describes liberation as the primordial, never-lost nature of Śiva — a perspective that grounds the path of recognition (*pratyabhijñā*). The *praśama* model addresses the actual situation of bound souls, for whom adjuncts must be actively dissolved.

2. **Non-Existence as a Real Category**: The use of *prāgabhāva* (prior non-existence) and *pradhvaṁsābhāva* (subsequent non-existence) draws on Nyāya-Vaiśeṣika ontological categories, but Abhinavagupta re-deploys them within a nondual framework where the only true "existence" is the light of consciousness itself.

3. **The Waveless Lake of Consciousness**: The image of the supreme principle as a waveless lake is one of the Tantrāloka's most evocative metaphors for the state before any stirring of differentiation — a state not of blankness but of dynamic, self-contained potential.

4. **Practical Priority of Cessation**: For the Trika practitioner, the path is not the impossible task of remaining in primordial non-emergence but the active dissolution of adjuncts that have already arisen — a dissolution that can be either gradual (peaceful) or sudden (violent).

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary Insights

Jayaratha emphasizes that the three limiting adjuncts (creation, persistence, destruction) pass away through specific means. The first method (*anullāsa*) is "very difficult" and "completely discarded" because bound souls require the dissolution of adjuncts that have already arisen. For that, the means called "peaceful" exists: it operates slowly, under a weak descent of grace (*mandaśaktipāta*), and brings about the passing away of adjuncts only at death. The third form of cessation — brought about by violent digestion (*haṭhapāka*) — operates under the intense descent of grace (*tīvraśaktipāta*) and destroys limiting adjuncts immediately. Grace is essential, but so is the cooperation of the recipient, whether through observances or through the purity and openness required to receive the most intense liberating grace.
