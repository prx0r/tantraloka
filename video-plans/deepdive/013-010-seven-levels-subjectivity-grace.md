# The Ascent Through Seven Levels of Subjectivity by Grace
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

---

### Core Teaching

Grace operates by awakening discrimination (viveka), which progressively refines the sense of "I" through seven levels of subjectivity. These seven levels — from the fully conditioned (sakala) subject to Śiva himself — correspond to the degrees of contraction and expansion of consciousness. Grace does not add anything new to the soul — it removes the obscuration that prevents the soul from recognizing its own depth. As the "I" expands through these levels, the soul's identity shifts from the body (sakala) to the universal subject (Śiva).

---

### Key Verses

#### Verses 13.271cd-276ab — The Seven Levels

**Lines approximately 16200–16400:**

The seven perceivers (pramātṛ) ascended by grace are:

1. **Sakala** — the "fully conditioned" subject identified with the body, senses, and ego. Bondage is most intense here. Grace begins by turning this subject toward spiritual life.

2. **Pralayākala** — the "deconditioned by dissolution" subject. This one has transcended the body through deep meditation or pralaya (cosmic dissolution) but still retains the subtle seeds of individuality.

3. **Vijñānākala** — the "knowledge-conditioned" subject. This one has realized the difference between consciousness and its objects but still maintains a sense of separate knower.

4. **Mantra** — the subject at the level of mantric awareness. Here, the "I" is experienced as a vibration (spanda) rather than a static entity.

5. **Mantreśvara** — the "lord of mantras." The subject realizes itself as the source of all mantras, the power of articulation inherent in consciousness.

6. **Mantramaheśvara** — the "great lord of mantras." The subject knows itself as the totality of all mantric energies, the fullness of divine speech.

7. **Śiva** — the absolute subject. Pure I-consciousness without any objectification. The end of the ascent.

---

#### Grace Refines the "I"

At each level, grace operates by:
1. **Refining the sense of "I"** — removing the identification with that level
2. **Revealing the next level** — disclosing a deeper subjectivity
3. **Dissolving the boundary** — between the current "I" and its source

This is not a process of addition but of subtraction. Grace does not give the soul something new — it removes the limitations that prevent the soul from knowing itself as it is.

---

#### The Ascent is Spontaneous

Jayaratha emphasizes that this ascent is not accomplished by effort (yatna) but by grace (anugraha). The soul does not "climb" these levels through its own power — grace lifts it. However, the soul must be receptive, and this receptivity is itself a form of grace.

The seven levels correspond to the seven states of consciousness in the Śaiva tradition:
1. Wakefulness (jāgrat) — sakala
2. Dream (svapna) — pralayākala
3. Deep sleep (suṣupti) — vijñānākala
4. The fourth (turya) — mantra
5. Beyond the fourth (turyātīta) — mantreśvara
6. The state beyond that — mantramaheśvara
7. The absolute — Śiva

### Narrative Arc

**Opening (0:00-1:30):** Grace does not just give knowledge — it transforms the one who knows. The seven levels of subjectivity.

**Segment 1 (1:30-3:30):** Sakala, pralayākala, vijñānākala — the three lower perceivers. From body-identification to knowledge.

**Segment 2 (3:30-5:30):** Mantra, mantreśvara, mantramaheśvara — the three mantric levels. The "I" as vibration, source, and totality.

**Segment 3 (5:30-7:30):** Śiva — the absolute subject. The end of the ascent, which is also the beginning.

**Segment 4 (7:30-9:30):** Grace as subtraction, not addition. Each level is a veil removed, not a stage achieved.

**Conclusion (9:30-10:00):** The seven levels are the soul's own depth. Grace plumbs that depth until nothing remains but the light of pure I-consciousness.
