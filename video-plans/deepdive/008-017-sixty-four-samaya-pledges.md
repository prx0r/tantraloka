# The 64 Samaya Pledges: The Rule of Freedom

**Tantrāloka 15.525cd–617cd | Lines 21901–23500**

## Overview

The pledges (samaya) are the ethical and spiritual commitments that the initiate undertakes. They are not external impositions but expressions of the inner recognition of one's identity with Śiva. To break them is not to "sin" but to contradict one's own nature. The eight groups of eight pledges (64 total) cover behavior toward the teacher, toward other initiates, toward the deities, and toward the world.

## Key Verses

- **525cd–535ab (lines 667–668):** The eight groups of eight according to the Devyāyāmala.
- **538–544ab (lines 672–673):** Rules for the disciple's behavior with the teacher — obedience, secrecy, service.
- **544cd–549 (line 674):** The pledges according to the Triśirobhairava.
- **555cd–557ab (line 677):** According to the Trikasāra.
- **557cd–566 (line 678):** According to the Gamatantra.
- **567–574 (line 679):** According to the Ūrmikaulārṇava.
- **575–594ab (line 683):** According to the Mādhavakula.
- **594cd–596 (line 685):** According to the Ānandaśāstra.
- **597–609 (line 690):** Rules from various Trika and Bhairava Tantras.
- **610–617cd (line 692):** Conclusion of the pledges.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Samaya as vow of non-contradiction:** The word samaya means "agreement," "convention," "rule." But Abhinavagupta interprets it metaphysically: to observe the samaya is to remain in the conviction (abhimāna) that one IS Śiva. Breaking the samaya is forgetting this identity.

2. **The eight groups — completeness:** Eight is the number of completeness (aṣṭaka). The 64 pledges (8×8) cover every dimension of the initiate's life — the four directions and four intermediate directions of one's existence.

3. **Secrecy and transmission:** Many pledges concern the secrecy of the teaching. The mantras and practices must not be revealed to the uninitiated — not because they are "secret" in the sense of hidden, but because they are only effective when received in the proper transmission lineage (guru-śiṣya-paramparā).

4. **Consequences of transgression:** To transgress the samaya is to become a flesh-eating demon (kravyāda) for a hundred years. This is not a punishment but a natural consequence — one who consciously breaks identity with Śiva falls into a state of contraction and feeds on the dead matter of objectivity.

## Internal vs. External Observance

The highest observance of the samaya is not the external rule but the internal abiding in the recognition of one's nature. For one whose realization is stable (sthitaprajña), all pledges are spontaneously fulfilled. For one still ascending, the external rule is a necessary support.
