# Varieties of Gradations and the Trika Triad
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1, Verses 186cd-189

---

### Core Teaching

The penetration (samāveśa) into Śiva consciousness is described in the Mālinīvijayottara as having fifty varieties, based on how the three clearly apparent categories — the Person (Nara), Knowledge (Vidyā), and Power (Śakti) — are discerned and conjoined amongst the thirty-six metaphysical principles (tattva). These three principles constitute the Trika Triad that pervades the entire series of principles, and they are distinguished as impure, being purified, and pure, respectively.

---

### Fifty Varieties of Penetration (Verses 186cd-187ab)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
pañcāśadvidhatā cāsya samāveśasya varṇitā |
tattvaṣaṭtriṁśakaitatsthasphuṭabhedābhiṣandhitaḥ || 186cd-187ab ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Fifty varieties of this penetration are described (in the Mālinīvijayottara-tantra) in accord with (how) these (three) clearly apparent categories (bheda), located amongst the thirty-six metaphysical principles (tattva), are discerned (and conjoined together) (abhisandhitaḥ)."

Jayaratha explains: The fifty varieties come from MV 2/17-19:
1. **Gross elements** (bhūta) — 5 kinds
2. **Metaphysical principles** (tattva) — 30 kinds
3. **The Self** (ātman) — 3 kinds
4. **Mantra** (vidyā) — 10 kinds
5. **Power** (śakti) — 2 kinds

The synthetic contemplation (anusandhāna) of these categories, located within the thirty-six principles, is the cause of these forms of penetration.

---

### The Trika Triad: Person, Knowledge, Power (Verses 187cd-189ab)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
etat tattvāntare yat puṁvidyāśaktyātmakaṁ trayam |
ambhodhikāṣṭhājvalanasaṁkhyair bhedair yataḥ kramāt || 187cd-188ab ||

puṅvidyāśaktisaṁjñaṁ yat tat sarvavyāpakaṁ yataḥ |
avyāpakebhyastenedaṁ bhedena gaṇitaṁ kila || 188cd-189ab ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"This is so because the three, namely, the Person (Nara), Knowledge (Vidyā) and Power (Śakti), that are amongst these (thirty-six) metaphysical principles (and span them), are of four, ten and three different kinds (bheda), respectively." (187cd-188ab)

"The three called the Person, Knowledge and Power pervade the entire (series of thirty-six metaphysical principles). Thus, they have been reckoned apart from the other (principles), which are not pervasive." (188cd-189ab)

---

### The Three Principles Explained

**Person (Pumān/Nara)** — 4 kinds:
Conditioned (Sakala), Dissolution-Deconditioned (Pralayākala), Consciousness-Deconditioned (Vijñānākala), and Pure (Śuddha). Pervades from Earth to Māyā.

**Knowledge (Vidyā)** — 10 kinds:
The phases of OM and seed-syllable mantras: Letter (varṇa), Point (bindu), Half Moon (ardhacandra), Obstructress (nirodhinī), Sound (nāda), End of Sound (nādānta), Power (śakti), the Pervasive One (vyāpinī), the Equal One (samanā), and the Transmental (unmanā). Pervades from Māyā to Sadāśiva.

**Power (Śakti)** — 3 kinds:
Will (icchā), Knowledge (jñāna), and Action (kriyā). Pervades from Sadāśiva to Śiva.

---

### The Triad Distinguished by Purity (Verse 189cd)

**Sanskrit (IAST):**
```
aśuddhiśuddhyamānatvaśuddhitastu mitho 'pi tat || 189 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"They are also mutually (related and yet distinguished) as (the Person, which is the principle that spans) the impure (principles), (Knowledge, those) in the process of being purified (and so are pure-cum-impure), and (Power, the one that) is pure."

Jayaratha: The Person is 'impure' because the principles up to Māyā consist of duality (bheda). Knowledge is 'in the process of being purified' because the principles from Śuddhavidyā to Sadāśiva consist of unity-in-diversity (bhedābheda). Power is 'pure' because it consists of oneness (abheda).

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary Insights

- The fifty samāveśas correspond to the fifty forms of reflective awareness (parāmarśa) constituting the recognition of AHAṀ of Bhairava (Chapter 3, verses 274-280)
- The Trika triad (Person, Knowledge, Power) pervades all principles, unlike the other principles which do not pervade each other
- Pervasion: Person extends Earth → Māyā; Knowledge extends Māyā → Sadāśiva; Power extends Sadāśiva → Śiva
- These three are not only separate from the other thirty-six principles but also from one another
- In ritual (initiation), these correspond to three paths of the soul's journey to union with Śiva: Forces (kalā), Principles (tattva), and World-orders (bhuvana)

---

### The Three States of Consciousness

| Principle | Pervasion | Nature | State of Consciousness |
|-----------|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| Person (Nara) | Earth → Māyā | Duality (bheda) | Impure |
| Knowledge (Vidyā) | Māyā → Sadāśiva | Unity-in-diversity (bhedābheda) | Being purified |
| Power (Śakti) | Sadāśiva → Śiva | Oneness (abheda) | Pure |

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Trika Structure**: Reality is triadic — Śiva (Power), Śakti (Knowledge), and Nara (Person). Each is a perspective on the one reality, and each corresponds to a means to realization.

2. **Pervasion and Hierarchy**: The three principles pervade all levels of reality, from the grossest to the subtlest. The path of liberation is the ascent through these levels.

3. **From Duality to Oneness**: The progression from Person (duality) through Knowledge (unity-in-diversity) to Power (pure oneness) maps the yogi's journey from contracted to expanded consciousness.

4. **Fifty as Fullness**: The fifty varieties of penetration represent the complete range of contemplative possibilities — a complete mandala of realization.
