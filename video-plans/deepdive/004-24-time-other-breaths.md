## Central Thesis
The four subsidiary vital breaths (apāna, samāna, udāna, vyāna) are not merely physiological functions — each is a complete temporal system with its own cycles, deities, and cosmic projections. By investigating time in each breath, the yogi realizes that all of time is pervaded by the single timeless consciousness.

## Script Outline

### 0. Opening: Beyond Prāṇa (0:00–1:30)
- Prāṇa (exhalation) is the most visible breath, but there are four others — line 471
- Each of the five breaths has its own domain and temporal rhythm
- The subsidiary breaths govern digestion, circulation, ascending energy, etc.
- Each contains its own projection of cosmic time

### 2. Apāna — The Inhaled Breath (1:30–3:00)
- TĀ 6.186–195 — lines 471
- Apāna descends from the End of Twelve to the Heart, then to the lower body
- It governs elimination and the downward movement
- Its time-cycle: the night, the dark fortnight, the waning year
- The Six Causal Deities in the body — lines 473–475
  - Associated with the six paths of transmigration
  - Located in the lower body from navel to perineum

### 3. The Lower Mouth (3:00–4:30)
- TĀ 6.194–195 — line 477
- The "lower mouth" (adharavaktra) = the perineum/genital centre
- This is where apāna completes its cycle
- It is also a dvādaśānta — the "lower End of Twelve"
- The lower mouth is the gate to the ancestors (pitṛyāna)
- Time here is measured in the descent of the life force

### 4. Samāna — The Equalizing Breath (4:30–6:00)
- TĀ 6.196–199 — lines 478–480
- Samāna circulates in the navel region, governing digestion
- It equalizes prāṇa and apāna — lines 3767–3769
- Its time-cycle: the equinoxes (viṣuvat) — lines 482–483
- The equinoxes are the points where day and night are equal
- In the breath, samāna is the moment of retention where all dualities are suspended — lines 4808–4811

### 5. The Circulation and Equinoxes (6:00–7:30)
- TĀ 6.200–208 — lines 482–483
- The circulation of samāna through the body follows the equinoctial rhythm
- When prāṇa and apāna are balanced, the breath enters suṣumnā
- This is the "inner equinox" — the moment of liberation
- The circulation of consciousness through all channels is "the pervasion of the equalizing breath"

### 6. Udāna and Vyāna (7:30–9:00)
- Udāna (TĀ 6.212–214ab) — line 487
  - The ascending breath that rises through suṣumnā
  - Governs the upward movement of consciousness
  - Its time: the moment of death and transcendence
- Vyāna (TĀ 6.214cd–216ab) — line 488
  - The pervasive breath that circulates through all channels
  - Governs the circulation of all energies
  - Its time: the timeless present that pervades all moments

### 7. Integration (9:00–9:30)
- The five breaths = five dimensions of time
  - Prāṇa: solar, diurnal
  - Apāna: lunar, nocturnal
  - Samāna: equinoctial, balancing
  - Udāna: ascendant, liberating
  - Vyāna: pervasive, all-containing
- Together they constitute the complete temporal body

### 8. Conclusion (9:30–10:00)
- All five breaths are modalities of consciousness
- Each has its own time, yet all are contained within the single pulse
- The yogi who investigates time in all breaths discovers the timeless
