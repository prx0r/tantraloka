# THE CREATED AND UNCREATED TEACHERS (KALPITA / AKALPITA GURU)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 69cd–78ab

---

### Core Teaching

After the spontaneously enlightened and the mixed types, Abhinavagupta describes the lower grades of teachers: the *kalpitākalpita* (created-cum-uncreated), the *kalpita* (created), and the *akalpita* (uncreated — a second type distinct from the *sāṁsiddhika*). These teachers have received a less intense descent of grace and have required more external support — initiation from another teacher, study of scripture, and practice of various means. The distinctions are based on the degree to which the teacher's realization is "created" (*kalpita*) through external means versus "uncreated" (*akalpita*) — spontaneously present. The key principle is that even the lowest type of authentic teacher possesses genuine knowledge of reality, albeit in a less developed form.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/69cd–78ab; Dyczkowski translation pp. 87–93, lines 6690–7135.

---

### The Created-cum-Uncreated Teacher (Kalpitākalpita)

**Line references:** 6690–6723.

This teacher is "created" in the sense that he has received formal initiation and instruction from another teacher, but "uncreated" in possessing genuine realization that has dawned through that instruction. Jayaratha explains: The external means (initiation, scripture, practice) are the occasion for the grace to descend, and the realization that arises is authentic even though it was catalyzed by external factors.

This type corresponds to the majority of authentic teachers in the tradition — those who have been formally initiated and have practiced assiduously, culminating in genuine insight.

---

### The Created Teacher (Kalpita) — Verses 70cd–72ab

**Line references:** 6724–6860.

The *kalpita* teacher is "formed" entirely through external means. His realization is primarily intellectual — derived from study of scripture and instruction — and has not yet become fully internalized as direct experience. However, he is distinguished from a false teacher because his understanding is correct (*samyag-jñāna*) even if his realization is not fully mature.

Jayaratha emphasizes: such a teacher should not be dismissed, for "even the knowledge derived from scripture is a form of grace." He can guide disciples up to the level of his own realization, and through teaching, his own understanding may deepen.

---

### The Uncreated Teacher (Akalpita) — Verses 73cd–78ab

**Line references:** 6901–7135.

This second type of *akalpita* (uncreated) teacher is distinct from the *sāṁsiddhika* (spontaneously enlightened). The *sāṁsiddhika* was uncreated in the sense that no external means were needed at all. This second *akalpita* is uncreated in the sense that his realization is not the product of human effort or instruction — it arises directly from Śiva's grace — but he may still have engaged in practice and study.

Jayaratha explains the distinction: "The *sāṁsiddhika* is uncreated because he is such by nature; this second *akalpita* is uncreated because his realization, though arising through practice, is ultimately recognized as the ever-present nature of consciousness, not a creation of practice."

| 2 | Uncreated-cum-created | Akalpitakalpaka | Mixed | Self-contemplation + grace |
| 3 | Created-cum-uncreated | Kalpitākalpita | Mixed | Initiation + genuine insight |
| 4 | Created | Kalpita | Created | Scripture and instruction |
| 5 | Uncreated (second type) | Akalpita (dvitīya) | Uncreated | Direct grace (through practice) |

---

### The Principle of Hierarchical Inclusion

Jayaratha notes that the types are not mutually exclusive but hierarchical: each higher type includes the lower, but not vice versa. The *sāṁsiddhika* possesses all that the lower types have and more; the *kalpita* teacher has correct understanding but lacks the fullness of direct experience.

This hierarchical inclusion reflects the degrees of the descent of grace (*śaktipāta*):
- **Tīvra** (intense) — produces spontaneously enlightened teachers
- **Tīvra-madhya** (intense-moderate) — produces the *akalpitakalpaka*
- **Madhya** (moderate) — produces the *kalpitākalpita*
- **Manda** (weak) — produces the *kalpita* and second *akalpita*

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Continuum of Realization**: There is no sharp break between the ignorant and the enlightened. Realization admits of degrees, and teachers are classified by the depth and stability of their insight.

2. **The Value of All Authentic Teachers**: Even the lowest type of authentic teacher has genuine knowledge and can guide disciples. The tradition recognizes multiple levels of authority.

3. **Practice and Grace Are Not Opposed**: Even teachers who attained realization through practice are ultimately "uncreated" — their realization is the manifestation of grace, not a product of effort. Practice is the occasion, not the cause.

4. **The Teacher as a Category of Grace**: The guru is ultimately a manifestation of Śiva's grace. The distinctions between teachers are distinctions in the manner and intensity of that grace's operation.
