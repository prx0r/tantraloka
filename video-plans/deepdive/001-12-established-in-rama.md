# ESTABLISHED IN RĀMA (RĀMASTHA) — Verses 85cd-89cd
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay

### Core Teaching

"Rāmastha" — established in Rāma — is a yogic practice of entering the central channel (suṣumnā) by suspending the flow of inhalation and exhalation. "Rāma" here does not refer to the epic hero but is derived from the root ram — to delight, to sport. It denotes the Supreme Self who delights in the cosmic play. The yogi who abides in the middle place between the two breaths becomes established in this delight, experiencing the inner Void (antarvyoman) from which the power of consciousness flows.

### Key Verses from Tantrāloka

#### Verse 1.85cd — Entering the Middle

**Line ~18561** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit:**
```
ūrdhvaṁ tyaktvādho viśet sa rāmastho madhyadeśagaḥ || 85 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation:**
"Having abandoned the 'upper' (exhaled breath), the 'lower' (inhaled breath) should enter (the Middle Channel). He who is established in Rāma (rāmastha) resides in the middle place."

**Explanation:**
The "upper" is prāṇa (exhalation), the "lower" is apāna (inhalation). The yogi interrupts their flow and enters the Middle Channel — suṣumnā — which runs between them. This central channel is the path to the inner Void where the Supreme Self resides.

#### Verse 1.86 — The Lotus Fibre and the Void

**Line ~18757** (Vol. 1)

**Sanskrit (from Triśirobhairava):**
```
madhyamā nāḍikā madhye sūkṣmarūpā tu tannibhā |
tāṁ dhyātvāntarvilīnātmā devyā devaḥ prakāśate || 
```

**Dyczkowski Translation (from VBh 35):**
"The Middle Channel is situated in the middle (between the two breaths) in (an extremely subtle) form like a lotus fibre. Having meditated on that as the Goddess Who is the inner Void, the God is made manifest by Her."

**Explanation:**
The suṣumnā is as subtle as a lotus fibre — barely perceptible, like a line without thickness. Meditating on this as the Goddess (the power of consciousness), the God (Śiva) is revealed. The Void within the Middle Channel is the inner sky of consciousness.

### Commentary from Jayaratha (Viveka)

**On rāma (line ~18591+):** "'Rāma' is derived from the root ram which means to delight or enjoy. It denotes the Supreme Self (paramātmā) characterized as 'the enjoyer, who sports with the cosmic variety, sentient and insentient, delighting within himself.'"

**On the practice (line ~18675+):** "Entry into the centre takes place with the flow of inhalation. The inhaled breath absorbed into the consciousness of the Centre brings the condition of the breath in which it moves outwardly to a halt."

**On the inner Void (line ~18700+):** Jayaratha explains that "the Void within the Middle Channel is the inner Sky of consciousness. The energy of the vital breath flows forth from that."

### Dyczkowski's Notes

**On the practice of rāmastha (note 694, line ~18683+):** "The yogi experiences his essential identity with Śiva when all that is external and internal is in a state of oneness with His supreme power. This is achieved by entering the Central Abode when the flux of breathing halts."

### Philosophical Context

The practice of rāmastha is a concrete yogic technique for realizing the teaching of grāmadharma. If the Self is the nature of all principles, then entering the center of one's own being — the middle place between the breaths — is entering the Self. This is the beginning of the Śāmbhava penetration, where the yogi experiences consciousness directly without the mediation of thought constructs.

### Visual Concepts

1. **The Breaths and the Middle:** Animated flow of prāṇa (upward, white) and apāna (downward, red) with the central suṣumnā appearing between them as a fine golden thread.

2. **The Lotus Fibre:** An extreme close-up of a lotus fibre, as fine as a hair, glowing from within, representing the subtlety of the Middle Channel.

3. **Rāma as Delight:** The figure of a dancer (Rāma as the Enjoyer) surrounded by cosmic forms, representing the delight of the Supreme Self in all things.

### Narration Script

The yogi who has realized that the Self is the nature of all principles now practices entering the center of his own being. "Rāma" means delight — the Supreme Self who sports in the cosmic play. To be established in Rāma is to enter the central channel between the two breaths, where the flow of inhalation and exhalation has ceased. There, in the inner Void, the Goddess of consciousness reveals the God. This is the beginning of direct realization — the Śāmbhava penetration.
