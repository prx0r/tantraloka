# 339 — Momentariness and the Succession of Cause and Effect
**Tantrāloka 9.11–12** | Chapter 9: Kāryakāraṇa (Causality)

## Hook
If everything is momentary, how can one moment cause the next? The Buddhist theory of momentariness collapses under its own logic.

## Core Teaching
Abhinavagupta refutes the Buddhist *kṣaṇikavāda* — the doctrine that all things last only a single moment. If cause and effect are both momentary, there can be no real relation between them.

## Key Verses

**9.11** — (Referenced in the text at lines 2170–2200): The Buddhist argues that the seed's *causal capacity* (*janakatva*) is simply its status as a generating cause. If the effect does not exist when the cause exists, what capacity does the cause have? If it does exist, why doesn't it produce immediately?

**9.12**: *sa pūrvam atha paścāt sa iti cet pūrvapaścimau / svabhāve 'natiriktau cet sama ity avaśiṣyate* — "If one were to say that the link is causal simply because they are perceived one after another: if the prior and subsequent phenomena are not different in their essential nature, then all that remains is that they are essentially the same."

## The Dilemma
The opponent says causality is just perceived regularity — "when this, then that." Abhinavagupta's rejoinder:
- If prior and subsequent are both momentary (same nature), there is nothing to distinguish cause from effect
- If they are different, no necessary connection can exist between them
- The supposed "causal capacity" must be either existent (then why wait?) or non-existent (then nothing produces)

## Traikālyaparīkṣā Citation
"Moreover, the prior non-existence of a subsequent effect is perceived when the cause exists." This shows the Buddhist own admission that a relation of succession alone is insufficient.

## Dharmālaṅkāra Citation
"The relationship between cause and effect is an existing thing (*vastusvabhāva*). It is not accomplished on the basis of common daily experience alone."

## The Real Solution
Causality requires:
1. A real agent who is conscious and free
2. A continuity of nature between cause and effect
3. The substratum of consciousness within which both appear

## Connection to Non-Successive Consciousness
The refutation of momentariness clears the ground for Abhinavagupta's positive doctrine: consciousness is non-successive (*akrama*). Temporal succession is a projection of consciousness, not its fundamental nature. This directly anticipates the teaching that the Seven Perceivers (Chapter 10) are simultaneous modes of awareness, not stages in time.
