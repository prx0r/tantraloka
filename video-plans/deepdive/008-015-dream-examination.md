# Dream Examination: Yogic Lucidity

**Tantrāloka 15.478cd–498ab | Lines 19862–21000**

## Overview

The examination of dreams (svapna-vicāraṇa) is a critical diagnostic tool in Trika initiation. The teacher and disciple both sleep with mantric intent, and their dreams are compared to determine whether the initiation is auspicious and which obstacles remain.

## Key Verses

- **482 (line 19869):** *haṭhanirmalacandrārkaprakāśaḥ satyam īkṣate svapnam* — "By the practice of forceful yoga, his light pure as sun and moon, he sees a true dream."
- **483 (line 19888):** *pūrṇāṁ ca kṛśāṁ dhyātvā dvādaśagocare / praviśya hṛdaye dhyāyet suptaḥ svācchandyam āpnuyāt* — Quoting Vijñānabhairava: meditating on the breath as full and thin, entering the heart, one attains freedom in sleep.
- **484–487 (lines 19954–19959):** The teacher worships Śiva, consumes the sacrificial pap, and sleeps with awareness in the heart.
- **487–491 (lines 20038–20049):** Auspicious dream-signs — pleasing women, wine-drinking, meat-eating, riding elephants and horses; inauspicious signs require a remedial homa of 108 oblations.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **Dreaming as a real state:** In Trika ontology, the dream state is not "less real" than waking. It is a different modality of consciousness — one where the objective supports are withdrawn and the mind creates its own objects. This freedom (svāpnasvātantrya) is a foretaste of liberation.

2. **Lucid dreaming as yoga:** The Vijñānabhairava verse (55) teaches that by meditating on the breath and entering the heart, one becomes "free while sleeping" (suptaḥ svācchandyam āpnuyāt). This is a form of yoga that continues 24/7.

3. **The teacher's dream is strongest:** Because the teacher's consciousness is more awakened (bodha), his dreams are more revelatory. If his and the disciple's dreams disagree, the teacher's assessment overrides.

4. **Dreams as diagnostic:** Auspicious dreams indicate the removal of obstacles; inauspicious dreams reveal residual karmic obscurations. The remedial homa (108 oblations) burns these obstructions.

## Consciousness Without Interruption

The ultimate goal is a continuity of awareness (bodhasāmya) that persists through waking, dreaming, and deep sleep. When teacher and disciple both achieve this, their dreams coincide.
