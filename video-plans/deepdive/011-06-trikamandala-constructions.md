# Deep-Dive Video Essay: The Trika Maṇḍala — Five Traditions of Sacred Geometry

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 11, Chapter 31, Verses 1cd-164ab (~lines 5339-6600)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
Chapter 31 presents the Trika Maṇḍala not as a single fixed diagram but as a living tradition with multiple forms. Abhinava presents five distinct versions of the maṇḍala from five authoritative sources: the Trikasadbhāva, Trikahṛdaya, Mālinīvijayottara, Devyāyāmala, Triśirobhairavatantra, and Siddhayogeśvarīmata. Each reveals a different aspect of the Trika vision.

## Part 1: The Square Base — Foundation of the Maṇḍala (0:45-2:30)
- Verse 1cd-10ab (~line 5339): From the Mālinīvijayottara
- The maṇḍala is drawn on a square base (caturasra)
- The square represents:
  - The Earth principle (pṛthivī tattva)
  - The foundation of all manifestation
  - The stabilized state of consciousness
  - The four directions and four gates
- Measurements and proportions are given
- The square is divided into smaller squares
- The entire structure is oriented to the east
- This is the "canvas" on which the maṇḍala is built

## Part 2: The Trikasadbhāva and Trikahṛdaya Versions (2:30-5:00)
- Verses 10cd-52: The Trikasadbhāva version
  - The central trident with lotuses
  - The three goddesses in the center (Parā, Parāparā, Aparā)
  - Their Bhairavas surrounding them
  - The Eight Mothers and Bhairavas
  - The Yoginīs on the petals
  - The guardians of the directions
- Verses 53-59: The Trikahṛdaya version
  - A simpler form
  - Focuses on the heart (hṛdaya) of the Trika
  - Emphasizes the three goddesses in the center
  - The outer circles contain all deities within the heart

## Part 3: The Mālinīvijayottara and Devyāyāmala Versions (5:00-7:00)
- Verses 60-85ab: The Mālinīvijayottara version
  - The most commonly used
  - Detailed instructions for drawing the lotus
  - The Trident Throne at the center
  - The 24 filaments, 8 petals, 3 circles
  - The Great Ghost (mahāpreta = Sadāśiva) beneath the lotus
- Verses 85cd-100ab: The Devyāyāmala version
  - More complex, more esoteric
  - Additional circles and deities
  - Specific mantras for each element
  - This is the version Abhinava learned from Śambhunātha

## Part 4: The Triśirobhairava and Siddhayogeśvarīmata Versions (7:00-9:30)
- Verses 100cd-131: The Triśirobhairava version
  - The most elaborate
  - Multiple layers of lotuses
  - Many subsidiary deities
  - The "Lord of the Sky" (Vyomeśa) and the Svastika (verses 132-154)
- Verses 155-164ab: The Siddhayogeśvarīmata version
  - The briefest
  - Focuses on the essential — the three goddesses
  - Suitable for daily practice
  - The entire maṇḍala is visualized in the heart
- **The meaning of the multiple versions:**
  - The truth is one, but its expression varies
  - Different practitioners need different forms
  - All maṇḍalas converge on the same reality
  - The Trika teaching accommodates all levels

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The five maṇḍalas of Chapter 31 show the richness of the Trika tradition. Each version is a different map of the same territory — consciousness itself. Chapter 32 moves from space (maṇḍala) to gesture (mudrā), exploring the seals through which the deities are invoked and realized.
