# MISTAKEN ATTACHMENT TO LOWER PATHS (HĪNAMĀRGĀBHIṆIVEŚA)
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 3, Chapter 4, Verses 17–32

---

### Core Teaching

Before presenting the true teacher, Abhinavagupta diagnoses the spiritual pathology of attachment to lower paths. Those who adhere to dualistic doctrines — Vaiṣṇavas, Sāṁkhyas, and others — are bound by the "snare of Māyā" through the power Vāmā (the goddess of delusion). Their error is not that their paths are worthless but that they mistake the part for the whole. They remain attached to limited conceptions of reality — a personal Lord distinct from the soul, a dualistic ontology of spirit and matter, or a merely moral conception of liberation. The freedom of the Supreme Lord, who can "accomplish the impossible," is unknown to them. Abhinavagupta presents this as necessary context for understanding the true teacher: one must first recognize the insufficiency of lower paths before one can seek the highest.

**Line references:** TĀ 4/17–32; Dyczkowski translation pp. 22–43, lines 1648–3672.

---

### The Kinds of Wrong Attachment

Jayaratha explains (lines 1648–1704) that there are various forms of mistaken attachment:

1. **Attachment to dualistic theism** — worshipping a personal God as other than oneself
2. **Attachment to impersonalism** — denying the reality of the self
3. **Attachment to ritualism** — believing that external acts alone suffice for liberation
4. **Attachment to asceticism** — believing that self-mortification is the path

All these are forms of *asattarka* — unsound reasoning — that fail to recognize the nondual nature of reality.

---

### The Power Vāmā

The power of delusion called Vāmā is one of the three goddesses mentioned earlier (line 3744): Vāmā (delusion), Jyeṣṭhā (knowledge), and Raudrī (will). Vāmā is the aspect of Śiva's freedom that conceals his own nature — the same power of self-concealment discussed in verses 8–12. When operating as Vāmā, consciousness contracts into limited, dualistic modes of perception.

Jayaratha notes (lines 3699–3720): "He, a Vaiṣṇava or the like, has been deceived by the hordes of teachers such as Kapila with their inferior doctrines" because "each of these teachers has been made to be intensely attached there by the Supreme Lord, by means of the snare of Māyā."

---

### The Gradual Path of Grace

The crucial point (made in verses 33–36) is that even those attached to lower paths are not abandoned. Through the practice of Sound Reasoning — which is itself a form of grace — "even he is led to a true teacher" (4/34). The descent of grace may be gradual (*kramika*), operating through the very doctrines that ultimately prove insufficient.

---

### The Content of the Mistaken Views

Abhinavagupta catalogs the errors (verses 17–32):

- **Sāṁkhya dualism**: puruṣa and prakṛti as separate realities — fails to recognize that both are manifestations of consciousness
- **Vaiṣṇava theism**: a Lord who is distinct from the soul — fails to recognize the identity of self and God
- **Buddhist momentariness**: all things are momentary — fails to recognize the continuous ground of consciousness
- **Mīmāṁsā ritualism**: the efficacy of ritual as independent of consciousness — fails to recognize that all power is consciousness

Each of these contains partial truth but becomes an obstacle when absolutized.

---

### The Principle of Adhikāra (Qualification)

Jayaratha explains (lines 2000–2200) that the lower paths are appropriate for those at their level of development. They are not errors in the sense of falsehoods but limitations in the sense of partial truths. The Supreme Lord, in his compassion, reveals lower paths for those who cannot yet receive the highest teaching. The error is not in following them but in mistaking them for the ultimate.

---

### The Transition to the True Teacher

This section sets the stage for the teaching on the true teacher (verses 33–78ab). Having seen the insufficiency of all lower paths, the sincere seeker is prepared to recognize the *sadguru* — the teacher who embodies nondual realization. The very dissatisfaction with partial truth is itself the operation of grace.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Inclusivism without Relativism**: Lower paths are valid at their level but are not the ultimate truth. Abhinavagupta maintains a hierarchical inclusivism that honors all paths while clearly indicating the highest.

2. **The Pedagogical Function of Error**: Mistaken attachment is not simply an obstacle; it is part of the pedagogical economy of grace. The experience of limitation is what drives the seeker beyond limitation.

3. **Vāmā and Jyeṣṭhā as One Power**: The same divine power that conceals (Vāmā) also reveals (Jyeṣṭhā). The difference is not in the power but in the degree of grace received by the individual.

4. **The Necessity of Discernment**: The path to liberation requires discriminating between the partial and the complete, the lower and the higher, the created and the uncreated. This discernment is itself *sattarka* — Sound Reasoning.
