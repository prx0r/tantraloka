# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Abhinavagupta's Teachers — The Four Great Lineages

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 11, Chapter 37, Verses 60-63 (~lines 17640-17880)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
After his father, Abhinavagupta studied under many teachers, but four stand out — representing the four great lineages of Śaivism. In four packed verses, he names Vāmanātha (Ānandasaṁtati), Bhūtirāja's son (Śrīnāthasaṁtati), Lakṣmaṇagupta (Tryambakasaṁtati), and Śambhunātha (the Ardhatryambaka/Trika lineage). Together, these four teachers transmitted the complete tradition to him.

## Part 1: Vāmanātha — The Lineage of Bliss (0:45-3:00)
- Verse 60a (~line 17663): "ānandasaṁtatimahārṇavakarṇadhāraḥ"
- **Vāmanātha** was the helmsman of the ship crossing the ocean of the Lineage of Bliss (Ānandasaṁtati)
- He was the son of Erakavara, the true teacher
- The Ānandasaṁtati is the dualistic Śaiva order founded by Āmardaka
- This lineage teaches:
  - The distinction between Śiva and the soul
  - The path of devotion and grace
  - The importance of ritual purity
- Even though Abhinava's final position is nondual, he respected the dualist path
- It is a necessary stage for many practitioners
- Jayaratha identifies Erakavara as a Krama teacher, disciple of Keyūravatī

## Part 2: The Son of Bhūtirāja — Cakradhara of the Śrīnātha Lineage (3:00-5:00)
- Verse 60b (~line 17666): "śrīnāthasaṁtatimahāmbaragharmakāntiḥ"
- The venerable son of **Bhūtirāja** was a radiant sun illumining the great sky of the lineage of Śrīnātha
- This is the dualist-cum-nondualist order
- The teacher is identified as Indurāja or Bhaṭṭendurāja, who taught Abhinava:
  - The Bhagavadgītā (Abhinava wrote a commentary on it)
  - Literature (sāhitya) and poetics (alaṅkāra)
  - The grammarian Bhartṛhari's philosophy of language
- Abhinava's Locana on the Dhvanyāloka acknowledges Bhaṭṭendurāja as his teacher
- Bhūtirāja himself was one of Abhinava's Krama teachers, praised in MVV 1/6ab as "tato gurutaraḥ" (more excellent than the teacher)

## Part 3: Lakṣmaṇagupta — The Pratyabhijñā Lineage (5:00-7:00)
- Verse 61a (~line 17670): "traiyambakaprasarasāgaraśāyiṣomā-nandātmajotpalajalakṣmaṇaguptanāthaḥ"
- **Lakṣmaṇagupta** was a disciple of Utpala, who was the disciple (or son) of Somānanda
- Somānanda lay on the ocean of the lineage that spread from Tryambaka
- This is the nondual Śaiva order — the Pratyabhijñā (Recognition) school
- The lineage:
  1. **Tryambaka** (founder of the nondual order)
  2. **Somānanda** (wrote the Śivadṛṣṭi, the foundational Pratyabhijñā text)
  3. **Utpaladeva** (wrote the Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā)
  4. **Lakṣmaṇagupta** (Abhinava's teacher)
- Lakṣmaṇagupta taught Abhinava the Pratyabhijñā philosophy
- This is the philosophical backbone of the Trika

## Part 4: Śambhunātha — The Trika Kaula Master (7:00-9:30)
- Verse 61b (~line 17671): "turyākhyasaṁtatimahodadhipūrṇacandraḥ śrīṣaumataḥ sakalavit kila śaṁbhunāthaḥ"
- **Śambhunātha**, the disciple of the venerable Sumati, was the full moon illumining the ocean of the lineage called "the Fourth"
- "The Fourth" (Turya) = the Ardhatryambaka (half-Tryambaka) lineage
- This is the Trika Kaula lineage — the highest
- Śambhunātha knew everything (sakalavit) of the Śaiva scriptures and traditions
- He transmitted to Abhinava:
  - The Trika rituals and initiations
  - The Krama system
  - The Devyāyāmala
  - The direct realization of nonduality
- **Other teachers** (verse 62-63):
  - Candraśarman, Bhavabhaktivilāsa, Yogānanda, Abhinanda, Śivaśakti, Vicitranātha
  - Dharmaśiva (Siddhāntin), Vāmanaka, Udbhaṭa (Krama), Bhūteśa (Krama), Bhāskara
- All these teachers bestowed their authority on Abhinava
- He served them all with devotion (sevārasa)
- Their grace made him the master of the entire tradition

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
Abhinavagupta's education was comprehensive: dualistic Śaivism, dual-cum-nondual, nondual Pratyabhijñā, and the Trika Kaula. No wonder his synthesis in the Tantrāloka is so complete. The next video presents his students — the fortunate few who received this complete transmission.
