# Deep-Dive: The Division of Mantras and Vidyās in the Breath

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 5, Chapter 7, verses 53–62ab  
**Catalog Entry:** #287  
**Lines:** 3272–3800

---

## Topic Overview

After concluding the Cakrodaya proper (the Yoginīkaula passage), Abhinava adds a supplementary teaching on the division of mantras and vidyās within the three phases of the breathing cycle: entry (praveśa/inhalation), oneness (ekātmya/rest), and exit (nirgama/exhalation). The "middle" — the moment of rest between breaths — is proclaimed the best for achieving success (siddhi).

---

## Key Verses

### Verse 53 (lines 3276–3282)
> niruddhya mānasīr vṛttīś cakre viśrāntim āgataḥ
> vyutthāya yāvad viśrāmyet tāvac cārodayo hy ayam
> *"This emerging movement of the breathing cycle continues as long as it extends through three phases: once the activities of the mind have been checked, a cycle comes to rest; then, having emerged from the Heart, it travels up with the flow of the breath to the End of the Twelve, returns, and is brought to rest again in the heart."*

### Verse 54 (lines 3331–3339)
> pūrṇe samudaye tv atra praveśaikātmyanirgamāḥ
> traya ity ata evoktaḥ siddhau madhyodayo varaḥ
> *"There are three phases in a complete breathing cycle: the entry (of inhalation), the exit (of exhalation), and the oneness of the two in between. Thus it is said that the arising of the breath in the centre is the best to achieve success."*

### Verses 55–56 (lines 3375–3384): The Dviśatika Citation
> ādyantodayanirmuktā madhyamodayasaṁyutāḥ
> mantravidyācakragaṇāḥ siddhibhājo bhavanti hi
> mantracakrodayajñas tu vidyācakrodayārthavit
> kṣipraṁ siddhyed iti proktaṁ śrīmaddviśatike trike
> *"All the cycles of Mantras and Vidyās are indeed successful if, free of emergence in the beginning and the end, they are linked to the emergence of consciousness in the centre. He who understands the arising of the cycles of Mantra and Vidyā quickly attains success in the three phases of the breathing cycle."*

### Verses 57–62ab (lines 3433–3600+): Practical Classification

Mantras of two, three, or four measures should be recited. Vidyās and cycles should be recited joined to the emergence of the reality (tattvodaya) constantly, separately, or together. Long mantras should be articulated quickly; mantras of the venerable Parā tradition up to 300 syllables are found in the accomplishment of the state of the Body of Mantra (mantradeha).

---

## Key Doctrinal Points

1. **Three Phases of Breath:**
   - **Praveśa (entry):** Inhalation — mantra recited as breath enters
   - **Ekātmya (oneness):** The still point between breaths — consciousness resting in itself
   - **Nirgama (exit):** Exhalation — mantra recited as breath leaves

2. **The Middle is Best:** The still point (madhyodaya) between breaths is the most powerful for siddhi because it is free from the dualistic movement of in/out.

3. **Mantra and Vidyā Cycles:** Mantras embody male deities; Vidyās embody female ones. Both operate according to the same three-phase cycle.

4. **The Dviśatika Authority:** Abhinava cites the venerable Dviśatika — a lost Trika text of 200 verses — as his source.
