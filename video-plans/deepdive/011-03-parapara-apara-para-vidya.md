# Deep-Dive Video Essay: Parāparā, Aparā, and Parā Vidyās — The Three Goddesses' Mantras

**Source:** Tantrāloka Vol. 11, Chapter 30, Verses 20-37ab (~lines 1947-2260)
**Duration:** 10 minutes

## Opening Hook (0:00-0:45)
The three goddesses of the Trika — Parā (Supreme), Parāparā (Supreme-non-supreme), and Aparā (Non-supreme) — are the foundational deities of the system. Their Vidyās (feminine mantras) are the sonic embodiments of the three levels of reality: transcendent, immanent-transcendent, and immanent. Understanding these mantras is understanding the Trika's entire theology.

## Part 1: Parāparā Vidyā — The Middle Goddess (0:45-3:30)
- Verses 20-26ab (~line 1966): The most elaborate of the three Vidyās
- **Structure (38 syllables + 2 half-letters):**
  - Three vocatives: Aghore, Paramaghore, Ghorarūpe
  - With seed syllables: HRĪḤ, HUṀ, HAḤ
  - Three more vocatives: Ghoramukhi, Bhīma, Bhīṣaṇe
  - Two powers: Vama, Piba
  - Elements: HE, RURU, RARA, PHAT, HUṀ, HAḤ, PHAT
  - Preceded by OṀ
- **Eight parts (pada) corresponding to eight Bhairavas:**
  1. Aghorī (5 syllables)
  2. Paramaghorī (6 syllables)
  3. Ghorarūpā (5 syllables)
  4. Ghoramukhī (4 syllables)
  5. Bhīmā (2 syllables)
  6. Bhīṣaṇe (3 syllables)
  7. Vamanī (2 syllables)
  8. Pibanī (11 syllables)
- Parāparā is the *between* goddess — mediating between the transcendent and the manifest
- Her Vidyā contains both peaceful (aghora) and terrifying (ghora) elements

## Part 2: Aparā Vidyā
(3:30-5:00)
- Verse 26cd: Brief — just the name
- Aparā is the goddess of the gross, manifest universe
- Her Vidyā is simpler, more direct
- She is associated with:
  - The lower three tattvas (earth, water, fire)
  - The waking state
  - The karmendriyas and jñānendriyas
- Her Bhairava is Navātmā
- Her worship involves external substances and rituals
- She is the gateway to the higher goddesses — one must first be purified by Aparā before approaching Parāparā and Parā

## Part 3: Parā Vidyā — The Supreme (5:00-7:30)
- Verses 27-37ab: The most secret and powerful Vidyā
- Parā is the transcendent goddess, pure consciousness itself
- Her Vidyā is the shortest but most potent
- **Her mantric structure:**
  - She is beyond phonemes — her "mantra" is pure awareness
  - She is identified with SAUḤ — the great heart-seed
  - She is the essence of all mantras
- Parā is associated with:
  - The pure path (śuddhādhvan)
  - Śiva tattva itself
  - The state of turīya (the fourth)
  - The supreme I-consciousness (pūrṇāhaṁbodha)
- Her worship is purely mental — no external substances
- She is the ultimate reality, the goal of all practice
- The three Vidyās together constitute the complete Trika theology

## Part 4: The Vitality of Mantras — Mantravīrya (7:30-9:30)
- Verses 121cd-123 (~line 2450): What gives mantras their power
- Mantravīrya is the "vitality" or "potency" of mantra
- It is not in the phonemes themselves but in the consciousness that animates them
- The vitality awakens when:
  1. The mantra is correctly pronounced
  2. The mantra is received from a qualified teacher
  3. The practitioner is established in the appropriate state of consciousness
  4. The mantra is practiced with devotion and repetition
- Without vīrya, a mantra is just sound
- With vīrya, a mantra is a living deity
- The source of all mantra-vīrya is Parā — supreme consciousness
- All mantras are powered by the same consciousness that powers the universe

## Conclusion (9:30-10:00)
The three Vidyās are the sonic theophanies of the three goddesses. They form a complete path: from Aparā's external worship through Parāparā's mixed practice to Parā's pure realization.
