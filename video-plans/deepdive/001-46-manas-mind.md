# MANAS TATTVA — The Mind
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Manas tattva is the 21st principle — the mind, the faculty of deliberation and doubt (*saṁkalpa-vikalpa*). It is the internal organ that receives impressions from the senses and presents them to Ahaṃkāra and Buddhi for appropriation and judgment. While Buddhi is the faculty of certainty and Ahaṃkāra of self-appropriation, Manas is the faculty of oscillation — it considers alternatives, doubts, and coordinates sensory input. In the Sāṁkhya evolution, Manas arises from the sāttvika aspect of Ahaṃkāra and is the last of the three inner senses (*antaḥkaraṇa*).

---

### Key Verse — The Impure Principles (TĀ 1.189cd)

**Dyczkowski notes (Vol. 1, line ~28876):**
"Then come the three inner mental senses: ego (ahaṁkāra), mind (manas), and intellect (buddhi). Above them is Nature (prakṛti) and the individual soul (puruṣa)."

**On the function of Manas (line ~28835):**
"One would expect the mind, including the intellect, to generate the senses and these, sensations, from which come the gross elements."

**Explanation:**
Manas is the coordinator — without it, the five senses would present disconnected data. Manas synthesizes sensory input into coherent perception.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On Manas (Vol. 6, line ~16659): Jayaratha describes Manas as the faculty that processes the data of the five senses of knowledge. It is the inner organ that receives, sorts, and presents sensory information to the ego and intellect.

Jayaratha notes that Manas is characterized by *saṁkalpa-vikalpa* — constructive and discriminative thought. Its nature is to move among objects, never resting in a single determination (which is Buddhi's function).

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **The Sixth Sense**: In the Śaiva analysis, Manas is often counted as the sixth sense of knowledge — the internal sense that coordinates the five external senses. Its function is synthetic, not decisional.

2. **The Gatekeeper**: Manas stands at the threshold between the external world (senses) and the inner self (ego, intellect). It is the interface through which the world enters consciousness. Meditation practices aim to quiet Manas so that deeper awareness can shine.

3. **Pure Mind vs. Agitated Mind**: In its pure state (*śuddha-manas*), the mind is a clear mirror reflecting consciousness. In its agitated state (the state of bondage), it is a storm of *vikalpas* — discursive thoughts that veil the Self. Yoga is the stilling of the mind (*mano-nirodha*).
