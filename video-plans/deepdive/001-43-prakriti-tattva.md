# PRAKṚTI TATTVA — The Matrix of Manifestation
## 10-Minute Deep-Dive Video Essay — Tantrāloka Vol. 1, Chapter 1

---

### Core Teaching

Prakṛti tattva is the 24th principle — the primordial material nature, the root cause of all objective existence. It is the equilibrium (*sāmya*) of the three *guṇas*: *sattva* (light, harmony), *rajas* (activity, passion), and *tamas* (inertia, obscurity). When disturbed from equilibrium, Prakṛti unfolds into the entire chain of evolutes: intellect (*buddhi*), ego (*ahaṁkāra*), mind (*manas*), the senses, the subtle elements (*tanmātras*), and the gross elements (*mahābhūtas*). In Abhinavagupta's nondual framework, Prakṛti is not separate from consciousness but the objective pole of the one reality — consciousness appearing as insentient materiality.

---

### Key Verse — The Impure Person (TĀ 1.189cd)

**Sanskrit:**
```
aśuddhiśuddhyamānatvaśuddhitastu mitho 'pi tat || 189 ||
```

**Dyczkowski Translation on Prakṛti (Vol. 1, line ~28877):**
"Above them (the three inner senses) is Nature (prakṛti) and the individual soul (puruṣa). These twenty-five principles are those of the Sāṁkhya."

**Explanation:**
Prakṛti is the material matrix of the lower 23 principles. Above it is Puruṣa; below it unfold all the evolutes of mental and physical existence.

---

### Jayaratha's Commentary

On Nature (Vol. 5, line ~19120): Jayaratha explains that Prakṛti is the state of equilibrium of sattva, rajas, and tamas. When disturbed by the Lord's will, it becomes the material cause (*pradhāna*) of the universe. The intellect (buddhi) is its first product.

On the relationship to Puruṣa: Prakṛti is insentient (*jaḍa*) — it is the object of experience for Puruṣa. Bondage arises from the failure to distinguish between them (*aviveka*). Liberation is the recognition that both are within the one consciousness.

---

### Philosophical Significance

1. **Equilibrium of Guṇas**: Prakṛti in its unmanifest state (*avyakta*) is the potentiality of all objectivity. The three guṇas are not substances but principles of experience: sattva = illumination, rajas = activity, tamas = obstruction.

2. **The Sāṁkhya Adoption**: Abhinavagupta adopts the Sāṁkhya cosmology from the intellect down to Earth but recontextualizes it. Prakṛti is not an independent reality (as in Sāṁkhya) but the appearance of consciousness as the objective world — the *idam* (this) pole of experience.

3. **Nondual Reinterpretation**: Unlike the dualist Sāṁkhya which separates Puruṣa and Prakṛti absolutely, Trika Śaivism sees Prakṛti as Śakti's power of objectivity — the "this" that the "I" posits within itself. Prakṛti is consciousness appearing as matter.
