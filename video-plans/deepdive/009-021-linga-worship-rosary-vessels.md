# Liṅga Worship: Rosaries, Vessels, and Types of Worship

**Tantrāloka 27.30–59 | Lines 784–789**

## Overview

The concluding section of Chapter 27 covers the varieties of rosaries (akṣasūtra), the nature of the sacrificial jar (kalaśa), and the various types of worship (pūjābheda). This section serves as a practical compendium of the "supports" (ādhāra) for worship, each of which — when used with the right awareness — becomes a door to the infinite.

## Key Verses

- **30–39 (lines 783–785):** A description of the varieties of rosaries — made from rudrākṣa, crystal, lotus seeds, etc. — each with its appropriate mantra and fruit.
- **40–43 (lines 787–788):** The sacrificial jar (kalaśa) — filled with water, adorned with cloth and flowers, installed with mantras.
- **44–49 (lines 788–789):** Other supports for worship — maṇḍalas, icons, books, even the empty sky.
- **50–59 (lines 789–790):** Various types of worship — from elaborate 16-step pūjā to simple mental offering.

## Philosophical Meaning

1. **The rosary as the cycle of phonemes:** The beads of the rosary are the 50 phonemes of the Sanskrit alphabet. Each bead is a goddess; each repetition of the mantra activates a phonemic energy. The rosary is a circle — no beginning, no end — like consciousness.

2. **The jar as the cosmic vessel:** The kalaśa is a microcosm of the universe. Its base is the Foundation Energy, its body is the mountain Meru, its water is the ocean of existence, its mouth is the access to the divine. The mantras installed in it make it a living presence.

3. **Any support can be the supreme:** Abhinavagupta lists increasingly subtle supports — from the gross (a liṅga) to the subtle (a maṇḍala) to the subtlest (the empty sky, the mind alone). The highest worship has no support at all (nirālamba).

4. **Types of worship correspond to types of practitioner:** The elaborate 16-step pūjā is not superior to simple mental offering — it is merely appropriate for a different type of practitioner. The value of worship is not in its complexity but in the depth of awareness with which it is performed.

## The Inward Turn

The entire Volume 9 ends with a quiet inward turn. After all the elaborate rituals of initiation — the maṇḍalas, the fire sacrifices, the depositions, the oblations — the final teaching is simple: any support can be used for worship, and the highest worship has no support at all. The goal was always within.
