CHAPTER ONE — THE TYPES OF LIBERATING KNOWLEDGE

== THE MAṄGALA VERSE ==

My mother, Vimalā, is joyous with the glory of the festival of my birth, the ever new creation, her foundation the pure energy of the Moon. My father, Narasiṁhagupta, is the hidden light with five faces and his body is full. May the body of the nectar of the Absolute, my Heart, the emission which is the vibrant emissive state of the united form of both of them, my father and mother, Śiva and Śakti, pulsate radiantly!

== THE INTRODUCTORY VERSES ==

May that divine abode, the Trika reality which is nonduality itself, and the full and perfect realisation of freedom, from which proceed the powers of will, knowledge and action that are the immanent planes of transcendental consciousness and bliss, and of which all this universe is the unfolding glory of its unrestrained power, be ever present within my mind, and thereby destroy all that within it obscures the secret of the transmission of Śiva's teachings.

Liberated as I am even whilst living in the body, by the drops of compassion from the ocean of the venerable Kalyāna who is auspicious good fortune, I take refuge in him, a true master, within my heart.

Although my heart is hard with the burden of phenomenal existence, I quickly place there His teaching that is borne on the head of all the kings of the earth like a crown jewel.

I am not engaged in this work to attain some unique status as a writer, nor am I doing this to show off my skill with language; rather my desire is that my thought be purified by being constantly occupied with this teaching.

Some ignorant people are constantly coming and going from this world, life to life; others are envious, some in doubt. What should we say of those who can hear that teaching but have not come to listen?

Not having heard that profound and pleasant teaching, whatever is said by others with an intense desire to incite people to act as they wish them to do, but which derives its skill only from their own way of thinking, simply makes the mind unstable.

Although my speech is powerless here in this task, whatever restriction may arise on one's understanding of the teachings is not my fault; rather that is all the venerable expansion of the Supreme Lord's great bliss which both hides and reveals the truth.

I bow to the goddess Parā, the Supreme One, who is the creative intuition of consciousness, Bhairava's Yoginī united with Him. She has made her place on top of the lotuses of the Trika Trident, the prongs of which are the aspects of perception: subject, object and means of knowledge.

I bow to the goddess Aparā, who resides in the body of Bhairava's dancing form and plays within it like a lightning flash in a sky covered with dense storm clouds.

May Parāparā, the pulsingly radiant prong of knowledge, that has burnt up the triple fetter with its blazing rays of the light of Śiva consciousness, have the power to uproot all that runs contrary to the truth.

Śiva, Śakti and Nara, the individual soul, manifest as the external objective expansion of Śambhu, just as the deities when they appear on earth assume a human form.

In this way there are many groups of deities, principal ones and those like them. And my teaching is composed in this same variety of ways. This is my intention that leads to the supreme reality, not the gratification of my own self.

May the pervasive lord Macchanda, the Fisherman, be pleased with me, he who cast along the outer path the net of Māyā that, red with colour and passion, catches and binds all living things.

May the venerable Śrīkaṇṭha be pleased, he whose neck is dark with the poison of the insentient world, who is the sole Moon of the sky of the heart of my venerable teacher.

I praise the Trika master Śambhunātha, O revered one, whose feet are a lotus that blossoms by the light of the sun of the recognition of the Heart of Supreme Śiva.

The venerable teacher who is attained only by intense contemplation, who manifests the true meaning of the sacred tradition, who has crossed over the ocean of phenomenal existence and is a true abode of the six currents of scripture. May he be pleased.

I praise my teacher who is an abode of compassion, mighty and wise, whose lineage originates from the venerable Triyambaka, who is full of the light of the bliss of the supreme Lord Bhairava.

To me, whose mind is made intent and whose inner darkness is destroyed by the full and perfect light of the collected teachings of the traditional transmission of the lineage of Tryambaka, clearly appears the truth.

May that venerable lord of the lineage of Tryambaka, who by an unbroken transmission of the supreme teaching bestows the secret of the venerable Trika, the supreme reality, be pleased.

The lord of the lineage of Tryambaka, whose consciousness has expanded with the rising of the sun of the truth of the reality of nondual Śaivism, has two aspects. One is the direct perception of reality and the other is public renown on earth.

The venerable Utpaladeva, who composed the Pratyabhijñā, illuminated the essence of reality, which was understood with discriminating intellect by the excellent teacher who was his disciple.

And Lakṣmaṇagupta, established in the true nature of reality, through whom the meaning of the Pratyabhijñā was clearly understood, from him I, Abhinavagupta, received the teaching.

My teacher Śambhunātha was a great being who acquired many powers and attained realization by identifying with the state of Bhairava through the practice of the Kula path.

From him I obtained the supreme state of the essential nature of the goddess Parā, the supreme Lady of the Trika, the Lord of the universe, which is attained by the Kula path.

Thus I have praised the teacher. Now I will duly set forth this teaching of the Trika, the supreme reality, for the sake of those good people who desire the highest good.

The text is called Tantrāloka because it sheds light on the Tantras. The word Tantra means that which saves by extending knowledge, and āloka means light. So this work is the Light on the Tantras.

This is the venerable and sacred teaching of the Trika, which is the essence of all the scriptures. It bestows the highest reality, the state of Bhairava, to those who are devoted to it.

== CONCERNING FREEDOM AND BONDAGE ==

Now I will explain the nature of freedom and bondage. The individual soul, though essentially one with Śiva, appears to be bound because of ignorance. This bondage is not real; it is only apparent, like the mistaken perception of a rope as a snake.

Liberation is not the production of a new state but the removal of ignorance. When ignorance is removed by right knowledge, one recognises one's own nature as Śiva. This recognition is liberation.

The means of liberation are three: the Divine Means, the Empowered Means, and the Individual Means. The Divine Means is the most direct, requiring no effort. The Empowered Means uses thought to purify thought. The Individual Means uses body, breath and external supports.

The Divine Means is for those of sharp intellect who can recognise their true nature by simply hearing the truth. The Empowered Means is for those of medium intellect who need to contemplate the truth. The Individual Means is for those of dull intellect who need practical methods.

All three means lead to the same goal: the recognition of one's identity with Śiva. The difference is only in the speed and directness of the approach.
