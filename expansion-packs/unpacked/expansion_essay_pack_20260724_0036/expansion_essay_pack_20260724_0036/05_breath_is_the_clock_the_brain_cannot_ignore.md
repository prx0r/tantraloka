# breath is the clock the brain cannot ignore

You do not merely breathe through time.

Breathing manufactures a repeating time inside the body.

Inhale.

Turn.

Exhale.

Pause.

Every cycle creates a before and after available to the nervous system.

The Śrīvidyā texts gathered on Shiva Shakti Mandalam make this rhythm cosmic.

The fifteen Nityās are lunar phases, powers of the goddess, segments of mantra, and divisions of the breaths said to unfold through one day and night.

The arithmetic is traditional, not a universal respiratory measurement.

The intuition beneath it is now experimentally difficult to dismiss.

Breath is the clock the brain cannot ignore.

---

The site repeatedly presents the number 21,600.

In the Nityā system, fifteen lunar powers each receive 1,440 breaths.

Fifteen times 1,440 yields 21,600.

One day and night become one complete cycle of the goddess.

In the Jvālāmukhī and *haṃsa* materials, the same number is distributed across bodily centres and associated with the unrecited mantra of breathing.

One convention interprets *ha* through exhalation and *sa* through inhalation.

Breathing repeats the mantra without deliberate speech.

The organism recites time simply by remaining alive.

---

The calculation assumes roughly one breath every four seconds, or fifteen breaths per minute.

Real respiratory rate varies with age, health, posture, sleep, emotion, altitude, exercise, and individual physiology.

The number should therefore not be presented as a timeless scientific constant.

Its function is ritual mathematics.

It binds minute, day, lunar phase, mantra, bodily centre, and goddess into one system of correspondences.

The practitioner’s ordinary respiration becomes a moving calendar.

Time is no longer only something read from the sky or clock.

It is counted by the body.

---

Modern neuroscience once treated respiration mainly as a nuisance.

Breathing moves the head and chest.

It changes blood gases.

It affects blood flow and therefore contaminates brain measurements.

Researchers developed methods to remove respiratory signals from EEG and fMRI.

But removal can discard structure as well as artefact.

Evidence now shows that breathing does not merely sustain the brain metabolically.

Respiratory phase modulates neural activity across widespread regions.

The “noise” was partly a clock.

---

A 2026 *Nature Communications* study used intracranial recordings from human participants and identified synchronization between breathing and forebrain neural oscillations across wakefulness, sleep, and external mechanical ventilation.

During wakefulness, respiration-linked activity appeared in regions involved in interoception and emotion, including insula, somatosensory cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, and amygdala.

During sleep, widespread coupling decreased, but persisted in hippocampus and amygdala.

The result is especially important because nasal airflow was not required for all observed synchronization.

Breathing reaches the brain through multiple routes:

airflow-related sensory input;

chest and lung mechanosensation;

brainstem respiratory commands;

chemical changes;

cardiovascular coupling.

The rhythm is bodily before it is olfactory.

---

This does not mean every neuron fires once per breath.

Coupling is regional, state-dependent, and variable.

Some neural oscillations align their phase with respiration.

Some firing probabilities change across the cycle.

Some effects appear transiently.

The 2025 position paper on brain rhythms emphasizes that oscillatory explanations remain contested: phase, amplitude, travelling waves, and aperiodic activity can interact, and apparent rhythm can arise from several mechanisms.

Breath is not one master metronome commanding the entire brain.

It is a slow, recurrent signal that can coordinate selected processes across distance.

A clock can influence a city without making every machine tick at its frequency.

---

Memory gives the rhythm behavioural significance.

A classic human study found that recognition memory and emotional judgements varied with respiratory phase, particularly during nasal breathing.

Later work showed respiration coordinating hippocampal ensembles during memory encoding and modulating sleep oscillations involved in consolidation.

In 2026, researchers reported that matching respiratory phase between encoding and retrieval affected recognition-task efficiency.

Late exhalation during both stages was associated with faster responses under their conditions.

The exact “best phase” is not settled across all tasks.

Different studies, designs, and sensory routes yield different phase effects.

The broader point is stronger:

the state in which information is received includes where the body is inside its breath.

---

The Nityā doctrine interprets this temporality through divine phases.

A Nityā is an “eternity,” yet the goddesses correspond to changing lunar days.

The paradox is deliberate.

Eternity does not mean the absence of phases.

It means the power present through every phase.

The moon waxes.

Breath rises and falls.

A day divides.

The goddess is not one frozen instant behind change.

She is the ordered power through which change remains one cycle.

Time becomes Śakti in transformation.

---

Neuroscience offers a non-metaphysical parallel.

A rhythm provides a temporal reference frame.

Events occurring at different phases encounter different levels of excitability, sensory input, autonomic state, and network coordination.

The same stimulus may be processed differently because it arrives during inhalation rather than exhalation.

Respiration supplies an internal context.

The brain does not only ask what happened.

It implicitly registers when within the bodily cycle it happened.

Time is encoded partly as phase.

The event acquires an address inside a repeating process.

---

This is why breathing can coordinate without carrying detailed content.

A clock signal does not tell a computer what data to process.

It helps organize when operations occur.

Respiration may similarly align windows of sensitivity.

It can influence when neurons are more likely to fire, when sensory input is sampled, or when distant regions synchronize.

The breath does not contain the remembered image.

It helps establish the temporal condition under which encoding or retrieval unfolds.

The Nityā is not the content of experience.

She is the phase through which content becomes available.

---

Slow-breathing practices alter this clock.

A 2025 arXiv study used a five-second inhalation and five-second exhalation protocol with single-channel EEG and reported changes in stress-related measures and subjective anxiety in a small healthy sample.

The results are preliminary.

Single-channel EEG has important limitations.

The study does not demonstrate a universal mechanism or validate Tantric breath cosmology.

But slow breathing is already known to influence heart-rate variability, baroreflex function, arousal, carbon dioxide, and interoceptive attention.

Changing the breath changes several coupled systems at once.

The practitioner does not manipulate one isolated brain rhythm.

They change the body’s timing environment.

---

This complexity explains why breathing techniques should not be reduced to “more oxygen.”

Deep or rapid breathing can lower carbon dioxide and produce dizziness, tingling, or distress.

Slow breathing can be calming for many people but uncomfortable for others.

Retention practices create additional physiological demands.

Traditional systems distinguish practices because breathing is not one generic wellness switch.

The current essays concern conceptual and scientific parallels, not instructions for advanced breath control.

The safest insight requires no heroic manipulation.

Ordinary respiration is already part of cognition.

---

The ritual number 21,600 compresses this ordinariness into sacred attention.

No special trance is required for breath to mark time.

The mantra is called unrecited because the body performs it before intention.

This reverses ordinary spiritual hierarchy.

Practice does not add sacred rhythm to an inert organism.

It recognizes that life has been repeating a rhythm unnoticed.

The deliberate mantra catches up with the involuntary one.

Awareness enters a process that was already happening.

---

Breath also destabilizes the boundary between voluntary and involuntary action.

You can choose to inhale now.

Soon, physiology resumes control.

Breathing belongs to conscious action, autonomic regulation, metabolism, emotion, and speech simultaneously.

It is both something you do and something that does you.

This makes it an ideal Tantric symbol.

Śiva as awareness.

Śakti as spontaneous power.

The individual can participate in the rhythm without becoming its sole author.

Breathing reveals agency nested inside a process larger than deliberate will.

---

The fifteen Nityās divide the waxing cycle, while the sixteenth power is associated with fullness.

A modern reader need not accept the ritual astronomy as physiology to understand the experiential mechanism.

Attention follows phases.

Difference becomes patterned.

Pattern becomes memory.

The cycle acquires a centre.

Instead of treating each breath as disposable, the practitioner encounters respiration as recurrence within one living whole.

Time becomes intimate enough to feel.

The day is no longer an abstract container.

It is an accumulation of embodied turns.

---

This can change the meaning of the present.

Clock time is homogeneous.

One second is equivalent to another in measurement.

Lived time is not.

A held breath differs from a released breath.

Waiting differs from arrival.

Fear accelerates the horizon.

Grief makes minutes heavy.

The respiratory cycle is one place where objective count and lived phase meet.

Four seconds can be a ritual unit.

The same four seconds can feel open, strained, calm, or urgent.

Time enters consciousness through a body with stakes.

---

You do not merely breathe through time.

Breath repeatedly gives the nervous system a phase from which to perceive, remember, and act.

The Nityās turn those phases into goddesses.

Neuroscience turns them into measurable coupling, excitability, and context.

The languages should not be collapsed.

The sacred number is not a universal respiratory fact.

The neural rhythm is not proof that the moon lives in the lungs.

But both refuse one mistake:

the body does not simply occupy time from outside.

Breath is the clock the brain cannot ignore.

Every cycle divides the present, coordinates a living field, and disappears.

What Tantra calls the unrecited mantra may be the oldest timing signal you have never stopped pronouncing.
