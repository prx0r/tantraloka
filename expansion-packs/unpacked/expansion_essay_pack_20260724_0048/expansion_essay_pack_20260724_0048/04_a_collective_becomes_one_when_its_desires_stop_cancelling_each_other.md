# a collective becomes one when its desires stop cancelling each other

A body contains many appetites.

Cells seek nutrients.

Organs protect local variables.

The immune system attacks.

The nervous system avoids pain.

The reproductive system prepares futures the conscious person may not want.

Most of the time, these tendencies do not appear as separate citizens.

They arrive as one decision:

I am hungry.

I am afraid.

I am moving.

The Kaula traditions call the integrated field *kula*—family, group, embodied totality.

A collective becomes one when its desires stop cancelling each other.

---

The *Kaulajñānanirṇaya*, attributed within tradition to Matsyendranātha, preserves an early Yoginī Kaula body.

Its chakra system is not the standardized seven-centre diagram familiar from modern yoga culture.

The material presented on Shiva Shakti Mandalam describes an eightfold arrangement connected with sixty-four yoginīs.

The body is organized as a differentiated host of powers.

Centres, petals, goddesses, breaths, and channels create a distributed sacred anatomy.

The practitioner is not imagined as one simple will controlling passive flesh.

Embodiment is a congress.

---

The word *kula* carries several linked meanings.

Family.

Clan.

Aggregate.

Body.

The manifested field of Śakti.

A Kaula is not merely someone who belongs to a social sect.

The doctrine asks how multiplicity can function as one living totality without erasing difference.

The yoginīs remain many.

The cakras remain distinct.

The senses and bodily processes have different tendencies.

Their unity is not uniformity.

It is coordination around a shared centre of practice and recognition.

The family becomes one through relation, not through reduction to one member.

---

Complexity science encounters the same problem.

How do many locally active components produce a higher-level agent?

Cells regulate their own membranes, energy, stress, and reproduction.

They possess limited competencies.

A tissue coordinates shape.

An organ coordinates a specialized function.

An organism coordinates movement, memory, and future action.

The higher level does not replace the lower ones.

It recruits them.

A heart cell remains a cell while participating in a heartbeat no single cell can generate alone.

Agency becomes nested.

---

Michael Levin describes biological selves through the scale of goals they can pursue.

A cell may regulate local voltage.

A tissue may restore anatomical polarity.

An animal may pursue food across space and remember danger across time.

Communication enlarges the problem-space available to the collective.

Gap junctions, nerves, hormones, mechanical forces, and chemical signals allow local agents to contribute to distant outcomes.

The “self” becomes the scale at which error is detected and corrected.

A body is one because disturbances in one region can become relevant elsewhere.

Concern crosses local boundaries.

---

This creates conflict as well as unity.

Cancer can be interpreted partly as local cellular success opposed to organism-level survival.

An autoimmune response protects according to one rule while harming the body under another.

Addiction recruits learning and reward systems into patterns that conflict with wider goals.

The components are not incompetent.

They are misaligned across scales.

A lower-level valence says:

continue this action.

A higher-level valence says:

this continuation destroys the life.

The self fractures when its reward systems optimize incompatible futures.

---

Valence means the positive or negative significance assigned to a state.

Pleasure and pain are familiar forms, but valence can be defined more broadly as movement toward or away from preferred conditions.

A cell need not feel pleasure like a human to behave as though some states are better for it.

A person, however, experiences valence.

Warmth comforts.

Threat contracts the field.

Success changes energy and expectation.

Valence gives direction to information.

A signal without value describes.

A valued signal recruits action.

---

A 2024 active-inference model derives valence from the difference between expected and obtained—or newly expected—utility, while treating arousal through uncertainty in posterior beliefs.

The exact equations are one proposal among several.

Affective science does not possess one settled computational definition of feeling.

But the model captures an important structure.

Valence tracks whether the agent’s prospects appear to be improving or worsening relative to its expectations.

Emotion is not simply attached to an object.

It evaluates the changing relation between system and possible future.

The same event can feel different when expectations change.

---

In a collective, whose prospects count?

A colony may sacrifice individuals.

An organism may suppress cellular reproduction.

A social group may demand actions harmful to one member.

Higher-level unity creates power by subordinating local values.

This can produce health or exploitation.

The mere emergence of a collective goal does not make it good.

A tumour is a collective.

A mob is a collective.

An empire coordinates enormous complexity.

Integration must be evaluated by which agents are included in the value function and whose suffering becomes invisible.

The scale of agency and the quality of agency are different questions.

---

Kaula language offers a corrective because the family is not supposed to become a dead hierarchy.

The many powers are divinized.

Each yoginī has a place.

The senses are not enemies.

The body is not raw matter beneath one disembodied ruler.

Unity is achieved through participation in Śakti.

This does not mean historical Kaula communities were free of hierarchy or conflict.

It means the ritual body imagines coordination through empowered plurality.

The centre is meaningful because the surrounding powers remain active.

A mandala without differentiated figures is only a circle.

---

The sixty-four yoginīs can therefore be read as an image of high-dimensional agency.

Not sixty-four neurons.

Not a literal computational architecture.

A symbolic reminder that the body’s intelligence exceeds conscious narration.

Digestion, balance, immunity, posture, memory, hormonal cycles, and sensory filtering operate through partially autonomous systems.

The conscious “I” receives their negotiated result.

It often mistakes the final decision for a command issued from one throne.

In reality, the throne is a summary generated by the court.

---

A 2026 multiscale kinetic framework for living systems emphasizes heterogeneity, adaptive decision-making, nonlinear interaction, and cross-scale feedback.

The model treats activity at one scale as emerging from lower-scale regulation while being influenced by higher-scale organization.

Competition and cooperation occur simultaneously.

The mathematical framework is not a theory of yoginīs.

It describes why a single-scale account fails.

A living system has no one privileged level at which causation resides.

Local actions create global states.

Global states reshape local options.

The collective continually renegotiates itself.

---

This is also why complexity cannot be measured only by number of parts.

A pile of cells is not necessarily an organism.

Sixty-four disconnected agents do not form a kula.

Complexity depends upon structured interaction.

Which components communicate?

Which signals integrate?

Which local differences remain preserved?

Which conflicts are resolved?

Which goals become common?

A perfectly centralized system loses local intelligence.

A perfectly decentralized system may fail to coordinate.

The living collective occupies a difficult middle.

Many voices.

Enough common rhythm to act.

---

Valence may be the glue.

Components align when the same event begins to count as better or worse across levels.

Tissue damage becomes locally painful, immunologically significant, behaviourally avoidable, and memorable.

One event receives several evaluations that converge toward repair.

The organism acts as one because different subsystems are recruited by compatible concern.

The process need not be harmonious.

Pain may protect the body while overwhelming the person.

Inflammation may heal while causing damage.

Unity is negotiated through competing costs.

A higher-order self appears when enough evaluations point toward one future.

---

Ritual can modify this alignment.

A vow changes which outcomes count as success.

A mantra changes attention and emotional association.

A communal rite synchronizes movement, sound, expectation, and memory.

The participant’s local impulses enter a larger pattern.

This can deepen agency by extending the horizon beyond immediate appetite.

It can also become coercive if collective value suppresses individual reality.

Kaula practice therefore cannot be understood as mere fusion.

A meaningful family must preserve the capacity of its members while creating shared action.

Otherwise integration becomes consumption.

---

The personal problem is often not lack of willpower.

It is disagreement among scales.

One part wants safety.

Another wants intimacy.

One wants immediate relief.

Another wants a future not dominated by relief-seeking.

One value is verbal.

Another is written into bodily prediction.

The person issues a command and discovers that the collective did not consent.

Calling this weakness makes coordination harder.

The system needs negotiation.

What threat is being protected against?

Which reward is too local?

What larger goal can include the smaller need without obeying its first policy?

---

The Kaula image suggests that integration begins by recognizing the powers rather than denying them.

Fear is given a place.

Desire is given a place.

Sensation, memory, and thought are acknowledged as active members.

Then relation changes.

The aim is not for every impulse to receive authority.

It is for no major subsystem to remain so excluded that it must sabotage the whole to become visible.

A family becomes governable when its conflicts can enter shared awareness.

The hidden yoginī becomes dangerous because she has no recognised seat.

---

A body contains many appetites.

It acts as one when those appetites become organized around a future large enough to recruit them.

The *Kaulajñānanirṇaya* imagines embodiment as a network of sacred centres and powers.

Complexity science describes nested agents, cross-scale feedback, and emergent goals.

Valence explains why the network moves rather than merely computes.

A collective becomes one when its desires stop cancelling each other.

Not because every desire becomes identical.

Because enough of them learn to recognise the same life as the thing they are trying to preserve.
