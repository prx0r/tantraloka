# Sources

## 1. look outward without leaving the heart

### Primary and traditional sources
- Abhinavagupta, *Tantrāloka*, especially materials concerning inward and outward contemplative orientation and the integration of ritual awareness.
- Kṣemarāja, *Pratyabhijñāhṛdayam*, commentarial material describing the inwardly anchored, outwardly directed gaze.
- Mark S. G. Dyczkowski, *The Doctrine of Vibration*.
- Lilian Silburn, *Kundalini: The Energy of the Depths*.

### Scholarship
- Jeffrey S. Lidke, “The Potential of the Bi-Directional Gaze,” *Religions* 7.11 (2016), DOI: 10.3390/rel7110132.
- Jeffrey S. Lidke, “A Day in the Life of an Aesthetic Tāntrika,” *Religions* 9.3 (2018), DOI: 10.3390/rel9030081.

### Epistemic note
The essay treats the Tantric states historically and phenomenologically. Proposed autonomic-neuroscience interpretations remain hypotheses rather than established experimental findings.

## 2. your senses are already eating the universe

### Primary source
- Abhinavagupta, *Tantrāloka* 3.262–264, on objects cast into the oblation-consuming consciousness and the divine sense-powers feasting upon the universe.

### Scholarship
- Alexis Sanderson, scholarship on aesthetic intensity, divinized senses, and nondual Śaiva ritual.
- Jeffrey S. Lidke, “A Day in the Life of an Aesthetic Tāntrika,” *Religions* 9.3 (2018).
- Abhinavagupta’s aesthetic writings and scholarship on *rasa* and universalized emotion.

### Epistemic note
“Sacrifice,” “fire,” “deities,” and “nectar” describe Abhinavagupta’s ritual-metaphysical model. They are not claims about literal combustion in sensory physiology.

## 3. nature builds bodies by looking inward

### Primary philosophical source
- Plotinus, *Enneads* III.8, “On Nature, Contemplation, and the One.”
- Lloyd P. Gerson et al., eds. and trans., *Plotinus: The Enneads*, Cambridge University Press.

### Scholarship
- John N. Deck, *Nature, Contemplation, and the One*.
- Scholarship on Plotinus’s account of Nature as silent, non-discursive contemplation and production.
- “Plotinus’s Language of Seeing: Marsilio Ficino on Enneads V.3, V.8 and III.8,” *International Journal of the Classical Tradition*.

### Current biology
- Custodio O. Nunes and Elias H. Barriga, “Bioelectricity in Morphogenesis,” *Annual Review of Cell and Developmental Biology* 41 (2025), DOI: 10.1146/annurev-cellbio-101323-032747.
- Current work on morphogenesis, tissue mechanics, developmental signaling, and basal cognition.

### Epistemic note
Plotinian contemplation is not equated with bioelectric signaling or cellular cognition. The comparison concerns immanent form-production without an external craftsman.

## 4. life is the loop that makes the loop

### Philosophical sources
- Proclus, *Elements of Theology*, propositions 40–51 concerning reversion and self-constituted beings.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Proclus.”
- Recent scholarship on Proclus’s *authypostaton* and the self-motion of soul.

### Biology and systems theory
- Humberto Maturana and Francisco Varela on molecular autopoiesis.
- Sergio Rubin, “Cartography of the multiple formal systems of molecular autopoiesis,” *BioSystems* 230 (2023), DOI: 10.1016/j.biosystems.2023.104955.
- “Flowing boundaries in autopoietic systems and microniche construction,” *BioSystems* 254 (2025), DOI: 10.1016/j.biosystems.2025.105477.
- Samson Abramsky et al., “Open Questions about Time and Self-reference in Living Systems” (2025 preprint).

### Epistemic note
Proclean self-constitution concerns incorporeal soul in a metaphysical hierarchy. Autopoiesis concerns molecular organization. Similarity of recursive structure is not identity of entity or evidence.

## 5. form needs a place that has no form

### Primary philosophical source
- Plato, *Timaeus* 48e–53b, especially 50–51 on the receptacle or *chōra*.
- Perseus Digital Library, *Timaeus* 51a, W. R. M. Lamb translation.
- Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, “Plato’s Timaeus,” section on the receptacle.

### Biology
- Nunes and Barriga, “Bioelectricity in Morphogenesis,” *Annual Review of Cell and Developmental Biology* 41 (2025).
- “A Morphospace Exploration Using a General Model of Development Reveals a Basic Set of Morphologies for Early Animal Development and Evolution,” 2024.
- Michael Levin and collaborators on morphospace, target morphology, developmental bioelectricity, and anatomical state spaces.

### Epistemic note
Chōra is an ontological principle in Platonic cosmology. Morphospace is a researcher-defined abstract model. Neither is used as evidence for the other.
