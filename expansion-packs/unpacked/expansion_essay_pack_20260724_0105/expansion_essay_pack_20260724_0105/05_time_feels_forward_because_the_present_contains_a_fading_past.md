# time feels forward because the present contains a fading past

A melody does not exist in one note.

By the time the second note arrives, the first is physically gone.

Yet it remains close enough to shape what the second becomes.

A rising interval.

A resolution.

A mistake.

If the first note vanished completely, there would be no melody.

If it remained with full force, the notes would pile into noise.

Time is felt through a controlled disappearance.

QRI calls one model of this structure the **pseudo-time arrow**.

The Krama tradition of Kashmir calls cognition a sequence of goddesses who manifest, stabilize, consume, and return experience to the unnameable ground.

Time feels forward because the present contains a fading past.

---

Physical time and phenomenal time are not identical.

A clock measures duration.

Experience stretches and contracts it.

Pain makes a minute heavy.

Absorption makes an hour disappear.

Shock can slow the world.

Dreams can compress years.

Altered states can produce loops, timelessness, or a sense that events are happening simultaneously.

Any theory of consciousness must therefore explain not only how the brain tracks external sequence but why sequence has a particular felt structure.

The arrow of physical causation does not automatically explain the arrow inside experience.

---

QRI’s pseudo-time-arrow model begins from discrete or approximately discrete moments of consciousness.

Each moment is represented as a network of locally bound qualia.

Colours bind to edges.

Sounds bind into objects.

Body sensations bind to locations.

The current moment also contains traces of immediately preceding moments.

These traces decay with temporal distance.

The most recent past remains vivid.

Older layers become fainter and sparser.

Within one present experience, the structure therefore contains an asymmetry.

One direction leads toward increasingly faded traces.

That direction is experienced as the past.

---

The model is similar to motion blur, but more general.

When a ball moves, the experienced present may contain the current position plus weaker remnants of earlier positions.

The ordering can be recovered from the gradient of decay.

QRI proposes that implicit causal structure inside this bound network generates a pseudo-arrow of time.

The present does not need to travel through physical time phenomenologically.

Its internal organization already points backward.

The flow is not an extra substance.

It is the shape of retention.

---

The proposal explains why complete erasure would destroy temporal experience.

Without traces, each moment would be a disconnected image.

No motion.

No melody.

No continuity.

It also explains why excessive retention could distort time.

If past layers faded too slowly, the present might become crowded with repetitions.

Events could feel looped.

Temporal order might flatten into a “moment of eternity.”

If the local causal structure became scrambled, the direction of time might become uncertain or unpleasantly fragmented.

These are toy-model deductions, not established neural diagnoses of unusual experiences.

---

The pseudo-time arrow is formal and phenomenological.

QRI has not identified one proven neural circuit implementing the graph exactly as described.

The model assumes a local-binding network and a particular relation between causal structure and felt temporality.

Both assumptions remain disputed.

Still, the underlying problem has attracted serious parallel work.

A 2024 arXiv paper from an Integrated Information Theory perspective argues that the conscious present can feel flowing because it contains directed phenomenal distinctions related by inclusion, connection, and fusion.

Time, on that account, is a cause-effect structure specified in the current state rather than a movie literally unrolling inside the brain.

---

Another 2024 arXiv proposal models subjective temporal extension through self-simulation and active inference.

A temporally deep generative model integrates past and expected future across hierarchical scales.

The width of the experienced “now” changes with attention, action, valence, boredom, flow, and meditative absorption.

The proposal suggests that subjective time is constructed through how a self-model simulates its own persistence.

This differs from QRI’s fading-trace graph.

Both reject the idea that temporal experience merely mirrors a clock.

The present is built.

Its apparent duration depends upon what the system must integrate to remain one agent.

---

The Krama tradition begins with sequence as sacred structure.

*Krama* means order, succession, or progression.

In Kashmir, the tradition internalized goddess worship as phases of cognition.

The Kālīs are not only external deities arranged in ritual order.

They represent powers through which awareness projects an object, apprehends it, assimilates it, and dissolves the subject-object structure.

Ritual sequence becomes a visible enactment of an invisible cognitive sequence.

The mind does not simply encounter a finished object.

It produces and consumes the phases through which the object becomes present.

---

Abhinavagupta receives the doctrine of the twelve Kālīs and interprets it as the arising of the wheel of consciousness—*saṃviccakrodaya*.

Later commentary groups the phases around the object, the means of knowledge, and the knowing subject.

The goddess-consciousness manifests these forms as though distinct and then devours their temporal differentiation.

This is not annihilation of cognition.

It is recognition that object, knowing, and knower are phases within one self-articulating consciousness.

Sequence is real as manifestation.

It is not final as separation.

---

This produces a very different time arrow.

QRI’s arrow points backward through decaying traces.

Krama’s sequence moves through production and reabsorption.

An appearance arises.

Attention grasps it.

Knowledge becomes determinate.

The object is consumed into awareness.

The next sequence becomes possible.

The direction of experience is not created only by memory fading.

It is created by a cycle of manifestation whose later phases transform the status of earlier ones.

The object begins as external.

It ends as assimilated knowledge.

Time is the route through which otherness becomes consciousness again.

---

The two models meet in the importance of **ordered loss**.

A perception must disappear enough for another to arise.

It must remain enough to be recognized, compared, and integrated.

The Krama goddess consumes the object.

The pseudo-time trace decays.

Neither process simply deletes information.

Consumption changes the relation of the content to the whole.

The note is no longer present as sound.

It remains as musical direction.

The object is no longer present as immediate perception.

It remains as knowledge, memory, disposition, or recognized manifestation.

The past survives through changed status.

---

This makes phenomenal time inseparable from memory.

Memory is not always deliberate recall.

Retention begins before narrative memory.

A word remains long enough for a sentence.

A bodily movement retains its beginning while completing its arc.

An emotion carries the interpretation that produced it.

The present has thickness because previous states continue exerting patterned influence.

William James called this the specious present.

Husserl spoke of retention.

QRI turns the intuition into a graph.

Krama turns it into a goddess cycle.

Both say that a point-like now could not contain lived time.

---

Valence changes the thickness and direction of the present.

In fear, attention samples rapidly and future threat becomes dominant.

In boredom, little changes while the system monitors elapsed duration.

In flow, action and feedback align so closely that explicit time-monitoring recedes.

Pain can make each moment resist disappearance.

Pleasure can create rhythmic continuity in which traces fit rather than clash.

QRI connects symmetry across the pseudo-time arrow with positive valence.

Repeated structures across temporal layers could produce consonance.

This remains speculative, but music gives the idea intuitive force.

Pleasure can be symmetry extended through memory.

---

Krama adds the problem of appropriation.

Time becomes oppressive when every phase is organized around maintaining one contracted owner.

What will happen to me?

What did this mean about me?

How can I preserve this?

The past is retained as identity.

The future is projected as completion or threat.

The present becomes a narrow checkpoint between debt and demand.

The Kālī cycle consumes this structure.

Object, knowledge, and knower are successively recognized as consciousness rather than as independent possessions.

Time is “devoured” when sequence no longer produces absolute separation.

---

This does not mean clocks stop or memory disappears.

Contemplative “timelessness” can refer to several different phenomena.

Reduced attention to duration.

Weakening of autobiographical projection.

A highly stable present.

Loss of ordinary sequence.

Intense absorption.

Some forms are peaceful.

Others are disorienting.

No report should be automatically interpreted as metaphysical access to eternity.

The Krama tradition provides a ritual-philosophical interpretation.

QRI provides candidate structures.

Neuroscience must still identify mechanisms and distinguish states that sound similar in language.

---

The strongest bridge is recognition.

A fading trace alone can explain continuity of content.

It may not explain why the current experience apprehends the trace as its own past.

Pratyabhijñā philosophy argues that memory and recognition presuppose a reflexive subject spanning changing cognitions.

The subject is not one unchanging image carried across moments.

It is the capacity through which moments can be related as one history.

QRI models the temporal structure inside a conscious slice.

Pratyabhijñā asks what knows the structure as temporal.

Krama models the phases.

Recognition identifies their ground.

---

Practice can make the construction visible.

Listen to a sound until it ends.

Notice that “ending” is not immediate nonexistence.

The sound fades through retention.

Now listen for the next sound.

The field is shaped by expectation.

Past and future are present differently.

Then notice the one who says:

the sound was there;

now it is gone.

That statement gathers several moments.

The purpose is not to solve the philosophy through introspection.

It is to encounter the ordinary miracle the theories are trying to explain.

The present remembers enough to know that it is passing.

---

A melody does not exist in one note.

It exists in the controlled survival and disappearance of notes across a living present.

QRI’s pseudo-time arrow makes fading traces into a directed phenomenal graph.

Krama makes cognition into a wheel of goddesses who manifest and consume object, knowledge, and knower.

Time feels forward because the present contains a fading past.

Liberation, in the Krama sense, is not escaping sequence.

It is recognizing the power that becomes the sequence, devours each phase, and remains available when the final trace has disappeared.
