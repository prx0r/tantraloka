# the same state can feel like birth from one direction and death from the other

Half-moon.

The geometry is identical.

Seen while the moon is waxing, it feels like arrival.

Seen while the moon is waning, it feels like disappearance.

The amount of light does not determine the meaning.

Direction does.

Shiva Shakti Mandalam presents two parallel cycles of fifteen Nityās.

The Lalitā Nityās belong to the waxing moon and are described through bright, affirmative powers.

The Kālī Nityās belong to the waning moon and carry darker, dissolving, sometimes threatening forms.

The same state can feel like birth from one direction and death from the other.

---

A static description cannot distinguish the two moons.

Same visible fraction.

Same approximate brightness.

Same shape.

To know whether it is waxing or waning, the observer needs memory.

What was the moon like yesterday?

What trajectory is the current state part of?

The present carries a sign only through relation to a past and anticipated future.

Without temporal context, increase and decrease collapse into one image.

The phase is not enough.

Meaning belongs to the derivative.

---

The Nityās are “eternities” divided into temporal phases.

In Śrīvidyā, the fifteen Lalitā Nityās correspond to the lunar days of waxing.

The full cycle culminates in fullness associated with the sixteenth power.

In the Kālī materials on the site, another set occupies the waning half.

The iconography and ritual applications differ sharply.

One series presents expansion, beauty, attraction, prosperity, and increasing luminosity.

The other presents time, dissolution, fierce protection, severance, and the stripping away of form.

The moon supplies one geometry.

Tradition reads the geometry through direction.

---

This is not only symbolic astronomy.

It reveals a structure of valence.

Pleasure and suffering are often treated as properties of states.

This temperature is pleasant.

This sound is unpleasant.

This amount of wealth is satisfying.

But organisms evaluate trajectories.

A temperature of twenty degrees feels warm after winter air and cold after a hot bath.

The same balance feels relieving when debt is shrinking and frightening when savings are disappearing.

The same solitude feels peaceful after overstimulation and painful after rejection.

Valence belongs partly to whether the system believes its prospects are improving or worsening.

---

Active-inference models make this directional structure explicit.

A 2024 arXiv paper places emotion in a two-dimensional circumplex of valence and arousal.

It derives a candidate valence signal from the difference between obtained or expected utility and what the agent previously expected.

Arousal is related to uncertainty in posterior beliefs.

The model is one computational proposal, not a final theory of emotion.

Its important move is to make valence dynamic.

A state is positive not only because it occupies a preferred region.

It can feel positive because the agent is moving toward a better expected future.

---

This distinction explains relief.

The body may still be injured.

The danger may not be completely gone.

Yet one signal indicates improvement.

Valence changes before the final state becomes good.

Dread works in reverse.

The current moment may be tolerable.

The model predicts worsening.

The future contaminates the present.

The organism does not simply inhabit a point in state-space.

It experiences the direction and expected velocity of movement.

The Nityā is not only a phase.

She is the phase as becoming.

---

Mood adds longer memory.

A 2024 study of dynamic emotion inference found that longer-term mood context improved predictions of moment-to-moment valence when combined with short-term emotional change.

The paper concerns machine analysis of video, not the inner generation of mood.

Still, its design reflects an established affective fact.

A brief event is interpreted against a slower background.

A neutral comment lands differently during trust and during suspicion.

A small success feels different inside hope and exhaustion.

The fast phase is embedded within a slow cycle.

Lunar time offers a ritual model of this nesting.

---

The same arousal can also receive opposite emotional labels.

A Bayesian drift-diffusion model published as a 2024 arXiv paper revisits the classic two-factor theory of emotion.

Physiological arousal is combined with contextual evidence until an emotional interpretation crosses a decision boundary.

The authors model conditions in which similar bodily activation becomes euphoria, anger, or reduced fear depending on context and attribution.

This does not mean emotions are arbitrary stories.

It means bodily state underdetermines emotional meaning.

The trajectory, setting, prior expectation, and available label participate in the result.

---

The Kālī and Lalitā cycles enact two contexts for the same lunar mathematics.

Waxing says:

more form is becoming available.

Waning says:

form is returning to darkness.

Neither direction is simply good or bad.

Increase can become excess.

Decrease can become release.

A growing tumour and a healing wound move in opposite directions under different value functions.

Accumulation is not inherently positive.

Dissolution is not inherently negative.

Valence depends upon what organization is being preserved and what future the transition opens.

---

This is why Kālī’s negative imagery cannot be reduced to pessimism.

Dissolution may be terrifying to the form being dissolved.

It may be liberating to the larger system trapped by that form.

An old identity weakens.

A compulsion loses energy.

A social role ends.

The immediate experience can be grief, uncertainty, or loss of control.

The trajectory may create new freedom.

The waning phase has negative local valence and positive global consequence.

The darkness is not pleasant merely because a tantra sacralizes it.

Its value depends upon what is being released.

---

Lalitā presents the complementary danger.

Expansion, attraction, beauty, and increase can become bondage when the system treats more as the only direction of flourishing.

More experience.

More recognition.

More possessions.

More intensity.

The waxing movement feels positive because it predicts increasing capacity.

At fullness, further increase becomes impossible.

The cycle must turn.

A system unable to reinterpret decrease as part of life experiences every decline as failure.

Lalitā without Kālī becomes accumulation unable to complete itself.

---

This creates a Tantric opponent process.

Every expansion generates the conditions of contraction.

Every intense pleasure adapts.

Every acquisition changes the baseline from which the next lack is measured.

Every form excludes alternatives and therefore carries its own limit.

The tradition does not merely place a bright goddess beside a dark goddess.

It constructs a complete temporal metabolism.

One power differentiates.

One power consumes differentiation.

The whole remains alive because neither direction claims permanence.

---

Contemporary affective science often uses the valence–arousal plane.

Pleasant calm.

Pleasant excitement.

Unpleasant agitation.

Unpleasant low energy.

The map is useful, but it is still a map of positions.

The Nityā comparison adds an arrow.

Where did the state come from?

What is it becoming?

What rate of change is expected?

What identity gains or loses control through the transition?

Two experiences can occupy the same valence–arousal coordinates while belonging to different narratives of movement.

One is opening.

One is collapse.

Static coordinates miss the phase history.

---

This is close to hysteresis in dynamical systems.

A system’s present output can depend upon the path by which it reached the current input.

Heating and cooling may produce different transitions at the same temperature.

A magnetic material can retain history after the external field changes.

Emotional and biological systems also carry memory.

The present state is not a function of current stimulus alone.

Previous trajectories alter thresholds, expectations, and available responses.

The waning half-moon is not physically marked with an arrow.

History supplies it.

---

The ritual calendar externalizes this memory.

A practitioner does not need to infer the phase from one glance.

The sequence is named.

Each Nityā has a place.

The cycle tells the participant how to interpret transition.

This can organize emotion by giving loss a lawful position rather than treating it as accidental collapse.

The form is not disappearing into meaningless absence.

It is entering a recognized phase of the whole.

Ritual time provides a container for direction.

It can make change intelligible before it becomes comfortable.

---

The danger is fatalism.

A sacred cycle should not be used to romanticize preventable loss or tell suffering people that decline is cosmically necessary.

Medical, social, and personal harms still require response.

The lunar analogy concerns the structure of interpretation.

Some forms of decrease are destructive.

Others are repair.

The difference must be judged through evidence, value, and consequence.

Calling every collapse “Kālī” can become an excuse for neglect.

A symbol is profound only while it preserves discrimination.

---

The practical insight is to ask a better question than:

How do I feel?

Ask:

What direction does the system believe it is moving?

Anxiety may be fear of accelerating loss.

Grief may be the nervous system learning a future from which someone is absent.

Relief may be the first evidence that worsening has stopped.

Restlessness may arise when growth is expected but blocked.

The feeling becomes more intelligible when placed on a trajectory.

State and direction together reveal the value model.

---

Half-moon.

The geometry is identical.

One tradition calls the phase an increasing eternity of Lalitā.

Another places its counterpart in Kālī’s waning cycle.

The same state can feel like birth from one direction and death from the other because valence is not located in the snapshot alone.

It belongs to memory, expectation, and the life whose form is changing.

The moon does not tell you whether to celebrate or mourn.

The arrow does—

and the deepest wisdom is learning when the arrow of loss is also the route by which another form becomes possible.
