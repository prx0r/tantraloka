# knowledge binds by making the world small enough to use

A living organism cannot perceive everything.

It cannot remember every detail.

It cannot test every interpretation before acting.

To survive, it must discard.

The nervous system compresses a world too large to inhabit into distinctions small enough to use:

food;

threat;

face;

path;

mine.

The *Śiva Sūtras* opens its second movement with a sentence as brutal as it is compact:

> *jñānaṃ bandhaḥ*  
> Knowledge is bondage.

The claim is not that truth enslaves and ignorance liberates.

It is that every usable knowledge closes a larger field into one determinate world.

Knowledge binds by making the world small enough to use.

---

The first section of the *Śiva Sūtras* identifies consciousness as the Self.

The next sutra declares knowledge bondage.

Read carelessly, the two statements conflict.

If consciousness is primary, why should knowing bind it?

The answer depends upon the meaning of knowledge.

The text is not attacking awareness itself.

It is attacking contracted knowledge: cognition that divides reality into fixed knower, known object, and rule connecting them.

The world becomes “this and not that.”

The subject becomes “the one who knows these things but not those.”

Each determination is useful.

Each determination hides the freedom that produced it.

---

A concept works by exclusion.

To recognize a chair, the system ignores almost everything about the object.

Exact molecular composition.

Every scratch.

Its complete manufacturing history.

The changing light across its surface.

The air moving around it.

The concept preserves features relevant to sitting, moving, buying, avoiding, or describing.

The object becomes manageable because most of it disappears.

This is not failure.

A representation containing every physical detail would be as difficult to use as the world itself.

Knowledge requires a bottleneck.

---

The Information Bottleneck principle formalizes this trade-off.

A representation should retain information useful for predicting a target while discarding variation irrelevant to that task.

Compression without prediction produces ignorance.

Prediction without compression produces an unusable copy.

The useful representation lies between them.

Modern machine learning uses this principle to construct low-dimensional features from high-dimensional data.

A current image classifier does not need to preserve every pixel.

It needs a representation that remains stable across lighting, angle, noise, and irrelevant background while separating categories important to the task.

Knowing becomes selective invariance.

---

A 2024 arXiv paper generalizes task-oriented lossy compression across reconstruction, perceptual quality, and classification.

Its mathematics concerns communication and machine learning, not spiritual bondage.

But the result exposes a universal structure.

Different tasks require different compressions.

A representation optimized for classification may discard features required for faithful reconstruction.

A representation preserving perceptual appearance may not preserve the variables needed for another decision.

There is no single “compressed truth” independent of purpose.

Every useful model preserves one family of differences by sacrificing others.

The goal decides what becomes real enough to retain.

---

This is why two people can occupy different worlds without either hallucinating the entire environment.

A botanist sees species.

A carpenter sees grain, stress, and joinery.

A frightened person sees exits and uncertain faces.

A child sees climbable surfaces.

The sensory field overlaps.

The bottleneck differs.

Memory, training, bodily state, and purpose determine which variations are compressed together and which distinctions are amplified.

The world does not arrive neutrally and receive interpretation afterward.

It appears through a task-shaped reduction.

---

The *Śiva Sūtras* calls this bondage because the reduction is forgotten.

A model that knows itself as a model remains flexible.

A model mistaken for the final structure of reality becomes a prison.

“I failed” compresses many events into one category.

The category predicts future behaviour.

Attention selects supporting evidence.

Memory retrieves compatible scenes.

Alternative descriptions become invisible because the representation has already discarded the features through which they could appear.

The concept does not merely summarize the past.

It controls what future can be learned.

---

Recent machine-learning research describes memorization and compression as alternating processes.

A 2025 arXiv paper reports cycles in which models first absorb detailed patterns and later compress representations, improving generalization and reducing interference under the authors’ experiments.

The comparison to human learning should remain cautious.

Large language models are not established models of conscious understanding.

Yet the cycle is suggestive.

Learning requires contact with detail.

Generalization requires selective forgetting.

A system unable to compress cannot transfer.

A system compressing too early repeats its prior categories and misses novelty.

Intelligence depends upon when the bottleneck tightens.

---

The Tantric critique is therefore not anti-conceptual.

Language, mantra, discrimination, ritual, and philosophy are all used.

The problem is identification with the product of discrimination.

A concept is one movement of consciousness.

Bondage occurs when consciousness appears only as the limited subject enclosed by that movement.

The knower says:

I am the one represented here.

The representation says:

Only what fits me is real.

The loop closes.

Knowledge becomes a local operating system that has forgotten it is running inside a larger field.

---

Kṣemarāja’s tradition distinguishes contracted knowledge from awakened knowledge.

In the second section of the *Śiva Sūtras*, awakened knowledge is connected with the circle of phonemic powers.

The same alphabet that binds through fixed concepts can liberate when recognized as living power.

This seems contradictory only if language is either true or false.

Tantric language can be binding at one level and revelatory at another.

A word binds when it hides the act of articulation.

A mantra can liberate when articulation becomes visible as consciousness’s own capacity.

The cage and key are made from the same letters.

---

Predictive processing gives this another form.

The nervous system uses prior expectations to interpret ambiguous signals.

Strong priors stabilize perception.

Without them, the world would be noisy and difficult to act within.

But excessive precision can make priors resistant to correction.

The system explains away conflicting evidence.

Anxiety interprets bodily arousal as danger.

Prejudice interprets ambiguous behaviour through a social category.

Habit interprets discomfort as a command to repeat the familiar action.

The model protects itself by changing the evidence it is willing to receive.

Compression becomes defensive.

---

The information bottleneck then becomes an ethical problem.

Which details are discarded when a person becomes a category?

Which experiences are treated as irrelevant to a diagnosis, identity, or theory?

A social system also compresses.

A school score reduces a student.

A credit score reduces a borrower.

A border reduces a traveller.

Compression makes large systems governable.

It also creates violence when the discarded dimensions contain the person’s actual needs.

The model remains efficient because the cost is paid by what it cannot represent.

---

This is the profound implication of *jñānaṃ bandhaḥ*.

Bondage is not merely having incorrect beliefs.

A perfectly accurate local model can bind if it claims universality.

The body is accurately located here.

The person’s knowledge is genuinely partial.

Cause and consequence operate.

The error is treating this scale of description as the whole nature of the knower.

The representation can be correct and metaphysically imprisoning.

A map does not need to be false to hide the existence of the landscape.

It needs only to become the only thing one is willing to see.

---

Recognition does not require throwing the map away.

It reveals the map as an activity within awareness.

Thought appears.

Category appears.

Prediction appears.

The feeling of certainty appears.

Each is known.

The one who knows cannot be found as another concept without generating another known object.

This is why Pratyabhijñā turns knowledge back toward the knower.

Not:

What final description contains me?

But:

What is present before, during, and after every description of me?

---

This can be practised in ordinary cognition.

Take one sentence that feels definitive:

I am incapable.

They do not respect me.

This sensation means danger.

Do not immediately replace it with a positive slogan.

Examine its compression.

Which events were merged?

Which differences were discarded?

What prediction does the sentence make cheap?

What evidence has become expensive to notice?

The sentence may contain truth.

Its bondage lies in the claim that nothing relevant remains outside it.

There is therefore no final escape from compression at the level of finite cognition. Even the doctrine “all is consciousness” is a summary that omits detail. The liberating difference is reflexive: the teaching points beyond its own conceptual boundary and remains available for revision in experience. A concept becomes transparent when it performs its task without claiming that its task is the measure of everything.

---

A living organism cannot perceive everything.

It survives by compressing.

The *Śiva Sūtras* does not ask the finite mind to become omniscient.

It asks consciousness to stop mistaking its necessary bottlenecks for the boundary of reality.

Knowledge binds by making the world small enough to use.

Wisdom begins when the user remembers that the compression was selected—

and that the field from which it was selected was never contained by the model.
